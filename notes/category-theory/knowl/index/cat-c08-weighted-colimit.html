Let \(P:\mathcal C^\op\to\Set_\U\) and \(A:\mathcal C\to\mathcal D\text{,}\) with \(\mathcal D\) cocomplete. The expression
\begin{equation*} P\star A:=\int^{c\in\mathcal C}P(c)\cdot A(c) \end{equation*}
means the coend of \(H(c,d)=P(c)\cdot A(d)\text{.}\) Consequently a map \(P\star A\to D\) is a family of arrows \(t_{c,x}:A(c)\to D\text{,}\) one for each \(x\in P(c)\text{,}\) satisfying
\begin{equation*} t_{c,P(f)(y)}=t_{d,y}A(f),\qquad f:c\to d,\quad y\in P(d). \end{equation*}
This is often called the colimit of \(A\) weighted by \(P\text{.}\) The displayed family description is its full ordinary-category meaning here; no separate theory of weights is needed.
