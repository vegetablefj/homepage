Definition 5.11 Mates and conjugate transformations.

The mates correspondence is the bijection
\begin{equation*} \Nat(FH,KF')\longrightarrow\Nat(HG',GK), \qquad\alpha\longmapsto\alpha^\sharp, \end{equation*}
defined by
\begin{equation*} \alpha^\sharp =(GK\varepsilon')\,(G\alpha G')\,(\eta HG'). \end{equation*}
Its inverse sends \(\beta:HG'\Rightarrow GK\) to
\begin{equation*} \beta^\flat =(\varepsilon KF')\,(F\beta F')\,(FH\eta'). \end{equation*}
Corresponding transformations are called mates. When \(\mathcal C'=\mathcal C\text{,}\) \(\mathcal D'=\mathcal D\text{,}\) and \(H,K\) are identity functors, they are also called conjugates: a transformation \(F\Rightarrow F'\) corresponds to a transformation \(G'\Rightarrow G\text{.}\)
