Theorem 3.2 Yoneda lemma.

Let \(\mathcal C\) be locally \(\U\)-small and set-encoded, and let \(X\in\mathcal C\text{.}\)
🔗
  1. For \(F:\mathcal C^\op\to\Set_\U\text{,}\) evaluation at the identity is a bijection
    \begin{equation*} \Phi_{X,F}:\Nat(h_X,F)\longrightarrow F(X),\qquad \eta\longmapsto\eta_X(\id_X). \end{equation*}
    Its inverse sends \(z\in F(X)\) to the transformation \(\eta^z\) given by
    \begin{equation*} \eta^z_T(f)=F(f)(z)\qquad(f:T\to X). \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
  2. For \(G:\mathcal C\to\Set_\U\text{,}\) there is a bijection
    \begin{equation*} \Psi_{X,G}:\Nat(h^X,G)\longrightarrow G(X),\qquad \eta\longmapsto\eta_X(\id_X), \end{equation*}
    whose inverse sends \(z\in G(X)\) to the transformation with component
    \begin{equation*} \eta^z_T(f)=G(f)(z)\qquad(f:X\to T). \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
🔗
Both bijections are natural in the object and in the set-valued functor.
🔗
🔗

Proof.
For the contravariant assertion, let \(z\in F(X)\text{.}\) If \(a:S\to T\) and \(f:T\to X\text{,}\) then
\begin{equation*} F(a)\bigl(\eta^z_T(f)\bigr) =F(a)F(f)(z)=F(fa)(z)=\eta^z_S\bigl(h_X(a)(f)\bigr). \end{equation*}
Thus the proposed components are natural. Evaluation gives \(\eta^z_X(\id_X)=F(\id_X)(z)=z\text{.}\)
🔗
Conversely, let \(\eta:h_X\Rightarrow F\) be natural, and put \(z=\eta_X(\id_X)\text{.}\) Apply its naturality equation for \(f:T\to X\) to \(\id_X\in h_X(X)\text{.}\) It gives
\begin{equation*} \eta_T(f)=\eta_T\bigl(h_X(f)(\id_X)\bigr) =F(f)\bigl(\eta_X(\id_X)\bigr)=F(f)(z). \end{equation*}
Hence \(\eta=\eta^z\text{,}\) proving that the displayed maps are inverse.
🔗
We spell out the two naturality assertions. If \(\alpha:F\Rightarrow F'\text{,}\) then
\begin{equation*} \Phi_{X,F'}(\alpha\eta)=\alpha_X\bigl(\Phi_{X,F}(\eta)\bigr). \end{equation*}
If \(u:X'\to X\text{,}\) then precomposition by \(h_u:h_{X'}\Rightarrow h_X\) satisfies
\begin{equation*} \Phi_{X',F}(\eta h_u) =\eta_{X'}(u) =F(u)\bigl(\Phi_{X,F}(\eta)\bigr). \end{equation*}
The first equality follows by evaluation, and the second is the naturality equation just used. These are precisely compatibility with arrows in the functor variable and in the object variable.
🔗
The covariant assertion is the contravariant assertion for \(\mathcal C^\op\text{.}\) Explicitly, naturality at \(a:S\to T\) reads \(G(a)G(f)(z)=G(af)(z)\) for \(f:X\to S\text{,}\) and recovery from the identity uses an arrow \(f:X\to T\text{.}\) For \(u:X\to Y\text{,}\) precomposition by \(u^*:h^Y\Rightarrow h^X\) corresponds under \(\Psi\) to \(G(u):G(X)\to G(Y)\text{.}\) Naturality in \(G\) again follows by evaluation.
