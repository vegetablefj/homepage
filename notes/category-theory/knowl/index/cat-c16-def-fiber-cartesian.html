Definition 16.1 Categories over a base and cartesian arrows.

  1. A category over \(\mathcal B\) is a functor \(p:\mathcal E\to\mathcal B\text{.}\) Its fiber \(\mathcal E_b\) has objects \(x\) with \(p(x)=b\) and arrows \(f:x\to y\) with \(p(f)=\id_b\text{.}\) Such arrows are called vertical. A functor over \(\mathcal B\) from \(p\) to \(q:\mathcal E'\to\mathcal B\) is a functor \(H:\mathcal E\to\mathcal E'\) satisfying \(qH=p\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  2. An arrow \(\chi:x\to y\text{,}\) with \(p(x)=a\text{,}\) \(p(y)=b\text{,}\) and \(p(\chi)=u:a\to b\text{,}\) is cartesian if the following factorization exists uniquely. For every \(z\in\mathcal E\text{,}\) every \(v:p(z)\to a\text{,}\) and every \(f:z\to y\) with \(p(f)=uv\text{,}\) there is a unique \(g:z\to x\) such that
    \begin{equation*} p(g)=v,\qquad \chi g=f. \end{equation*}
    In this situation \(\chi\) is a cartesian lift of \(u\) with target \(y\text{.}\)
