Proposition 5.4.

The unit and counit are natural and satisfy
\begin{equation*} \varepsilon_{FX}F(\eta_X)=\id_{FX},\qquad G(\varepsilon_Y)\eta_{GY}=\id_{GY}. \end{equation*}
These are the triangle identities. Conversely, any two natural transformations of the displayed types satisfying these identities determine an adjunction. The correspondences are
\begin{equation*} f^\sharp=G(f)\eta_X,\qquad g^\flat=\varepsilon_YF(g). \end{equation*}
For fixed \(F,G\text{,}\) this is a bijection between adjunction correspondences and pairs \((\eta,\varepsilon)\) satisfying the triangle identities.
🔗
🔗

Proof.
Naturality of \(\Phi\) applied to an identity gives the two transpose formulas. For \(a:X\to X'\text{,}\) apply the first formula to \(F(a)\text{,}\) and also use naturality in \(X\text{;}\) the resulting equality is \(GF(a)\eta_X=\eta_{X'}a\text{.}\) This proves naturality of \(\eta\text{.}\) The inverse correspondence proves naturality of \(\varepsilon\) in the same way.
🔗
By definition, \(\eta_X^\flat=\id_{FX}\) and \(\varepsilon_Y^\sharp=\id_{GY}\text{.}\) Substituting the transpose formulas gives the triangle identities.
🔗
Conversely, define the two maps by the stated formulas. Their naturality follows from that of \(\eta,\varepsilon\text{.}\) For \(f:FX\to Y\text{,}\) their composite is
\begin{equation*} \varepsilon_YFG(f)F(\eta_X) =f\varepsilon_{FX}F(\eta_X)=f. \end{equation*}
The first equality uses naturality of the counit and the second uses a triangle identity. For \(g:X\to GY\text{,}\) the other composite is
\begin{equation*} G(\varepsilon_Y)GF(g)\eta_X =G(\varepsilon_Y)\eta_{GY}g=g. \end{equation*}
Here naturality of the unit is followed by the other triangle identity. Finally, evaluating the correspondence and its inverse at identities recovers the original unit and counit, proving the assertion about data.
