Definition 17.16 Left and right duals.

A dual pair consists of objects \(L,R\) and maps \(\operatorname{ev}:L\otimes R\to I\text{,}\) \(\operatorname{coev}:I\to R\otimes L\text{,}\) for which both composites
\begin{equation*} R\cong I\otimes R\longrightarrow(R\otimes L)\otimes R \cong R\otimes(L\otimes R)\longrightarrow R\otimes I\cong R \end{equation*}
and
\begin{equation*} L\cong L\otimes I\longrightarrow L\otimes(R\otimes L) \cong(L\otimes R)\otimes L\longrightarrow I\otimes L\cong L \end{equation*}
are identities. The nonstructural arrows are induced by coevaluation and evaluation in the displayed order. We call \(L\) a left dual of \(R\text{,}\) and \(R\) a right dual of \(L\text{.}\)
🔗
An object is left dualizable if it has a left dual, and right dualizable if it has a right dual. Unqualified dualizable here means that both exist. In a braided category either one implies the other, by transporting evaluation and coevaluation through the braiding.
 4 
For a dual pair \((L,R)\) with maps \(\operatorname{ev},\operatorname{coev}\text{,}\) the reversed pair uses \(\operatorname{ev}'=\operatorname{ev}\,\beta_{R,L}\) and \(\operatorname{coev}'=\beta_{L,R}^{-1}\operatorname{coev}\text{.}\) In the two new zigzags, expand the crossings with the hexagons, then use naturality with evaluation and coevaluation and the unit compatibilities. The crossings cancel, leaving the original zigzags. The indices and inverse on the second braiding matter without symmetry.
Some sources use dualizable for one chosen side; outside the braided case, the displayed evaluation and coevaluation maps fix which assertion is meant.
