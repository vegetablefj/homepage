Definition 9.16 Two different splitting conditions.

  1. As in Definition 6.20, a parallel pair \(f,g:A\rightrightarrows B\) is reflexive if it has a common section \(r:B\to A\text{,}\) with \(fr=gr=\id_B\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  2. A split coequalizer diagram consists of \(f,g:A\rightrightarrows B\text{,}\) \(q:B\to Q\text{,}\) and maps \(s:Q\to B\text{,}\) \(t:B\to A\text{,}\) satisfying
    \begin{equation*} qf=qg,\qquad qs=\id_Q,\qquad gt=\id_B,\qquad ft=sq. \end{equation*}
    The choices \(s,t\) are splitting data. They need not be morphisms preserving any additional structure on the objects.
