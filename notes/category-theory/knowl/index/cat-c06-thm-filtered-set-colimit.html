Theorem 6.10.

Let \(I\) be small and filtered, and \(F:I\to\Set_\U\text{.}\) For \(x\in F(i)\) and \(y\in F(j)\text{,}\) set \(x\sim y\) if there exist arrows \(a:i\to k\text{,}\) \(b:j\to k\) with \(F(a)(x)=F(b)(y)\text{.}\) Then this is an equivalence relation on the tagged disjoint union, and
\begin{equation*} \operatorname{colim}_IF\cong \left(\coprod_{i\in\Ob(I)}F(i)\right)\big/\sim. \end{equation*}
Write \([i,x]\text{,}\) or \([x]\) when unambiguous, for the corresponding class. For two elements at the same stage, the sharper criterion is
\begin{equation*} [i,x]=[i,y]\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \text{there exists }c:i\to k\text{ with }F(c)(x)=F(c)(y). \end{equation*}
🔗
🔗

Proof.
Identities prove reflexivity, and symmetry follows by exchanging the two arrows. For transitivity, suppose \(x\in F(i)\text{,}\) \(y\in F(j)\text{,}\) and \(z\in F(l)\text{,}\) with witnesses
\begin{equation*} a:i\to p,\quad b:j\to p,\quad c:j\to q,\quad d:l\to q, \end{equation*}
so that \(F(a)(x)=F(b)(y)\) and \(F(c)(y)=F(d)(z)\text{.}\) Choose common-target arrows \(r:p\to t\) and \(s:q\to t\text{.}\) The composites \(rb,sc:j\to t\) may be different. Choose \(e:t\to v\) equalizing them. Then
\begin{equation*} F(era)(x)=F(erb)(y)=F(esc)(y)=F(esd)(z). \end{equation*}
The arrows \(era:i\to v\) and \(esd:l\to v\) witness \(x\sim z\text{.}\)
🔗
Every generating colimit identification \(x\sim F(a)(x)\) satisfies this relation, using \(a\) and the identity of its target. Conversely, a common-stage equality relates both elements to the same image through generating identifications. Thus the equivalence relation is precisely the colimit relation from the set construction in Theorem 4.9. The quotient belongs to \(\U\) because the index and all values are small.
🔗
For the sharper assertion, the common-stage criterion initially gives possibly different arrows \(a,b:i\to k\) with \(F(a)(x)=F(b)(y)\text{.}\) Equalize \(a,b\) by \(e:k\to l\) and put \(c=ea=eb\text{.}\) Applying \(F(e)\) gives \(F(c)(x)=F(c)(y)\text{.}\) The converse uses \(c\) as both common-stage arrows.
