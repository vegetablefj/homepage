Definition 1.15 Vertical composition, whiskering, and horizontal composition.

  1. For \(\eta:F\Rightarrow G\) and \(\theta:G\Rightarrow K\text{,}\) their vertical composite is \(\theta\cdot\eta:F\Rightarrow K\) with components \((\theta\cdot\eta)_X=\theta_X\eta_X\text{.}\) The identity transformation \(\id_F:F\Rightarrow F\) has components \(\id_{FX}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  2. For \(L:\mathcal B\to\mathcal C\) and \(H:\mathcal D\to\mathcal E\text{,}\) the whiskered transformations \(\eta L:FL\Rightarrow GL\) and \(H\eta:HF\Rightarrow HG\) have components \(\eta_{LB}\) and \(H(\eta_X)\text{,}\) respectively.
    🔗
    🔗
  3. For \(\eta:F\Rightarrow G\) with \(F,G:\mathcal C\to\mathcal D\) and \(\theta:H\Rightarrow K\) with \(H,K:\mathcal D\to\mathcal E\text{,}\) their horizontal composite \(\theta*\eta:HF\Rightarrow KG\) has components
    \begin{equation*} (\theta*\eta)_X=\theta_{GX}H(\eta_X)=K(\eta_X)\theta_{FX}. \end{equation*}
    The equality is the naturality of \(\theta\) applied to the arrow \(\eta_X\text{.}\)
