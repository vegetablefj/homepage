Definition 13.6 Small families of presentable generators.

  1. A category \(\mathcal E\) is \(\kappa\)-accessible if it admits small \(\kappa\)-filtered colimits and there is a small family \((A_s)_{s\in S}\) of \(\kappa\)-presentable objects such that every object is a small \(\kappa\)-filtered colimit of objects from this family. Small family means \(S\in\U\text{;}\) its externally named values need not themselves belong to \(\U\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  2. It is locally \(\kappa\)-presentable if it is \(\kappa\)-accessible and admits all small colimits. For \(\kappa=\aleph_0\text{,}\) these are finitely accessible and locally finitely presentable, respectively.
    🔗
    🔗
  3. A category is accessible, or locally presentable, if it has the corresponding property for some infinite regular \(\kappa\in\U\text{.}\) A functor is \(\kappa\)-accessible if it preserves small \(\kappa\)-filtered colimits between categories admitting them. An accessible functor between accessible categories is one preserving these colimits for some regular cardinal at which its source and target are accessible.
