Theorem 6.5 Cofinality theorem.

Let \(u:J\to I\) be final and \(D:I\to\mathcal C\) a diagram. Restriction induces an isomorphism of categories over \(\mathcal C\text{,}\)
\begin{equation*} \operatorname{Cocone}(D)\longrightarrow \operatorname{Cocone}(Du),\qquad (Q,(\lambda_i)_i)\longmapsto(Q,(\lambda_{u(j)})_j). \end{equation*}
Consequently \(\operatorname{colim}_I D\) exists if and only if \(\operatorname{colim}_J Du\) exists, and the canonical comparison
\begin{equation*} \operatorname{colim}_J Du\longrightarrow\operatorname{colim}_I D \end{equation*}
is then an isomorphism. Dually, if \(u\) is initial, restriction identifies the cone categories and gives a canonical isomorphism
\begin{equation*} \lim_I D\longrightarrow\lim_J Du \end{equation*}
whenever either side exists.
🔗
🔗

Proof.
Let \((Q,(\mu_j)_j)\) be a cocone under \(Du\text{.}\) For each \(i\in I\text{,}\) choose an object \((j,a:i\to u(j))\) of \((i\downarrow u)\) and set
\begin{equation*} \lambda_i=\mu_jD(a):D(i)\longrightarrow Q. \end{equation*}
We first check independence of this choice. An arrow \(b:(j,a)\to(j',a')\) in the comma category satisfies \(a'=u(b)a\text{.}\) The cocone equation gives
\begin{equation*} \mu_{j'}D(a') =\mu_{j'}D(u(b))D(a)=\mu_jD(a). \end{equation*}
The same equality can be read in either direction. Connectedness therefore proves equality along a zigzag between any two choices.
🔗
For an arrow \(c:i\to i'\text{,}\) choose \(a':i'\to u(j)\text{.}\) The composite \(a'c\) is an allowed choice for \(i\text{,}\) so independence gives
\begin{equation*} \lambda_{i'}D(c)=\mu_jD(a')D(c) =\mu_jD(a'c)=\lambda_i. \end{equation*}
Thus \(\lambda\) is a cocone. At \(i=u(j)\text{,}\) the choice \((j,\id_{u(j)})\) gives \(\lambda_{u(j)}=\mu_j\text{;}\) extension followed by restriction is the identity. Conversely, any extension of \(\mu\) must satisfy \(\lambda_i=\lambda_{u(j)}D(a)=\mu_jD(a)\text{,}\) so it is the unique extension. Restriction followed by extension is also the identity.
🔗
It remains to check arrows between cocones. If \(q:Q\to Q'\) satisfies \(q\mu_j=\mu'_j\) at every \(j\text{,}\) then
\begin{equation*} q\lambda_i=q\mu_jD(a)=\mu'_jD(a)=\lambda'_i. \end{equation*}
The converse follows by restriction. Hence extension sends the vertex map \(q\) to the same arrow \(q\) and is a strict inverse to restriction. Initial objects of these cocone categories correspond, which proves existence in both directions and the asserted comparison isomorphism. Taking opposite categories proves the statement about limits and initial functors.
