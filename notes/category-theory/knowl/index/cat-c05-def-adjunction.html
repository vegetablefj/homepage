Definition 5.1 Adjunction and transpose.

An adjunction consists of functors \(F:\mathcal C\to\mathcal D\) and \(G:\mathcal D\to\mathcal C\text{,}\) together with bijections
\begin{equation*} \Phi_{X,Y}:\Hom_{\mathcal D}(FX,Y)\longrightarrow\Hom_{\mathcal C}(X,GY) \end{equation*}
natural in \(X\in\mathcal C\) and \(Y\in\mathcal D\text{.}\) We call \(F\) the left adjoint and \(G\) the right adjoint. The transpose of \(f:FX\to Y\) is \(f^\sharp=\Phi_{X,Y}(f)\text{;}\) the inverse transpose of \(g:X\to GY\) is \(g^\flat=\Phi_{X,Y}^{-1}(g)\text{.}\)
🔗
Explicitly, if \(a:X'\to X\) and \(b:Y\to Y'\text{,}\) naturality says
\begin{equation*} \Phi_{X',Y'}\bigl(bfF(a)\bigr)=G(b)\Phi_{X,Y}(f)a. \end{equation*}
Thus the bijections form a natural isomorphism of ordinary set-valued functors on \(\mathcal C^{\op}\times\mathcal D\text{.}\)
