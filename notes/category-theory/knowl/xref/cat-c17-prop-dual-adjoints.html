Proposition 17.17.

For a dual pair \((L,R)\text{,}\) there are adjunctions
\begin{equation*} -\otimes R\dashv-\otimes L,\qquad L\otimes-\dashv R\otimes-. \end{equation*}
Thus a left dual \(L\) of \(A\) gives \([A,B]_r\cong B\otimes L\text{,}\) while a right dual \(R\) of \(A\) gives \([A,B]_l\cong R\otimes B\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗

Proof.
Given \(f:T\otimes R\to B\text{,}\) insert coevaluation to form
\begin{equation*} T\cong T\otimes I\longrightarrow T\otimes(R\otimes L) \cong(T\otimes R)\otimes L\xrightarrow{f\otimes\id_L}B\otimes L. \end{equation*}
Conversely, for \(g:T\to B\otimes L\text{,}\) tensor with \(R\) and evaluate:
\begin{equation*} T\otimes R\xrightarrow{g\otimes\id_R}(B\otimes L)\otimes R \cong B\otimes(L\otimes R)\longrightarrow B\otimes I\cong B. \end{equation*}
In either composite of these operations, naturality moves \(f\) or \(g\) past the inserted structural maps, and one of the two dual-pair identities removes the remaining evaluation–coevaluation pair. The operations are therefore inverse. Their formulas are natural in \(T,B\text{,}\) proving the first adjunction. Inserting coevaluation on the left and evaluating there proves the second. The internal-Hom formulas are their defining representability statements.
