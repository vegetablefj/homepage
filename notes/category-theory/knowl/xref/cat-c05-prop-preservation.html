Proposition 5.19.

  1. A right adjoint preserves every existing small limit. A left adjoint preserves every existing small colimit. Neither assertion requires all diagrams of that shape to have a limit or colimit.
    🔗
    🔗
  2. If \(\mathcal C\) has all \(\mathcal J\)-shaped limits, a chosen limit functor is right adjoint to the diagonal functor. If it has all \(\mathcal J\)-shaped colimits, a chosen colimit functor is left adjoint to the diagonal:
    \begin{equation*} \operatorname{colim}_{\mathcal J}\dashv\Delta_{\mathcal J}\dashv\lim_{\mathcal J}, \end{equation*}
    with each adjunction asserted when its indicated functor exists.
    🔗
    🔗
🔗
🔗

Proof.
Let \(D:\mathcal J\to\mathcal D\) have limit cone \(L\to D_j\text{.}\) For each \(X\in\mathcal C\text{,}\) the adjunction and that limit give natural bijections
\begin{equation*} \Hom_{\mathcal C}(X,GL)\cong\Hom_{\mathcal D}(FX,L) \cong\lim_{j\in\mathcal J}\Hom_{\mathcal D}(FX,D_j) \cong\lim_{j\in\mathcal J}\Hom_{\mathcal C}(X,GD_j). \end{equation*}
Under these bijections, an arrow \(X\to GL\) goes to its composites with the transported cone maps. This proves that the transported cone is a limit cone, not merely that its vertex is isomorphic to some limit object. The colimit statement follows by passing to opposite categories.
🔗
For the second assertion, the limit correspondence is
\begin{equation*} \Hom_{\Fun(\mathcal J,\mathcal C)}(\Delta_{\mathcal J}X,D) \cong\Hom_{\mathcal C}(X,\lim_{\mathcal J}D). \end{equation*}
A map on the left is exactly a cone with vertex \(X\text{.}\) Its universal factorization is natural in both \(X\) and \(D\text{,}\) by the construction of the limit functor. The colimit correspondence is dual.
