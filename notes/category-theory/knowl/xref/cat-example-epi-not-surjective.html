Example 1.6 Cancellation and underlying functions.

In \(\Set\text{,}\) monomorphisms are precisely injective functions: singleton test objects detect equality of individual elements. Epimorphisms are precisely surjective functions. To verify the latter converse, if \(y\in Y\) is outside the image of \(f:X\to Y\text{,}\) the zero function \(Y\to\{0,1\}\) and the function taking value one only at \(y\) agree after composition with \(f\text{,}\) but differ.
🔗
These identifications must be checked in each category. The inclusion \(\Z\to\Q\) in \(\Ring\) is monic and epic, but is not an isomorphism. Monicity follows from injectivity. If two ring maps \(a,b:\Q\to R\) agree on \(\Z\text{,}\) then for every nonzero integer \(n\) they agree on \(n^{-1}\text{,}\) since its image must be the inverse of their common image of \(n\text{.}\) They consequently agree on every rational number. The inclusion is not surjective, and a ring inverse would in particular be an inverse function. This example is also given in [Rie, Exercise 1.2.iv].
🔗
Consequently \(\Set\) is balanced, whereas \(\Ring\) and \(\CRing\) are not. In these ring categories, cancellation does not force bijectivity of the underlying function.
