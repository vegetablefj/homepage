Proposition 6.8.

A category is filtered if and only if every diagram in it indexed by a finite category admits a cocone. Here finite means finitely many arrows, and includes the empty category.
🔗
🔗

Proof.
Suppose \(I\) is filtered and \(D:K\to I\) has finite index. If \(K\) is empty, any object of \(I\) is a cocone vertex. Otherwise, repeated use of common targets gives an object \(q\) and arrows \(s_k:D(k)\to q\) for every object of \(K\text{.}\) These arrows need not yet form a cocone. For each arrow \(a:k\to l\) of \(K\text{,}\) the pair
\begin{equation*} s_k,\ s_lD(a):D(k)\rightrightarrows q \end{equation*}
can be equalized by postcomposition. Process the finitely many arrows one at a time, postcomposing every \(s_k\) at each step. Equalities already obtained remain true after further postcomposition. At the end all cocone equations hold.
🔗
Conversely, a cocone on the empty diagram supplies an object. A cocone on two discrete objects supplies their common target. A cocone on a parallel pair \(i\rightrightarrows j\) has a leg from \(j\) that equalizes the pair. These three finite shapes give exactly the defining conditions.
