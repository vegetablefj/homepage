Proposition 15.14.

In a regular category:
🔗
  1. A relation \(R\rightrightarrows X\) is effective exactly when it has a coequalizer \(q:X\to Q\) and the induced map \(R\to X\times_QX\) is invertible.
    🔗
    🔗
  2. Kernel pair gives a bijection between regular quotients of \(X\) up to compatible isomorphism and effective equivalence relations on \(X\) as subobjects. For regular quotients \(q_1,q_2\text{,}\)
    \begin{equation*} q_2=hq_1\text{ for some }h\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\operatorname{Eq}(q_1)\subseteq\operatorname{Eq}(q_2). \end{equation*}
    Thus the correspondence reverses the quotient order fixed earlier.
    🔗
    🔗
🔗
🔗

Proof.
If \(R=\operatorname{Eq}(f)\text{,}\) factor \(f=me\) regularly. Its relation equals \(\operatorname{Eq}(e)\text{,}\) and \(e\) is its coequalizer. This proves the first condition for an effective relation. The converse follows directly from the asserted kernel-pair identification. In particular, every regular quotient is recovered from its kernel pair by taking a coequalizer, so the correspondence is injective as well as surjective.
🔗
If \(q_2=hq_1\text{,}\) every pair identified by \(q_1\) is identified by \(q_2\text{,}\) and the pullback property gives the inclusion of relations. Conversely, such an inclusion makes \(q_2\) coequalize the kernel pair of \(q_1\text{,}\) so universality supplies \(h\text{.}\) It is unique because \(q_1\) is epic. This also verifies the order assertion.
