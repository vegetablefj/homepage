Theorem 14.9.

For a category \(\mathcal C\) with pullbacks, the following are equivalent:
🔗
  1. Every slice \(\mathcal C/B\) is cartesian closed.
    🔗
    🔗
  2. For every arrow \(f\text{,}\) pullback has a right adjoint \(\Pi_f\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
🔗
Under these conditions, \(\Sigma_f\dashv f^*\dashv\Pi_f\text{.}\) For objects \(a:A\to B\) and \(p:Y\to B\) in a slice, its exponential is
\begin{equation*} p^a\cong\Pi_a(a^*p). \end{equation*}
🔗
🔗

Proof.
Suppose first that all slices are cartesian closed. Fix \(f:A\to B\) and \(p:X\to A\text{.}\) Work inside \(\mathcal D=\mathcal C/B\text{.}\) The map \(p\) is an arrow in \(\mathcal D\) from the object \(fp\) to the object \(f\text{.}\) Exponentiating by \(f\) gives an arrow \(p^f:(fp)^f\to f^f\text{.}\) Let \(e:1_{\mathcal D}\to f^f\) be the transpose of \(\id_f\text{,}\) and define \(\Pi_f p\) by the pullback in \(\mathcal D\)
\begin{equation*} \Pi_f p=1_{\mathcal D}\times_{f^f}(fp)^f. \end{equation*}
Such a pullback exists: every slice has its identity object as terminal object and inherits pullbacks, hence has finite limits.
🔗
For an object \(q:Y\to B\text{,}\) a map \(q\to\Pi_f p\) is a map \(v:q\to(fp)^f\) with \(p^fv=e!_q\text{.}\) Transpose in the slice. The map \(v\) becomes \(r:Y\times_B A\to X\) over \(B\text{,}\) and the displayed equation becomes \(pr=\operatorname{pr}_A\text{.}\) This is exactly a map \(f^*q\to p\) over \(A\text{,}\) after the canonical interchange of the two pullback factors. The correspondence is natural in \(q\) and \(p\text{,}\) so it constructs the right adjoint and its arrow maps.
🔗
Conversely, suppose all dependent products exist. In \(\mathcal C/B\text{,}\) product with \(a:A\to B\) is the composite \(\Sigma_a a^*\text{,}\) up to its canonical product comparison. The two adjunctions \(\Sigma_a\dashv a^*\) and \(a^*\dashv\Pi_a\) make this composite left adjoint to \(\Pi_a a^*\text{.}\) Hence every product functor in the slice has a right adjoint, with the displayed exponential formula. Its terminal object is already \(\id_B\text{,}\) so the slice is cartesian closed.
