Theorem 5.24 Freyd’s general adjoint functor theorem.

Let \(\mathcal A\) be set-encoded, locally \(\U\)-small, and complete for \(\U\)-small diagrams. Let \(\mathcal B\) be any set-encoded category. A functor \(G:\mathcal A\to\mathcal B\) has a left adjoint if and only if it preserves \(\U\)-small limits and satisfies the \(\U\)-solution-set condition.
🔗
🔗

Proof.
If \(F\dashv G\text{,}\) preservation follows from Proposition 5.19. For \(B\text{,}\) the single universal arrow \(B\to GFB\) is a solution family.
🔗
Conversely, fix \(B\in\mathcal B\text{.}\) The comma category \(\mathcal E=(B\downarrow G)\) is locally \(\U\)-small: its morphisms between two fixed objects form a subset of a hom-set in \(\mathcal A\text{.}\) It is also complete for \(\U\)-small diagrams. Indeed, take such a diagram of pairs \((A_j,u_j:B\to GA_j)\) and form its limit \(L\) in \(\mathcal A\text{.}\) Since \(G\) preserves that limit, the compatible arrows \(u_j\) induce a unique \(u:B\to GL\text{.}\) The resulting pair \((L,u)\) is a limit in the comma category: the required map between vertices is first determined in \(\mathcal A\text{,}\) and the defining equation involving \(u\) follows by composing with the limit projections in \(\mathcal B\text{.}\)
🔗
The solution-set condition supplies a small weakly initial family in \(\mathcal E\text{.}\) The initial-object lemma therefore gives an initial object of \(\mathcal E\text{.}\) This holds for every \(B\text{,}\) so Proposition 5.5 constructs a left adjoint. The collection of input objects is a set; making these choices does not require a separate global choice principle.
