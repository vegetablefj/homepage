Theorem 13.10.

For every small category \(\mathcal C\text{,}\) its presheaf category \(\widehat{\mathcal C}\) is locally finitely presentable. A presheaf is finitely presentable exactly when it is isomorphic to a finite colimit of representables, equivalently when it is a retract of such a finite colimit. Here a finite colimit is indexed by a category with finitely many arrows, and may be empty.
🔗
🔗

Proof.
Representables are finitely presentable: their Hom functors are evaluations by Yoneda, and all small colimits of presheaves are pointwise. Finite colimits and retracts of representables are therefore finitely presentable by Proposition 13.5.
🔗
Choose a small full category \(\mathcal A\) of finite colimits of representables which contains all representables and is closed under finite colimits up to isomorphism. Such a choice is possible without enlarging the universe: finite diagrams and maps among any small family form a small collection, and iterating finite-colimit closure through \(\N\in\U\) still gives a small family. All presheaf encodings here have small data because their source is small.
🔗
Finite iterations reduce to a single finite colimit of representables. Such an object has a presentation as a coequalizer of two maps between finite coproducts of representables. Finite coproducts of such presentations combine their generators and relations. For a coequalizer of maps \(f,g:A\rightrightarrows B\) between two such objects, consider the images under \(f,g\) of each of the finitely many representable generators of \(A\text{.}\) Each map from a representable to \(B\) lifts to a generator summand of its presenting coproduct: by Yoneda it is an element of a pointwise quotient, and every such element has a representative in that coproduct. Choose these finitely many lifts and add the equalities between the two lifted images to the existing relations for \(B\text{.}\) A map from the resulting quotient is exactly a map out of \(B\) equalizing \(f,g\text{,}\) since the generators of \(A\) are jointly epic. This gives another finite presentation. Finally, any such presentation is a finite colimit of representables, using one object for each generator and relation summand, and the two arrows from each relation summand to its designated generator summands. Closure under finite coproducts and coequalizers proves the claim.
🔗
For a presheaf \(P\text{,}\) the comma category \((\mathcal A\downarrow P)\) is small and filtered. An initial presheaf in \(\mathcal A\) supplies an object. The coproduct of two candidate maps supplies their common target. For parallel arrows between two candidates, take their coequalizer in the presheaf category; it lies in \(\mathcal A\) up to the chosen comparison, and the common map to \(P\) descends to it. These are exactly the three filteredness conditions.
🔗
The inclusion of \(\mathcal A\) in the presheaf category is dense. To verify fullness of its nerve, let a natural transformation assign compatible maps between the Hom tests for \(P,Q\text{.}\) Restricting to the representables gives a unique map \(v:P\to Q\) by Yoneda. For \(A\in\mathcal A\) and \(u:A\to P\text{,}\) naturality with every \(h_c\to A\) implies that the assigned map \(A\to Q\) has the same composite as \(vu\) with every such map. Yoneda detects equality pointwise, so the assigned map is \(vu\text{.}\) The representables also detect faithfulness. The canonical density criterion therefore identifies
\begin{equation*} P\cong\operatorname{colim}_{(A,u)\in(\mathcal A\downarrow P)}A. \end{equation*}
This is a small filtered colimit of finitely presentable objects. Arbitrary small colimits already exist pointwise, proving local finite presentability.
🔗
Finally, if \(P\) is finitely presentable, applying \(\Hom(P,-)\) to this filtered presentation makes \(\id_P\) factor through one candidate \(r:A\to P\text{.}\) Choose \(i:P\to A\) with \(ri=\id_P\) and put \(e=ir\text{.}\) Then \(r\) is the coequalizer of \(e,\id_A\text{:}\) if \(te=t\text{,}\) the unique factor through \(r\) is \(ti\text{.}\) The finite-colimit closure proved above therefore makes \(P\) itself a finite colimit of representables. The reverse implication, including retracts, was proved in the first paragraph.
