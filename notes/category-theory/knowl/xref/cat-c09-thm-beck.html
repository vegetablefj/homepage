Theorem 9.20 Beck’s monadicity theorem.

Let \(F:\mathcal C\to\mathcal D\) be left adjoint to \(G:\mathcal D\to\mathcal C\text{.}\) Its comparison \(K:\mathcal D\to\mathcal C^T\) is an equivalence if and only if the following conditions hold:
🔗
  1. \(G\) reflects isomorphisms.
    🔗
    🔗
  2. Every \(G\)-split parallel pair in \(\mathcal D\) has a coequalizer there.
    🔗
    🔗
  3. \(G\) sends each such coequalizer fork to a coequalizer fork in \(\mathcal C\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
🔗
No existence or preservation of all coequalizers is assumed. The splitting is in \(\mathcal C\text{,}\) and the pairs quantified over are in \(\mathcal D\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗

Proof.
If \(K\) is an equivalence, \(G=U^TK\) is conservative. A \(G\)-split pair becomes a pair split underneath in \(\mathcal C^T\text{.}\) The created coequalizer just proved transfers across the equivalence to a coequalizer in \(\mathcal D\text{,}\) whose image is the required underlying coequalizer. Uniqueness of colimits shows preservation for any choice of that coequalizer.
🔗
Assume now the three conditions. First, for each \(Y\in\mathcal D\text{,}\) the counit \(\varepsilon_Y:FGY\to Y\) is the coequalizer of
\begin{equation*} FGFGY\ \underset{\varepsilon_{FGY}}{\overset{FG(\varepsilon_Y)}{\rightrightarrows}}\ FGY. \end{equation*}
Its image fork is the canonical split fork for \(K(Y)\text{.}\) The pair therefore has a coequalizer \(q:FGY\to Z\) preserved by \(G\text{.}\) Naturality of \(\varepsilon\) shows that \(\varepsilon_Y\) coequalizes the pair, so \(\varepsilon_Y=hq\text{.}\) The map \(G(h)\) compares two coequalizers of the same pair and is invertible. Conservativity makes \(h\) invertible, proving the assertion about the counit.
🔗
To prove full faithfulness, abbreviate \(a_Y=G\varepsilon_Y\text{.}\) An algebra map \(u:K(Y)\to K(Z)\) satisfies \(ua_Y=a_ZT(u)\text{.}\) Put \(k=\varepsilon_ZF(u):FGY\to Z\text{.}\) Naturality of the counit at \(k\) gives
\begin{equation*} k\varepsilon_{FGY}=\varepsilon_ZFG(k)=\varepsilon_ZF(a_ZT(u))=\varepsilon_ZF(ua_Y)=kFG(\varepsilon_Y). \end{equation*}
Thus \(k\) factors uniquely as \(h\varepsilon_Y\text{.}\) Applying \(G\) gives \(G(h)a_Y=u a_Y\text{.}\) Since \(a_Y\) has section \(\eta_{GY}\text{,}\) cancel it to obtain \(G(h)=u\text{.}\) Conversely, if two maps \(Y\to Z\) have the same image under \(G\text{,}\) naturality of the counit shows that they agree after \(\varepsilon_Y\text{;}\) this coequalizer is epic, so they agree. This proves full faithfulness of \(K\text{.}\)
🔗
Finally take any algebra \((A,a)\text{.}\) The pair \(F(a),\varepsilon_{FA}:FTA\rightrightarrows FA\) becomes its canonical split pair after applying \(G\text{.}\) Choose its preserved coequalizer \(q:FA\to Y\text{.}\) The canonical coequalizer \(a:TA\to A\) supplies an isomorphism \(v:GY\to A\) with \(vG(q)=a\text{.}\) It is an algebra map: after precomposition with \(TG(q)\text{,}\) naturality of \(\varepsilon\) and the algebra law give
\begin{equation*} vG(\varepsilon_Y)TG(q)=a\mu_A=aT(a)=aT(v)TG(q). \end{equation*}
The map \(G(q)\) is split epic, since it is isomorphic to \(a\text{;}\) hence \(TG(q)\) is epic and cancels. Thus \(v:K(Y)\to(A,a)\) is an algebra isomorphism. The comparison is fully faithful and essentially surjective, and therefore an equivalence.
