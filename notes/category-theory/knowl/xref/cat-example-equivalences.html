Example 1.21 Equivalent presentations.

A skeleton inclusion is fully faithful by definition and essentially surjective because every object has a representative. It is therefore an equivalence. For a preorder, the poset of equivalence classes described earlier gives the same phenomenon without having to choose a full subcategory first.
🔗
The groupoid with two distinct objects and exactly one arrow between every pair is equivalent to \(\mathbf 1\text{,}\) but is not isomorphic to it as a category. An isomorphism of categories would induce a bijection on object sets. Every arrow in this groupoid, including the arrows between its two objects, is invertible; its two objects nevertheless have distinct labels.
🔗
For a field \(k\) whose underlying set belongs to \(\U\text{,}\) the category with objects the nonnegative integers and arrows \(n\to m\) the \(m\times n\) matrices over \(k\) is equivalent to the category of finite-dimensional vector spaces whose underlying sets belong to \(\U\text{.}\) The functor sends \(n\) to \(k^n\) and a matrix to its linear map. It is fully faithful by matrix representation and essentially surjective by the existence of a basis. Zero-dimensional spaces and empty matrices include the object \(n=0\text{.}\) No assertion that every vector space literally equals one of these coordinate spaces is required.
