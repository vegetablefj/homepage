Proposition 13.2.

Let \(I\) be small and \(\kappa\)-filtered, and let \(K\) have fewer than \(\kappa\) arrows. For \(D:I\times K\to\Set_\U\text{,}\) the canonical comparison is a bijection:
\begin{equation*} \operatorname{colim}_{i\in I}\lim_{k\in K}D(i,k)\longrightarrow \lim_{k\in K}\operatorname{colim}_{i\in I}D(i,k). \end{equation*}
🔗
🔗

Proof.
We first make explicit the common-stage argument for fewer than \(\kappa\) equations. Suppose elements at a stage \(i\) become equal in the colimit, with fewer than \(\kappa\) equalities, possibly in different diagrams on \(I\text{.}\) For each equality, the ordinary filtered-colimit criterion supplies a transition \(a_s:i\to j_s\) witnessing it. The diagram consisting of these arrows and their common source has fewer than \(\kappa\) arrows. A cocone gives a single map \(i\to j\) factoring through every \(a_s\text{,}\) and hence enforces all equations at once. The same argument on a discrete set of fewer than \(\kappa\) stages brings their representatives to one stage.
🔗
An element on the right is a compatible family with fewer than \(\kappa\) components. Bring chosen representatives to one stage. There are fewer than \(\kappa\) compatibility equations, one for each arrow of \(K\text{,}\) so the preceding argument makes them all hold after one transition. This gives an element on the left. If two elements on the left have the same image, first bring their stages together and then enforce equality of every component after one transition. This proves injectivity. If \(K\) is empty, the assertion says that the filtered colimit of singleton sets is a singleton, using nonemptiness and connectedness of \(I\text{.}\)
