Theorem 9.9.

Let a small diagram in \(\mathcal C^T\) have objects \((A_j,a_j)\text{.}\) If its underlying diagram has a specified limit cone \(p_j:L\to A_j\) in \(\mathcal C\text{,}\) there is a unique algebra structure \(l:TL\to L\) making every \(p_j\) an algebra map. The lifted cone is a limit in \(\mathcal C^T\text{.}\) In this precise sense \(U^T\) creates all limits that exist underneath.
🔗
🔗

Proof.
The maps \(a_jT(p_j):TL\to A_j\) form a cone: for a diagram arrow \(f:(A_j,a_j)\to(A_k,a_k)\text{,}\) its algebra equation gives \(fa_jT(p_j)=a_kT(fp_j)=a_kT(p_k)\text{.}\) Thus there is a unique \(l\) with \(p_jl=a_jT(p_j)\text{.}\) Comparing after every projection shows
\begin{equation*} p_jl\eta_L=p_j,\qquad p_jl\mu_L=p_jlT(l), \end{equation*}
using naturality of the unit and multiplication and the two laws for \(a_j\text{.}\) The joint uniqueness property of the limit gives the algebra laws for \(l\text{.}\)
🔗
A cone of algebra maps from \((B,b)\) factors uniquely through a map \(u:B\to L\) underneath. Its composites show \(p_jub=a_jT(p_ju)=p_jlT(u)\) for all \(j\text{.}\) Hence \(ub=lT(u)\text{,}\) so the factorization is an algebra map. Its uniqueness remains true after forgetting. Finally, any algebra structure making all projections algebra maps must satisfy the equations that defined \(l\text{,}\) proving uniqueness of the lift.
