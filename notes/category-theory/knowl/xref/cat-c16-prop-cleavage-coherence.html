Proposition 16.5.

A cleavage determines natural isomorphisms
\begin{equation*} c_{u,v}:u^*v^*\xrightarrow{\ \cong\ }(vu)^*, \qquad e_b:(\id_b)^*\xrightarrow{\ \cong\ }\id_{\mathcal E_b}, \end{equation*}
characterized by
\begin{equation*} \chi_{vu,y}\,c_{u,v,y} =\chi_{v,y}\,\chi_{u,v^*y},\qquad e_{b,y}=\chi_{\id_b,y}. \end{equation*}
For \(w:c\to d\text{,}\) they satisfy
\begin{equation*} c_{u,wv}\circ u^*(c_{v,w}) =c_{vu,w}\circ(c_{u,v}w^*): u^*v^*w^*\longrightarrow(wvu)^*. \end{equation*}
Their unit equations are
\begin{equation*} c_{\id_a,u}=e_a u^*,\qquad c_{u,\id_b}=u^*e_b. \end{equation*}
Here juxtaposition with a functor denotes the whiskering of natural transformations from Chapter 1.
🔗
🔗

Proof.
The comparison of the two cartesian lifts supplies each \(c_{u,v,y}\) and its inverse; the vertical identity lift supplies \(e_{b,y}\text{.}\) For a vertical \(h:y\to y'\text{,}\) compose the two proposed naturality composites with \(\chi_{vu,y'}\text{.}\) The defining equations for \(c\) and for restriction make their composites equal to \(h\chi_{v,y}\chi_{u,v^*y}\text{.}\) Both lie over the identity in the source fiber, so cartesian uniqueness proves naturality. The equation for restriction along an identity proves naturality of \(e\) in exactly the same way.
🔗
For the associativity equation, compose each side at \(y\) with \(\chi_{wvu,y}\text{.}\) Each becomes the same three successive chosen lifts
\begin{equation*} \chi_{w,y}\, \chi_{v,w^*y}\,\chi_{u,v^*w^*y}. \end{equation*}
Cartesian uniqueness gives equality. For the two unit equations, the same argument reduces to the defining identity lift and, for the second equation, to naturality of \(\chi_u\) in the vertical arrow \(e_{b,y}\text{.}\)
