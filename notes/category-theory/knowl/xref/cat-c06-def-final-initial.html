Definition 6.3 The two cofinality conditions.

  1. The functor \(u:J\to I\) is final, or cofinal, if \((i\downarrow u)\) is nonempty and connected for every \(i\in I\text{.}\) Its objects are pairs \((j,a:i\to u(j))\text{;}\) an arrow to \((j',a')\) is \(b:j\to j'\) with \(u(b)a=a'\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  2. The functor \(u\) is initial, or coinitial, if \((u\downarrow i)\) is nonempty and connected for every \(i\in I\text{.}\) Its objects are pairs \((j,a:u(j)\to i)\text{;}\) an arrow to \((j',a')\) satisfies \(a'u(b)=a\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
🔗
Thus \(u\) is initial exactly when \(u^\op:J^\op\to I^\op\) is final. A subcategory has either property when its inclusion does.
