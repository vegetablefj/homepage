Proposition 16.7.

Let \(F:\mathcal B^{\op}\to\Cat\) be a strict functor, with every \(F(b)\) set-encoded, and write \(u^*=F(u)\text{.}\) Define \(\int_{\mathcal B}F\) by objects \((b,y)\text{,}\) \(y\in F(b)\text{,}\) and arrows
\begin{equation*} (u,\alpha):(a,x)\longrightarrow(b,y), \qquad u:a\to b,\quad\alpha:x\to u^*y\text{ in }F(a). \end{equation*}
Identities are \((\id_b,\id_y)\text{,}\) and composition is
\begin{equation*} (v,\beta)\circ(u,\alpha) =(vu,u^*(\beta)\alpha). \end{equation*}
Projection onto the base defines a split fibration. Its chosen lift is
\begin{equation*} (u,\id_{u^*y}):(a,u^*y)\longrightarrow(b,y). \end{equation*}
🔗
🔗

Proof.
The second component of the composite goes from \(x\) to \(u^*v^*z=(vu)^*z\text{,}\) as required. Functoriality of \(u^*\text{,}\) together with strict composition of restrictions, proves associativity. Strict identity restrictions prove the identity laws. All object and arrow collections are sets, since the base and the assigned categories are set-encoded.
🔗
For the cartesian property, suppose an arrow from \((d,t)\) to \((b,y)\) is \((us,\gamma)\text{,}\) with \(s:d\to a\text{.}\) Its unique factor over \(s\) through the proposed lift is \((s,\gamma)\text{:}\) its required target component is legitimate because \(s^*u^*y=(us)^*y\text{.}\) The composition rule verifies the factorization and its uniqueness. The chosen lifts of identities and composites are strictly the required identities and composites.
