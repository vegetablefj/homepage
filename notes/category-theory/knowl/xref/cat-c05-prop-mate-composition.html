Proposition 5.13.

The identity comparison for one adjunction has identity mate. For adjacent comparisons as above,
\begin{equation*} \bigl((K\alpha')(\alpha H')\bigr)^\sharp =(\alpha^\sharp K')(H(\alpha'^\sharp)): HH'G''\Rightarrow GKK'. \end{equation*}
In particular, for three adjunctions \(F_i\dashv G_i\) between fixed categories and transformations \(\alpha:F_0\Rightarrow F_1\text{,}\) \(\gamma:F_1\Rightarrow F_2\text{,}\) conjugation satisfies
\begin{equation*} (\gamma\alpha)^\sharp=\alpha^\sharp\gamma^\sharp. \end{equation*}
Conjugation consequently sends natural isomorphisms to natural isomorphisms.

Proof.
The identity of \(G\) satisfies the characterizing equation for the identity comparison of \(F\text{.}\) For composition, let \(\beta=\alpha^\sharp\) and \(\beta'=\alpha'^\sharp\text{.}\) Apply \(F\) to \((\beta K')(H\beta')\) and then compose with \(\varepsilon KK'\text{.}\) The characterizing equation for \(\beta\text{,}\) naturality of \(\alpha\text{,}\) and the equation for \(\beta'\) give
\begin{equation*} \begin{aligned} (\varepsilon KK')F\bigl((\beta K')(H\beta')\bigr) &=(K\varepsilon'K')(\alpha G'K')(FH\beta')\\ &=(K\varepsilon'K')(KF'\beta')(\alpha H'G'')\\ &=(KK'\varepsilon'')(K\alpha'G'')(\alpha H'G''). \end{aligned} \end{equation*}
This is the equation characterizing the mate of \((K\alpha')(\alpha H')\text{.}\) The conjugation rule is its case with the connecting functors equal to identities. Conjugating an isomorphism and its inverse therefore gives inverse transformations.
