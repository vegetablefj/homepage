Proposition 6.2.

For a small category \(I\text{,}\) let \(\Delta A:I\to\Set_\U\) be the constant diagram at a set \(A\in\U\text{,}\) with identity transition maps. Then
\begin{equation*} \operatorname{colim}_I\Delta A\cong\pi_0(I)\times A. \end{equation*}
In particular, \(\operatorname{colim}_I\Delta1\cong\pi_0(I)\text{.}\) More generally, for \(F:I\to\Set_\U\text{,}\) there is a canonical bijection
\begin{equation*} \operatorname{colim}_I F\cong\pi_0\bigl(\operatorname{el}(F)\bigr), \end{equation*}
with the covariant element-category convention of Definition 3.6.
🔗
🔗

Proof.
A cocone from \(\Delta A\) to \(S\) is a family of maps \(A\to S\) constant along every arrow, hence constant on each component. Such a family is exactly a map \(\pi_0(I)\times A\to S\text{.}\) For the second assertion, the quotient construction of a set colimit identifies \((i,x)\) with \((j,F(a)(x))\) for each \(a:i\to j\text{.}\) These are exactly the identifications generated by the arrows in \(\operatorname{el}(F)\text{,}\) so its component relation is the colimit relation.
