Proposition 10.4.

Any localization in the functor-category sense is equivalent to the path localization, compatibly with the structure functors up to natural isomorphism. Two models with the strict extension property are uniquely isomorphic by an isomorphism commuting strictly with the structure functors. Moreover,
\begin{equation*} \bigl(\mathcal C[W^{-1}]\bigr)^\op \cong\mathcal C^\op[(W^\op)^{-1}] \end{equation*}
for the path models, compatibly with \(Q^\op\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗

Proof.
Write \(P:\mathcal C\to\mathcal F\) for the path model and \(Q:\mathcal C\to\mathcal L\) for another localization. Strict extension gives \(H:\mathcal F\to\mathcal L\) with \(HP=Q\text{;}\) essential surjectivity of \(Q^*\) gives \(K:\mathcal L\to\mathcal F\) with \(KQ\cong P\text{.}\) Full faithfulness of \(P^*\) and \(Q^*\) lifts these comparisons uniquely to \(KH\cong\id_{\mathcal F}\) and \(HK\cong\id_{\mathcal L}\text{.}\) Thus \(H\) is an equivalence. With strict universal properties, the same argument uses equalities and uniqueness of extensions, giving strict inverse functors. Finally, reversing paths and all their generating relations gives the asserted isomorphism of opposite path models.
