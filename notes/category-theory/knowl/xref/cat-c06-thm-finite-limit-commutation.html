Theorem 6.13.

Let \(I\) be small and filtered, \(K\) a finite category, and \(X:I\times K\to\Set_\U\) a functor. The canonical comparison is a bijection:
\begin{equation*} \kappa:\operatorname{colim}_{i\in I}\lim_{k\in K}X(i,k) \longrightarrow \lim_{k\in K}\operatorname{colim}_{i\in I}X(i,k). \end{equation*}
It is natural in \(X\text{.}\) In particular, the functor \(\operatorname{colim}_I:\Fun(I,\Set_\U)\to\Set_\U\) preserves finite limits, including the terminal object, finite products, equalizers, and pullbacks.
🔗
🔗

Proof.
For each \(i\text{,}\) the maps \(X(i,k)\to\operatorname{colim}_{i'}X(i',k)\) give a map from the limit over \(k\) to the limit on the right. These maps are compatible in \(i\text{,}\) so they induce \(\kappa\text{.}\) This construction uses only the universal properties and proves its naturality.
🔗
First suppose \(K\) is nonempty. An element of the right-hand limit is a family \((z_k)_k\) of colimit classes, compatible under the arrows of \(K\text{.}\) Since there are finitely many objects, Lemma 6.11 supplies one stage \(i\) and representatives \(x_k\in X(i,k)\text{.}\) For every arrow \(b:k\to l\text{,}\) compatibility says
\begin{equation*} [X(i,b)(x_k)]=[x_l] \quad\text{in }\operatorname{colim}_{i'}X(i',l). \end{equation*}
There are finitely many such equations. Apply the second part of the lemma to the diagrams \(X(-,l)\text{,}\) allowing repeated diagrams for different equations. We obtain one arrow \(a:i\to j\) after which they all hold. The transported elements form an actual member of \(\lim_kX(j,k)\text{.}\) Its colimit class maps to \((z_k)_k\text{,}\) proving surjectivity.
🔗
For injectivity, represent two elements of the left-hand colimit by compatible families at stages \(i\) and \(j\text{.}\) Move both to a common stage \(q\text{.}\) If their images under \(\kappa\) agree, each pair of coordinates has equal class in the corresponding colimit. There are only finitely many coordinates, so one arrow out of \(q\) makes all the pairs equal simultaneously. At its target the two compatible families are identical elements of the limit. The common-stage criterion, now applied to the diagram \(i\mapsto\lim_kX(i,k)\text{,}\) proves equality in the left-hand colimit.
🔗
If \(K\) is empty, every limit over \(K\) is a singleton. The right-hand side is therefore a singleton, and the left-hand side is \(\operatorname{colim}_I\Delta1\text{.}\) A filtered category is nonempty and connected, so this is a singleton by Proposition 6.2. Thus \(\kappa\) is bijective in the empty case as well. Pointwise computation of limits in the functor category gives the final preservation statement.
