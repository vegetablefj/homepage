Lemma 6.11.

Let \(I\) be small and filtered.
🔗
  1. Given finitely many classes in colimits of set-valued diagrams on \(I\text{,}\) there is one stage at which all these classes have representatives.
    🔗
    🔗
  2. Let \(F_1,\ldots,F_r:I\to\Set_\U\text{,}\) and suppose \(x_s,y_s\in F_s(i)\) have equal colimit classes for each \(s\text{.}\) There is one arrow \(a:i\to j\) such that \(F_s(a)(x_s)=F_s(a)(y_s)\) for every \(s\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
🔗
Both assertions include empty finite collections.
🔗
🔗

Proof.
For the first assertion, choose a representative for each class and then a common target for the finitely many chosen stages. Transporting the representatives gives the result. For no classes, choose any stage, using nonemptiness.
🔗
For the second assertion, use the same-stage criterion to make the first pair equal after an arrow out of \(i\text{.}\) Transport all remaining pairs by that arrow. Their classes are still equal, so apply the criterion at the new stage to the next pair. Continue finitely many times. Previously established equalities are preserved by subsequent transition maps, and the composite arrow works for every pair. For no equations, take \(\id_i\text{.}\)
