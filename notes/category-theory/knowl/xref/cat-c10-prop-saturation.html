Proposition 10.8.

  1. An arrow belongs to \(W^{\mathrm{sat}}\) if and only if every \(W\)-inverting functor to every set-encoded target sends it to an isomorphism. Localizing at \(W^{\mathrm{sat}}\) gives the same universal problem as localizing at \(W\text{,}\) and \((W^{\mathrm{sat}})^{\mathrm{sat}}=W^{\mathrm{sat}}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  2. The class \(W^{\mathrm{sat}}\) contains all isomorphisms and is closed under composition and retracts in the arrow category. It has the two-out-of-three property: for composable \(f,g\text{,}\) membership of any two of \(f,g,gf\) implies membership of the third. It also has the two-out-of-six property: if \(f,g,h\) are consecutively composable and \(gf,hg\) belong to it, then \(f,g,h,hgf\) all belong to it.
    🔗
    🔗
🔗
🔗

Proof.
An inverting functor factors through \(Q\) up to natural isomorphism and hence sends every member of \(W^{\mathrm{sat}}\) to an isomorphism. The converse takes \(Q\) itself as the target functor. Thus the \(W\)-inverting functors and the \(W^{\mathrm{sat}}\)-inverting functors coincide, proving the localization and idempotence assertions.
🔗
It remains to recall the relevant facts about isomorphisms and apply them after \(Q\text{.}\) Composition and two-out-of-three follow by composing inverses. If \(gf\) and \(hg\) are invertible, then \(f(gf)^{-1}\) is a right inverse to \(g\text{,}\) while \((hg)^{-1}h\) is a left inverse. They coincide, so \(g\text{,}\) and then \(f,h,hgf\text{,}\) are invertible.
🔗
For retracts, suppose an arrow \(f:A\to B\) is a retract of an isomorphism \(g:C\to D\text{.}\) Write the two commuting squares using maps \(a:A\to C\text{,}\) \(b:B\to D\text{,}\) \(r:C\to A\text{,}\) \(s:D\to B\text{,}\) with \(ra=\id_A\) and \(sb=\id_B\text{.}\) The commutation equations are \(ga=bf\) and \(fr=sg\text{.}\) Then \(rg^{-1}b\) is an inverse to \(f\text{.}\) Applying this calculation to the images of a retract diagram under \(Q\) proves the claim.
