Proposition 3.5.

For a presheaf \(F\text{,}\) a pair \((X,u)\) with \(u\in F(X)\) determines a representation exactly when, for every \(T\in\mathcal C\text{,}\) the function
\begin{equation*} \Hom_{\mathcal C}(T,X)\longrightarrow F(T),\qquad f\longmapsto F(f)(u) \end{equation*}
is bijective. For a covariant functor \(G\text{,}\) the corresponding criterion is bijectivity of
\begin{equation*} \Hom_{\mathcal C}(X,T)\longrightarrow G(T),\qquad f\longmapsto G(f)(u). \end{equation*}
Thus choosing a representation is equivalent to choosing such a universal pair.
🔗
🔗

Proof.
The Yoneda lemma constructs a natural transformation from \(u\text{,}\) with precisely these component functions. A natural transformation is invertible exactly when every component is bijective. Conversely, evaluation at the identity recovers \(u\) from the representation.
