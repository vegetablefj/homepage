Proposition 10.14.

Suppose \(L:\mathcal C\rightleftarrows\mathcal A:i\) is an adjunction \(L\dashv i\) with \(i\) fully faithful. Put
\begin{equation*} W_L=\{f\in\Ar(\mathcal C):L(f)\text{ is invertible}\}. \end{equation*}
Then \(L\) is a localization at \(W_L\) in the functor-category sense. The induced functor \(\mathcal C[W_L^{-1}]\to\mathcal A\) is an equivalence. The associated monad \(iL\) is idempotent, as developed in Chapter 9.
🔗
🔗

Proof.
Write \(\eta:\id_{\mathcal C}\Rightarrow iL\) and \(\epsilon:Li\Rightarrow\id_{\mathcal A}\) for unit and counit. Full faithfulness makes \(\epsilon\) invertible. The triangle identity implies that \(L(\eta_X)\) is inverse to \(\epsilon_{LX}\text{,}\) so each \(\eta_X\) belongs to \(W_L\text{.}\)
🔗
For any \(F:\mathcal C\to\mathcal D\) inverting \(W_L\text{,}\) the transformation \(F\eta:F\Rightarrow FiL\) is a natural isomorphism. Thus \(F\) factors up to natural isomorphism through \(L\text{,}\) using \(Fi\text{.}\) More precisely, restriction along \(i\) is a quasi-inverse to \(L^*:\Fun(\mathcal A,\mathcal D)\to\Fun_{W_L}(\mathcal C,\mathcal D)\text{.}\) The two required natural isomorphisms are supplied by \(H\epsilon:HLi\cong H\) for a functor \(H\) on \(\mathcal A\text{,}\) and by \(F\eta:F\cong FiL\) for an inverting \(F\text{.}\) These comparisons are also natural in transformations, by naturality and whiskering. Hence \(L^*\) is an equivalence. Comparison with the path model now follows from Proposition 10.4.
