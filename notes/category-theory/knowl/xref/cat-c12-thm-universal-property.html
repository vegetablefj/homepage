Theorem 12.7.

Restriction along \(\iota\) gives an equivalence
\begin{equation*} \Fun_{\mathrm{filt}}(\operatorname{Ind}(\mathcal C),\mathcal D) \simeq\Fun(\mathcal C,\mathcal D), \end{equation*}
where the left side is the full subcategory on functors preserving small filtered colimits. An extension of \(F:\mathcal C\to\mathcal D\) is
\begin{equation*} \overline F(P)=\operatorname{colim}_{(c,x)\in\operatorname{el}(P)}F(c). \end{equation*}
For any filtered presentation of \(P\text{,}\) this gives a canonical isomorphism \(\overline F(P)\cong\operatorname{colim}_iF(X_i)\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗

Proof.
Yoneda identifies \((\iota\downarrow P)\) with \(\operatorname{el}(P)\text{.}\) The latter is small and filtered. Thus the pointwise left Kan extension \(\overline F=\operatorname{Lan}_{\iota}F\) exists by the formula in Chapter 7, even though arbitrary small colimits in \(\mathcal D\) have not been assumed. It is a functor with its specified comparison. Full faithfulness of \(\iota\) makes \(F\Rightarrow\overline F\iota\) invertible. Finality of a presentation’s functor \(I\to\operatorname{el}(P)\) gives the claimed presentation formula.
🔗
We must prove that this extension preserves filtered colimits of Ind objects. Let \(P=\operatorname{colim}_{i\in I}P_i\text{,}\) computed pointwise. For an object \(D\in\mathcal D\text{,}\) a compatible family of cocones on all the diagrams \(F\pi_i:\operatorname{el}(P_i)\to\mathcal D\) assigns a map \(F(c)\to D\) to every stage element \(x_i\in P_i(c)\text{,}\) compatibly both with element morphisms and with stage transitions.
🔗
Such a family defines a cocone on \(F\pi:\operatorname{el}(P)\to\mathcal D\text{.}\) For \(x\in P(c)\text{,}\) choose a stage representative and use its assigned map. Any two representatives become equal at a common stage, proving independence. For an element arrow \(f:(c,x)\to(d,y)\text{,}\) represent \(y\) at one stage. Its image under that stage’s \(P_i(f)\) represents \(x\text{,}\) so the needed cocone equation holds at that same stage. Conversely, any cocone on the final element diagram restricts to just such a compatible family.
🔗
This establishes a bijection, natural in \(D\text{,}\) between cocones on \(F\pi\) and compatible families of maps \(\overline F(P_i)\to D\text{.}\) Its correspondence uses exactly the canonical transition maps into \(\overline F(P)\text{.}\) Therefore \(\overline F(P)\text{,}\) with those maps, is their colimit. This proves preservation, including compatibility with the specified colimit cocone.
🔗
Finally let \(H:\operatorname{Ind}(\mathcal C)\to\mathcal D\) preserve filtered colimits. Density expresses every \(P\) as its canonical filtered colimit of representables, so the canonical Kan comparison
\begin{equation*} \overline{H\iota}(P)\longrightarrow H(P) \end{equation*}
is invertible. These comparisons are natural by the Kan extension property. Together with the invertible comparison on \(\mathcal C\text{,}\) they give inverse equivalences between extension and restriction. Their action on natural transformations is the unique action supplied by Kan universality, so the assertion includes transformations, not only the list of extendable functors.
