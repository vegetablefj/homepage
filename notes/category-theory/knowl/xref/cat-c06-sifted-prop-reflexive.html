Proposition 6.21.

The category \(\mathcal R\) is sifted and is not filtered. Consequently reflexive coequalizers commute with finite products in sets.
🔗
🔗

Proof.
Take two reflexive pairs of sets \(d_0,d_1:A\rightrightarrows B\) and \(e_0,e_1:A'\rightrightarrows B'\text{,}\) with common sections \(s,t\text{.}\) Write their coequalizers as \(B/{\sim}\) and \(B'/{\approx}\text{.}\) Let \(\equiv\) be the equivalence relation generated on \(B\times B'\) by
\begin{equation*} (d_0a,e_0a')\equiv(d_1a,e_1a'). \end{equation*}
Each generating identification respects \(\sim\) and \(\approx\text{,}\) so \(\equiv\) is contained in the coordinatewise equivalence relation. Conversely, choosing \(a'=t(b')\) shows
\begin{equation*} (d_0a,b')\equiv(d_1a,b'), \end{equation*}
and choosing \(a=s(b)\) allows the second coordinate to change while the first stays fixed. Taking equivalence closures gives every coordinatewise equivalence. Hence
\begin{equation*} (B\times B')/{\equiv}\cong(B/{\sim})\times(B'/{\approx}). \end{equation*}
This is the canonical comparison for the two diagrams. The constant singleton diagram has singleton coequalizer, so the empty-product test also holds. Binary and empty products establish siftedness.
🔗
To disprove filteredness, reduce words using \(d_i s=\id_B\text{.}\) The only arrows with source \(B\) are \(\id_B\) and \(s\text{.}\) Neither equalizes \(d_0,d_1\text{:}\) both \(d_0\ne d_1\) and \(sd_0\ne sd_1\text{.}\) These inequalities can be checked in a set-valued diagram with \(A=\{0,1,2\}\text{,}\) \(B=\{0,1\}\text{,}\) the inclusion as section, and \(d_0,d_1\) fixing \(0,1\) but sending \(2\) to \(0,1\text{,}\) respectively. Thus no arrow out of \(B\) equalizes the generating pair.
