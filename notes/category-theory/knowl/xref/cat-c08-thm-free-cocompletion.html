Theorem 8.9.

Let \(A:\mathcal C\to\mathcal D\text{,}\) with \(\mathcal D\) \(\U\)-cocomplete. Define
\begin{equation*} L_A(P)=\int^cP(c)\cdot A(c),\qquad N_A(D)(c)=\Hom_{\mathcal D}(A(c),D). \end{equation*}
There is an adjunction \(L_A\dashv N_A\text{,}\) and a canonical natural isomorphism \(L_Ah\cong A\text{.}\) Moreover, restriction along \(h\) gives an equivalence
\begin{equation*} \Fun_{\U\text{-colim}}(\widehat{\mathcal C},\mathcal D)\longrightarrow\Fun(\mathcal C,\mathcal D), \end{equation*}
where the subscript denotes the full subcategory of functors preserving \(\U\)-small colimits. Every such functor is a left adjoint.
🔗
🔗

Proof.
A map \(L_A(P)\to D\) is a compatible family \(t_{c,x}:A(c)\to D\text{.}\) Sending \(x\) to \(t_{c,x}\) identifies it with a map \(P(c)\to N_A(D)(c)\) for each \(c\text{.}\) The compatibility equation from Proposition 8.7 says exactly that these maps form a natural transformation. Thus
\begin{equation*} \Hom_{\mathcal D}(L_A(P),D)\cong\Nat(P,N_A(D)), \end{equation*}
naturally in both variables. Universal maps make \(L_A\) functorial; ordinary choice supplies one colimit for each presheaf, if no choices have been fixed. Since the collection of presheaves is an ordinary set, this uses the choice convention of Chapter 0. The formula gives the adjunction and therefore preservation of small colimits.
🔗
Setting \(P=h_c\) and applying Yoneda identifies the right side with \(\Hom_{\mathcal D}(A(c),D)\text{,}\) naturally in \(c,D\text{.}\) Uniqueness of representing objects gives \(L_Ah_c\cong A(c)\text{.}\) Explicitly, the cocone consists of \(A(f):A(d)\to A(c)\) for \(f:d\to c\text{;}\) the category of these pairs has terminal object \((c,\id_c)\text{.}\)
🔗
Now let \(T:\widehat{\mathcal C}\to\mathcal D\) preserve small colimits. Applying it to the density presentation gives, naturally in \(P\text{,}\)
\begin{equation*} T(P)\cong\operatorname{colim}_{(c,x)\in\operatorname{el}(P)}T(h_c)\cong L_{Th}(P). \end{equation*}
This recovers every colimit-preserving functor from its restriction; essential surjectivity of restriction follows from \(L_Ah\cong A\text{.}\) A natural transformation \(a:A\Rightarrow B\) induces \(L_A\Rightarrow L_B\) by applying \(a_c\) to every colimit summand. Conversely, a transformation between two colimit-preserving functors is determined by its components on representables: its component at \(P\) must commute with every map \(\widehat x:h_c\to P\text{,}\) and the colimit cocone is jointly epic. The induced transformation has exactly that property, proving fullness as well as faithfulness. Finally \(T\cong L_{Th}\) gives its right adjoint \(N_{Th}\text{.}\)
