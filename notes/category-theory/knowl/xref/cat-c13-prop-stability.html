Proposition 13.5.

Assume \(\mathcal E\) admits small \(\kappa\)-filtered colimits.
🔗
  1. A colimit of \(\kappa\)-presentable objects indexed by a category with fewer than \(\kappa\) arrows is \(\kappa\)-presentable, whenever this colimit exists in \(\mathcal E\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  2. A retract of a \(\kappa\)-presentable object is \(\kappa\)-presentable.
    🔗
    🔗
  3. If \(\lambda\geq\kappa\) is regular and the relevant colimits exist, every \(\kappa\)-presentable object is \(\lambda\)-presentable.
    🔗
    🔗
🔗
🔗

Proof.
For the first assertion write \(A=\operatorname{colim}_{k\in K}A_k\text{.}\) The functor \(\Hom(A,-)\) is the limit over \(K^\op\) of \(\Hom(A_k,-)\text{.}\) Apply their preservation properties and then the set interchange proposition to commute this limit with a \(\kappa\)-filtered colimit. The resulting bijection is the canonical comparison, since every step commutes with the colimit maps.
🔗
If \(A\) is a retract of \(B\text{,}\) the functor \(\Hom(A,-)\) is a retract of \(\Hom(B,-)\text{,}\) naturally. Its comparison map for any fixed colimit is correspondingly a retract, in the arrow category of sets, of the comparison for \(B\text{.}\) A retract of an isomorphism is an isomorphism, as proved in Proposition 10.8. Finally, a \(\lambda\)-filtered index is already \(\kappa\)-filtered, so the third assertion follows directly from the definition.
