Proposition 6.6.

For \(u:J\to I\) between small categories, the following conditions are equivalent.
🔗
  1. The functor \(u\) is final.
    🔗
    🔗
  2. For every \(D:I\to\Set_\U\text{,}\) the canonical map \(\operatorname{colim}_JDu\to\operatorname{colim}_ID\) is bijective.
    🔗
    🔗
  3. For every \(i\in I\text{,}\) \(\operatorname{colim}_{j\in J}\Hom_I(i,u(j))\) is a singleton.
    🔗
    🔗
🔗
Final functors compose. If \(v:K\to J\) and both \(v\) and \(uv\) are final, then \(u\) is final. A final functor induces a bijection \(\pi_0(J)\to\pi_0(I)\text{.}\) Every right adjoint is final, and every left adjoint is initial.
🔗
🔗

Proof.
The cofinality theorem proves the first implication. For the second, apply the set-valued condition to \(D=\Hom_I(i,-)\text{.}\) Its colimit is a singleton: every element \(a:i\to i'\) is the image of \(\id_i\) under \(D(a)\text{,}\) so all elements are identified. For the last implication, the covariant element category of \(j\mapsto\Hom_I(i,u(j))\) is \((i\downarrow u)\text{.}\) The component description in Proposition 6.2 identifies its colimit with \(\pi_0(i\downarrow u)\text{.}\) A singleton component set is exactly nonempty connectedness.
🔗
The comparison for a composite is the composite of the two comparisons. The equivalence just proved therefore gives composition and the stated cancellation property. Apply the comparison to the constant singleton diagram to obtain the assertion about components.
🔗
If \(L:I\to J\) is left adjoint to \(u:J\to I\text{,}\) its unit \(i\to uL(i)\) is an initial object of \((i\downarrow u)\) by the universal-arrow criterion for an adjunction. Thus each comma category is connected and nonempty, and \(u\) is final. If \(u\) is a left adjoint, its counit instead provides a terminal object of \((u\downarrow i)\text{,}\) proving initiality.
