Example 13.13 Accessibility has a rank and does not alone give an adjoint.

For a fixed set \(S\in\U\text{,}\) the power functor \((-)^S:\Set_\U\to\Set_\U\) is a right adjoint to \((-)\times S\text{.}\) It is \(\kappa\)-accessible for every regular small \(\kappa\) with \(|S|\lt\kappa\text{,}\) by the set interchange proposition. Such a regular cardinal exists; see [LiII, Lemma A.2.10]. If \(S\) is infinite, it does not preserve all filtered colimits: apply \(\Hom(S,-)\) to the union of finite subsets of \(S\text{.}\) The identity of \(S\) cannot come from any finite stage. Thus a right adjoint need not be finitely accessible.
🔗
The constant two-element-set functor on \(\Set_\U\) is finitely accessible, since a filtered colimit of the constant diagram with identity maps is the same two-element set. It is neither a left nor a right adjoint: it preserves neither the initial set nor the terminal set. Accessibility is a colimit condition; it does not replace the preservation hypotheses in the theorem.
