Proposition 5.17.

For an adjunction \(F\dashv G\text{,}\) the following assertions hold.
🔗
  1. The functor \(F\) is fully faithful if and only if every \(\eta_X\) is invertible.
    🔗
    🔗
  2. The functor \(G\) is fully faithful if and only if every \(\varepsilon_Y\) is invertible.
    🔗
    🔗
  3. The adjunction is an adjoint equivalence if both its unit and counit are natural isomorphisms. Every equivalence functor can be equipped with such an adjunction structure with a quasi-inverse.
    🔗
    🔗
🔗
🔗

Proof.
The composite of the map on hom-sets induced by \(F\) with the adjunction correspondence is
\begin{equation*} \Hom_{\mathcal C}(X,X')\longrightarrow\Hom_{\mathcal C}(X,GFX'),\qquad a\longmapsto\eta_{X'}a. \end{equation*}
Indeed, \(\Phi(F(a))=GF(a)\eta_X=\eta_{X'}a\text{.}\) Since \(\Phi\) is bijective, full faithfulness of \(F\) is equivalent to bijectivity of all these postcomposition maps. Testing an arrow by all objects, as in the Yoneda discussion, says exactly that \(\eta_{X'}\) is invertible. The second assertion is dual.
🔗
Invertible unit and counit exhibit quasi-inverse functors. Conversely, suppose \(F\) is an equivalence, and choose a quasi-inverse \(G\) and a natural isomorphism \(\eta:\id_{\mathcal C}\Rightarrow GF\text{.}\) The functor \(G\) is fully faithful. Thus applying \(G\text{,}\) followed by precomposition with \(\eta_X\text{,}\) gives natural bijections
\begin{equation*} \Hom_{\mathcal D}(FX,Y)\cong\Hom_{\mathcal C}(GFX,GY)\cong\Hom_{\mathcal C}(X,GY). \end{equation*}
These define an adjunction whose unit is the chosen \(\eta\text{.}\) Its counit is invertible by the second assertion, since \(G\) is fully faithful.
