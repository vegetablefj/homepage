Theorem 11.4.

Let \(f:(\mathcal E,\mathcal O_{\mathcal E})\to(\mathcal F,\mathcal O_{\mathcal F})\) be a morphism of ringed topoi. There are exact functors
\begin{equation*} Lf^*:\D(\mathcal O_{\mathcal F})\longrightarrow\D(\mathcal O_{\mathcal E}),\qquad Rf_*:\D(\mathcal O_{\mathcal E})\longrightarrow\D(\mathcal O_{\mathcal F}) \end{equation*}
with \(Lf^*\dashv Rf_*\text{.}\) If \(P\to K\) is K-flat with flat terms and \(L\to I\) is K-injective, they are computed by
\begin{equation*} Lf^*K=f^*P,\qquad Rf_*L=f_*I. \end{equation*}
Equivalently, the pullback is extension of scalars in the derived sense:
\begin{equation*} Lf^*K=\mathcal O_{\mathcal E}\Ltensor_{f^{-1}\mathcal O_{\mathcal F}}f^{-1}K. \end{equation*}
The functor \(Lf^*\) is symmetric monoidal and takes the unit to the unit.
🔗
🔗

Proof.
For the direct image, apply the K-injective construction. For pullback, use the K-flat replacements with flat terms. Pullback preserves this class, and takes quasi-isomorphisms between its objects to quasi-isomorphisms. This is a point-free resolution result for ringed topoi, proved in Stacks, Lemmas 21.18.1–21.18.2. It is what justifies the unbounded formula; exactness of the underlying \(f^{-1}\) alone would not justify extension of scalars.
🔗
The derived-adjunction theorem applied to \(f^*\dashv f_*\) gives the adjunction; see Stacks, Lemma 21.19.1. Tensor compatibility follows by replacing both factors by K-flat complexes with flat terms: their tensor and their pullbacks remain in that class, and the ordinary scalar-extension isomorphism gives the result.
