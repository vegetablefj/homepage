Theorem 7.2.

For every epimorphism \(q:U\to S\) in \(\mathcal E\text{,}\) pullback induces an equivalence
\begin{equation*} \mathcal E/S\xrightarrow{\sim}\operatorname{Desc}(q). \end{equation*}
In particular, objects and their morphisms descend effectively along epimorphisms.
🔗
🔗

Proof.
Given \((X,\theta)\text{,}\) define a subobject \(R\hookrightarrow X\times X\) by declaring \((x_1,x_2)\) to belong to \(R\) exactly when their images \(u_1,u_2\) have the same image in \(S\) and \(x_1=\theta_{12}(x_2)\text{.}\) This is a finite-limit construction. The diagonal identity, inverse transport, and cocycle identity make \(R\) an equivalence relation. By Proposition 3.7, its quotient \(r:X\to Y\) is effective: \(R\simeq X\times_YX\text{.}\) The map \(X\to S\) is constant on \(R\text{,}\) so it factors uniquely through \(Y\to S\text{.}\)
🔗
The comparison
\begin{equation*} \beta:X\longrightarrow Y\times_SU,\qquad x\longmapsto(r(x),u(x)) \end{equation*}
is a monomorphism. Indeed, if two elements have the same image in \(Y\text{,}\) effectiveness makes them related by \(R\text{;}\) if their images in \(U\) also agree, the diagonal identity for \(\theta\) makes them equal. This argument is valid over every test object.
🔗
It is also an epimorphism. A local element \((y,u)\) of its target locally lifts along the epimorphism \(X\to Y\) to an element \(x'\) over some \(u'\text{.}\) The equality \(q(u')=q(u)\) permits transport of \(x'\) to an element \(x\) over \(u\text{.}\) Related elements have the same image in \(Y\text{,}\) so \(\beta(x)=(y,u)\text{.}\) The local epimorphism criterion, rather than a test on global elements, proves the assertion. A map that is both monic and epic in a topos is an isomorphism. Under \(\beta\text{,}\) the original transport is precisely the canonical descent identification on the pullback of \(Y\text{.}\)
🔗
A morphism between descent data respects their equivalence relations and therefore descends to a unique map between the quotients. Conversely, the pullback of a map over \(S\) commutes with transport. Finally, beginning with \(Y\to S\text{,}\) the projection \(Y\times_SU\to Y\) is an epimorphism by stability under pullback, and its kernel pair is exactly the relation just constructed. Its quotient is \(Y\text{.}\) These constructions are inverse equivalences.
