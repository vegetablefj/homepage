Theorem 9.14.

Pullback of a flat module along any ringed geometric morphism is flat. If \((p_i)\) is a conservative family of points of \(\mathcal E\text{,}\) an \(\mathcal O\)-module \(M\) is flat if and only if every \(M_{p_i}\) is flat over \(\mathcal O_{p_i}\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗

Proof.
The first assertion is the point-independent theorem Stacks, Lemma 18.39.1. Its proof transports finite relations and local factorizations from Proposition 9.10 through a presentation of the geometric morphism, then extends scalars. This is not a formal consequence of right exactness or monoidality alone.
🔗
Apply that assertion to a point with its stalk ring to obtain necessity in the second assertion. For sufficiency, take a monomorphism and tensor with \(M\text{.}\) Its inverse image at \(p_i\) is the tensor of the corresponding module injection with \(M_{p_i}\text{,}\) hence is injective. Conservative points detect that the kernel sheaf is zero. This is Lemma 18.39.3 of the same section. Without such a conservative family, flatness remains the exact-functor condition, not an unqualified stalk test.
