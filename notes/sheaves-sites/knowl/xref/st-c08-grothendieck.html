Theorem 8.6.

The category \(\Mod(\mathcal O)\) is Grothendieck. In a small site presentation, \((P_U)_{U\in\mathcal C}\) is a generating family. Every \(M\) admits an epimorphism
\begin{equation*} \bigoplus_{(U,s),\ s\in M(U)}P_U\longrightarrow M. \end{equation*}
In particular, enough injectives and the Grothendieck-category existence results of homological algebra apply.
🔗
🔗

Proof.
The summand indexed by \((U,s)\) maps its universal generator to \(s\text{.}\) Every section over a site object occurs in this way, so the map is locally surjective, and in fact every such section is in its sectionwise image. The index is a set because the site is small and every \(M(U)\) is a set. If two module morphisms differ, they differ on some section and hence after precomposition with some \(P_U\text{.}\) Thus their coproduct is a generator. Cocompleteness and exact filtered colimits were proved in Proposition 8.4, which gives the Grothendieck property. The assertion about injectives now calls the general theorem, rather than a stalkwise construction.
