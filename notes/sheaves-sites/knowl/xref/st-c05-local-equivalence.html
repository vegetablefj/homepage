Proposition 5.10.

Let \(u:(\mathcal B,L)\to(\mathcal C,J)\) be continuous and cocontinuous. Assume the following conditions.
  1. If \(a,b:V\to U\) have \(u(a)=u(b)\text{,}\) they become equal after restriction along an \(L\)-covering sieve on \(V\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  2. Every arrow \(u(V)\to u(U)\) is locally of the form \(u(c)\text{:}\) after an \(L\)-cover of \(V\text{,}\) its composites lift to arrows to \(U\) in \(\mathcal B\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  3. Every object of \(\mathcal C\) has a \(J\)-covering sieve generated by arrows from objects \(u(U)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
Then the cocontinuous geometric morphism \(\Sh(\mathcal B,L)\to\Sh(\mathcal C,J)\) is an equivalence. Its inverse image is ordinary restriction.
🔗
🔗

Proof.
Since \(u\) is continuous, cocontinuous inverse image is already restriction. Its right adjoint has value \(\Nat(u^{\mathrm{pre}}h_V,F)\) at \(V\in\mathcal C\text{.}\) At \(V=u(U)\text{,}\) evaluation at \(\id_{u(U)}\) gives a map to \(F(U)\text{.}\) Local lifting of arrows determines any other component from this one. If two choices of a lift occur, local faithfulness makes them equal after a further cover; separatedness of \(F\) then gives equality. The locally prescribed components therefore glue and give an inverse to evaluation.
🔗
This proves the counit is an isomorphism. The unit is consequently an isomorphism when tested at the objects \(u(U)\text{.}\) Those objects cover every target object by the third condition, so the sheaf axiom makes the unit an isomorphism everywhere. Both adjunction maps are isomorphisms, proving the claim.
