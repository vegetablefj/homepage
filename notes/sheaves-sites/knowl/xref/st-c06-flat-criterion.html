Proposition 6.3.

The functor \(L_A:\PSh(\mathcal C)\to\Set\) preserves finite limits if and only if \(A\) is flat. It is always a left adjoint, with right adjoint
\begin{equation*} R_A(S)(U)=\operatorname{Map}(A(U),S). \end{equation*}
There is a canonical bijection \(L_A(h_U)\simeq A(U)\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗

Proof.
A map from the germ colimit to \(S\) is a compatible family of maps \(F(U)\times A(U)\to S\text{.}\) This is precisely a natural transformation \(F\to R_A(S)\text{,}\) proving the adjunction. The representable formula sends the class \((V,b,v:V\to U)\) to \(A(v)b\text{,}\) with inverse represented by \((U,a,\id_U)\text{.}\)
🔗
If \(A\) is flat, filtered colimits of sets commute with finite limits, so the stalk formula proves finite-limit preservation. Conversely, assume finite-limit preservation. Since \(L_A(1)=1\text{,}\) the neighborhood category is nonempty. For \(a\in A(U)\text{,}\) \(b\in A(V)\text{,}\) preservation of the product \(h_U\times h_V\) gives a representative \((W,c)\) and arrows \(W\to U\text{,}\) \(W\to V\) carrying \(c\) to \(a,b\text{.}\) This is a common refinement.
🔗
For parallel arrows \(r,s:(V,b)\rightrightarrows(U,a)\text{,}\) consider the presheaf equalizer of \(h_V\rightrightarrows h_U\text{.}\) Since \(A(r)b=A(s)b\text{,}\) preservation of this equalizer gives a representative \((W,c)\) and a map \(t:W\to V\) with \(A(t)c=b\) and \(rt=st\text{.}\) This is the required refinement. Thus \(\int A\) is cofiltered.
