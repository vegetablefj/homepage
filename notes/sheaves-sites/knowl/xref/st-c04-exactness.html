Proposition 4.7.

For a geometric morphism, inverse image preserves monomorphisms, epimorphisms, images, and quotients by equivalence relations. It induces an exact functor
\begin{equation*} f^{-1}:\Ab(\mathcal F)\longrightarrow\Ab(\mathcal E). \end{equation*}
Direct image induces a left exact functor on Abelian objects.
🔗
🔗

Proof.
Finite-limit preservation gives monomorphisms and kernel pairs. Epimorphisms in a topos are effective, and a left adjoint preserves their coequalizers; hence inverse image preserves epimorphisms. Apply this to the image factorization. For equivalence relations, finite limits give the pulled-back relation and preservation of its coequalizer gives its quotient. Abelian group operations use finite products and equations, so inverse image preserves those structures. Kernels and cokernels of Abelian objects are the corresponding finite-limit and coequalizer constructions, proving exactness. A right adjoint preserves limits, giving left exactness of direct image.
