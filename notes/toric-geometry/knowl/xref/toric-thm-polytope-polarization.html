Theorem 4.15 Polytopes and ample polarizations.

Let \(X_P=X_{\Sigma_P}\text{.}\) For each ray of the normal fan put
\begin{equation*} a_\rho=-\min_{m\in P}\langle m,u_\rho\rangle,\qquad D_P=\sum_\rho a_\rho D_\rho. \end{equation*}
Then \(D_P\) is Cartier and ample, and \(P_{D_P}=P\text{.}\) Conversely, an invariant ample Cartier divisor on a complete toric variety has a full-dimensional lattice polytope whose normal fan is precisely the original fan.
🔗
🔗

Proof.
On the normal cone of a vertex \(p\text{,}\) the support function is the integral linear form \(\langle p,\cdot\rangle\text{.}\) These give Cartier data. The minimum is attained at \(p\) exactly on that normal cone, giving strict concavity and therefore ampleness. The facet inequalities recover \(P\text{.}\)
🔗
Conversely generation makes \(P_D\) the convex hull of the Cartier points. Strict concavity says that the vectors where \(m_\sigma\) minimizes pairing are exactly \(\sigma\text{,}\) identifying the normal fan. The polytope has full dimension: the proof of ampleness puts \(qm_\sigma\) and \(qm_\sigma+u_{\sigma,j}\) in a sufficiently large dilate, and those differences generate \(M\) as a group. Its affine span is therefore \(M_{\R}\text{.}\)
