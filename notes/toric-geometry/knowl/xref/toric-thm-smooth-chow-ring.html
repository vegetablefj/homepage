Theorem 5.4 An integral presentation.

If every cone of \(\Sigma\) is smooth, then
\begin{equation} A^*(X_\Sigma)\cong R_\Sigma/(I_\Sigma+J_\Sigma),\qquad x_\rho\longmapsto[D_\rho].\tag{5.2} \end{equation}
This algebraic statement holds over \(k\) and does not require completeness.
🔗
🔗

Proof.
The character-divisor formula kills \(J_\Sigma\text{.}\) Invariant divisors for rays not lying in a common cone have empty intersection, by the orbit–cone correspondence, so their product is zero and \(I_\Sigma\) is killed as well. For the rays of a cone \(\sigma\text{,}\) smooth coordinates on each chart show that the corresponding divisors meet transversely with intersection \(V(\sigma)\text{,}\) of multiplicity one. Therefore the induced map from the quotient ring is surjective by orbit generation.
🔗
Define a homomorphism of graded groups in the other direction by
\begin{equation*} [V(\sigma)]\longmapsto x_\sigma:=\prod_{\rho\in\sigma(1)}x_\rho. \end{equation*}
We check the complete relation list (5.1). Smoothness makes \(u_{\sigma/\tau}\) the primitive image of the one new ray of \(\sigma\) outside \(\tau\text{.}\) Since \(m\in M(\tau)\text{,}\) the proposed image of the relation equals
\begin{equation*} x_\tau\sum_{\rho\in\Sigma(1)}\langle m,u_\rho\rangle x_\rho. \end{equation*}
Terms on rays of \(\tau\) have coefficient zero. A remaining term vanishes modulo \(I_\Sigma\) unless the ray and \(\tau\) lie in a cone; when they do, their union generates the unique next-dimensional face of that smooth cone. Thus the expression is exactly the asserted relation and is zero modulo \(J_\Sigma\text{.}\) The inverse is well defined.
🔗
Its composite with the first map is the identity on the generating orbit classes. In the other order it fixes every squarefree cone monomial \(x_\sigma\text{,}\) because the product calculation sends that monomial to its orbit class. These monomials span the quotient as a group: monomials with repeated factors reduce to linear combinations of the \(x_\sigma\) by choosing a character taking value \(1\) on one repeated ray and \(0\) on the other rays in its smooth supporting cone, and multiplying its linear relation by the remaining monomial. At each nonzero term the number of distinct ray factors increases. The reduction stops at squarefree cone monomials. Hence the two maps are inverse on every graded piece.
