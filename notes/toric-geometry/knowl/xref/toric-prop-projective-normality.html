Proposition 4.17 Projective normality is integer decomposition.

If \(D\) is very ample, the embedding by its complete linear system is projectively normal if and only if \(P_D\) is normal. For an ample divisor, sufficiently large multiples are both very ample and normally generated.
🔗
🔗

Proof.
The homogeneous coordinate ring of the embedding is the degree-one generated subring of \(R(D)\text{.}\) Its degree-\(q\) exponents are sums of \(q\) lattice points of \(P_D\text{.}\) Its group of exponent differences is \(M\oplus\Z\text{:}\) on the affine chart at a vertex, very ampleness makes the differences of section weights generate \(k[S_\sigma]\text{,}\) hence the whole character lattice as a group, and one degree-one point supplies the degree direction. The semigroup normalization theorem now identifies its integral closure with \(R(D)\text{.}\) Equality is exactly the integer decomposition property.
🔗
The dilation theorem for lattice polytopes states that if \(\dim P=n\geq2\text{,}\) then \(qP\) is normal for every \(q\geq n-1\text{;}\) see [CLS11, Theorem 2.2.12]. Segments and points are already normal. Combine this with sufficiently large very ample multiples from the ample criterion.
