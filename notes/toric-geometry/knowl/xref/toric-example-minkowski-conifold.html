Example 15.5 The unit square smooths the conifold.

In \(N_0=\Z^2\text{,}\) take \(Q_0=[0,e_1]\) and \(Q_1=[0,e_2]\text{.}\) Their sum is the unit square. The enlarged cone has four primitive generators
\begin{gather*} v_1=(0,0,1,0),\quad v_2=(1,0,1,0),\\ v_3=(0,0,0,1),\quad v_4=(0,1,0,1). \end{gather*}
They form a lattice basis: their differences give the first two coordinate directions, and \(v_1,v_3\) give the last two. Hence \(\widetilde Y\cong\A^4\text{.}\) Let \(a,b,c,d\) be the characters dual to this ordered basis. The exponent of \(T_0\) pairs as \((1,1,0,0)\) with these generators, and that of \(T_1\) pairs as \((0,0,1,1)\text{.}\) Thus
\begin{equation*} T_0=ab,\qquad T_1=cd,\qquad\pi(a,b,c,d)=ab-cd. \end{equation*}
The family is the explicit hypersurface family
\begin{equation} ab-cd=t.\tag{15.4} \end{equation}
🔗
The zero fiber is the affine conifold of Example 8.13. For a nonzero parameter \(\tau\text{,}\) its partial derivatives are \(b,a,-d,-c\text{.}\) Simultaneous vanishing would force all four coordinates to vanish, which is incompatible with \(ab-cd=\tau\text{.}\) Thus every nonzero fiber is smooth, whereas the central fiber has its singular point at the origin. This is a smoothing obtained from a decomposition into two segments, even though neither segment is full-dimensional in \(\R^2\text{.}\)
🔗
The small toric resolutions calculated in Section 7.4 perform a different operation: a proper birational morphism from a smooth variety to the fixed singular central fiber. Here the parameter changes the fiber itself. The smooth total space \(\A^4\) in this construction is one dimension larger than that central threefold; it is not one of its small resolutions.
