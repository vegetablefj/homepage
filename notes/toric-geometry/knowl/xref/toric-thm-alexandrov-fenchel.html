Theorem 13.5 The Alexandrov–Fenchel inequality.

For nonempty compact convex sets \(K,L,K_3,\ldots,K_n\subseteq\R^n\text{,}\) with \(n\geq2\text{,}\)
\begin{equation} V(K,L,K_3,\ldots,K_n)^2\geq V(K,K,K_3,\ldots,K_n)V(L,L,K_3,\ldots,K_n).\tag{13.3} \end{equation}
🔗
🔗

Proof.
First suppose all sets are full-dimensional lattice polytopes \(P_i\text{.}\) The normal fan of \(P_1+\cdots+P_n\) refines the normal fan of each summand. Choose a smooth refinement whose toric morphism is projective, using Subsection 7.2.3. Its toric variety \(X\) is smooth and projective: the original normal-fan variety is projective, and the composite of projective morphisms is projective.
🔗
The minimum support function of each \(P_i\) is integral and linear on this common fan. It defines a globally generated, hence nef, Cartier divisor \(D_i\) on \(X\text{,}\) whose divisor polytope is exactly \(P_i\text{.}\) Refinement does not alter that function or its supporting inequalities. Apply the mixed intersection inequality to these divisors. Every top product equals \(n!\) times the corresponding mixed volume. Its square on the left and product on the right have the same factor \((n!)^2\text{,}\) which cancels. This proves the lattice-polytope case.
🔗
For full-dimensional rational polytopes, scale all of them by one positive integer so their vertices become integral. Mixed volumes scale by its \(n\)-th power, and the inequality scales by its \(2n\)-th power. Cancelling proves the rational case. The remaining approximation steps, including sets of smaller dimension, are justified below and complete the proof.
