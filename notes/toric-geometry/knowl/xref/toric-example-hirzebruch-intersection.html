Example 5.7 The intersection form of a Hirzebruch surface.

Use the rays and classes of Example 3.18. The nonadjacent pairs are \(13\) and \(24\text{.}\) Since \([D_1]=[D_3]=F\) and \([D_4]=S+aF\text{,}\)
\begin{equation*} A^*(\mathbb F_a)=\Z[S,F]/(F^2,S(S+aF)),\qquad\deg SF=1. \end{equation*}
It follows that
\begin{equation*} F^2=0,\qquad S^2=-a,\qquad(\alpha S+\beta F)^2=2\alpha\beta-a\alpha^2. \end{equation*}
Thus the class denoted by \(S\) is the negative section when \(a>0\text{.}\) Its square has been derived from the fan, rather than imported from the projective-bundle description.
🔗
For the invariant representative \(D=\alpha D_2+\beta D_1\text{,}\) the inequalities are
\begin{equation*} p\geq-\beta,\qquad q\geq-\alpha,\qquad p\leq aq,\qquad q\leq0. \end{equation*}
If \(\alpha\geq0\) and \(\beta\geq a\alpha\text{,}\) the polytope has area \(\alpha\beta-a\alpha^2/2\) in the measure for \(M=\Z^2\text{.}\) Twice this area is precisely the displayed self-intersection. Degenerate segments and points have zero two-dimensional area, as required.
