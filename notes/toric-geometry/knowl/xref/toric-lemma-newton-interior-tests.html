Lemma 12.2 Support inequalities and finite ray tests.

The polyhedron and its interior satisfy
\begin{equation*} P=\{q:\langle q,v\rangle\ge h_I(v) \text{ for every }v\in\sigma\}, \end{equation*}
and, for \(c>0\text{,}\)
\begin{equation*} q\in\operatorname{Int}(cP) \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \langle q,v\rangle>c h_I(v) \text{ for every nonzero }v\in\sigma. \end{equation*}
If \(\Sigma\) is a subdivision of \(\sigma\) on whose cones \(h_I\) is linear, it suffices in both tests to use the primitive generators of the finitely many rays of \(\Sigma\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗

Proof.
Each displayed weak inequality holds on \(P\text{.}\) Conversely, separation of a point outside the closed convex polyhedron from \(P\) gives a linear functional bounded below on \(P\text{.}\) Its pairing with every recession direction must be nonnegative, so its vector belongs to \(\sigma\text{.}\) Its minimum on \(P\) is \(h_I(v)\text{;}\) the separating point fails that inequality. The minimum for \(cP\) is \(c h_I(v)\text{.}\)
🔗
An interior point cannot have equality in a nonzero supporting inequality. To prove the converse and the finite test, choose a cone \(\tau\in\Sigma\text{.}\) On it the function \(\langle q,v\rangle-c h_I(v)\) is linear. Every nonzero vector of \(\tau\) is a nonnegative combination of its ray generators, with at least one positive coefficient. Strict positivity on those generators gives strict positivity on every such vector; the same argument applies to weak inequalities. Finally, finitely many strict linear inequalities hold on a neighborhood of \(q\text{.}\) Their weak versions define \(cP\text{,}\) so that neighborhood lies in \(cP\text{,}\) proving interior membership.
