Theorem 8.3 Reflexive polytopes and Gorenstein toric Fano varieties.

Full-dimensional reflexive polytopes correspond to projective normal toric varieties for which \(-K_X\) is Cartier and ample, through
\begin{equation*} P=P_{-K_X},\qquad X=X_{\Sigma_P}. \end{equation*}
Equivalence of polytopes means lattice isomorphism preserving the origin. In positive rank,
\begin{equation*} P_{-K_X}=\{m:\langle m,u_\rho\rangle\geq-1\},\qquad P_{-K_X}^{\circ}=\operatorname{conv}\{u_\rho\}. \end{equation*}
These varieties are Gorenstein Fano; smoothness is not required.
🔗
🔗

Proof.
For such a toric variety, \(-K_X=\sum_\rho D_\rho\) by Theorem 3.13. Its ample Cartier polytope is a full-dimensional lattice polytope with normal fan \(\Sigma\text{,}\) by Theorem 4.15. The origin satisfies all its inequalities strictly. Its facets have primitive normal vectors \(u_\rho\) and constant \(-1\text{,}\) so it is reflexive.
🔗
Conversely, the normal fan of a reflexive polytope gives an ample Cartier divisor with these facet coefficients equal to one. This divisor is exactly \(-K_X\text{.}\) The Cartier canonical divisor and the toric Cohen–Macaulay property give Gorenstein local rings, as explained in Section 3.5. The two constructions are inverse because an ample polytope recovers its fan. The rank-zero case is the point with polytope \(\{0\}\text{.}\)
