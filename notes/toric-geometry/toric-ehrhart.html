    Chapter 16 Lattice-point enumeration and Hilbert series

    The section formula turns a polarized toric variety into a lattice-point counting problem. Its Hilbert series retains more information than the leading volume coefficient: a finite numerator records boundary and interior points, and a reciprocity identity recovers interior counts from the same polynomial. We establish these statements through a disjoint half-open decomposition of the cone over a lattice polytope. Nonunimodular simplices contribute extra lattice points in their fundamental parallelepipeds, rather than being replaced by unimodular ones.
    The prerequisites are affine semigroup algebras and the polytope dictionary of Chapter 4. Our proofs are combinatorial and work over any field \(k\text{.}\) The algebraic applications therefore retain the original ground-field convention. The standard reference for Ehrhart polynomiality and reciprocity is [CLS11, Theorem 9.4.2]; the proof below includes the numerator and boundary conventions needed for our examples.

    Section 16.1 The intrinsic lattice and the Ehrhart algebra

    Let \(P\) be a nonempty lattice polytope in \(M_\R\text{,}\) of dimension \(d\text{.}\) Choose a lattice vertex \(v\text{,}\) and replace it temporarily by \(\overline P=P-v\) in
    \begin{equation*} L=M\cap\operatorname{span}_\R(P-P). \end{equation*}
    This is a rank-\(d\) lattice, and \(\overline P\) is full-dimensional in \(L_\R\text{.}\) Translation gives the bijection
    \begin{equation*} qP\cap M\longrightarrow q\overline P\cap L,\qquad x\longmapsto x-qv. \end{equation*}
    For \(q>0\) it also identifies the relative interiors. Thus all counts below use the intrinsic lattice; they do not treat a lower-dimensional polytope as having positive ambient volume. We now rename \(\overline P\) as \(P\subset L_\R\text{.}\)

    Definition 16.1 The cone and its height grading.

    In \(\Lambda=L\oplus\Z\text{,}\) let
    \begin{equation*} C_P=\Cone\{(p,1):p\in P\},\qquad S_P=C_P\cap\Lambda,\qquad R_P=k[S_P]. \end{equation*}
    Grade this algebra by the last coordinate. Set
    \begin{equation*} E_P(q)=\#(qP\cap L)\quad(q\geq0),\qquad F_P(z)=\sum_{q\geq0}E_P(q)z^q. \end{equation*}
    The degree-zero part is \(k\text{,}\) because the only height-zero point of \(C_P\) is zero.
    The cone is rational, strongly convex, and full-dimensional in \(\Lambda_\R\text{.}\) Its lattice-point monoid is finitely generated by Gordan’s lemma and saturated in \(\Lambda\text{.}\) Its group of differences is all of \(\Lambda\text{:}\) choose an integral interior vector \(w\text{;}\) for any \(\lambda\in\Lambda\text{,}\) a sufficiently large \(q\) puts both \(qw\) and \(qw+\lambda\) in the cone. Their difference is \(\lambda\text{.}\) The normalization theorem of Theorem 1.17 therefore makes \(R_P\) a normal domain with this full fraction-field lattice.
    Degree \(q\) has basis \(\chi^{(m,q)}\) for \(m\in qP\cap L\text{,}\) so \(F_P\) is precisely its graded Hilbert series. On the normal-fan variety \(X_P\text{,}\) the ample Cartier divisor \(D_P\) has these section bases. Multiplication adds exponents, giving
    \begin{equation*} R_P\cong\bigoplus_{q\geq0}H^0(X_P,\mathcal O(qD_P)). \end{equation*}
    This is the full section ring. It need not be generated in degree one. In particular, saturation of this monoid does not assert the integer decomposition property of \(P\text{.}\)
    For \(d=0\) the translated polytope is a point in the zero lattice. Then \(R_P=k[u]\) with \(\deg u=1\text{,}\) and \(E_P(q)=1\text{.}\) We retain this case in the algebraic formulas, while interpreting geometric interiors only for positive dilates.

    Section 16.2 A disjoint half-open cone decomposition

    Triangulate \(P\) into lattice simplices using its vertices, with compatible intersections. Such a triangulation can be obtained by pulling: order the vertices, choose the first vertex in each face, and cone over the triangulated facets not containing it. Induction on faces gives compatible triangulations, and the resulting cones cover the polytope. No unimodularity or shelling property is required here. Coning the maximal simplices gives a subdivision of \(C_P\) into simplicial cones \(C_\nu\text{,}\) each with \(D=d+1\) independent height-one generators \(a_{\nu,1},\ldots,a_{\nu,D}\text{.}\)
    Choose \(w\in\operatorname{Int}(C_P)\) avoiding the linear spans of all facets of these cones. In the real basis of each cone write \(w=\sum_j\mu_{\nu,j}a_{\nu,j}\text{.}\) Every \(\mu_{\nu,j}\) is nonzero. Define a half-open cone by
    \begin{equation*} C_\nu^+=\left\{\sum_j\lambda_ja_{\nu,j}: \lambda_j\geq0,\ \lambda_j>0\text{ if }\mu_{\nu,j}<0\right\}. \end{equation*}
    These cones partition the closed cone \(C_P\text{.}\) To check this, for \(x\in C_P\) move to \(x+\epsilon w\) for a sufficiently small positive \(\epsilon\text{.}\) It lies in the interior of \(C_P\) and in the interior of a unique subdivision cone. Genericity excludes motion along a facet, so that cone is unchanged for all sufficiently small \(\epsilon\text{.}\) Its coordinate inequalities give exactly the displayed condition.
    Let \(\Pi_\nu^+\) be the bounded parallelepiped with coordinates
    \begin{equation*} \theta_j\in \begin{cases}(0,1]&\mu_{\nu,j}<0,\\{[0,1)}&\mu_{\nu,j}>0. \end{cases} \end{equation*}
    Every lattice point of \(C_\nu^+\) has a unique expression
    \begin{equation*} p+\sum_j n_ja_{\nu,j},\qquad p\in\Pi_\nu^+\cap\Lambda,\quad n_j\in\N. \end{equation*}
    For a closed coordinate use its integer part. For an open coordinate use \(n_j=\lceil\lambda_j\rceil-1\text{,}\) leaving a remainder in \((0,1]\text{.}\) Subtracting integral generators shows that the remaining point \(p\) is integral. This proves both existence and uniqueness.

    Theorem 16.2 The nonnegative numerator.

    There is a polynomial
    \begin{equation*} h_P^*(z)=h_0^*+h_1^*z+\cdots+h_d^*z^d \end{equation*}
    with nonnegative integer coefficients such that
    \begin{equation} F_P(z)=\frac{h_P^*(z)}{(1-z)^{d+1}}.\tag{16.1} \end{equation}
    Its constant coefficient is \(h_0^*=1\text{.}\)

    Proof.
    Each height-one generator contributes the geometric series \((1-z)^{-1}\text{.}\) The disjoint decomposition gives
    \begin{equation*} h_P^*(z)=\sum_\nu\sum_{p\in\Pi_\nu^+\cap\Lambda}z^{\operatorname{height}(p)}. \end{equation*}
    These are finite sums with nonnegative integer coefficients. Since \(\operatorname{height}(w)=\sum_j\mu_{\nu,j}>0\text{,}\) at least one coordinate in each parallelepiped has interval \([0,1)\text{.}\) Therefore its height is strictly less than \(D\text{.}\) Integral heights lie between zero and \(d\text{,}\) proving the degree bound. Finally the degree-zero coefficient of \(F_P\) is one, so its numerator has constant coefficient one.
    This proves nonnegativity by counting actual lattice points in disjoint half-open pieces. Adding the generating series of closed simplices would count their common faces repeatedly and would not prove the same assertion. A simplex can have many parallelepiped representatives; those points are essential when its determinant is not one.

    Section 16.3 Polynomiality and reciprocity

    Theorem 16.3 The counting polynomial and its coefficients.

    The function \(E_P(q)\) is the restriction of a degree-\(d\) polynomial, including at \(q=0\text{,}\) and
    \begin{equation} E_P(q)=\sum_{j=0}^d h_j^*\binom{q+d-j}{d}.\tag{16.2} \end{equation}
    For \(d\geq1\text{,}\)
    \begin{equation*} h_1^*=\#(P\cap L)-d-1,\qquad \sum_jh_j^*=d!\vol_L(P). \end{equation*}
    The leading coefficient of the polynomial is \(\vol_L(P)\text{.}\)

    Proof.
    Expand \((1-z)^{-d-1}\text{.}\) Coefficient extraction gives the displayed expression for \(q\geq0\text{.}\) Interpret each binomial as its falling-product polynomial. When \(q<j\leq d\text{,}\) its upper argument is a nonnegative integer smaller than \(d\text{,}\) so it vanishes, exactly matching the absent power-series coefficient. Hence this expression is a polynomial valid at every nonnegative integer. At \(q=1\) only its first two terms survive, giving the formula for \(h_1^*\text{.}\)
    Its leading coefficient is \((\sum_jh_j^*)/d!\text{.}\) On the other hand \(q^{-d}\#(qP\cap L)\to\vol_L(P)\text{:}\) in lattice coordinates this is the grid Riemann sum of the indicator function of \(P\text{.}\) The boundary lies in finitely many hyperplanes and has measure zero, so these Riemann sums converge to the volume. The positive volume shows that the degree is exactly \(d\) and identifies the coefficient and numerator sum. The dimension-zero formulas instead read \(E_P=1\) and \(h_P^*=1\text{,}\) with volume one.

    Theorem 16.4 Interior points and reciprocity.

    For every positive integer \(q\text{,}\) let \(E_P^\circ(q)\) count the lattice points in the relative interior of \(qP\text{.}\) Then
    \begin{equation} E_P^\circ(q)=(-1)^dE_P(-q) =\sum_{j=0}^d h_j^*\binom{q+j-1}{d}.\tag{16.3} \end{equation}
    In particular \(h_d^*\) counts the interior lattice points of \(P\) when \(d\geq1\text{.}\)

    Proof.
    Repeat the half-open construction with \(-w\text{.}\) Its pieces partition \(\operatorname{Int}(C_P)\text{:}\) an interior point stays in the cone after a small displacement by \(-w\text{,}\) whereas at a boundary point some supporting functional is zero and becomes negative. In each simplicial cone the open and closed facets are exchanged. Reflection
    \begin{equation*} p\longmapsto\sum_j a_{\nu,j}-p \end{equation*}
    exchanges its two half-open parallelepipeds and changes height \(j\) into \(d+1-j\text{.}\) Thus the interior generating series is
    \begin{equation} \sum_{q\geq1}E_P^\circ(q)z^q =\frac{z^{d+1}h_P^*(z^{-1})}{(1-z)^{d+1}}.\tag{16.4} \end{equation}
    Its height-\(q\) slice is the interior of \(qP\) for \(q>0\text{.}\) Coefficient extraction gives the last expression in the statement. Finally the polynomial identity
    \begin{equation*} \binom{-q+d-j}{d}=(-1)^d\binom{q+j-1}{d} \end{equation*}
    proves reciprocity from the binomial formula for \(E_P\text{.}\) Setting \(q=1\) leaves only \(j=d\text{,}\) proving the interior coefficient assertion.
    Negative arguments refer to polynomial evaluation, not to counting a negatively scaled polytope. The interior formula is asserted only for \(q>0\text{.}\) For \(d>0\text{,}\) at zero the geometric set \(0P=\{0\}\) has changed dimension; its relative interior must not be substituted into the reciprocity statement for the original dimension \(d\text{.}\) For a zero-dimensional polytope, both positive-dilate counts are one and reciprocity holds with sign one.
    For an ample divisor on a smooth projective toric variety, Section 17.3 interprets the same reciprocity identity through Serre duality and adjoint vanishing. Section 17.5 uses the polynomial and reciprocity proved here to derive Pick’s formula for every lattice polygon.

    Section 16.4 Simplices, boxes, and the Reeve tetrahedron

    Example 16.5 A basic simplex and an interval.

    For the standard simplex \(\Delta_d=\conv(0,e_1,\ldots,e_d)\text{,}\) the lifted generators form a lattice basis of \(\Z^d\oplus\Z\text{.}\) The corresponding half-open fundamental parallelepiped, with coefficients in \([0,1)\text{,}\) contains only zero. Hence
    \begin{equation*} h_{\Delta_d}^*(z)=1,\qquad E_{\Delta_d}(q)=\binom{q+d}{d},\qquad E_{\Delta_d}^\circ(q)=\binom{q-1}{d}\quad(q>0). \end{equation*}
    The closed count also follows by counting nonnegative integer coordinates with sum at most \(q\text{;}\) the interior count requires all coordinates positive and their sum strictly less than \(q\text{.}\) In particular no interior lattice point occurs before dilation \(d+1\text{.}\)
    For \(P=[0,r]\) in \(\Z\text{,}\) with integer \(r\geq1\text{,}\) direct counting gives \(E_P(q)=rq+1\text{.}\) Therefore
    \begin{equation*} F_P(z)=\frac{1+(r-1)z}{(1-z)^2},\qquad E_P^\circ(q)=rq-1\quad(q>0). \end{equation*}
    The numerator sum is the lattice length \(r\text{,}\) and its last coefficient counts the \(r-1\) interior points of the interval itself. This is a simple nonunimodular fundamental-parallelepiped calculation.

    Example 16.6 The square and the cube.

    For the unit square, \(E_P(q)=(q+1)^2\text{.}\) Since it has four lattice points and no interior lattice point, the coefficient identities yield
    \begin{equation*} h_P^*(z)=1+z,\qquad F_P(z)=\frac{1+z}{(1-z)^3}. \end{equation*}
    Its numerator sum is two, agreeing with \(2!\) times area one. Reciprocity gives the familiar interior count \((q-1)^2\text{.}\)
    For the unit cube in \(\Z^3\text{,}\) the closed count is \((q+1)^3\text{.}\) It has eight lattice points, so \(h_1^*=8-4=4\text{,}\) and no interior lattice point, so \(h_3^*=0\text{.}\) The numerator sum is \(3!=6\text{,}\) leaving \(h_2^*=1\text{.}\) Thus
    \begin{equation*} h_P^*(z)=1+4z+z^2,\qquad F_P(z)=\frac{1+4z+z^2}{(1-z)^4}. \end{equation*}
    Its interior count is \((q-1)^3\) for \(q>0\text{.}\) Counts multiply for Cartesian products, but Ehrhart series do not multiply as ordinary series: the product polytope requires the same dilation degree in both factors. The square is already a check that multiplying the interval numerators would give the wrong answer.

    Example 16.7 A normal Ehrhart algebra with missing degree-one generators.

    For integer \(r\geq2\text{,}\) let
    \begin{equation*} Q_r=\conv\{0,e_1,e_2,(1,1,r)\}\subset\R^3, \qquad L=\Z^3. \end{equation*}
    Its only lattice points are its four vertices, as checked in Example 4.18. We now count the fundamental parallelepiped of its lifted simplex. Its four generators are
    \begin{equation*} a_0=(0,0,0,1),\quad a_1=(1,0,0,1),\quad a_2=(0,1,0,1),\quad a_3=(1,1,r,1). \end{equation*}
    Their determinant has absolute value \(r\text{.}\) With all coefficients in \([0,1)\text{,}\) the lattice representatives are exactly
    \begin{equation*} 0,\qquad (1,1,j,2)\quad(1\leq j\leq r-1). \end{equation*}
    Indeed the third coordinate fixes the coefficient of \(a_3\) to \(j/r\text{.}\) For \(j>0\text{,}\) integrality of the first two coordinates forces the coefficients of \(a_1,a_2\) to be \(1-j/r\text{.}\) Integrality of the height then forces the coefficient of \(a_0\) to be \(j/r\text{,}\) giving height two. For \(j=0\) all coefficients are zero.
    Consequently
    \begin{gather*} h_{Q_r}^*(z)=1+(r-1)z^2,\\ E_{Q_r}(q)=\binom{q+3}{3}+(r-1)\binom{q+1}{3},\\ E_{Q_r}^\circ(q)=\binom{q-1}{3}+(r-1)\binom{q+1}{3}\quad(q>0). \end{gather*}
    Binomials with nonnegative upper argument smaller than three are zero. In particular \(2Q_r\) has exactly \(r-1\) interior lattice points, namely \((1,1,j)\) with \(1\leq j<r\text{.}\) None is a sum of two lattice points of \(Q_r\text{,}\) since the third coordinates of such a sum are multiples of \(r\text{.}\) The polytope fails integer decomposition, while its full Ehrhart algebra remains normal.
    The fraction-field distinction is essential. The subalgebra generated only by the four height-one points is a polynomial ring: those lifted vectors are linearly independent, so they have no binomial relation. Their difference group has index \(r\) in \(\Z^3\oplus\Z\text{.}\) This polynomial ring is already normal in its own fraction field. The full Ehrhart algebra uses the larger lattice and a larger fraction field; it is not its birational normalization. The projective-normality comparison in Proposition 4.17 assumes an actual very ample embedding, which this four-point degree-one system does not supply for \(X_{Q_r}\text{.}\)

    Section 16.5 A reciprocity consequence for reflexive polytopes

    For this calculation we use the preferred reflexive representative, rather than the vertex-translated copy used to introduce the intrinsic lattice. Lattice translation leaves \(E_P\) and \(h_P^*\) unchanged. For a positive-dimensional reflexive polytope in this preferred origin normalization, every facet inequality is \(\langle x,u_\rho\rangle\geq-1\text{.}\) For a lattice point \(x\) and positive integer \(q\text{,}\) strict membership in \(qP\) is equivalent to
    \begin{equation*} \langle x,u_\rho\rangle>-q \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \langle x,u_\rho\rangle\geq-(q-1). \end{equation*}
    Hence \(E_P^\circ(q)=E_P(q-1)\text{.}\) Comparing its generating series with the interior-series formula gives
    \begin{equation*} h_P^*(z)=z^d h_P^*(z^{-1}). \end{equation*}
    The numerator is palindromic, and \(h_d^*=h_0^*=1\text{.}\) This is a consequence in the reflexive case, not a claim that an arbitrary chosen translation preserves its facet-distance-one normalization.
    The calculations illustrate the data retained by the full section ring: degree-zero normalization, boundary and interior counts, and asymptotic volume. The half-open proof keeps the ambient lattice fixed throughout. That is what allows nonunimodular simplices and the Reeve example to contribute their higher-degree lattice points without mistaking normality of a different monomial algebra for normal generation of the polarization.
