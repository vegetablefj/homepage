    Chapter 10 Tropicalization and toric compactifications

    A subvariety of a torus need not be toric. Nevertheless, its possible directions of escape can determine which toric boundary orbits its closure meets. This gives a further use of the fan dictionary: choose an ambient toric variety whose boundary records the geometry of the subvariety, then impose flatness or smoothness on the multiplication map to control the intersections.
    This chapter works over \(\C\) with its trivial valuation. Let \(T=\Spec\C[M]\text{,}\) let \(N=\Hom(M,\Z)\text{,}\) and put \(n=\rk N\text{.}\) Unless a hypersurface is explicitly considered, \(Y\subset T\) is a nonempty integral closed subvariety of dimension \(d\text{,}\) with ideal \(I\text{.}\) All fans are finite rational fans of strongly convex cones in \(N_{\R}\text{.}\) We prove the hypersurface boundary calculation and the smooth-boundary consequence directly. The general tropical lifting and compactification theorems are identified as external inputs.

    Section 10.1 Initial ideals and directions of escape

    Definition 10.1 The least-weight convention.

    For \(w\in N_{\R}\) and a nonzero Laurent polynomial \(f=\sum_m c_m\chi^m\text{,}\) set
    \begin{equation*} \lambda_w(f)=\min_{c_m\ne0}\langle m,w\rangle, \qquad \operatorname{in}_w(f)= \sum_{\langle m,w\rangle=\lambda_w(f)}c_m\chi^m. \end{equation*}
    Put \(\operatorname{in}_w(0)=0\text{.}\) The initial ideal and tropicalization are
    \begin{equation*} \operatorname{in}_w(I)= \bigl(\operatorname{in}_w(f):f\in I\bigr)\subset\C[M], \qquad \operatorname{Trop}(Y)= \{w:\operatorname{in}_w(I)\text{ contains no monomial}\}. \end{equation*}
    A monomial means a nonzero scalar multiple of a character. Since every character is a unit in the Laurent ring, the condition is equivalently \(\operatorname{in}_w(I)\ne(1)\text{.}\) Its zero scheme in \(T\) is the initial degeneration at \(w\text{.}\)
    The coefficients have weight zero. A nontrivially valued coefficient field would change the minimum to \(\operatorname{val}(c_m)+\langle m,w\rangle\) and would require a corresponding definition of the initial coefficients. No such extension is implicit here.

    Proposition 10.2 A hypersurface is detected by tied minima.

    If \(I=(f)\text{,}\) then \(\operatorname{in}_w(I)=(\operatorname{in}_w(f))\text{.}\) Thus \(w\in\operatorname{Trop}(V(f))\) exactly when the least weight among the terms of \(f\) occurs at least twice.

    Proof.
    The least-weight parts satisfy \(\operatorname{in}_w(fh)=\operatorname{in}_w(f)\operatorname{in}_w(h)\text{:}\) their product is nonzero in the Laurent domain and all other products have greater weight. This proves the ideal identity. The units of \(\C[M]\) are precisely its nonzero scalar monomials, by Proposition 1.24 applied to the zero cone. Hence the principal initial ideal is the unit ideal exactly when the initial polynomial consists of one term.
    For an ideal with several generators, taking the initial polynomials of just those generators can lose equations. For example, in \(\C[x^{\pm1},y^{\pm1}]\) let
    \begin{equation*} f=x^2+y+1,\qquad g=x^2+y+x,\qquad I=(f,g). \end{equation*}
    Here \(Y=\{(1,-2)\}\text{.}\) At \(w=(-1,-2)\text{,}\) both selected initial polynomials equal \(x^2+y\) and have common torus zeros. But \(g-f=x-1\in I\text{,}\) whose initial polynomial is the unit \(x\text{.}\) Therefore \(w\notin\operatorname{Trop}(Y)\text{.}\) The initial-system hypothesis in Definition 5.10 deliberately concerns a chosen system of equations. Tropicalization concerns the full ideal; the two tests should not be identified.
    We use the fundamental theorem of tropical geometry, together with the finite rational polyhedral structure supplied by Gröbner theory, as external inputs. For
    \begin{equation*} K=\bigcup_{q\ge1}\C((t^{1/q})), \end{equation*}
    with valuation given by the least exponent, they identify \(\operatorname{Trop}(Y)\) with the Euclidean closure of the coordinatewise valuations of \(Y(K)\text{;}\) every rational tropical point is attained by such a point. In particular, a relative interior of a rational cone meets \(\operatorname{Trop}(Y)\) if and only if it contains an attained rational tropical point. The passage from the trivial valuation on \(\C\) to the nontrivial valuation on \(K\) uses valued-field extension invariance. See [MS15, Theorems 3.2.3–3.2.4 and Remark 3.2.5]; the polyhedral structure is supplied by Proposition 3.2.8 and, in this constant-coefficient case, Section 2.5 of the same reference. We will use this bridge only for the general orbit criterion.

    Section 10.2 Which boundary orbits does a closure meet?

    Write \(\overline Y\) for the scheme-theoretic closure in \(X_\Sigma\text{.}\) The orbit \(O(\sigma)\) has codimension \(\dim\sigma\text{.}\) Its intersection with \(\overline Y\) is governed by an existential condition inside \(\sigma\text{,}\) rather than by the initial polynomial at every weight in that cone.

    Proposition 10.3 The chart calculation for a hypersurface.

    Let \(Y=V(f)\subset T\text{,}\) where \(f\) is nonzero and not a unit. If \(\sigma\) is smooth, then
    \begin{equation*} \overline Y\cap O(\sigma)\ne\varnothing \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \relint(\sigma)\cap\operatorname{Trop}(Y)\ne\varnothing. \end{equation*}

    Proof.
    For \(\sigma=0\text{,}\) both sides hold: the orbit is \(T\) and the initial ideal at zero is \((f)\text{.}\) Suppose \(r=\dim\sigma>0\text{.}\) Choose a lattice basis extending its primitive ray generators. The chart is
    \begin{equation*} U_\sigma=\Spec R,\qquad R=\C[x_1,\ldots,x_r,x_{r+1}^{\pm1},\ldots,x_n^{\pm1}], \end{equation*}
    and its closed orbit is defined by \(x_1=\cdots=x_r=0\text{.}\) Multiply \(f\) by a character so that the resulting polynomial \(g\in R\) has minimum exponent zero in each of the first \(r\) variables. No \(x_i\) divides \(g\text{.}\) The ring \(R\) is a unique factorization domain, so \((g)\) is saturated with respect to \(x_1\cdots x_r\text{.}\) Consequently it is exactly the contraction of the hypersurface ideal from the torus: \(V(g)\) is the closure in this chart.
    Restrict \(g\) to the orbit, obtaining a Laurent polynomial \(g_0\) in the remaining variables. The intersection is nonempty precisely when \(g_0\) is not a unit; this includes \(g_0=0\text{.}\) A nonzero Laurent nonunit has a torus zero by the Nullstellensatz. A weight in \(\relint(\sigma)\) assigns strictly positive weights to the first \(r\) variables and weight zero to the others. If \(g_0\) is a single monomial, it is the unique least-weight term at every such weight. If \(g_0\) has at least two terms, all of those terms have weight zero and give a tropical point at every such weight.
    It remains to treat \(g_0=0\text{.}\) Suppose, for contradiction, that every strictly positive weight has a unique least-weight term of \(g\text{.}\) The minimizing term is locally constant as a function of the weight. The positive orthant is connected, so one fixed term minimizes everywhere. Letting a positive weight approach each coordinate vector shows that its exponent in every \(x_i\text{,}\) \(i\le r\text{,}\) is the minimum exponent of that variable among all terms, hence zero. This term would survive in \(g_0\text{,}\) a contradiction. Thus some strictly positive weight has a tied minimum. Multiplication by the character used to obtain \(g\) does not change tied minima. The hypersurface criterion now proves the assertion.
    The last case matters. The closure of \(x+y=0\) in \(\A^2\) meets the origin, but inside the positive quadrant its tropicalization is only the diagonal ray. Most interior weights have a monomial initial polynomial.

    Theorem 10.4 Tevelev’s orbit criterion.

    For any fan \(\Sigma\) and any \(\sigma\in\Sigma\text{,}\)
    \begin{equation*} \overline Y\cap O(\sigma)\ne\varnothing \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \relint(\sigma)\cap\operatorname{Trop}(Y)\ne\varnothing. \end{equation*}
    Neither flatness of a multiplication map nor completeness of the ambient fan is assumed.

    Proof.
    For smooth \(\sigma\text{,}\) this is the external result [Tev07, Lemma 2.2], expressed through the tropical lifting theorem above. In its chart \(\A^r\times\Gm^{n-r}\text{,}\) a Puiseux point specializes to the closed orbit exactly when its first \(r\) valuations are positive and the others are zero. Conversely, a curve germ approaching that orbit from \(Y\) gives such a point. The cited lemma establishes this passage for a general subvariety.
    Here is the extension to an arbitrary cone, so the scope of that citation is explicit. Take a proper smooth subdivision of the fan, using Subsection 7.2.3, and let \(\overline Y'\) be the closure upstairs. Its map to \(\overline Y\) is proper and surjective: its closed image contains the dense subset \(Y\text{.}\) An orbit \(O(\tau)\) upstairs maps to \(O(\sigma)\) exactly when \(\relint(\tau)\subset\relint(\sigma)\text{.}\) If the latter orbit meets the closure, lift a point and apply the smooth-cone criterion to the orbit containing its lift. Conversely, a tropical point in \(\relint(\sigma)\) lies in the relative interior of one cone of the subdivision; apply the smooth criterion there and map its intersection point down.

    Section 10.3 Proper closure and flat multiplication

    Definition 10.5 Tropical compactification.

    The closure \(\overline Y\subset X_\Sigma\) is a tropical compactification if \(\overline Y\) is proper and the multiplication morphism
    \begin{equation*} \mu:T\times\overline Y\longrightarrow X_\Sigma, \qquad (t,y)\longmapsto t\cdot y \end{equation*}
    is faithfully flat. The ambient toric variety need not be complete.
    This is [Tev07, Definition 1.1]. For these finite-type morphisms, faithful flatness includes both flatness and surjectivity. Surjectivity means that every ambient orbit meets the closure, but it does not supply the flatness condition.

    Theorem 10.6 Existence and compatible subdivisions.

    Every \(Y\) as above has a tropical compactification with smooth toric ambient space. For any tropical compactification, \(|\Sigma|=\operatorname{Trop}(Y)\text{.}\) A subdivision gives another tropical compactification; its closure is the scheme-theoretic inverse image of \(\overline Y\text{,}\) and its multiplication map is obtained by base change.
    These are external inputs from [Tev07, Theorem 1.2 and Proposition 2.5]. Smoothness here concerns the ambient toric variety. It does not assert that an arbitrary \(Y\) acquires a smooth closure. In particular, the dimension conditions on boundary intersections below do not assert that those intersections are nonsingular.

    Proposition 10.7 Expected dimensions from flatness.

    If \(\overline Y\subset X_\Sigma\) is a tropical compactification, then every intersection \(\overline Y\cap O(\sigma)\) is nonempty and pure of dimension
    \begin{equation*} d-\dim\sigma. \end{equation*}
    In particular, no cone of \(\Sigma\) has dimension greater than \(d\text{.}\)

    Proof.
    Nonemptiness follows from surjectivity. The source \(T\times\overline Y\) is integral of dimension \(n+d\text{.}\) The flat dimension formula over the \(n\)-dimensional normal toric target gives pure \(d\)-dimensional geometric fibers of \(\mu\text{.}\) Fix \(p\in O(\sigma)\text{.}\) The projection of \(\mu^{-1}(p)\) to \(\overline Y\cap O(\sigma)\) is the pullback of the orbit map \(T\to O(\sigma)\text{,}\) after inversion and translation. This orbit map is a torsor under its stabilizer, a split torus of dimension \(\dim\sigma\text{;}\) it is smooth and surjective. Subtracting this dimension gives the asserted purity and dimension. A nonempty finite-type scheme cannot have negative dimension.
    The support equality records directions of escape. Flatness controls how the closure varies along each orbit, and is the additional hypothesis used in this dimension argument. A fan with the right support is therefore a candidate ambient space; this support calculation alone is not a verification of tropical compactification.

    Section 10.4 Smooth multiplication and normal crossings

    Definition 10.8 Schön subvarieties.

    The embedding \(Y\subset T\) is schön if some tropical compactification has smooth multiplication morphism.
    The existence of one such compactification implies smooth multiplication for every tropical compactification of the same embedding; this is [Tev07, Definition 1.3 and Theorem 1.4]. The quantifier concerns tropical compactifications, including their flatness condition.

    Proposition 10.9 A smooth ambient fan gives a smooth pair.

    Suppose \(Y\) is schön and \(\overline Y\subset X_\Sigma\) is a tropical compactification with \(\Sigma\) smooth. Then \(\overline Y\) is smooth, and its reduced boundary \(B=\overline Y\setminus Y\) is a simple normal crossings divisor. At a point in \(O(\sigma)\text{,}\) exactly \(\dim\sigma\) local boundary branches occur.

    Proof.
    The multiplication map is smooth and the ambient variety is smooth, so \(T\times\overline Y\) is smooth. Smoothness descends along its smooth surjective projection to \(\overline Y\text{.}\) Equivalently, at a complex point \((1,y)\text{,}\) both local dimension and tangent-space dimension of the product are those of \(\overline Y\) plus \(n\text{,}\) so regularity of the product gives regularity of \(\overline Y\text{.}\)
    Let \(y\in\overline Y\cap O(\sigma)\) and \(r=\dim\sigma\text{.}\) On the smooth chart \(U_\sigma=\A^r\times\Gm^{n-r}\text{,}\) denote the first coordinates by \(z_1,\ldots,z_r\text{.}\) They vanish on the orbit and their product defines the ambient boundary. The differential of \(\mu\) at \((1,y)\) is surjective. Its torus-factor directions lie in the tangent space of \(O(\sigma)\text{,}\) since the torus preserves each orbit. Consequently the tangent map from \(T_y\overline Y\) to the normal space of that orbit is surjective. The restricted differentials \(dz_1,\ldots,dz_r\) on \(\overline Y\) are therefore independent.
    Extend these restricted functions to local parameters on the smooth variety \(\overline Y\text{.}\) The boundary has equation \(z_1\cdots z_r=0\) there, with each factor occurring once. Its branches are smooth and meet transversely. Each branch belongs to the restriction of a distinct ambient invariant divisor. For any one ambient divisor, this argument also shows that its restriction is smooth wherever nonempty; its distinct irreducible components are thus disjoint. Hence the boundary components themselves are smooth, establishing simple normal crossings rather than only the local normal-crossing equation.

    Section 10.5 A three-punctured line in a toric surface

    Example 10.10 The pair of pants.

    Consider \(Y=\{x+y=1\}\subset(\C^*)^2\text{.}\) For \(w=(a,b)\text{,}\) the three term weights are \(a,b,0\text{.}\) The tied-minimum criterion gives
    \begin{equation*} \operatorname{Trop}(Y)= \R_{\ge0}(1,0)\ \cup\ \R_{\ge0}(0,1)\ \cup\ \R_{\ge0}(-1,-1). \end{equation*}
    Let \(\Sigma\) consist of these three rays and the zero cone, with no two-dimensional cones. This is a subfan of the fan of \(\P^2\text{.}\) Its toric variety is \(\P^2\) with its three torus fixed points removed.
    In coordinates \(x=X/Z\text{,}\) \(y=Y/Z\text{,}\) the projective closure is the line \(X+Y=Z\text{.}\) It avoids the three removed points. Thus its closure in \(X_\Sigma\) is still this proper line \(\overline Y\simeq\P^1\text{,}\) with boundary
    \begin{equation*} [0:1:1],\qquad [1:0:1],\qquad [1:-1:0]. \end{equation*}
    Each point belongs to the orbit of its corresponding ray. The curve meets all ambient orbits, so its multiplication map is surjective.
    We can also verify smoothness without invoking a general hypersurface theorem. Both \(T\times\overline Y\) and \(X_\Sigma\) are smooth. Over the dense torus, varying the torus factor makes the differential of multiplication surjective. Near a boundary point choose the chart \(\A^1\times\Gm\text{,}\) with normal coordinate \(z\) and invertible orbit coordinate \(u\text{.}\) At the first two boundary points the line has equation \(u=1-z\text{.}\) At the third use \(z=Z/X\text{,}\) \(u=Y/X\text{,}\) so its equation is \(u=z-1\text{.}\) In each case write it as \(u=c+\epsilon z\text{,}\) with \(c\ne0\text{.}\) The two independent torus characters defining this chart give action coordinates \((s,t)\text{,}\) and multiplication is locally
    \begin{equation*} (s,t,z)\longmapsto \bigl(sz,\ t(c+\epsilon z)\bigr). \end{equation*}
    At \(z=0\text{,}\) the derivatives in the \(z\) and \(t\) directions are independent. Hence the map is smooth at the boundary too, by the differential criterion for morphisms of smooth complex varieties. Smooth surjectivity implies faithful flatness. We have obtained a tropical compactification and proved that this embedding is schön.
    The ambient open subset is essential to this statement. If one instead keeps the complete fan of \(\P^2\text{,}\) the same line still has a proper closure, but multiplication misses the three fixed points. It is not surjective and does not define a tropical compactification in that ambient space. Its full fan has support \(\R^2\text{,}\) whereas the tropicalization has only the three rays.
    This example compactifies a non-toric curve using a toric surface. Indeed \(Y\simeq\P^1\setminus\{0,1,\infty\}\) is not a toric curve: its unit group modulo constants has rank two, generated by \(x\) and \(1-x\text{,}\) whereas the smooth toric curves \(\A^1\text{,}\) \(\Gm\text{,}\) and \(\P^1\) have ranks zero, one, and zero. To check the first rank directly, its coordinate ring is \(\C[x,x^{-1},(1-x)^{-1}]\text{;}\) unique factorization in \(\C[x]\) shows that the units of this localization are exactly \(c x^a(1-x)^b\text{,}\) with \(c\in\C^*\) and \(a,b\in\Z\text{.}\) The fan controls boundary incidence and, through smooth multiplication, transversality. It does not turn the original subvariety into a toric variety.
