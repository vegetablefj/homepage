    Chapter 7 Uniform bounds and the Mori factorization

    The second calculation in OpenAI’s manuscript is a vanishing statement for maps between four-dimensional Mori fibre spaces over a point. Its threshold must be uniform in the varieties, the map, the length of a factorization, and the chosen subcollection \(\mathcal S\text{.}\) A bound depending on one factorization would not give a threshold for every admissible Hassett divisor.
    The argument has three parts. A degree-\(d\) Picard-number-one K3 surface cannot admit a pencil of uniformly bounded genus when \(d\) grows. Bounded marked link diagrams make this observation applicable to their exceptional and ramification contributions. Finally, a correction attached to each Mori fibre space makes the contributions of successive links telescope.

    Section 7.1 Bounding a K3 degree by a pencil

    Lemma 7.1.

    Let \(S\) be a K3 surface with \(\Pic(S)=\Z L\text{,}\) where \(L\) is ample and \(L^2=d\text{.}\) If a dominant rational map to a curve has connected general fibre of genus \(g\) on a resolution, then the curve is \(\Pp^1\) and
    \begin{equation*} 2g-2\geq\sqrt d. \end{equation*}

    Proof.
    Resolve the pencil by point blowups \(\pi:Y\to S\text{.}\) The base has genus zero since its one-forms pull back to \(Y\text{,}\) whose irregularity is zero. The images of the general fibres have class \(nL\) for some \(n\geq1\text{.}\) In the orthogonal total-transform exceptional basis, write
    \begin{equation*} F=\pi^*(nL)-\sum_i m_iE_i^*. \end{equation*}
    The equation \(F^2=0\) gives
    \begin{equation*} \sum_i m_i^2=n^2d. \end{equation*}
    Adjunction and \(K_Y=\sum_iE_i^*\) give \(2g-2=\sum_i m_i\text{.}\) All \(m_i\) are nonnegative, so \(\sum_i m_i\geq(\sum_i m_i^2)^{1/2}\geq\sqrt d\text{.}\)
    This is [OAI, Lemma 4.1]. For a map with disconnected general fibre, Stein factorization gives the same bound for each normalized geometric general fibre component. Hence a genus bound gives a degree bound independently of the surface or the particular pencil.
    The branch locus of a cover must also be controlled. For a degree-\(e\) cover of a smooth curve \(A\text{,}\) unramified away from at most \(r\) specified points, Riemann–Hurwitz gives
    \begin{equation*} 2g(A')-2\leq e(2g(A)-2)+(e-1)r. \end{equation*}
    If \(e\text{,}\) \(g(A)\text{,}\) and \(r\) are uniformly bounded, the genera of covering curves are bounded. Applied to a hyperplane pencil in a bounded family of surface pairs, this excludes high-degree K3 function fields among finite covers of bounded degree that are étale off the marked boundary.
    The manuscript develops analogous exclusions for bounded varieties of dimension at most three, for surface fields fibred over curves, and for threefolds over curves whose geometric generic surfaces are bounded and uniruled [OAI, Theorem 4.3]. Only the geometric generic data need be bounded in the last two cases. The original total spaces and the maps to the curve may vary without a boundedness assumption.

    Section 7.2 What must be bounded in a Sarkisov link

    The ordinary Sarkisov program factors a specified birational map between Mori fibre spaces into links. In dimension four, the common base \(Q\) of a link can have dimension zero, one, two, or three. OpenAI constructs bounded families for the diagrams on geometric generic fibres over \(Q\) [OAI, Theorem 4.9]. Its assertion includes the maps, small modifications, base contractions, exceptional loci, and marked bad loci.
    The construction passes through central log Fano models with a fixed lower bound on log discrepancies. Birkar’s boundedness theorem applies to their underlying varieties. Further arguments control the marked divisors, stratify the Cartier data, and bound the contraction diagrams. These further arguments are part of the manuscript. Boundedness of the central varieties alone would not control where the later finite covers branch.
    The auxiliary comparison of conic presentations uses three-dimensional log Sarkisov paths for a pair \((Y,cD_Y)\text{.}\) Here \(D_Y\) is the full visible divisorial ramification support of a fixed Brauer class, with simple normal crossings on a smooth common model. The coefficient is chosen once with \(0<c<1/4\text{.}\) For an eligible genus-zero curve presentation with at most seven ramification points,
    \begin{equation*} \deg(K+cD)=-2+cr<0. \end{equation*}
    A relative log minimal model program therefore has a Mori output over the same embedded surface field.
    The order of choices is essential [OAI, Corollary 4.13]. The ordinary fourfold diagrams first give a uniform coefficient for the ramification boundary. With that coefficient fixed, the log threefold diagrams and low-discrepancy terminalization diagrams are bounded. Only then is the K3 degree threshold chosen. The construction takes maxima over fixed finite-type families, so the threshold does not accumulate with the number of links.

    Section 7.3 Generic surface links and their correction

    For a contraction \(V\to B\) with rationally connected generic fibre, define the vertical correction
    \begin{equation*} v(V/B)=\sum_{E\text{ vertical over }B}u(E)-\sum_{D\text{ prime in }B}u(D). \end{equation*}
    This is a finite cancelled sum. Over a suitable dense open of \(B\text{,}\) each base divisor has one dominating divisor with the same MRC quotient, and their test values cancel. Only components in the complement and its inverse image remain.
    For a link from \(V/B\) to \(V'/B'\) over \(Q\text{,}\) write \(\Delta\) for final minus initial value. Separating horizontal and vertical divisors gives
    \begin{equation*} c_u(\text{link})-\Delta v=c_{u,\mathrm{hor}/Q}-\Delta h_Q, \end{equation*}
    where \(h_Q\) is the portion of the vertical correction whose divisors dominate \(Q\text{.}\) This identity is a valuation calculation and needs no degree threshold [OAI, Proposition 5.3].
    For \(\dim Q\leq1\text{,}\) the boundedness exclusions make the right-hand side zero once \(d\) is uniformly large. When \(\dim Q=3\text{,}\) the generic link is an isomorphism of smooth conics, and the horizontal contribution vanishes for every \(d\text{.}\) The remaining case is \(\dim Q=2\text{,}\) where the link becomes a birational map of Mori surfaces over \(k=\C(Q)\text{.}\) The common base is dominated by a rationally connected fourfold, so a smooth model of \(Q\) is a rational surface and \(k\simeq\C(x,y)\) [OAI, Lemma 5.5]. Thus orbits of size one have zero test value. Orbits of size two also have zero test value, since the quadratic residue field has an involution, while the K3 surfaces in \(\mathcal S\) have trivial automorphism group.
    The manuscript computes this case by finite Galois sets. For a del Pezzo surface \(S/k\text{,}\) let
    \begin{equation*} A(S)=\{F\in\Pic(S_{\overline k}):F^2=0,\ -K_S\cdot F=2\}. \end{equation*}
    These are the conic-pencil classes. There are five in degree five and three in degree six. For a conic bundle with singular-fibre set \(\Delta\text{,}\) let \(P_\Delta\) be the set of choices of one component in every singular geometric fibre. If there are three such fibres, this set has eight elements.
    Define \(w(S)=u(A(S))\) for a rank-one del Pezzo surface of degree five or six, and \(w(S)=u(P_\Delta)/2\) for a conic bundle with three singular geometric fibres. Set \(w(S)=0\) in the other cases. Complementing every component choice is an equivariant involution. A preserved orbit has a nonidentity automorphism of its residue field and cannot be the function field of a K3 surface in \(\mathcal S\text{.}\) The counted orbits occur in pairs, so the half is an integer.
    The two-ray surface calculation gives
    \begin{equation*} c_{u,\mathrm{hor}/Q}(\text{link})=\Delta w. \end{equation*}
    This is [OAI, Proposition 5.10]. Its finite sets retain the residue fields of the orbits. Counting geometric points or replacing these sets by isomorphic rational permutation representations would not suffice for the test \(u\text{.}\) For example, blowing up a degree-four point on a degree-nine del Pezzo surface produces a conic pencil with three singular fibres. Its eight component choices are two copies of the four-point extraction set as Galois sets, giving exactly the same test value on the two sides.

    Section 7.4 Independence of the surface field

    A conic-bundle node with three-dimensional base has a function field \(L\) and a generic conic class \(\alpha\in\Br(L)[2]\text{.}\) A generic surface calculation uses an embedded surface field \(k\subset L\) for which \(L\) is the function field of a geometrically integral genus-zero curve \(C_k/k\text{.}\) Call such a field eligible if the reduced ramification scheme \(\Delta_k\subset C_k\) has degree \(r_k\leq7\text{.}\)
    The residues of \(\alpha\) give a quadratic cover \(\widetilde\Delta_k\to\Delta_k\text{.}\) When \(r_k=3\text{,}\) let \(P_k\) be the degree-eight finite étale scheme of choices of one point in each geometric fibre of this cover. The candidate correction is
    \begin{equation} \sigma_k(L,\alpha)=u(\Delta_k)+\mathbf1_{r_k=3}\frac12u(P_k).\tag{7.1} \end{equation}
    Ramification is recorded over \(k\) before extension of constants. A residue that vanishes after base change can still have a supporting divisor that must remain marked.
    For the conic surface of a generic link, the component residue fields have quadratic involutions and therefore contribute zero. The vertical term is \(h_Q=u(\widetilde\Delta_k)-u(\Delta_k)=-u(\Delta_k)\text{.}\) Consequently \(\sigma_k=w-h_Q\text{;}\) on a del Pezzo side, \(h_Q=0\text{.}\) This relates the candidate to the horizontal residual in the surface-link formula.
    To attach a correction to the node, the manuscript proves that a nonzero candidate determines the eligible embedded surface field uniquely for sufficiently large \(d\) [OAI, Proposition 6.2]. Resolve two presentations \(k,k'\subset L\) on a common smooth model with the full visible ramification boundary. Their relative log minimal model programs give outputs over \(k\) and \(k'\text{.}\) Connect these by a log Sarkisov path.
    A link with two-dimensional common base preserves the embedded surface field. If \(k'\ne k\text{,}\) the first link leaving \(k\) has common base of dimension at most one. Its marked geometric generic diagram bounds the branch curves and the finite residue covers that contribute to \(\sigma_k\text{.}\) The degree and genus exclusions then force \(\sigma_k=0\text{,}\) contradicting the assumed nonzero value. This proves equality as subfields of \(L\text{,}\) which is stronger than an abstract isomorphism of surface fields.
    A second comparison shows that this correction vanishes at ends of ordinary fourfold links with common base of dimension at most one [OAI, Proposition 6.4]. It also requires the bounded terminalization result: an auxiliary model may extract primes, and the proof must show that their log discrepancies are at most one before applying the bound [OAI, Theorem 4.11]. An arbitrary resolution is not assumed to belong to a bounded family.
    Consequently there is an intrinsic node function \(s(V/B)\text{.}\) It is zero for base dimension at most one, equals \(w\) for base dimension two, and equals the unique nonzero candidate for a conic node with base dimension three. If no candidate is nonzero, it is zero. The uniqueness statement ensures that every eligible presentation gives this same value, including presentations whose candidate is zero [OAI, Theorem 6.5].
    The link identities now become
    \begin{equation*} c_u(\text{link})=\Delta(v+s). \end{equation*}
    Summing over a Sarkisov path cancels the intermediate values. At a Mori fibre space over a point, both \(v\) and \(s\) are zero. Thus the manuscript obtains, uniformly for large \(d\text{,}\)
    \begin{equation} c_u(f)=0\tag{7.2} \end{equation}
    for every birational map between terminal rationally connected four-dimensional Mori fibre spaces over a point [OAI, Theorem 6.6].

    Section 7.5 Application to a Hassett divisor

    For a very general \(X\in\mathcal C_d\text{,}\) the lattice of integral Hodge classes is the labelled rank-two lattice \(K\text{.}\) Hence \(\rank T_X=21\text{.}\) The middle integral lattice is unimodular, and \(K\) and \(T_X\) are primitive orthogonal sublattices. Their discriminant groups have the same order, so \(|\det T_X|=d\text{.}\)
    One can also exclude nonscalar rational Hodge endomorphisms very generally. On a marked period chart, a rational endomorphism preserving the Hodge structure must preserve the period line. For a nonscalar endomorphism, its eigenlines lie in finitely many proper linear sections of the period quadric. There are countably many rational endomorphisms. Algebraicity of Hodge loci makes the corresponding exceptional loci algebraic; the same argument excludes additional Hodge classes [OAI, Lemma 7.2]. This gives \(\End_{\mathrm{Hdg}}(T_{X,\Q})=\Q\) outside a countable union of proper closed subsets.
    If such an \(X\) were rational, (6.2) would give \(c_u(\Pp^4\dashrightarrow X)=1\text{.}\) Both endpoints are Mori fibre spaces over a point: \(\Pic(X)=\Z\cO_X(1)\) and \(-K_X=3H\text{.}\) For \(d>d_0\text{,}\) (7.2) gives zero for the same map and the same test collection. This is the contradiction asserted by [OAI, Proposition 7.1].
    Admissibility supplies the integral Hodge-theoretic K3 association and the ordinary categorical K3 equivalence. Those established results give the remaining assertion in Theorem 6.1. The two surfaces witnessing the Hodge and categorical associations need not be identified. The discriminants \(d=2\cdot7^j\text{,}\) for \(j\geq1\text{,}\) are admissible and unbounded, so the asserted theorem applies to all sufficiently large terms of this sequence without specifying a first term.
    The manuscript records two further consequences. For \(d=2n^2+2n+2\) with \(n\geq2\text{,}\) Addington’s criterion gives a K3 surface whose Hilbert square is birational to the Fano variety of lines \(F(X)\) [Add16, Theorem 2]. These discriminants are admissible and unbounded. The asserted irrationality for large \(d\) would therefore persist even with this stronger property of \(F(X)\) [OAI, Corollary 1.3].
    Voisin’s theorem gives universally trivial \(\CH_0(X)\) for a special cubic whose labelling has discriminant not divisible by four [Voi17, Theorem 5.6]. In particular, it applies throughout the admissible divisors. Equivalently, there are an integral cycle \(\Gamma\) supported on \(D\times X\) for a proper closed subset \(D\subsetneq X\text{,}\) and a point \(x\in X(\C)\text{,}\) such that
    \begin{equation*} [\Delta_X]=[X\times\{x\}]+[\Gamma]\quad\text{in }\CH^4(X\times X). \end{equation*}
    The OpenAI manuscript therefore asserts irrational examples with an integral Chow decomposition of the diagonal [OAI, Corollary 1.4]. This explains why that necessary condition for stable rationality cannot detect its proposed examples. The manuscript makes no conclusion about their stable rationality.
