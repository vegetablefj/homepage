    Chapter 2 Hassett divisors and associated K3 surfaces

    Section 2.1 Algebraic classes and discriminants

    The middle cohomology of a cubic fourfold is a free lattice of rank \(23\text{,}\) with \(h^{3,1}=h^{1,3}=1\) and \(h^{2,2}=21\text{.}\) Let \(A_X=H^4(X,\mathbb Z)\cap H^{2,2}(X)\) and let \(T_X=A_X^\perp\subset H^4(X,\mathbb Z)\text{.}\) Orthogonality refers to the cup product. The integral Hodge conjecture for cubic fourfolds identifies \(A_X\) with the classes of algebraic cycles of codimension two. A very general cubic has \(A_X=\mathbb Z h^2\text{.}\) A cubic is special if \(\operatorname{rank}A_X\geq2\text{.}\)
    A label is a saturated rank-two sublattice \(K_d\subset A_X\) containing \(h^2\text{,}\) with discriminant \(d=\det(K_d)\text{.}\) For a basis \(h^2,\gamma\text{,}\) the formula is
    \begin{gather*} d=3(\gamma\cdot\gamma)-(h^2\cdot\gamma)^2. \end{gather*}
    Saturation is part of the definition: replacing a label by a sublattice of finite index multiplies its discriminant by the square of that index. The locus \(\mathcal C_d\) consists of cubics admitting such a label. Hassett proves that it is a nonempty irreducible divisor precisely when \(d\gt6\) and \(d\equiv0,2\pmod6\) [Has00, Theorem 1.0.1]. A plane gives the matrix \(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}3&1\\1&3\end{smallmatrix}\right)\) of discriminant eight. A quartic scroll gives \(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}3&4\\4&10\end{smallmatrix}\right)\) of discriminant fourteen.
    A cubic can admit several labels, especially at intersections of Hassett divisors. Its discriminant is therefore not a unique invariant unless a label is specified. The very general point of \(\mathcal C_d\) has \(A_X=K_d\text{,}\) whereas special points can have additional classes. Rational members of \(\mathcal C_{14}\) can lie on other Hassett divisors. An irrationality assertion about a very general point of a divisor must preserve this quantifier.

    Section 2.2 Hodge structures and derived categories

    A polarized K3 surface \((S,\ell)\) with \(\ell^2=d\) is associated to a labelled cubic \((X,K_d)\) if there is an integral Hodge isometry
    \begin{gather*} K_d^\perp\simeq H^2(S,\mathbb Z)_{\mathrm{prim}}(-1). \end{gather*}
    Here the primitive part is \(\ell^\perp\text{.}\) The Tate twist raises weight two to weight four and shifts Hodge bidegrees by \((1,1)\text{.}\) With the usual cup-product conventions, the bilinear form on the K3 side is negated. Its signature is then \((19,2)\text{,}\) agreeing with \(K_d^\perp\text{.}\) This sign is necessary when comparing lattices, even when the notation for a Tate twist suppresses it.

    Theorem 2.1.

    A labelled special cubic of discriminant \(d\) has an associated polarized K3 surface if and only if \(d\) is divisible by neither \(4\) nor \(9\text{,}\) and by no odd prime \(p\equiv2\pmod3\text{.}\)
    This is Hassett’s theorem [Has00, Theorem 5.1.3]. A discriminant satisfying these conditions and the nonemptiness conditions is called admissible. The proof compares discriminant forms of integral lattices and applies the period theorem for K3 surfaces. Matching Hodge numbers alone is insufficient. The condition belongs to the label: a cubic in \(\mathcal C_8\cap\mathcal C_{14}\) can have an associated K3 surface through its label of discriminant fourteen.
    Kuznetsov defines the component
    \begin{gather*} \operatorname{Ku}(X)=\langle\mathcal O_X,\mathcal O_X(1),\mathcal O_X(2)\rangle^\perp \end{gather*}
    in the bounded derived category of coherent sheaves, giving a semiorthogonal decomposition
    \begin{gather*} D^b(X)=\langle\operatorname{Ku}(X),\mathcal O_X,\mathcal O_X(1),\mathcal O_X(2)\rangle. \end{gather*}
    The component has Serre functor equal to the shift by two and Hochschild homology of K3 type. Kuznetsov conjectures that \(X\) is rational exactly when \(\operatorname{Ku}(X)\simeq D^b(S)\) for a smooth projective K3 surface [Kuz10, Conjecture 1.1]. For a cubic containing a plane with smooth discriminant, he constructs \(\operatorname{Ku}(X)\simeq D^b(S,\alpha)\text{,}\) where \(\alpha\) is the Clifford Brauer class [Kuz10, Theorem 4.3]. The conjecture requires an untwisted K3 category. A nonzero twist on one K3 surface can nevertheless be equivalent to the untwisted category of a different K3 surface, as the examples in [ABBV14, Corollary B] show.
    Addington and Thomas compared the Hodge and categorical conditions using the Mukai lattice of \(\operatorname{Ku}(X)\text{.}\) The admissible Hassett condition is equivalent to the existence of a hyperbolic plane in its numerical lattice [AT14, Theorem 3.1]. They proved that a geometric K3 component implies an admissible label, and proved the converse on a dense open subset of every admissible divisor [AT14, Theorem 1.1]. Bayer, Lahoz, Macrì, Nuer, Perry, and Stellari subsequently proved the equivalence for every smooth cubic fourfold [BLMNPS21, Corollary 1.7 (Corollary 29.7)]. Their moduli spaces of stable objects produce a K3 surface from an isotropic Mukai class and the categorical equivalence. These theorems identify the K3 conditions; rationality is the additional assertion of the Hassett–Kuznetsov conjecture.
    Addington gives an equivalent formulation through \(T_X\text{:}\) an admissible label exists if and only if \(T_X\simeq T(S)(-1)\) for a projective K3 surface, and if and only if the variety of lines \(F(X)\) is birational to a moduli space of stable sheaves on a K3 surface [Add16, Theorem 1]. Requiring \(F(X)\) to be birational to a Hilbert square is stronger. The latter condition requires a label satisfying \(d=2(n^2+n+1)/a^2\) for integers \(n,a\text{,}\) with \(a\neq0\) [Add16, Theorem 2]. Admissibility alone does not impose this equation.

    Section 2.3 Surface cohomology and the limitation of indecomposability

    The Hodge-theoretic obstruction sought in dimension four must respect the cohomology added by blowing up surfaces. If a smooth fourfold \(Y\) is blown up along a smooth surface \(S\text{,}\) the added summand in degree four is \(H^2(S,\mathbb Z)(-1)\text{,}\) with the negative of its intersection form. Centers of dimension at most one add only classes of type \((2,2)\) in degree four. A resolution of a birational map \(\mathbb P^4\dashrightarrow X\) therefore compares \(T_X\) with transcendental cohomology of surface centers.
    Kulikov proposed that the integral polarized transcendental Hodge structure of every smooth projective surface is indecomposable. Under this hypothesis he proved irrationality for cubics outside a countable union of proper period loci [Kul08, Proposition 1]. For a cubic with sufficiently general periods, the endomorphisms of its transcendental Hodge structure are scalar. His comparison of the two decompositions on a resolution forces its transcendental structure to occur as a direct summand of that of a surface. The complementary summand is nonzero: a surface with geometric genus one has, on a minimal model, \(h^{1,1}\leq20\text{,}\) whereas the rank of the transcendental lattice of a nonspecial cubic is \(22\) [Kul08, Lemmas 2–3].
    Auel, Böhning, and Graf von Bothmer disproved the proposed hypothesis by showing that the integral polarized transcendental Hodge structure of the sextic Fermat surface decomposes [ABB13, Theorem 1.2]. Rational decompositions had already been possible; their example is a decomposition over the integers, including the polarization. Kulikov’s argument remains a conditional result. Any later obstruction using Hodge structures must justify the required restrictions on summands contributed by surfaces, rather than assume indecomposability for every surface.
