    Chapter 1 Classical rationality constructions

    Section 1.1 Rationality and the dimension four problem

    Throughout these notes, a cubic fourfold is a smooth hypersurface \(X\subset\mathbb P^5_{\mathbb C}\) of degree three. It is rational if there is a birational map \(\mathbb P^4\dashrightarrow X\text{,}\) stably rational if \(X\times\mathbb P^r\) is rational for some \(r\geq0\text{,}\) and unirational if it admits a dominant rational map from a projective space. These implications become weaker in that order. A general member of an irreducible family lies in a specified nonempty Zariski open subset. A very general member lies outside a specified countable union of proper closed algebraic subsets. The distinction matters when a statement excludes infinitely many loci of additional Hodge classes.
    Smooth complex cubic hypersurfaces of positive dimension at least two are unirational. For a cubic fourfold, the construction using a line gives a parametrization of degree two, as described in [Has17, Proposition 10]. Degree two does not give a birational map. Rationality constructions must remove the extra choice responsible for that degree, usually by imposing a surface inside the cubic.
    A singular cubic with an ordinary double point is rational by projection from that point. After placing the point at \([1:0:\cdots:0]\text{,}\) its equation has the form \(x_0q_2(x_1,\ldots,x_5)+q_3(x_1,\ldots,x_5)=0\text{.}\) Away from \(q_2=0\text{,}\) the projected coordinates determine \(x_0\text{.}\) These singular examples are distinct from the smooth cubics considered in the rationality problem.
    Clemens and Griffiths proved that every smooth cubic threefold is irrational by showing that its principally polarized intermediate Jacobian is not a product of Jacobians of curves [CG72, Section 13]. Blow-ups of a smooth threefold along curves explain why such a product is necessary for rationality. A cubic fourfold has vanishing odd cohomology, so its intermediate Jacobians do not contain the corresponding information. Its nontrivial Hodge structure occurs in degree four. Blow-ups along surfaces contribute their degree-two cohomology, which requires a different comparison.

    Section 1.2 Scrolls, del Pezzo surfaces, and Pfaffian cubics

    Let \(\Sigma\subset\mathbb P^5\) be a smooth quartic rational normal scroll. Through a general point of \(\mathbb P^5\) there is a unique secant line to \(\Sigma\text{.}\) If \(X\) contains \(\Sigma\text{,}\) the residual point of intersection of that secant with \(X\) parametrizes \(X\) by unordered pairs of points of \(\Sigma\text{.}\) Since the symmetric square of a rational surface is rational, this proves rationality. Equivalently, the quadrics containing \(\Sigma\) define a birational map from \(X\) to a smooth quadric fourfold. The blow-up of \(\Sigma\) resolves the map [Fan43]; a modern proof is given in [BRS19, Theorem 2.2].
    Morin attempted to deduce rationality of the general cubic from the existence of quartic scrolls [Mor40, Sections 7–8]. The incidence variety of a scroll and a cubic containing it has dimension \(56\text{,}\) whereas all cubic equations form \(\mathbb P^{55}\text{.}\) The missing point is the dimension of the family of scrolls inside a fixed cubic. Fano showed that this family has dimension two on the general cubic admitting such scrolls. The image of the incidence variety therefore has dimension \(54\text{,}\) giving a divisor, now denoted \(\mathcal C_{14}\text{,}\) rather than the full space of cubics. The secant construction remains valid on that divisor.
    A smooth quintic del Pezzo surface \(S\subset\mathbb P^5\text{,}\) embedded by its anticanonical system, gives another rationality construction. Its five defining quadrics contract its secant lines. Restricting the resulting map \(\mathbb P^5\dashrightarrow\mathbb P^4\) to a cubic containing \(S\) gives a birational map: a general contracted secant meets the cubic in its two points on \(S\) and one residual point. Tregub studied the relation between this construction and quartic scrolls [Tre93].
    A Pfaffian cubic is defined by the Pfaffian of a \(6\times6\) skew-symmetric matrix of linear forms. A general point of such a cubic represents a skew form of rank four on a six-dimensional vector space \(V\text{.}\) Fix a hyperplane \(W\subset V\text{.}\) The line \(\ker(\varphi)\cap W\) gives a point of \(\mathbb P(W)\simeq\mathbb P^4\) and defines a birational map from the general Pfaffian cubic. The orthogonal linear section of \(\operatorname{Gr}(2,V)\) is a K3 surface of degree \(14\text{.}\) Beauville and Donagi related the variety of lines of a general Pfaffian cubic to the Hilbert square of this K3 surface [BD85, Propositions 5–6]. This K3 surface explains the Hodge structure appearing in the rationality construction.
    Pfaffian cubics are dense in \(\mathcal C_{14}\text{.}\) Bolognesi, Russo, and Staglianò proved rationality for every smooth member of \(\mathcal C_{14}\) by studying degenerations of quartic scrolls and their secants [BRS19, Theorems 2.9 and 3.4]. The distinction between the divisor and its Pfaffian subset is necessary: the Pfaffian locus is not open in \(\mathcal C_{14}\) [BRS19, Theorem 3.7].

    Section 1.3 Projection from a plane and higher secant curves

    If \(X\) contains a plane \(P\text{,}\) projection from \(P\) gives a quadric surface fibration
    \begin{gather*} q:\operatorname{Bl}_P(X)\longrightarrow\mathbb P^2. \end{gather*}
    Its generic fiber is a quadric over \(K=\mathbb C(\mathbb P^2)\text{.}\) A rational section gives a \(K\)-point, and projection from that point proves that the generic quadric is \(K\)-rational. Consequently \(X\) is rational. Springer’s theorem gives the same conclusion from a multisection of odd degree.

    Theorem 1.1.

    Suppose that \(X\) contains a plane \(P\text{,}\) and let \(h\) be its hyperplane class. If there is an integral Hodge class \(\gamma\in H^4(X,\mathbb Z)\cap H^{2,2}(X)\) such that \(\gamma\cdot(h^2-[P])\) is odd, then \(X\) is rational.
    Hassett proves this criterion in [Has99, Theorem 3.1]. The class \(h^2-[P]\) is the class of a residual quadric. The criterion gives a countable collection of rational loci of codimension two in the moduli space, lying inside the divisor \(\mathcal C_8\) of cubics containing a plane [Has99, Theorem 4.2]. Two disjoint planes give the simplest section. Tregub’s example of a quartic Veronese surface meeting \(P\) transversely in three points gives another, since its intersection number with a residual quadric is \(4-3=1\text{.}\)
    When the sextic discriminant curve is smooth, its double cover is a K3 surface \(S\text{.}\) The two rulings of the quadrics give a conic bundle over \(S\) and a Brauer class \(\alpha\in\operatorname{Br}(S)[2]\text{.}\) Vanishing of \(\alpha\) is equivalent to existence of a rational section of \(q\text{.}\) This is a sufficient condition for rationality of the total space. It is not necessary: Auel, Bernardara, Bolognesi, and Várilly-Alvarado exhibit rational Pfaffian cubics containing a plane with nonzero Clifford invariant [ABBV14, Theorem A and Theorem 9].
    Russo and Staglianò extended secant constructions to surfaces admitting a four-dimensional family of five-secant conics, with exactly one such conic through a general point of \(\mathbb P^5\text{.}\) A cubic containing the surface meets a general conic in six points counted with multiplicity; five lie on the surface, leaving one residual point. Rationality follows when the parameter space of conics is rational. They proved rationality of all smooth cubics in \(\mathcal C_{26}\) and \(\mathcal C_{38}\) [RS19, Theorems 4 and 7]. For \(\mathcal C_{42}\text{,}\) an eight-secant twisted cubic gives the residual point because its intersection degree with a cubic is nine [RS23, Theorem 5.12]. Their geometric constructions apply to general members. Specialization of rationality in smooth proper families, proved by Kontsevich and Tschinkel, extends the conclusions to every smooth member of these divisors [KT19, Theorem 1].
