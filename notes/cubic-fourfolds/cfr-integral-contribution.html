    Chapter 6 Large Hassett discriminants and integral surface contributions

    The Hodge-atom obstruction for a very general cubic in the full moduli space does not automatically apply to a very general cubic in a prescribed Hassett divisor. The transcendental rank is twenty-one at a very general point of that divisor, compared with twenty-two for a nonspecial cubic. For an admissible discriminant, the resulting lattice is already the shifted transcendental lattice of a polarized K3 surface. OpenAI’s manuscript [OAI] studies this remaining case by keeping the integral pairing and by counting surface contributions to an individual birational map.
    This chapter and the next reorganize the argument of that manuscript. Its new operator decomposition and uniform Sarkisov statements are stated with their original attribution. They are the additional results on which its irrationality conclusion depends.
    OpenAI states the following result [OAI, Theorem 1.1].

    Theorem 6.1.

    There is an integer \(d_0\) such that, for every admissible discriminant \(d>d_0\text{,}\) a very general \(X\in\mathcal C_d\) is irrational and has an exact \(\C\)-linear equivalence
    \begin{equation*} \Ku(X)\simeq D^b(S) \end{equation*}
    for a smooth projective K3 surface \(S\text{.}\)
    The threshold is ineffective. The exceptional countable union is chosen separately in each \(\mathcal C_d\text{.}\) The conclusion concerns very general members, so it permits rational subloci in those same divisors. Its categorical assertion uses the ordinary derived category of a K3 surface.

    Section 6.1 A divisorial invariant of a birational map

    Fix \(d>2\) and a collection \(\mathcal S\) of Picard-number-one K3 surfaces of degree \(d\) with trivial automorphism group. For a finitely generated field \(F/\C\text{,}\) choose a smooth projective model \(V\) and its maximal rationally connected quotient \(R(V)\text{.}\) Define \(u(F)=1\) when \(R(V)\) is birational to a surface in \(\mathcal S\text{,}\) and set \(u(F)=0\) otherwise. The birational uniqueness of the quotient makes \(u\) independent of the model. In particular, \(u(V)=u(\C(V))\) is zero for rationally connected varieties and for varieties of dimension at most one.
    A fibration with geometrically integral rationally connected general fibre preserves the MRC quotient. Hence a projective bundle over a surface has the same test value as that surface. On surfaces, \(u\) selects precisely those birational to members of \(\mathcal S\text{.}\) Two birational smooth projective K3 surfaces are isomorphic.
    For a birational map \(f:V\dashrightarrow W\text{,}\) use \(f^*\) to place both sets of prime divisorial valuations in the same function field. Let \(\mathcal D(V)\) and \(\mathcal D(W)\) denote these sets. Following Lin and Shinder [LS24, Definition 2.1 and Lemma 2.2], the manuscript defines
    \begin{equation} c_u(f)=\sum_{\nu\in\mathcal D(W)\setminus\mathcal D(V)}u(\kappa(\nu))-\sum_{\nu\in\mathcal D(V)\setminus\mathcal D(W)}u(\kappa(\nu)).\tag{6.1} \end{equation}
    Thus a divisor acquired by the target has positive sign. Only finitely many divisors occur: a birational map is an isomorphism on dense opens, and all differences lie in divisorial components of their complements.
    For composable birational maps, differences of indicator functions give
    \begin{equation*} c_u(g\circ f)=c_u(g)+c_u(f). \end{equation*}
    If \(\pi:\widetilde V\to V\) is a blowup along a smooth connected center \(Z\) of codimension at least two, its exceptional divisor is a projective bundle over \(Z\text{.}\) Consequently \(c_u(\pi^{-1})=u(Z)\) and \(c_u(\pi)=-u(Z)\text{.}\) In a weak factorization of a map from \(\Pp^4\text{,}\) only surface centers can contribute.
    For a finite étale algebra \(A=\prod_i F_i\) over a surface function field, write \(u(A)=\sum_i u(F_i)\text{.}\) Each field factor is counted once. No factor \([F_i:k]\) enters this definition. This convention will agree with the count of exceptional divisors over closed points of a generic surface.

    Section 6.2 Separating whole transcendental lattices

    For a rationally connected smooth projective fourfold \(M\text{,}\) let \(T_M\) be the integral orthogonal complement of the rational Hodge classes in \(H^4(M,\Z)/\mathrm{tors}\text{.}\) For a surface \(Z\text{,}\) let \(T_Z\) be the analogous lattice in degree two. Write \(q_M\) and \(q_Z\) for the cup pairings. If \(Y\) is the blowup of \(M\) along a smooth surface \(Z\text{,}\) the classical integral blowup formula gives
    \begin{equation*} (T_Y,q_Y)\simeq(T_M,q_M)\perp(T_Z(-1),-q_Z). \end{equation*}
    Point and curve centers add only diagonal classes to degree-four cohomology.
    An integral direct sum at each blowup does not, by itself, imply that a later blowdown removes one of the summands previously added. This is related to the need to control surface summands that motivated Kulikov’s proposal. The manuscript introduces an algebra of genus-zero operators to distinguish the whole contributions of surface centers [OAI, Definition 3.2].
    Take a connected genus-zero Gromov–Witten invariant with two variable insertions \(u,v\in T_{M,\Q}\text{,}\) arbitrary descendant powers, and all remaining insertions rational of diagonal Hodge type. It defines a bilinear form \(B(u,v)\text{.}\) Nondegeneracy of the cup pairing determines an operator \(A_B\) by
    \begin{equation*} B(u,v)=q_M(A_Bu,v). \end{equation*}
    Let \(G_M\subset\End_{\Q}(T_{M,\Q})\) be the unital algebra generated by all these operators. Compatibility with Hodge structures makes every generator a Hodge endomorphism.
    The additional assertion of the manuscript is the following, in the classical integral splitting above [OAI, Theorem 3.3].

    Proposition 6.2.

    For a surface blowup, the operator algebra is
    \begin{equation*} G_Y=G_M\oplus\Q\operatorname{id}_{T_Z}. \end{equation*}
    The two factors act independently on \(T_{M,\Q}\) and \(T_{Z,\Q}(-1)\text{.}\) The second factor is omitted if \(T_Z=0\text{.}\) For point or curve blowups, \(G_Y=G_M\text{.}\)
    The proof in the manuscript uses degeneration to the normal cone and relative reconstruction. It shows that mixed operators vanish, that the exceptional block is scalar, and that the old block belongs to \(G_M\text{.}\) An invariant supported on an exceptional line supplies the projector onto the exceptional block. A separate reconstruction recovers all old operators, giving the opposite inclusion. Both inclusions and the projector are required for the asserted product of algebras. Iritani’s formal quantum blowup decomposition, which involves extensions of coefficients and a change of variables, does not alone state this assertion for the classical integral splitting.
    Starting from \(T_{\Pp^4}=0\text{,}\) the manuscript deduces that every nonzero surface blowup adds one whole lattice and one independent scalar factor. At a blowdown, the identity of the removed scalar factor is a primitive central idempotent of the current algebra. In a product \(\Q^m\text{,}\) such idempotents are exactly the projectors onto the existing factors. Thus the removed rational subspace is one whole earlier summand, even when some summands have isomorphic or reducible Hodge structures [OAI, Lemma 3.8].
    The integral assertion follows by saturation. Each summand is the intersection of its rational subspace with the ambient integral lattice, both in the historical decomposition and in the new geometric blowup decomposition. Equality of rational subspaces therefore identifies the integral lattices and their cup pairings. This retains the determinant that a rational Hodge isomorphism would lose.

    Section 6.3 The rank-twenty-one surface

    The relevant surface classification can be proved directly [OAI, Lemma 3.9]. Suppose that a smooth projective surface \(Z\) has \(p_g(Z)=1\) and \(\rank T_Z=21\text{.}\) Pass to its minimal model. It is not ruled, so its canonical divisor is nef and \(K_Z^2\geq0\text{.}\) Noether’s formula and the Euler characteristic give
    \begin{equation*} b_2(Z)=22-8q(Z)-K_Z^2. \end{equation*}
    Projectivity and the transcendental rank imply \(b_2(Z)\geq22\text{.}\) Hence \(q(Z)=0\text{,}\) \(K_Z^2=0\text{,}\) and \(\rho(Z)=1\text{.}\)
    A minimal surface of general type has positive canonical square. A surface of Kodaira dimension one has a nonzero isotropic fibre class, independent of an ample class, and therefore Picard rank at least two. The remaining possibility, with \(p_g=1\) and \(q=0\text{,}\) is a K3 surface. If its Néron–Severi lattice is \(\Z L\) with \(L\) primitive and ample, unimodularity of the K3 lattice gives
    \begin{equation*} L^2=|\det T_Z|. \end{equation*}
    If this degree is \(d>2\) and \(\End_{\mathrm{Hdg}}(T_{Z,\Q})=\Q\text{,}\) every automorphism acts by \(+1\) or \(-1\) on the transcendental lattice and fixes \(L\text{.}\) The action \(-1\) is incompatible with the discriminant gluing to \(\Z L\text{,}\) whose cyclic discriminant group has order \(d>2\text{.}\) The action must be the identity on all of \(H^2(Z,\Z)\text{,}\) and global Torelli makes the automorphism the identity.
    Now assume that \(M\) is a rational fourfold with \(h^{3,1}(M)=1\text{,}\) \(\rank T_M=21\text{,}\) \(|\det T_M|=d>2\text{,}\) and \(\End_{\mathrm{Hdg}}(T_{M,\Q})=\Q\text{.}\) Choose \(\mathcal S\) to consist of the K3 surfaces whose shifted integral transcendental lattice is Hodge-isometric to \(T_M\text{.}\) In a weak factorization of \(\Pp^4\dashrightarrow M\text{,}\) whole-block cancellation leaves exactly one block of this isometry class. Indeed, every nonzero transcendental block has a nonzero \((3,1)\) part, while the final such part is one-dimensional.
    Every center in that class is birational to a degree-\(d\) Picard-number-one K3 surface with trivial automorphism group by the surface calculation. Conversely, every center counted by \(u\) has precisely that integral class. The number of additions minus removals of this class is one. The manuscript concludes
    \begin{equation} c_u(\Pp^4\dashrightarrow M)=1.\tag{6.2} \end{equation}
    This is [OAI, Proposition 3.10]. The next chapter explains its incompatible calculation for large \(d\text{.}\)
