    Chapter 5 K3 realizations and subsequent results

    Section 5.1 Rational cohomology of a K3 surface

    Guéré strengthens the consequence of rationality by retaining the nilpotent part of the Euler operator. His theorem states that if a smooth cubic fourfold \(X\) is rational, there is a projective K3 surface \(S\) such that
    \begin{equation*} H^4(X,\mathbb Q)_{\mathrm{prim}}\simeq H^2(S,\mathbb Q)(-1) \end{equation*}
    as rational Hodge structures [Gue26, Theorem 63]. The conclusion concerns the whole primitive cohomology, including its algebraic part.
    At a small evaluation, the zero generalized eigenspace of a cubic has nilpotent rank one, one class of Hochschild degree two, and no odd cohomology. Following these data through a weak factorization forces a surface center with a minimal model having \(q=0\text{,}\) \(p_g=1\text{,}\) and numerically trivial canonical class. It is a K3 surface. The rank-one nilpotent operator permits removal of the two remaining diagonal directions and gives the stated Hodge isomorphism. The proof includes descent from the coefficient extension used in the blowup decomposition.
    A Hodge isomorphism need not preserve an integral lattice or a specified quadratic form. Guéré’s conclusion is therefore a rational necessary condition. It is distinct from Hassett’s condition involving a primitive isometric embedding of integral polarized Hodge structures.
    Guéré distinguishes a property holding at generic evaluations from its strong version holding at every evaluation. Blowup centers require the strong version, since restriction of parameters to a center need not be generic. The required implications for surfaces are proved in [Gue26, Definition 33, Corollary 47, and Sections 3–4]. This distinction becomes necessary when considering birational maps in higher dimensions.

    Section 5.2 A rational quadratic obstruction on Hassett divisors

    Fay uses Guéré’s theorem to obtain an arithmetic obstruction for special cubics. A member of \(\mathcal C_d\) is Hodge general in her terminology when its algebraic middle cohomology has rank two and its transcendental Hodge endomorphism algebra is \(\mathbb Q\text{.}\) The very general member of every nonempty divisor has these properties. Define
    \begin{equation*} \beta_d=(d/2,-3)\in\operatorname{Br}(\mathbb Q)[2], \end{equation*}
    where \((a,b)\) denotes a quaternion Brauer class. Fay’s preprint proves that rationality of a Hodge-general member implies \(\beta_d=0\) [Fay26, Theorem 1.1].
    The transcendental structure has rank \(21\text{.}\) Its Hodge endomorphism hypothesis turns a rational Hodge isomorphism into a similitude of polarizations. In this rank, the Hasse invariant is invariant under scalar multiplication. Comparison with the transcendental quadratic form of a K3 surface of Picard rank one gives \(\beta_d\text{.}\) The norm criterion for \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3})\) gives
    \begin{equation*} \beta_d=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad v_p(d/2)\text{ is even for every prime }p\equiv2\pmod3. \end{equation*}
    Thus \(\mathcal C_{12}\) and \(\mathcal C_{20}\) have very general irrational members. This criterion is Huybrechts’ twisted-K3 condition, which is weaker than admissibility.
    The prime \(2\) is included in the displayed condition. For example, \(d=12\) gives \(d/2=6\text{,}\) whose valuation at \(2\) is odd. At inert primes the norm of an element has even valuation, so this is already an obstruction. Omitting \(2\) would miss the conclusion for \(\mathcal C_{12}\text{.}\)
    For an admissible discriminant, \(4\nmid d\text{,}\) \(9\nmid d\text{,}\) and no odd prime congruent to two modulo three divides \(d\text{.}\) Since the discriminant is even, \(v_2(d)=1\) and \(v_2(d/2)=0\text{.}\) Every inert-prime valuation in the norm criterion is consequently even. Fay’s quaternion class vanishes on all admissible discriminants. A further obstruction is required for those divisors.

    Section 5.3 Other fourfolds of K3 type

    Benedetti, Manivel, and Perrin compute small quantum multiplication for Gushel–Mukai fourfolds. On the six-dimensional ambient cohomology, the hyperplane operator has four simple nonzero eigenvalues and a two-dimensional kernel. This proves irrationality of Hodge-general members [BMP26, Theorems 1 and 4]. A deformation into big quantum cohomology produces a nontrivial Jordan block. Their [BMP26, Theorem 13] then proves that rationality forces the vanishing middle cohomology to be rationally Hodge isomorphic to \(H^2(S,\mathbb Q)(-1)\) for a projective K3 surface. The vanishing part here is the complement of the cohomology inherited from the Grassmannian.
    Benedetti, Fay, Guéré, Manivel, and Perrin obtain a criterion requiring no explicit quantum computation. Let \(X\) be a smooth Fano hyperplane section of a smooth Fano fivefold \(Y\text{.}\) Suppose that \(b_1(X)=b_3(X)=0\text{,}\) that
    \begin{equation*} h^{3,1}(X)>h^{3,1}(Y), \end{equation*}
    and that
    \begin{equation*} b_4(X)_{\mathrm{van}}\geq10+12h^{3,1}(X). \end{equation*}
    If every rational middle Hodge class is inherited from \(Y\text{,}\) then \(X\) is irrational [BFGMP26, Theorem 4.1]. Monodromy forces quantum multiplication to be scalar on vanishing cohomology. The inequality compares its non-Hodge dimension with the bound for a surface center obtained from the Noether inequality. The criterion recovers cubic and Gushel–Mukai irrationality and applies to Küchle fourfolds of type \((c5)\) [BFGMP26, Proposition 5.1].
    Verra fourfolds are double covers of \(\mathbb P^2\times\mathbb P^2\) branched in bidegree \((2,2)\text{.}\) Their K3 geometry permits a different application. Benedetti, Guéré, Manivel, and Perrin prove that a smooth Verra fourfold cannot be birational to a very general cubic or Gushel–Mukai fourfold. If such a birational map exists for a special cubic or Gushel–Mukai fourfold, its vanishing cohomology must be rationally Hodge isomorphic to that of a projective K3 surface [BGMP26, Theorems 1 and 21].
    Equivariant atoms retain the action of a finite group. Cavenaghi, Katzarkov, and Kontsevich develop these invariants in [CKK25, Sections 2–3]. Their proposed Chen–Ruan extension assumes an orbifold quantum blowup formula. Fay proves that a very general symmetric Verra fourfold, with its involution interchanging the two projective-plane factors, is not equivariantly birational to \(\mathbb P^4\) with any regular involution [FayV26, Theorem 6.4]. The equivariance of the blowup decomposition is part of her proof. This result concerns the pair consisting of the variety and its involution. The earlier claim of ordinary irrationality was withdrawn because ordinary weak factorization did not provide equivariant centers [FayW26, withdrawal notice].

    Section 5.4 Small quantum computations and their scope

    Böhning, Graf von Bothmer, and Su’a define a naive atomic decomposition by the multiplicities of eigenvalues of small multiplication by \(-K_X\) on algebraic cohomology. Their small atomic decomposition is instead the list of lengths of connected components of the spectrum of the small quantum algebra [BBS26, Definition 1.1]. For a cubic with algebraic middle cohomology of rank \(r\text{,}\) the naive decomposition is \((r+1,1,1,1)\text{.}\) These numbers concern algebraic classes, not the total dimensions of Hodge atoms.
    The authors give examples where these smaller decompositions fail the concatenation formula suggested by a blowup. A four-dimensional quadric has naive decomposition \((2,1,1,1,1)\) but small decomposition consisting of six ones. Its blowup in a plane has nine ones for both decompositions [BBS26, Example 12.11]. Their Castelnuovo-surface example shows that even the small formula can fail. Big quantum parameters are necessary for Iritani’s theorem and for Hodge atoms.
    The refinements proposed in [KKPY26, Section 6.4] include pairings, Serre automorphisms, and integral structures. The full compatibility of these enhancements with the analytic blowup decomposition is assigned there to further work. Applications depending on that compatibility should be stated with their additional hypotheses. The rational Hodge theorems above are the appropriate conclusions to use without an established integral enhancement.
