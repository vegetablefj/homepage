    Chapter 4 The atom of a cubic fourfold

    Section 4.1 The quantum spectrum

    Let \(X\subset\mathbb P^5\) be a smooth cubic fourfold and let \(h\) be its hyperplane class. Its total cohomology has dimension \(27\text{.}\) The five-dimensional ambient part is spanned by \(1,h,h^2,h^3,h^4\text{,}\) and its orthogonal complement is the primitive middle cohomology of dimension \(22\text{.}\) The primitive Hodge numbers are
    \begin{equation*} (h^{3,1}_{\mathrm{prim}},h^{2,2}_{\mathrm{prim}},h^{1,3}_{\mathrm{prim}})=(1,20,1). \end{equation*}
    A cubic is Noether–Lefschetz general when its rational Hodge classes are exactly the ambient classes. Such cubics form the complement of the countable union of Hassett divisors.
    The quantum multiplication matrix can be obtained from the quantum cohomology of complete intersections [Bea95, Theorem]. In the basis just specified, small quantum multiplication by \(c_1(TX)=3h\) is
    \begin{equation*} K(q)=3\begin{pmatrix}0&0&6q&0&0\\1&0&0&15q&0\\0&1&0&0&6q\\0&0&1&0&0\\0&0&0&1&0\end{pmatrix}. \end{equation*}
    Its characteristic polynomial is
    \begin{equation*} \det(\lambda I-K(q))=\lambda^2(\lambda^3-729q). \end{equation*}
    At the algebraic specialization \(q=1\text{,}\) the nonzero eigenvalues are \(9,9\zeta,9\zeta^2\text{,}\) where \(\zeta^3=1\) and \(\zeta\ne1\text{.}\) Each is simple. The zero eigenvalue has one Jordan block of size two. The matrix and its non-archimedean interpretation are given in [KKPY26, Example 6.6(iii) and the proof of Theorem 6.8].
    On primitive cohomology, small multiplication by \(h\) is zero. This can be seen directly from monodromy and grading: the primitive representation is irreducible, so a monodromy-equivariant endomorphism is scalar there; a nonzero term of multiplication by \(h\) preserving middle degree would require a curve class of anticanonical degree one, whereas these degrees are multiples of three. The full zero generalized eigenspace consequently has dimension \(24\text{.}\)
    At \(q=1\text{,}\) the two ambient directions in the zero generalized eigenspace are spanned by \(w_3=h^3-21\) and \(w_4=h^4-6h\text{.}\) They satisfy \(K(1)w_3=3w_4\) and \(K(1)w_4=0\text{.}\) Their summands have different cohomological degrees, but all have Hochschild degree zero. This is compatible with the Tate-folded Hodge convention.
    For a very general cubic, the zero part persists as a single Hodge atom. Its representation consists of primitive cohomology and these two ambient directions. The atomic multiset has three one-dimensional point atoms and one atom \(\alpha_X\) of dimension \(24\text{.}\) The atom satisfies \(\rho_{\alpha_X}=2\) and has Hodge polynomial
    \begin{equation*} P_{\alpha_X}(t)=t^2+22+t^{-2}. \end{equation*}
    This is [KKPY26, Corollary 6.12]. The assertion concerns the decomposition over the locus of Hodge parameters. The spectrum of one specialized matrix alone would not establish it.

    Section 4.2 The surface obstruction

    Points and curves have no classes of Hochschild degree two. Suppose that an atom with a nonzero degree-two part comes from a projective surface. Passing to a minimal model removes only point atoms. A minimal surface with \(p_g>0\) has nef canonical class. The virtual dimension constraint on Gromov–Witten invariants forces its Euler operator to have a single eigenvalue, so its total cohomology is one atom.
    That surface atom contains the unit, an ample divisor class, and the point class. These give three independent rational Hodge classes. Every atom from a surface with a nonzero degree-two contribution must therefore satisfy \(\rho\geq3\text{.}\) The obstruction is the incompatibility of a holomorphic two-form with only two Hodge classes in the same atom.

    Theorem 4.1.

    A very general smooth complex cubic fourfold is irrational.

    Proof.
    The atom \(\alpha_X\) has a degree-two part of dimension one and \(\rho_{\alpha_X}=2\text{.}\) It occurs on no point, curve, or surface. The obstruction in Proposition 3.1 applies in dimension four. This is the argument of [KKPY26, Theorem 6.8].
    Ordinary cohomology does not retain the condition that the period line and the two Hodge classes belong to one quantum summand. A decomposition into Hodge substructures can split the cohomology of a surface and distribute its algebraic classes among other summands. The atom condition prevents this for a minimal surface with a holomorphic two-form. This is why replacing the atom by primitive cohomology alone loses the numerical obstruction.
    Special cubics have additional rational Hodge classes. The degree-two part of their zero sector remains, but its Hodge-class count can increase. The inequality \(\rho\leq2\) used in this proof no longer applies. The irrationality of very general cubics consequently leaves a separate problem on each Hassett divisor.
