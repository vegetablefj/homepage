    Chapter 13 Algebraic cycles and canonical classes

    Cohomology records the class of an algebraic cycle but usually loses much of its geometry. Two points on a connected smooth projective variety have the same cohomology class; they need not be rationally equivalent. For a K3 surface this distinction is already substantial. The Beauville–Voisin theorem identifies a small, geometrically defined part of its Chow ring on which cohomology does determine rational equivalence.
    Throughout this chapter varieties are projective over \(\mathbb C\text{.}\) We define Chow groups and correspondences, and assume the intersection product, refined pullback, specialization, and Grothendieck–Riemann–Roch as developed in [Ful98, Chapters 1, 6, 15–16, and §20.3].

    Section 13.1 Rational equivalence and the cycle class map

    Definition 13.1.

    Let \(Y\) be a smooth projective variety of dimension \(d\text{.}\) The group \(Z^p(Y)\) of codimension-\(p\) cycles is the free abelian group on its integral codimension-\(p\) subvarieties. The Chow group is
    \begin{equation*} \operatorname{CH}^p(Y)=Z^p(Y)/{\sim_{\mathrm{rat}}}, \end{equation*}
    where rational equivalence is generated by the cycles \(\iota_*\operatorname{div}(f)\text{,}\) for integral codimension-\((p-1)\) subvarieties \(\iota:W\hookrightarrow Y\) and nonzero rational functions \(f\in\mathbb C(W)^*\text{.}\) For singular \(W\text{,}\) the divisor can be computed on its normalization and pushed forward.
    We write \(\operatorname{CH}^p(Y)_{\mathbb Q}=\operatorname{CH}^p(Y)\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathbb Q\) and \(\operatorname{CH}_0(Y)=\operatorname{CH}^d(Y)\text{.}\) Unless integral coefficients are specified, formulas below use rational Chow groups.
    For example, on a curve the divisor of a rational function gives a relation between its zeros and poles, with their multiplicities. On a variety, the same relation is imposed along every integral curve to define rational equivalence of zero-cycles. A curve need not be rational for its principal divisors to give relations. If the curve is rational, all of its points become rationally equivalent after pushforward from its normalization.
    Intersection theory equips \(\operatorname{CH}^*(Y)_{\mathbb Q}\) with a commutative graded ring structure. A divisor acts by intersection, and vector bundles have Chern classes in this ring. We distinguish a Chern cycle \(c_i(E)\in\operatorname{CH}^i(Y)_{\mathbb Q}\) from its cohomology class when both occur. External products are written \(\alpha\times\beta=p_1^*\alpha\cdot p_2^*\beta\text{.}\)

    Definition 13.2.

    The cycle class map sends a codimension-\(p\) subvariety to its Poincaré-dual cohomology class:
    \begin{equation*} \operatorname{cl}:\operatorname{CH}^p(Y)_{\mathbb Q}\longrightarrow H^{2p}(Y,\mathbb Q). \end{equation*}
    It respects products, Chern classes, and proper pushforward. Its kernel is denoted \(\operatorname{CH}^p(Y)_{\mathrm{hom},\mathbb Q}\text{.}\) A cycle \(z\in\operatorname{CH}^p(Y)_{\mathbb Q}\) is numerically trivial if \(\deg(z\cdot w)=0\) for every \(w\in\operatorname{CH}^{d-p}(Y)_{\mathbb Q}\text{.}\)
    Rational equivalence implies homological equivalence, which implies numerical equivalence. The converse from numerical to homological equivalence is not a general formal property of the cycle class map. In particular, we specify below when a theorem proves injectivity modulo numerical equivalence, a stronger assertion than injectivity into cohomology.
    The degree homomorphism on zero-cycles is
    \begin{equation*} \deg:\operatorname{CH}_0(Y)\longrightarrow\mathbb Z,\qquad \sum_j a_j[y_j]\longmapsto\sum_j a_j. \end{equation*}
    Here the closed points are complex points. Principal divisors on projective curves have degree zero, so the map is well defined. If \(Y\) is connected, its top cohomology is one-dimensional and
    \begin{equation*} \operatorname{cl}(z)=\deg(z)\,[\mathrm{pt}]\in H^{2d}(Y,\mathbb Q). \end{equation*}
    Thus \(\operatorname{CH}_0(Y)_{\mathrm{hom},\mathbb Q}\) is exactly the kernel of degree.

    Proposition 13.3.

    If \(X\) is a projective IHS manifold, the cycle class map is injective on \(\operatorname{CH}^1(X)_{\mathbb Q}\text{.}\)

    Proof.
    For a smooth variety, Cartier divisors modulo rational equivalence identify \(\operatorname{CH}^1(X)\) with \(\operatorname{Pic}(X)\text{.}\) The exponential sequence shows that the kernel of \(c_1:\operatorname{Pic}(X)\to H^2(X,\mathbb Z)\) is the image of \(H^1(X,\mathcal O_X)\text{.}\) This group vanishes for an IHS manifold. Passing to rational coefficients preserves injectivity, since \(\mathbb Q\) is flat over \(\mathbb Z\text{.}\)
    The difficulty begins with products of divisors and higher-codimension cycles. A cohomological relation between such classes can hold while the corresponding Chow cycle is nonzero. The LLV representation and the relations in \(\SH^*(X)\) from Chapter 11 describe cohomology; by themselves they give no implication in this reverse direction.

    Section 13.2 The Beauville–Voisin class of a K3 surface

    Let \(S\) be a projective K3 surface. Its second Chern number is \(24\text{,}\) but that numerical statement does not determine its second Chern cycle. The following result determines it as an element of the Chow group.

    Theorem 13.4 (Beauville–Voisin).

    There is a degree-one class \(o_S\in\operatorname{CH}_0(S)\text{,}\) represented by any point on any rational curve on \(S\text{,}\) with the following properties. For divisors \(D,D'\) on \(S\text{,}\)
    \begin{equation*} D\cdot D'=\deg(D\cdot D')\,o_S,\qquad c_2(T_S)=24o_S \quad\text{in }\operatorname{CH}_0(S). \end{equation*}
    These identities hold with integral coefficients.
    This is [BV04, Theorem 1]. The existence of rational curves and the independence of the chosen curve are part of the theorem’s geometric content. They are not consequences of the equality of the cohomology classes of points. We use the name Beauville–Voisin class for \(o_S\text{.}\)
    If \(\nu:\mathbb P^1\to S\) parametrizes a rational curve and \(a,b\in\mathbb P^1\text{,}\) a rational function with divisor \([a]-[b]\) gives \([\nu(a)]=[\nu(b)]\) in \(\operatorname{CH}_0(S)\text{.}\) The theorem shows that these classes also agree for points on different rational curves.

    Definition 13.5.

    The Beauville–Voisin subring of \(S\) is
    \begin{equation*} R^*(S):=\mathbb Q[S]\oplus \operatorname{CH}^1(S)_{\mathbb Q}\oplus\mathbb Q o_S \ \subseteq\ \operatorname{CH}^*(S)_{\mathbb Q}. \end{equation*}

    Proposition 13.6.

    The displayed subspace is a subring. It is the subring generated by divisor classes, and also the subring generated by divisor classes and the Chern classes of \(T_S\text{.}\) The cycle class map is injective on it.

    Proof.
    The only nontrivial product of positive-codimension terms is the product of two divisor classes, which belongs to \(\mathbb Q o_S\) by Theorem 13.4. For an ample divisor \(H\text{,}\) the number \(\deg(H^2)\) is nonzero, and \(o_S=H^2/\deg(H^2)\text{.}\) Thus divisors generate the whole displayed ring. The Chern classes add no generators, since \(c_1(T_S)=0\) and \(c_2(T_S)=24o_S\text{.}\)
    In codimension zero, the class of \([S]\) is nonzero. In codimension one, injectivity follows from Proposition 13.3. In codimension two, the degree of \(a\,o_S\) is \(a\text{,}\) so its cohomology class vanishes only if \(a=0\text{.}\) These three graded pieces prove injectivity.
    For instance, if \(H^2=2g-2\text{,}\) then \(H^2=(2g-2)o_S\) in the Chow ring. Any polynomial relation between divisor classes and the tangent Chern classes that is true in cohomology therefore holds in the Chow ring. The assertion concerns this subring.

    Theorem 13.7 (Mumford).

    For a smooth projective surface \(S\) with \(H^0(S,\Omega_S^2)\ne0\text{,}\) the group of degree-zero zero-cycles is not parametrized by differences of effective cycles of any fixed degree: there is no integer \(N\) such that every class in \(\ker(\deg:\operatorname{CH}_0(S)\to\mathbb Z)\) has the form \([z]-[z']\) for effective zero-cycles \(z,z'\) of degree \(N\text{.}\)
    This is the meaning of the infinite-dimensionality of \(\operatorname{CH}_0(S)\) in Mumford’s theorem; it concerns algebraic parameter spaces. See [Mum69]. The generalized form in [Voi14, §2.1.1] says that if \(\operatorname{CH}_0(S)_{\mathbb Q}\) were supported on a fixed curve \(C\subset S\text{,}\) meaning that \(\operatorname{CH}_0(C)_{\mathbb Q}\to\operatorname{CH}_0(S)_{\mathbb Q}\) is surjective, then \(H^0(S,\Omega_S^2)=0\text{.}\) In particular, for a K3 surface,
    \begin{equation*} \operatorname{CH}_0(S)_{\mathbb Q}\ne\mathbb Q o_S. \end{equation*}
    There are therefore points \(x\) for which \([x]-o_S\) is a nonzero homologically trivial cycle.

    Section 13.3 The small diagonal

    The product of two classes can be written as a correspondence using the small diagonal. A cycle relation on \(S^3\) can therefore imply relations between products on \(S\text{.}\)
    On \(S^3\text{,}\) write \(p_i:S^3\to S\) and \(p_{ij}:S^3\to S^2\) for the projections. Put
    \begin{equation*} o_i=p_i^*o_S,\qquad \Delta_{ij}=p_{ij}^*[\Delta_S],\qquad \Delta_{123}=\{(x,x,x):x\in S\}. \end{equation*}
    The cycles \(\Delta_{ij}\) and \(o_i\) have codimension two, while \(\Delta_{123}\) has codimension four. A product such as \(\Delta_{12}\cdot o_3\) is the class of the diagonal in the first two factors times the canonical point in the third.

    Theorem 13.8 (Beauville–Voisin).

    For a projective K3 surface,
    \begin{equation} \Delta_{123} =\Delta_{12}\cdot o_3+\Delta_{13}\cdot o_2+\Delta_{23}\cdot o_1 -o_1o_2-o_1o_3-o_2o_3 \quad\text{in }\operatorname{CH}^4(S^3)_{\mathbb Q}.\tag{13.1} \end{equation}
    We cite [Voi14, Theorem 4.1.3 and §4.2] for the relation and its proof. Its equality in Chow is essential. The equality of its cohomology classes is much weaker. We spell out several consequences using the intersection-theoretic operations already specified.

    Proposition 13.9.

    The small-diagonal relation implies \(D\cdot D'=\deg(D\cdot D')o_S\) with rational coefficients.

    Proof.
    Intersect (13.1) with \(p_1^*D\cdot p_2^*D'\) and push forward by \(p_3\text{.}\) The small diagonal gives \(D\cdot D'\text{.}\) The term \(\Delta_{12}\cdot o_3\) gives \(\deg(D\cdot D')o_S\text{.}\) Each other term contains, on at least one factor, the product of a divisor with a zero-cycle. Such a product has codimension three on the surface and vanishes. This leaves exactly the asserted equality.

    Proposition 13.10.

    The small-diagonal relation implies \(c_2(T_S)=24o_S\) with rational coefficients.

    Proof.
    Let \(j:S^2\to S^3\) be \(j(x,y)=(x,x,y)\text{,}\) and let \(\delta:S\to S^2\) be the diagonal inclusion. Let \(c=c_2(T_S)\text{.}\) Pull back the relation by \(j\text{.}\) The normal bundle of the diagonal \(S\hookrightarrow S^2\) is \(T_S\text{.}\) The excess-intersection and self-intersection formulas therefore give
    \begin{equation*} j^*\Delta_{123}=\delta_*c,\qquad j^*(\Delta_{12}\cdot o_3)=c\times o_S. \end{equation*}
    The two remaining diagonal terms each pull back to \(\delta_*o_S\text{.}\) The terms \(o_1o_3\) and \(o_2o_3\) pull back to \(o_S\times o_S\text{,}\) while \(j^*(o_1o_2)=0\text{,}\) since \(o_S^2=0\) on \(S\text{.}\) Hence
    \begin{equation*} \delta_*c=c\times o_S+ 2\delta_*o_S-2o_S\times o_S. \end{equation*}
    Choose a point \(a\) representing \(o_S\text{.}\) Then \(\delta_*o_S=[(a,a)]=o_S\times o_S\text{,}\) so the last two terms cancel. Push the remaining equality forward by the second projection \(S^2\to S\text{.}\) Its left side is \(c\text{;}\) its right side is \(\deg(c)o_S=24o_S\text{.}\)
    The excess-intersection formula is needed precisely because \(j(S^2)=\Delta_{12}\) already contains the small diagonal. Treating this pullback as a transverse set-theoretic intersection would miss the class \(c_2(T_S)\text{.}\)

    Proposition 13.11.

    For any \(z\in\operatorname{CH}_0(S)_{\mathbb Q}\text{,}\)
    \begin{equation*} \delta_*z=z\times o_S+o_S\times z -\deg(z)\,o_S\times o_S \quad\text{in }\operatorname{CH}_0(S^2)_{\mathbb Q}. \end{equation*}

    Proof.
    Intersect (13.1) with \(p_1^*z\) and push forward by \(p_{23}\text{.}\) The left side gives \(\delta_*z\text{.}\) The terms \(\Delta_{12}o_3\) and \(\Delta_{13}o_2\) give \(z\times o_S\) and \(o_S\times z\text{.}\) The term \(-o_2o_3\) gives \(-\deg(z)o_S\times o_S\text{.}\) All other terms contain the product of two zero-cycles on the first surface factor and vanish.
    Thus the small diagonal controls products and operations on zero-cycles simultaneously. Even when \(\deg(z)=0\text{,}\) the terms \(z\times o_S\) and \(o_S\times z\) can be nonzero in Chow, although all of their cohomology classes vanish.

    Section 13.4 Divisors, Chern classes, and splitting conjectures

    Let \(X\) be a projective IHS manifold. The K3 theorem suggests looking for a naturally specified subring of \(\operatorname{CH}^*(X)_{\mathbb Q}\) on which the cycle class map is injective.

    Definition 13.12.

    Define
    \begin{equation*} \begin{aligned} \operatorname{DCH}^*(X) &:=\mathbb Q[c_1(L):L\in\operatorname{Pic}(X)],\\ R^*(X) &:=\mathbb Q[c_1(L),c_i(T_X): L\in\operatorname{Pic}(X),\ 1\leq i\leq2n] \ \subseteq\ \operatorname{CH}^*(X)_{\mathbb Q}. \end{aligned} \end{equation*}
    Both rings include the identity. For a K3 surface, they agree with Definition 13.5.

    Conjecture 13.13 (Beauville).

    For every projective IHS manifold \(X\text{,}\) the restriction \(\operatorname{cl}|_{\operatorname{DCH}^*(X)}\) is injective.

    Conjecture 13.14 (Voisin).

    For every projective IHS manifold \(X\text{,}\) the restriction \(\operatorname{cl}|_{R^*(X)}\) is injective.
    The first formulation is the weak splitting property in [Bea07]. The second is the strengthening in [Voi08, Conjecture 1.3]. Equivalently, a polynomial cohomological relation between the specified generators should already hold as a rational equivalence of cycles. The word weak refers to a consequence of a proposed splitting of the Bloch–Beilinson filtration.
    The two conjectures are also different from the assertion that Chern cohomology classes lie in \(\SH^*(X)\text{.}\) The latter can fail, as explained in Corollary 11.17, whereas the definition of \(R^*(X)\) simply includes the tangent Chern cycles as generators.

    Proposition 13.15.

    Suppose \(X\) has dimension \(d=2n\) and satisfies weak splitting. For an ample divisor \(H\text{,}\) the class
    \begin{equation*} o_X^{\mathrm{div}}:=\frac{H^d}{\deg(H^d)} \in\operatorname{CH}_0(X)_{\mathbb Q} \end{equation*}
    is independent of \(H\text{.}\) Every product of \(d\) divisor classes equals its degree times \(o_X^{\mathrm{div}}\text{.}\) If the cycle class map is injective on \(R^*(X)\text{,}\) then every class in \(R^d(X)\) has the same property; in particular,
    \begin{equation*} c_d(T_X)=\chi_{\mathrm{top}}(X)\,o_X^{\mathrm{div}} \quad\text{in }\operatorname{CH}_0(X)_{\mathbb Q}. \end{equation*}

    Proof.
    The denominator is nonzero because \(H\) is ample, and \(o_X^{\mathrm{div}}\) has degree one. For any product \(z=D_1\cdots D_d\text{,}\) the class \(z-\deg(z)o_X^{\mathrm{div}}\) belongs to \(\operatorname{DCH}^d(X)\) and has degree zero, hence zero cohomology class. Weak splitting makes it zero in Chow. Taking \(z\) to be a power of another ample divisor proves independence. The same argument works for every \(z\in R^d(X)\) under the stronger injectivity assumption. The top Chern number is \(\deg c_d(T_X)=\chi_{\mathrm{top}}(X)\text{,}\) which proves the final identity.
    The class \(o_X^{\mathrm{div}}\) is a canonical rational zero-cycle under the stated hypothesis.1 For K3 surfaces, \(o_S^{\mathrm{div}}=o_S\text{.}\)

    Proposition 13.16.

    Assuming the semiampleness theorem quoted in Theorem 10.23, let \(L\) be a nontrivial nef line bundle on a projective IHS manifold \(X\) of dimension \(2n\text{,}\) with \(q_X(c_1(L))=0\text{.}\) Then
    \begin{equation*} c_1(L)^{n+1}=0 \quad\text{in }\operatorname{CH}^{n+1}(X)_{\mathbb Q}. \end{equation*}

    Proof.
    By Proposition 10.22, a positive power \(L^{\otimes m}\) is the pullback of an ample line bundle \(A\) on the base \(B\) of a Lagrangian fibration \(f:X\to B\text{.}\) Replace \(m\) and \(A\) by further powers so that \(A\) is very ample. Since \(\dim B=n\text{,}\) we can choose \(n+1\) hyperplane sections of \(B\) with empty common intersection. Their pullback sections of \(L^{\otimes m}\) also have empty common zero locus. The product of their Cartier-divisor classes on the smooth variety \(X\) is therefore zero:
    \begin{equation*} c_1(L^{\otimes m})^{n+1} =m^{n+1}c_1(L)^{n+1}=0. \end{equation*}
    Division by \(m^{n+1}\) gives the rational Chow relation. This argument uses line bundles and their sections, and requires no pullback homomorphism for arbitrary Chow cycles on the possibly singular base.
    A further theorem relates these isotropic relations to weak splitting. We use Rieß’s reduction in the formulation of [Voi25, Theorem 1.1]: if \(\operatorname{NS}(X)_{\mathbb Q}\) contains a nonzero isotropic class and the IHS manifolds in its deformation class satisfy the hyperkähler SYZ statement, then \(X\) satisfies weak splitting. This reduction is an external theorem.2

    Corollary 13.17.

    Assume the semiampleness theorem quoted in Theorem 10.23. If a projective IHS manifold \(X\) has a nonzero class \(\lambda\in\operatorname{NS}(X)_{\mathbb Q}\) with \(q_X(\lambda)=0\text{,}\) then \(X\) satisfies weak splitting.

    Proof.
    The quoted semiampleness theorem applies to nef line bundles on every compact IHS manifold. By Proposition 10.22, it gives the SYZ statement on every member of the deformation class of \(X\text{.}\) The preceding reduction therefore applies.

    Theorem 13.18 (Voisin; Fu).

    The cycle class map is injective on \(R^*(X)\) in the following cases:
    1. \(X=S^{[n]}\text{,}\) where \(S\) is a projective K3 surface and \(n\leq2b_{\mathrm{tr}}+4\text{,}\) with \(b_{\mathrm{tr}}=22-\rho(S)\text{;}\)
    2. \(X=F(Y)\text{,}\) the variety of lines on any smooth cubic fourfold \(Y\text{;}\)
    3. \(X=K_n(A)\text{,}\) the generalized Kummer variety of an abelian surface \(A\text{,}\) with the convention \(\dim K_n(A)=2n\text{.}\)
    In the third case, a cycle in \(R^*(X)\) that is numerically trivial is already zero in Chow. For \(X=S^{[n]}\) with arbitrary \(n\text{,}\) injectivity on \(R^k(X)\) holds in the three codimensions \(k=2n-2,2n-1,2n\text{.}\)
    The first two cases are [Voi08, Theorem 1.4], and the arbitrary-\(n\) assertion in the last three codimensions is its Theorem 1.5; the third case is [Fu15, Theorem 1.6]. These are statements about the indicated varieties. Applying them to every deformation of the same deformation type requires a further argument; a Chow relation does not transport through an arbitrary smooth deformation as a cohomology class does.
    For every \(n\text{,}\) the divisor-only conjecture for \(S^{[n]}\) follows from a theorem about the following tautological classes.
    Let \(Z_n\subset S^{[n]}\times S\) be the universal subscheme, with projections \(p:S^{[n]}\times S\to S^{[n]}\) and \(q:S^{[n]}\times S\to S\text{.}\) A small tautological class is a class
    \begin{equation*} \tau_k(\gamma):= p_*\bigl(\operatorname{ch}_k(\mathcal O_{Z_n})\cdot q^*\gamma\bigr), \qquad \gamma\in R^*(S),\quad k\geq0. \end{equation*}
    The word small specifies a single Chern-character component before pushforward. Let \(T_{\mathrm{small}}^*(S^{[n]})\) be the subring generated by these classes.

    Theorem 13.19 (Maulik–Neguţ).

    For every projective K3 surface \(S\) and every \(n\geq1\text{,}\) the cycle class map is injective on \(T_{\mathrm{small}}^*(S^{[n]})\text{.}\) This ring contains all divisor classes, so \(S^{[n]}\) satisfies weak splitting for every \(n\text{.}\)
    See [MN22, Theorem 1.2 and Proposition 2.8]. The proof lifts Hilbert-scheme operator identities from cohomology to Chow. It is an external theorem here.3
    The stronger conjecture also leads to rings generated by divisors, tangent Chern classes, and Lagrangian constant-cycle subvarieties.3 For Hilbert squares of K3 surfaces, varieties of lines on smooth cubic fourfolds, and smooth double EPW sextics, the 2026 preprint [Lat26, Theorems 2.2, 3.2, and 4.2] states that these rings inject into cohomology in codimensions three and four.

    Section 13.5 Cycles from moduli spaces

    Universal families on moduli spaces define Chow classes. On a Hilbert scheme, the universal subscheme gives tautological bundles and sends points of the Hilbert scheme to zero-cycles on the surface.

    Proposition 13.20.

    Let \(E\) be a vector bundle on a projective K3 surface \(S\text{,}\) and suppose its Chow Chern character belongs to \(R^*(S)\text{.}\) The tautological bundle
    \begin{equation*} E^{[n]}:=p_*(\mathcal O_{Z_n}\otimes q^*E) \end{equation*}
    on \(S^{[n]}\) has rank \(n\operatorname{rk}(E)\text{,}\) and all its Chern classes belong to \(T_{\mathrm{small}}^*(S^{[n]})\text{.}\) The cycle class map is injective on the subring generated by these Chern classes and divisor classes.

    Proof.
    The universal subscheme is finite flat of degree \(n\) over \(S^{[n]}\text{.}\) Its pushforward of the restriction of \(q^*E\) is locally free of the stated rank, and there are no higher direct images. Grothendieck–Riemann–Roch for the smooth projection \(p\) gives
    \begin{equation*} \operatorname{ch}(E^{[n]}) =p_*\bigl(\operatorname{ch}(\mathcal O_{Z_n}) \cdot q^*(\operatorname{ch}(E)\operatorname{td}(S))\bigr). \end{equation*}
    Since \(c_1(T_S)=0\) and \(c_2(T_S)=24o_S\text{,}\) we have \(\operatorname{td}(S)=1+2o_S\text{.}\) The class \(\operatorname{ch}(E)\operatorname{td}(S)\) belongs to \(R^*(S)\text{.}\) Each homogeneous component on the right is consequently a sum of small tautological classes. Over \(\mathbb Q\text{,}\) the Chern classes are universal polynomials in the components of the Chern character. They lie in the small tautological ring, and Theorem 13.19 proves the final assertion.
    Every line bundle on \(S\) satisfies the hypothesis: if \(c_1(L)=D\text{,}\) then \(\operatorname{ch}(L)=1+D+D^2/2\in R^*(S)\text{.}\) An arbitrary vector bundle need not satisfy it, because its second Chern cycle need not be a multiple of \(o_S\text{.}\) Equality of its second Chern number with some integer is not enough.
    For a general moduli space of stable objects, rational equivalence of points has a particularly precise description. We use the Mukai-vector and stability conventions of Chapter 9.

    Theorem 13.21 (Marian–Zhao).

    Let \(S\) be a projective K3 surface, \(v\) a primitive Mukai vector, and \(\sigma\) a \(v\)-generic stability condition in the distinguished component. Suppose \(M=M_\sigma(v)\) is a nonempty smooth projective moduli space of stable objects of one fixed phase. For two points represented by objects \(E,F\text{,}\)
    \begin{equation*} [E]=[F]\text{ in }\operatorname{CH}_0(M) \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad c_2(E)=c_2(F)\text{ in }\operatorname{CH}_0(S). \end{equation*}
    The Chern classes on the right are Chow Chern classes of the corresponding complexes.
    This is the main theorem of [MZ20]. Both equalities have integral coefficients. Grothendieck–Riemann–Roch first yields rational equalities. Roitman’s theorem identifies torsion in the degree-zero Chow group of a smooth projective complex variety with torsion in its Albanese variety. Here \(H^1(S,\mathbb Q)=H^1(M,\mathbb Q)=0\text{,}\) so both Albanese varieties vanish and the possible torsion disappears. A universal family on the coarse moduli space is not required: the proof also treats quasi-universal families and the descent obstruction.

    Corollary 13.22.

    If \(Z,Z'\subset S\) are zero-dimensional subschemes of the same length \(n\text{,}\) their points on the Hilbert scheme satisfy
    \begin{equation*} [Z]=[Z']\text{ in }\operatorname{CH}_0(S^{[n]}) \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad [Z]_{\mathrm{cyc}}=[Z']_{\mathrm{cyc}} \text{ in }\operatorname{CH}_0(S), \end{equation*}
    where \([Z]_{\mathrm{cyc}}\) is the zero-cycle on \(S\) with the local lengths of \(Z\) as multiplicities.

    Proof.
    The Hilbert scheme is the moduli space of ideal sheaves \(I_Z\) with Mukai vector \((1,0,1-n)\text{,}\) a primitive vector. It is also obtained in the large-volume chamber of stability conditions. The exact sequence \(0\to I_Z\to\mathcal O_S\to\mathcal O_Z\to0\) gives
    \begin{equation*} \operatorname{ch}(I_Z)=1-[Z]_{\mathrm{cyc}},\qquad c_1(I_Z)=0,\qquad c_2(I_Z)=[Z]_{\mathrm{cyc}}. \end{equation*}
    For the last identity, the integral top Chern class of a finite-colength ideal on a smooth surface is its associated zero-cycle; equivalently this follows from the zero-dimensional sheaf calculation in intersection theory. Apply Theorem 13.21 to the two ideal sheaves.
    For example, any two length-\(n\) subschemes whose associated cycles equal \(n o_S\) give rationally equivalent points on \(S^{[n]}\text{.}\) This produces distinguished zero-cycle classes on a higher-dimensional IHS variety by using the canonical class on the surface. It also shows why fixing a Mukai vector in cohomology is insufficient: all objects in the moduli space have that vector, while their second Chern cycles can differ in \(\operatorname{CH}_0(S)\text{.}\)
    For moduli spaces in a Kuznetsov component, Chapter 9 gives the cohomological and stability-theoretic construction. The surface theorem above cannot be transferred by replacing \(S\) with the component: its conclusion uses an actual surface Chow group and actual Chern cycles. The cubic-fourfold Fano variety already provides a proved divisors-and-Chern case in Theorem 13.18. Further cycle statements for other moduli spaces require their own correspondences and theorems.

    Section 13.6 Birational invariance of the Chow ring

    Birational IHS manifolds have isomorphic cohomology rings through a suitable parallel-transport correspondence. The analogous statement for Chow rings requires a construction at the level of cycles. Injectivity of the cycle class map on the whole Chow ring is unavailable, so a cohomological isomorphism alone cannot prove it.
    For smooth projective varieties \(X,Y\text{,}\) with \(\dim X=a\text{,}\) a degree-\(r\) correspondence from \(X\) to \(Y\) is a class \(Z\in\operatorname{CH}^{a+r}(X\times Y)_{\mathbb Q}\text{.}\) It acts by
    \begin{equation*} Z_*:\operatorname{CH}^p(X)_{\mathbb Q}\longrightarrow \operatorname{CH}^{p+r}(Y)_{\mathbb Q},\qquad Z_*(\alpha)=(p_Y)_*(p_X^*\alpha\cdot Z). \end{equation*}
    Its transpose \({}^tZ\) is obtained by exchanging the factors; its degree is \(a+r-\dim Y\text{.}\) For a degree-\(s\) correspondence \(W\) from \(Y\) to \(U\text{,}\) composition is
    \begin{equation*} W\circ Z=(p_{13})_*(p_{12}^*Z\cdot p_{23}^*W) \in\operatorname{CH}^{a+r+s}(X\times U)_{\mathbb Q}. \end{equation*}
    The projections are from \(X\times Y\times U\text{.}\) The diagonal acts as the identity, and composition agrees with composition of the induced maps. In particular, for varieties of the same dimension \(d\text{,}\) degree-zero correspondences have codimension \(d\) and preserve codimension.

    Theorem 13.23 (Rieß).

    Let \(X\) and \(X'\) be birational projective IHS manifolds of dimension \(d=2n\text{.}\) There is a cycle \(Z\in\operatorname{CH}^d(X\times X')\) whose action is an isomorphism of graded Chow rings,
    \begin{equation*} Z_*:\operatorname{CH}^*(X) \xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }\operatorname{CH}^*(X'). \end{equation*}
    Its inverse is \(({}^tZ)_*\text{,}\) and \(Z_*c_i(T_X)=c_i(T_{X'})\) for all \(i\text{.}\) The theorem holds with integral coefficients.
    See [Rie14, Theorem 3.2 and Lemma 4.4]. We explain the construction and the multiplicativity argument, keeping its deformation theorem external.
    Rieß constructs smooth proper families \(\mathcal X,\mathcal X'\to T\) over a smooth curve with a distinguished point \(0\text{,}\) with central fibres \(X,X'\text{,}\) and an isomorphism over \(T\setminus\{0\}\text{.}\) One family may have an algebraic-space total space; the intersection and specialization formalism is applied in that setting. Let \(\eta\) be the generic point of \(T\text{,}\) and \(\Gamma_\eta\) the graph of the generic-fibre isomorphism. The class \(Z\) is its specialization:
    \begin{equation*} Z=\operatorname{sp}[\Gamma_\eta]. \end{equation*}
    Equivalently it is the cycle of the special fibre of the closure of that graph. The existence of these families is [Rie14, Proposition 2.1].

    Proposition 13.24.

    For these families, the correspondence \(Z=\operatorname{sp}[\Gamma_\eta]\) acts as an isomorphism of graded Chow rings with inverse \(({}^tZ)_*\text{,}\) and satisfies \(Z_*c_i(T_X)=c_i(T_{X'})\text{.}\)

    Proof.
    On the generic fibres, the graph of an isomorphism and its transpose satisfy the correspondence identities
    \begin{equation*} {}^t\Gamma_\eta\circ\Gamma_\eta=\Delta_{\mathcal X_\eta}, \qquad \Gamma_\eta\circ{}^t\Gamma_\eta=\Delta_{\mathcal X'_\eta}. \end{equation*}
    Composition uses only intersection and proper pushforward. Specialization therefore gives
    \begin{equation*} {}^tZ\circ Z=\Delta_X,\qquad Z\circ{}^tZ=\Delta_{X'}. \end{equation*}
    Their actions are mutually inverse.
    For multiplicativity, let \(\mu_X=\Delta_{123,X}\in\operatorname{CH}^{2d}(X^3)\) be the small diagonal regarded as a degree-zero correspondence from \(X\times X\) to \(X\text{;}\) its action sends \(\alpha\times\beta\) to \(\alpha\cdot\beta\text{.}\) Define \(\mu_{X'}\) similarly. The generic-fibre isomorphism \(\varphi_\eta\) has graph satisfying the cycle identity
    \begin{equation*} \Gamma_\eta\circ\mu_{\mathcal X_\eta} =\mu_{\mathcal X'_\eta}\circ (\Gamma_\eta\times\Gamma_\eta). \end{equation*}
    Both sides are the class of \(\{(x,x,\varphi_\eta(x)):x\in\mathcal X_\eta\}\) in \(\mathcal X_\eta\times\mathcal X_\eta\times\mathcal X'_\eta\text{;}\) the factors in the product of graph correspondences are ordered as source followed by target. Specializing this identity yields
    \begin{equation*} Z\circ\mu_X=\mu_{X'}\circ(Z\times Z). \end{equation*}
    Applying the correspondence to \(\alpha\times\beta\) gives \(Z_*(\alpha\cdot\beta)=Z_*\alpha\cdot Z_*\beta\text{.}\) This proves multiplicativity for arbitrary central-fibre Chow classes, without assuming that those classes extend over \(T\text{.}\)
    Finally, the generic-fibre isomorphism identifies the tangent bundles, so \((\Gamma_\eta)_*c_i(T_{\mathcal X_\eta})=c_i(T_{\mathcal X'_\eta})\text{.}\) The relative tangent bundles specialize to \(T_X\) and \(T_{X'}\text{.}\) Specializing the equality gives the asserted compatibility with Chern classes.
    5

    Corollary 13.25.

    Weak splitting and the divisors-and-tangent-Chern property are birational invariants of projective IHS manifolds.

    Proof.
    The graded ring isomorphism \(Z_*\) identifies codimension-one Chow groups and hence the subrings \(\operatorname{DCH}^*(X)\) and \(\operatorname{DCH}^*(X')\text{.}\) Its compatibility with tangent Chern classes identifies \(R^*(X)\) and \(R^*(X')\text{.}\) The cycle class map commutes with correspondences. If a class in one of these subrings has zero cohomology class, its inverse image under \(Z_*\) also has zero cohomology class. Injectivity for one variety therefore implies injectivity for the other, and the converse follows by transposing \(Z\text{.}\)
    The established statements above therefore apply to every projective birational model of the stated examples. In particular, a birational wall crossing from Chapter 9 transfers these Chow-ring properties between its two moduli spaces.
