    Appendix A. Notation

    Section A.1 Metrics and periods

    \(X,M,2n\)
    An IHS manifold, its underlying smooth manifold, and its dimension; \(\dim_\R M=4n\text{.}\) Section 0.2, Definition 1.12.
    \(g,I,J,K;\ \omega_L\)
    A hyperkähler metric and parallel quaternionic triple; \(\omega_L(u,v)=g(Lu,v)\) for an induced complex structure \(L\text{.}\) Definition 1.3, Lemma 1.5.
    \(\sigma\)
    A holomorphic symplectic two-form in the geometric chapters. In stable-object moduli, \(\sigma=(Z,\mathcal P)\) is instead a stability condition. Definition 1.7, Definition 9.3.
    \(I_\zeta,X_\zeta,Z\)
    The induced complex structure, the fibre \(X_\zeta=(M,I_\zeta)\text{,}\) and the twistor space \(Z\to\PP^1\text{.}\) (3.1), Definition 3.2.
    \(P_g\)
    For a compact hyperkähler metric of holonomy \(\Sp(n)\text{,}\) the oriented positive real three-plane spanned by \([\omega_I],[\omega_J],[\omega_K]\text{.}\) Section 3.4.
    \(L_\zeta\)
    The oriented period two-plane \(\R\operatorname{Re}[\sigma_\zeta]\oplus\R\operatorname{Im}[\sigma_\zeta]\) inside \(P_g\text{.}\) Section 3.4.
    \(\operatorname{Def}(X)\)
    The Kuranishi germ of deformations of \(X\) with identified central fibre. Section 4.1.
    \(\eta,\Lambda\)
    A marking \(\eta:H^2(X,\Z)\to\Lambda\) and its fixed target lattice. Definition 4.2.
    \(\Omega_\Lambda,\operatorname{Per}\)
    The period domain and the period map, which sends a marked manifold to \(\eta(H^{2,0}(X))\text{.}\) Definition 4.5, (6.1).

    Section A.2 Lattices and moduli

    \(q_X\)
    The primitive integral BBF bilinear form; \(q_X(a)=q_X(a,a)\text{.}\) Theorem 2.1.
    \(L^\vee,A_L,\bar q_L\)
    The dual lattice, the finite group \(L^\vee/L\text{,}\) and its quadratic form with values in \(\Q/2\Z\) when \(L\) is even. Definition 6.4.
    \(\Mon^2(X),O^+\)
    Degree-two parallel-transport operators from \(X\) to itself, and the group preserving the positive orientation. The first is a subgroup of the second. Definition 6.2, positive orientation.
    \(\mathcal I_X\)
    For \(\mathrm{K3}^{[n]}\) type, \(n\geq2\text{,}\) the distinguished \(O(\widetilde\Lambda)\)-orbit of primitive isometric embeddings of \(H^2(X,\Z)\) into the Mukai lattice. Theorem 9.20.
    \(\delta\)
    On \(S^{[n]}\text{,}\) \(n\geq2\text{,}\) half the reduced Hilbert–Chow exceptional divisor class: \([E]=2\delta\text{,}\) \(q(\delta)=-2(n-1)\text{.}\) (5.2).
    \(S^{[n]},K_n(A)\)
    The Hilbert scheme of length-\(n\) subschemes and the sum-zero fibre in \(A^{[n+1]}\text{,}\) respectively. Both have dimension \(2n\text{.}\) Definition 5.5, Definition 5.10.
    \(v(E),v^2\)
    For objects on a surface, the Mukai vector \(\operatorname{ch}(E)\sqrt{\operatorname{td}}\) and its Mukai square \(\langle v,v\rangle\text{.}\) On an abelian surface, \(v(E)=\operatorname{ch}(E)\text{.}\) Definition 8.1, Section 8.7.
    \(N(S),N(A)\)
    The algebraic Mukai lattices \(\Z\oplus\operatorname{NS}\oplus\Z\) of a projective K3 or abelian surface. Section 9.1, Section 9.11.
    \(M_\sigma(v)\)
    Stable-object moduli with a fixed vector and a fixed lift of its phase. In the nonempty cases of the moduli theorems, its dimension is \(v^2+2\text{.}\) Section 9.4, Theorem 9.25.
    \(K_\sigma(v)\)
    The Albanese fibre for objects on an abelian surface. Under the primitive-vector and genericity hypotheses of Theorem 9.25, with \(v^2\geq6\text{,}\) its dimension is \(v^2-2\) and its second cohomology is Mukai-isometric to \(v^\perp\text{.}\)

    Section A.3 Cohomology and characteristic classes

    \(H^k,b_k,h^{p,q}\)
    Singular cohomology, Betti numbers, and Hodge numbers. The superscript \(k\) is ordinary degree; omitted coefficients mean \(\Q\text{,}\) and \(H^{p,q}\subset H^{p+q}(X,\C)\text{.}\) Section 0.2, Section 11.5.
    \(\operatorname{Pic},\operatorname{NS},\rho\)
    Line bundles, \(\operatorname{Pic}/\operatorname{Pic}^0\text{,}\) and the rank of this quotient. For IHS manifolds, \(\operatorname{Pic}^0=0\text{.}\) Section 0.2, Proposition 2.6.
    \(c_X,C_X(\eta)\)
    The constants in \(\int_Xa^{2n}=c_Xq_X(a)^n\) and \(\int_X\eta a^{2n-2j}=C_X(\eta)q_X(a)^{n-j}\text{.}\) The second requires \(\eta\in H^{4j}\text{,}\) \(0\leq j\leq n\text{,}\) whose parallel transport has type \((2j,2j)\) on all small deformations; \(C_X(1)=c_X\text{.}\) (2.1), Theorem 2.10.
    \(Q_X=q_X^\vee\)
    The degree-four cup-product image of the inverse BBF tensor: \(Q_X=\sum q^{ij}e_ie_j\text{,}\) with no factor \(1/2\text{.}\) Definition 2.11.
    \(c_i(E),\operatorname{td}_{2j}(X)\)
    Chern classes have degree \(2i\text{,}\) with \(c_i(X)=c_i(T_X)\text{;}\) the indicated Todd component has degree \(4j\text{.}\) In the Chow chapter, Chern classes also denote the specified Chow classes. Section 0.2, Section 2.5, Definition 13.12.
    \(\Delta(E)\)
    The discriminant \(2r c_2(E)-(r-1)c_1(E)^2\) of a rank-\(r\) bundle. Its \(SU(2)\)-invariance characterizes projective hyperholomorphicity under the polystability hypothesis. Proposition 12.11, Proposition 12.18.
    \(\operatorname{RR}_X(t)\)
    The Riemann–Roch polynomial, satisfying \(\chi(X,L)=\operatorname{RR}_X(q_X(c_1L))\text{.}\) Proposition 2.13.
    \(\SH^k(X)\)
    The degree-\(k\) part of the subalgebra generated by \(H^2(X,\Q)\text{,}\) with ordinary cohomological grading; \(\SH^{2j}\) is the image of \(\operatorname{Sym}^jH^2\text{.}\) Definition 11.7.
    \(L_a,\Lambda_a,h\)
    Cup product by \(a\text{,}\) its Lefschetz lowering operator when defined, and \(h|_{H^k}=(k-2n)\operatorname{id}\text{.}\) Section 11.1, Definition 11.1.
    \(\mathfrak g,\mathfrak g_0\)
    The Lie subalgebra of \(\operatorname{End}_{\C}H^*(X,\C)\) generated by the Lefschetz triples, and its subalgebra \(\{D\in\mathfrak g:[h,D]=0\}\text{.}\) Definition 11.3.
    \(\overline{\mathfrak g}\)
    The reduced LLV subalgebra \([\mathfrak g_0,\mathfrak g_0]\text{,}\) identified by restriction with \(\mathfrak{so}(H^2(X,\C),q_X)\text{.}\) (11.2), Theorem 11.5.
    \(W,Q,e_\pm\)
    The auxiliary orthogonal space \(W=\C e_-\oplus H^2(X,\C)\oplus\C e_+\text{,}\) with \(Q(e_-,e_+)=1\) and \(Q|_{H^2}=q_X\text{.}\) The respective \(h\)-weights are \(-2,0,2\text{.}\) Definition 11.4, Theorem 11.5.

    Section A.4 Cones, cycles, and degenerations

    \(\mathcal C_X,\mathcal K_X,\mathcal{BK}_X\)
    The positive cone, Kähler cone, and union of pullbacks of Kähler cones of birational IHS models. Definition 2.7, Definition 7.6.
    \(\operatorname{Nef}(X),\operatorname{Mov}(X)\)
    Divisor nef classes and the cone generated by movable integral divisors; \(\overline{\operatorname{Mov}}(X)\) denotes its closure. Section 7.1, Section 7.3.
    \(\operatorname{Nef}^+(X),\operatorname{Mov}^+(X)\)
    The cones generated by rational points of the nef cone and of the closed movable cone, respectively. Definition 14.8.
    \(z_C\)
    The rational BBF dual of a curve: \(q_X(z_C,a)=\int_Ca\text{.}\) Corollary 7.4.
    \(\operatorname{CH}^p,\operatorname{CH}_0,\operatorname{cl}\)
    Codimension-\(p\) cycles and zero-cycles modulo rational equivalence, and the cycle class map to degree-\(2p\) cohomology. A subscript \(\Q\) means rational coefficients. Definition 13.1, Definition 13.2.
    \(\operatorname{DCH}^*(X),R^*(X)\)
    The rational Chow subrings generated by divisors, and by divisors together with the Chern classes of \(T_X\text{,}\) respectively. Definition 13.12.
    \(F_\nabla,F\)
    In bundle theory, \(F_\nabla=\nabla^2\) is curvature, also abbreviated \(F\text{.}\) In fibration arguments, \(F=f^{-1}(b)\) is a fibre. Section 12.2, Proposition 12.11, Proposition 10.5.
    \(T_d,N_d,\nu_d\)
    Monodromy on \(H^d\text{,}\) its logarithm after unipotent base change, and \(\nu_d=\max\{j:N_d^j\ne0\}\) when \(N_d\ne0\text{,}\) with \(\nu(0)=0\text{.}\) A block of length \(r+1\) has index \(r\text{.}\) Section 15.1, Definition 15.3.
    \(W_\bullet(A)[m]\)
    The nilpotent weight filtration centered at \(m\text{.}\) For limiting cohomology in degree \(d\text{,}\) the center of \(W_\bullet(N_d)[d]\) is \(d\text{.}\) Definition 15.20, Theorem 15.23.
    \(\mathcal W^\beta,P_iH^d\)
    For a projective Lagrangian fibration and an ample rational base class \(\alpha\text{,}\) let \(\beta=f^*\alpha\text{.}\) Then \(\mathcal W^\beta=W_\bullet(L_\beta)[n]\) has center \(n\) on total cohomology. The perverse normalization is \(P_iH^d=\mathcal W^\beta_{i-d+2n}\cap H^d\text{,}\) with indices \(0\leq i\leq2n\text{.}\) Section 15.6, Theorem 15.26.
