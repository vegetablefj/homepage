Corollary 1.14.

A compact Kähler holomorphic symplectic manifold has a finite étale cover of the form
\begin{equation*} T\times Z_1\times\cdots\times Z_r, \end{equation*}
where \(T\) is an even-dimensional complex torus with a nondegenerate translation-invariant holomorphic two-form and the \(Z_j\) are IHS. If the original manifold is simply connected, it is itself a product of IHS manifolds.
🔗
🔗

Proof.
Pull back \(\sigma\) to the product cover in Theorem 1.13. Each simply connected compact Kähler factor has \(H^0(\Omega^1)=0\text{:}\) its first Betti number is zero, and Hodge decomposition gives \(h^{1,0}=0\text{.}\) Thus holomorphic two-forms have no mixed terms involving two different non-torus factors or one such factor and the torus. On a strict Calabi–Yau factor, \(H^0(\Omega^2)=0\) because its dimension is at least three. A tangent vector in that factor would be in the kernel of the pulled-back form. Nondegeneracy therefore excludes all strict Calabi–Yau factors.
🔗
The remaining form is the sum of a form on \(T\) and forms on the \(Z_j\text{.}\) It is nondegenerate on each factor separately. Holomorphic forms on a complex torus are translation invariant, as follows by lifting their coefficients to periodic holomorphic functions and using compactness. Hence \(T\) has even dimension and the stated symplectic structure. If \(X\) is simply connected, its universal cover is \(X\) itself. The Euclidean factor in the universal-cover statement is absent because \(X\) is compact, leaving the asserted product.
