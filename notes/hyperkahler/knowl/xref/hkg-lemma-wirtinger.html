Lemma 12.24.

Let \((V,g,L)\) be a Hermitian real vector space, and \(U\subset V\) an oriented real subspace of dimension \(2d\text{.}\) Then
\begin{equation*} \left|\frac{\omega_L^d}{d!}\Big|_U\right|\leq\operatorname{vol}_U. \end{equation*}
Equality holds exactly when \(U\) is \(L\)-invariant. Equality without the absolute value uses its complex orientation.
🔗
🔗

Proof.
The restriction of \(\omega_L\) is represented by the skew-adjoint operator \(S=\operatorname{pr}_U L|_U\text{.}\) In an orthonormal basis of \(U\text{,}\) its skew-normal form has \(d\) two-dimensional blocks with parameters \(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_d\text{,}\) all of absolute value at most \(1\text{,}\) because orthogonal projection does not increase length. The coefficient of \(\omega_L^d/d!\) relative to the oriented volume is, up to the orientation sign, \(\lambda_1\cdots\lambda_d\text{.}\) This proves the inequality.
🔗
Equality requires every singular value of \(S\) to be \(1\text{,}\) so \(|\operatorname{pr}_ULv|=|Lv|\) for every \(v\in U\text{.}\) The component of \(Lv\) perpendicular to \(U\) then vanishes, proving \(L(U)=U\text{.}\) Conversely an \(L\)-complex subspace has its Kähler volume form \(\omega_L^d/d!\text{,}\) positive for its complex orientation.
