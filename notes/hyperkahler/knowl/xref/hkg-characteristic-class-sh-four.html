Corollary 11.17.

Assume \(n\geq2\text{.}\) The subspace of \(\SH^4(X,\C)\) annihilated by the reduced LLV algebra is the line generated by the inverse quadratic class \(Q_X\) of Definition 2.11. Consequently,
\begin{equation*} c_2(T_X)\in\SH^4(X) \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad c_2(T_X)\in\Q Q_X. \end{equation*}
🔗
🔗

Proof.
Verbitsky’s injection identifies \(\SH^4(X,\C)\) with \(\operatorname{Sym}^2V\text{.}\) Through \(q_X\text{,}\) a symmetric tensor in this space determines a self-adjoint endomorphism of \(V\text{.}\) It is invariant under \(\mathfrak{so}(V,q_X)\) precisely when this endomorphism commutes with the standard orthogonal representation. That representation is irreducible for \(b\geq3\text{,}\) so Schur’s lemma makes the endomorphism scalar. The corresponding symmetric tensor is a multiple of \(q_X^{-1}\text{,}\) whose cup-product image is \(Q_X\text{.}\) The second Chern class is invariant by Proposition 11.15. If it lies in the subalgebra, it therefore lies on this line; the coefficient is rational because both classes are rational and \(Q_X\ne0\text{.}\) The reverse implication follows from the definition of \(Q_X\text{.}\)
