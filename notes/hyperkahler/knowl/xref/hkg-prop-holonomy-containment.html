Proposition 1.4.

A Riemannian manifold of real dimension \(4n\) admits a hyperkähler structure if and only if, after an orthogonal identification of one tangent space with a quaternionic Hermitian space,
\begin{equation*} \operatorname{Hol}_x(g)\subseteq\mathrm{Sp}(n). \end{equation*}
Containment of the restricted holonomy alone guarantees the structure on the universal cover, but need not make a chosen triple descend to \(M\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗

Proof.
A parallel endomorphism commutes with parallel transport around every loop. Thus a hyperkähler triple makes \(\operatorname{Hol}_x(g)\) commute with \(I_x,J_x,K_x\text{,}\) and Lemma 1.2 identifies this centralizer with \(\mathrm{Sp}(n)\text{.}\)
🔗
Conversely, choose a quaternionic triple on \(T_xM\) fixed by the holonomy. For a path from \(x\) to \(y\text{,}\) transport the three endomorphisms to \(T_yM\text{.}\) Two paths differ by a loop, and holonomy invariance makes their transports equal. This defines smooth parallel endomorphisms on \(TM\text{.}\) Their orthogonality and multiplication rules hold at \(x\) and are preserved by parallel transport, so they hold everywhere. On the universal cover all loops downstairs arising from loops upstairs are null-homotopic; the same argument applies to restricted holonomy there.
