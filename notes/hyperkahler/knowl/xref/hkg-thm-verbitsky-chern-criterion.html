Theorem 12.13 (Verbitsky).

Let \(M\) be compact hyperkähler, and let \(E\) be a slope polystable holomorphic vector bundle on \((M,I)\text{,}\) for the metric Kähler form \(\omega_I\text{.}\) Then \(E\) admits a hyperholomorphic connection if and only if \(c_1(E)\) and \(c_2(E)\) are \(SU(2)\)-invariant in real cohomology. In that case its Hermitian–Einstein connection is hyperholomorphic, and its slope is zero.
🔗
🔗

Proof.
Necessity is Proposition 12.5. Conversely, an invariant class \(c_1(E)\) has an invariant harmonic representative, which is primitive by Lemma 12.2. Its degree against \(\omega_I\) is therefore zero. By Theorem 12.8, polystability gives a Hermitian–Einstein connection \(\nabla\text{.}\) Its curvature \(F\) has type \((1,1)\) for \(I\) and satisfies \(\Lambda_I F=0\text{,}\) since the slope is zero.
🔗
For each induced complex structure \(L\text{,}\) put
\begin{equation*} \mathcal E_L=\int_M\operatorname{Tr}(F\wedge F)\frac{\omega_L^{d-2}}{(d-2)!},\qquad d=2n. \end{equation*}
The Chern–Weil class of \(\operatorname{Tr}(F\wedge F)\) is
\begin{equation*} 8\pi^2\left(c_2(E)-\frac12c_1(E)^2\right). \end{equation*}
This class is \(SU(2)\)-invariant by hypothesis. The cohomological action preserves cup product and integration, and takes \([\omega_I]\) to \([\omega_L]\text{.}\) Hence \(\mathcal E_L=\mathcal E_I\text{.}\) The energy identity at \(I\) gives \(\mathcal E_I=\|F\|_{L^2}^2\text{.}\) The Riemannian metric and complex orientation are the same for every induced structure, so this norm is independent of \(L\text{.}\) Applying (12.1) at \(L\) now gives
\begin{equation*} 2\|F^{2,0;L}+F^{0,2;L}\|_{L^2}^2 +d\left\|\frac{\Lambda_LF}{d}\omega_L\right\|_{L^2}^2=0. \end{equation*}
Both terms vanish. Thus \(F\) is primitive of type \((1,1)\) for every induced structure, and Proposition 12.4 proves hyperholomorphicity.
