Proposition 3.8.

The three classes \(\alpha,\beta,\gamma\) are pairwise orthogonal for \(q_X\) and have the same positive square \(t\text{.}\) Thus \(q_X|_{P_g}\) is positive definite. Its orthogonal complement in \(H^2(M,\mathbb R)\) is negative definite.
🔗
🔗

Proof.
For the fiber \((M,I)\text{,}\) the Hodge orthogonality of the BBF form gives \(q_X(\alpha,s)=0\) and \(q_X(s)=0\text{.}\) Taking real and imaginary parts yields
\begin{equation*} q_X(\alpha,\beta)=q_X(\alpha,\gamma)=q_X(\beta,\gamma)=0,\qquad q_X(\beta)=q_X(\gamma). \end{equation*}
All three classes are Kähler on their respective induced complex manifolds, so their squares are positive. The metric volume is the same for these three complex structures, and their complex orientations agree. Hence
\begin{equation*} \int_M\omega_I^{2n}=\int_M\omega_J^{2n}=\int_M\omega_K^{2n}=(2n)!\operatorname{Vol}_g(M). \end{equation*}
Fujiki’s relation, with the same transported form and constant, gives equality of the positive \(n\)th powers of their squares; positivity gives equality of the squares. The BBF signature is \((3,b_2-3)\text{,}\) so the orthogonal complement of this positive three-plane is negative definite.
