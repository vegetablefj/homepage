Proposition 3.12.

Every class in \(\Lambda_g\) has type \((1,1)\) on every fiber. A class \(\lambda\in\Lambda\setminus\Lambda_g\) has type \((1,1)\) at exactly two antipodal points of the twistor sphere. Consequently, outside a countable set of parameters,
\begin{equation} \operatorname{Pic}(X_\zeta)\cong\Lambda_g.\tag{3.3} \end{equation}
The restriction of \(q_X\) to the real span of this group is negative definite.
🔗
🔗

Proof.
For a real degree-two class, type \((1,1)\) is equivalent to orthogonality to both the real and imaginary parts of the period. Hence \(\lambda\) has this type exactly when \(q_X(\lambda,L_\zeta)=0\text{.}\) Let \(\lambda_P\) be its orthogonal projection to \(P_g\text{.}\) If \(\lambda_P=0\text{,}\) this condition holds for all \(\zeta\text{.}\) Otherwise, the condition says that the two-plane \(L_\zeta\) has normal line \(\mathbb R\lambda_P\text{.}\) There are exactly two oriented unit normals on this line. They correspond to antipodal parameters, or equivalently to the two complex structures with opposite signs.
🔗
The lattice \(\Lambda\) is countable. Removing the two points for every class not in \(\Lambda_g\) therefore removes at most a countable set. At every remaining parameter, its integral \((1,1)\) classes are precisely \(\Lambda_g\text{.}\) Negative definiteness follows from \(\Lambda_g\subset P_g^\perp\) and Proposition 3.8.
