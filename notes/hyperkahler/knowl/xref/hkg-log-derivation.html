Proposition 15.4.

The logarithmic monodromy is a degree-zero derivation of the cohomology ring:
\begin{equation*} N(u\smile v)=N(u)\smile v+u\smile N(v). \end{equation*}
On degree two,
\begin{equation*} q_X(N_2a,b)+q_X(a,N_2b)=0,\qquad N_2h=0. \end{equation*}
🔗
🔗

Proof.
The endomorphism \(\exp(sN)\) is polynomial in \(s\text{.}\) For every nonnegative integer \(m\) it equals \(T^m\text{,}\) which preserves cup product. Therefore
\begin{equation*} \exp(sN)(u\smile v)=\exp(sN)u\smile\exp(sN)v \end{equation*}
is an identity of vector-valued polynomials: it holds for infinitely many integers. Differentiating at \(s=0\) proves the derivation formula. Its degree is zero because every term of \(\log T\) preserves degree.
🔗
Parallel transport preserves the BBF form. Apply the same polynomial argument to \(q_X(\exp(sN_2)a,\exp(sN_2)b)=q_X(a,b)\) and differentiate. Finally, \((T_2-\id)h=0\) makes every term in \(\log T_2(h)\) vanish. This proof follows [HM22t, Lemma 2.1].
