Proposition 9.17.

An exact Fourier–Mukai equivalence \(\Phi:D^b(S)\to D^b(T)\) transports a stability condition by
\begin{equation*} \Phi(\sigma)=\bigl(Z\circ(\Phi^H)^{-1},\ \phi\mapsto\Phi(\mathcal P(\phi))\bigr). \end{equation*}
It gives an isomorphism of the corresponding moduli problems and, when the coarse spaces exist, an isomorphism
\begin{equation*} M_\sigma(v)\cong M_{\Phi(\sigma)}(\Phi^H(v)). \end{equation*}
🔗
🔗

Proof.
Exactness preserves shifts and distinguished triangles, so it preserves the slicing and filtration axioms. Full faithfulness preserves the Hom vanishing. Central charges and phases agree by construction. Pulling back a norm by the lattice isomorphism preserves the support inequality. The equivalence therefore sends stable objects of the selected vector and phase exactly to stable objects of the transformed vector and the same phase. A Fourier–Mukai kernel and its inverse act in families and commute with base change in the moduli problem. They give inverse transformations of these moduli problems, and hence inverse isomorphisms of their coarse spaces.
