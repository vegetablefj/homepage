Lemma 1.2.

Let \((E,g,I,J,K)\) be quaternionic Hermitian. Then \(\dim_{\mathbb R}E=4n\) for an integer \(n\text{,}\) and \(E\) is an orthogonal direct sum of quaternionic lines. The orthogonal transformations commuting with \(I,J,K\) form the compact symplectic group \(\mathrm{Sp}(n)\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗

Proof.
Choose a unit vector \(e\text{.}\) The vectors \(e,Ie,Je,Ke\) are orthonormal. For example, \(g(Ie,Je)=-g(e,IJe)=-g(e,Ke)=0\text{;}\) all other pairings follow from the same skew-adjointness and multiplication rules. Their span is preserved by \(I,J,K\text{.}\) Its orthogonal complement is also preserved: if \(u\) lies in the complement and \(v\) lies in the span, then \(g(Iu,v)=-g(u,Iv)=0\text{.}\) Induction gives the orthogonal decomposition and the dimension.
🔗
In a basis obtained this way, commuting with the quaternionic action is quaternionic linearity. Preserving \(g\) is preservation of the corresponding quaternionic Hermitian inner product. Its group of isometries is the definition of the compact group \(\mathrm{Sp}(n)\text{.}\) This group is different from the complex group \(\mathrm{Sp}(2n,\mathbb C)\text{,}\) which preserves a nondegenerate alternating form on a complex vector space of dimension \(2n\text{.}\)
