Proposition 9.29.

If \(M=M_\sigma(v)\) for a projective K3 surface, a primitive vector of positive square, and a generic distinguished stability condition, then
\begin{equation*} \rho(M)=\rho(S)+1\geq2. \end{equation*}
The same equality holds for a moduli space on a projective twisted K3 surface under the corresponding primitive-vector and generic-stability hypotheses. In particular, a projective IHS manifold of Picard number one is not birational to either kind of surface moduli space.
🔗
🔗

Proof.
The full Mukai Hodge isometry identifies the integral \((1,1)\) part of \(H^2(M)\) with \(v^\perp\cap N(S)\text{.}\) Since \(N(S)\) has rank \(\rho(S)+2\) and pairing with the nonzero positive vector \(v\) is a nonzero rational linear functional, this kernel has rank \(\rho(S)+1\text{.}\)
🔗
For a rational B-field lift, multiplication by \(e^B\) identifies the untwisted and twisted algebraic Mukai spaces over \(\Q\text{.}\) It need not preserve their integral lattices, but it preserves their ranks and rational pairings. The twisted algebraic lattice therefore also has rank \(\rho(S)+2\text{.}\) The twisted Mukai isometry identifies the Néron–Severi space of the moduli space with its orthogonal complement to \(v\text{,}\) giving the same count. Finally, birational IHS manifolds have Hodge-isometric second cohomology and hence the same Picard number.
