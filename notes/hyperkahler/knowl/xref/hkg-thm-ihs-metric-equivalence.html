Theorem 1.16.

Let \(X\) be IHS of dimension \(2n\text{.}\) The Ricci-flat Kähler metric in every Kähler class has full holonomy exactly \(\mathrm{Sp}(n)\text{.}\) Conversely, if a compact Riemannian manifold of real dimension \(4n\) has holonomy exactly \(\mathrm{Sp}(n)\text{,}\) every complex structure in its induced sphere makes it IHS.
🔗
🔗

Proof.
For the first implication, apply Corollary 1.14 to \(X\text{.}\) Simple connectedness removes the torus and the finite quotient. Each symplectic factor contributes a nonzero independent holomorphic two-form, so \(h^{2,0}(X)=1\) leaves only one factor. The metric part of Theorem 1.13 then gives restricted holonomy \(\mathrm{Sp}(n)\text{.}\) Full and restricted holonomy agree because \(X\) is simply connected.
🔗
Conversely, Proposition 1.4 gives a parallel quaternionic triple, and Proposition 1.6 gives a holomorphic symplectic form for each induced complex structure. Since \(\mathrm{Sp}(n)\) is connected, it is also the restricted holonomy. Its real representation on the tangent space is irreducible. Thus the product decomposition in Theorem 1.13 has no flat factor and only one nonflat factor. By Corollary 1.14, this gives a finite étale cover \(\pi:\widetilde X\to X\) with \(\widetilde X\) IHS.
🔗
The lifted metric on the simply connected cover has the same restricted, and hence full, holonomy \(\mathrm{Sp}(n)\text{.}\) The Bochner principle and Lemma 1.15 therefore give \(h^{2,0}(X)=1\) and
\begin{equation*} \chi(X,\mathcal O_X)=n+1=\chi(\widetilde X,\mathcal O_{\widetilde X}). \end{equation*}
Let \(\td(T_X)\) be the Todd class of the tangent bundle. Hirzebruch–Riemann–Roch and \(T_{\widetilde X}\cong\pi^*T_X\) give
\begin{equation*} \chi(\widetilde X,\mathcal O_{\widetilde X})=\int_{\widetilde X}\pi^*\td(T_X)=(\deg\pi)\chi(X,\mathcal O_X). \end{equation*}
Hence \(\deg\pi=1\text{,}\) so \(X\) is simply connected and therefore IHS.
