Definition 16.2 (Beauville).

A symplectic variety is a pair \((Z,\sigma)\) such that \(Z\) is normal, \(\sigma\in H^0(U,\Omega_U^2)\) is closed and everywhere nondegenerate on \(U=Z_{\mathrm{reg}}\text{,}\) and for a resolution \(f:Y\to Z\) the form \(f^*\sigma\) extends to a regular two-form \(\widetilde\sigma\) on \(Y\text{.}\) Here a resolution is a proper birational morphism from a smooth variety, chosen to be an isomorphism over \(U\text{.}\) We also say that \(Z\) has symplectic singularities.
