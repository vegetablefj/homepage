Proposition 2.3.

The decomposition \(H^2(X,\C)=\C\sigma\oplus H^{1,1}(X)\oplus\C\bar\sigma\) is orthogonal except for the pairing of \(\sigma\) with \(\bar\sigma\text{.}\) More precisely,
\begin{equation*} q_X(\sigma)=0,\quad q_X(\sigma,\bar\sigma)>0,\quad q_X(\sigma,H^{1,1}(X))=q_X(\bar\sigma,H^{1,1}(X))=0. \end{equation*}
In particular, \(H^{1,1}(X)\) is the orthogonal complement of \(\C\sigma\oplus\C\bar\sigma\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗

Proof.
Write \(a=x\sigma+\beta+y\bar\sigma\) with \(\beta\in H^{1,1}(X)\text{.}\) In (2.2), type considerations eliminate the cross terms involving \(\beta\) and one of \(\sigma,\bar\sigma\text{.}\) The first term contributes \(nxy\text{,}\) and the product in the second term contributes \((1-n)xy\text{.}\) Consequently,
\begin{equation*} q_\sigma(a)=xy+\frac n2\int_X\beta^2(\sigma\bar\sigma)^{n-1}. \end{equation*}
It follows that \(q_\sigma(\sigma,\bar\sigma)=1/2\) and all the stated orthogonalities hold. Positive rescaling gives the assertions for \(q_X\text{.}\) Nondegeneracy identifies the indicated orthogonal complement.
