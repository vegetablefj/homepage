Lemma 1.15.

Let \(V\) be the standard complex representation of \(\mathrm{Sp}(n)\text{,}\) with invariant symplectic form \(\sigma\text{.}\) Then
\begin{equation*} (\Lambda^{2k}V^*)^{\mathrm{Sp}(n)}=\mathbb C\sigma^k\quad(0\leq k\leq n),\qquad (\Lambda^{2k+1}V^*)^{\mathrm{Sp}(n)}=0. \end{equation*}
🔗
🔗

Proof.
Choose a unitary symplectic coframe \(x_1,y_1,\ldots,x_n,y_n\) with \(\sigma=\sum_jx_j\wedge y_j\text{.}\) A maximal compact torus acts on \(x_j,y_j\) by inverse phases, independently for each \(j\text{.}\) A basis wedge monomial fixed by all these phases must contain both \(x_j,y_j\) or neither for every \(j\text{.}\) There are no such monomials in odd degree. In degree \(2k\text{,}\) every invariant is a linear combination of
\begin{equation*} \prod_{j\in S}(x_j\wedge y_j),\qquad |S|=k. \end{equation*}
Symplectic unitary transformations permute the pairs. Since the factors in this product have even degree, no signs appear when pairs are permuted. Invariance therefore makes all coefficients equal. The resulting sum is \(\sigma^k/k!\text{.}\) Conversely, \(\sigma^k\) is invariant under the whole group, so this one-dimensional upper bound is attained.
