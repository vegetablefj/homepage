Proposition 1.17.

Let \(X\) be IHS of dimension \(2n\text{.}\) Then
\begin{equation*} H^0(X,\Omega_X^{2k})=\mathbb C\sigma^k\quad(0\leq k\leq n),\qquad H^0(X,\Omega_X^{2k+1})=0. \end{equation*}
Consequently,
\begin{equation*} \chi(X,\mathcal O_X)=n+1,\qquad b_1(X)=0,\qquad b_2(X)\geq3,\qquad H^0(X,T_X)=0. \end{equation*}
The abelian group \(H^2(X,\mathbb Z)\) is torsion free.
🔗
🔗

Proof.
Use a Ricci-flat metric in a Kähler class. Its holonomy is \(\mathrm{Sp}(n)\) by Theorem 1.16. The Bochner principle and Lemma 1.15 give the spaces of holomorphic forms. Hodge symmetry gives \(h^{0,2k}=1\) and \(h^{0,2k+1}=0\text{,}\) hence \(\chi(\mathcal O_X)=\sum_q(-1)^qh^{0,q}=n+1\text{.}\)
🔗
Simple connectedness implies \(H_1(X,\mathbb Z)=0\) and thus \(b_1=0\text{.}\) The nonzero classes \([\sigma]\text{,}\) \([\bar\sigma]\text{,}\) and a Kähler class lie in three different Hodge components of \(H^2(X,\mathbb C)\text{,}\) so \(b_2\geq3\text{.}\) Contraction with \(\sigma\) identifies holomorphic vector fields with holomorphic one-forms, and the latter vanish. Finally, the universal coefficient exact sequence has left term \(\operatorname{Ext}^1(H_1(X,\mathbb Z),\mathbb Z)=0\text{,}\) so
\begin{equation*} H^2(X,\mathbb Z)\cong\operatorname{Hom}(H_2(X,\mathbb Z),\mathbb Z). \end{equation*}
A compact smooth manifold has finitely generated homology, and this Hom group is free abelian.
