Lemma 10.11 (Voisin).

Let \(f:Y\to B\) be a proper surjective holomorphic map from a connected compact Kähler manifold onto a normal complex space. Let \(F\) be a connected smooth fiber over the submersion locus, of codimension \(r\text{,}\) with inclusion \(j:F\hookrightarrow Y\text{.}\) Then, in every degree,
\begin{equation*} \ker\bigl(j^*:H^k(Y,\R)\to H^k(F,\R)\bigr) =\ker\bigl([F]\smile-:H^k(Y,\R)\to H^{k+2r}(Y,\R)\bigr). \end{equation*}
🔗
🔗

Proof.
Let \(I^k=\operatorname{im}j^*\text{,}\) and let \(h_F\) be the restriction of a Kähler class on \(Y\text{.}\) We use the global invariant cycle theorem in its compact Kähler form: \(I^k\) is the monodromy-invariant part of the fiber cohomology. This theorem, and its application to relative Lefschetz decomposition, are quoted from [SY21, Appendix B, proof of Lemma B.4]. Over the submersion locus, the class \(h_F\) is a flat section, and its relative Lefschetz decomposition commutes with monodromy. Thus \(I^*\) is stable under that decomposition and under both Lefschetz operators. It is also a real Hodge substructure, since restriction preserves Hodge type.
🔗
Write \(d=\dim F\text{.}\) The Hodge–Riemann bilinear relations make the Lefschetz pairing nondegenerate on each primitive real Hodge substructure of \(I^*\text{:}\) the associated Hermitian form is definite on each primitive Hodge component, so its restriction has zero radical. Primitive summands in a fixed degree are orthogonal. Together with the Lefschetz isomorphisms, this proves that
\begin{equation*} I^k\times I^{2d-k}\longrightarrow\R,\qquad(a,b)\longmapsto\int_F ab \end{equation*}
is nondegenerate in every degree.
🔗
If \(j^*a=0\text{,}\) the projection formula gives \([F]a=j_*j^*a=0\text{.}\) Conversely, if \(j^*a\ne0\text{,}\) nondegeneracy provides \(b\in H^{2d-k}(Y,\R)\) with
\begin{equation*} 0\ne\int_F j^*a\,j^*b=\int_Y[F]ab. \end{equation*}
Hence \([F]a\ne0\text{.}\) This proves the reverse inclusion.
