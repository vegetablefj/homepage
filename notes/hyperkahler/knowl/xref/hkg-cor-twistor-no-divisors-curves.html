Corollary 3.14.

Outside a countable set of parameters, an IHS twistor fiber has no nonzero effective divisors and no compact curves. In particular it is not projective and has algebraic dimension zero.
🔗
🔗

Proof.
First choose a parameter satisfying (3.3). If \(D\) were a nonzero effective divisor, its class \(\lambda=c_1(\mathcal O(D))\) would lie in \(\Lambda_g\text{.}\) The polarized Fujiki formula gives
\begin{equation*} \int_{X_\zeta}\lambda[\omega_{I_\zeta}]^{2n-1}=c_Xq_X([\omega_{I_\zeta}])^{n-1}q_X(\lambda,[\omega_{I_\zeta}])=0. \end{equation*}
On the other hand, this integral is the positive Kähler volume \(\int_D\omega_{I_\zeta}^{2n-1}\text{.}\) This excludes effective divisors.
🔗
The same parameter choice controls rational \((1,1)\) classes, since an integer multiple of any rational class is integral. If \(C\subset X_\zeta\) were a compact irreducible curve, nondegeneracy and rationality of \(q_X\) would give a unique \(v_C\in H^2(M,\mathbb Q)\) such that
\begin{equation*} q_X(v_C,a)=\int_Ca\qquad\text{for every }a\in H^2(M,\mathbb Q). \end{equation*}
The integral of a holomorphic or antiholomorphic two-form over a curve is zero. Hence \(q_X(v_C,[\sigma_\zeta])=q_X(v_C,[\bar\sigma_\zeta])=0\text{,}\) which means \(v_C\) has type \((1,1)\text{.}\) Our parameter choice forces \(v_C\in P_g^\perp\text{.}\) But then \(\int_C\omega_{I_\zeta}=q_X(v_C,[\omega_{I_\zeta}])=0\text{,}\) contradicting Kähler positivity.
🔗
A projective manifold of positive dimension has effective hyperplane divisors, so these fibers are not projective. A nonconstant meromorphic function on a compact complex manifold has a nonzero polar divisor: if it had none, extension across subsets of codimension at least two would make it holomorphic everywhere, and compactness would make it constant. With no effective divisors, every meromorphic function is constant. This is algebraic dimension zero.
