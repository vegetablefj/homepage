Proposition 1.6.

Let \((M,g,I,J,K)\) be hyperkähler. Then
\begin{equation} \sigma:=\omega_J+i\omega_K\tag{1.2} \end{equation}
is a parallel holomorphic symplectic form on \(X=(M,I)\text{.}\) The metric \(g\) is Ricci flat.
🔗
🔗

Proof.
The quaternion relations give
\begin{equation*} \sigma(Iu,v)=-\omega_K(u,v)+i\omega_J(u,v)=i\sigma(u,v). \end{equation*}
Skew-symmetry gives the same identity in the second argument. Thus \(\sigma\) has type \((2,0)\text{.}\) It is parallel, so it is closed. The \((0,1)\) part of the induced connection on holomorphic forms is \(\bar\partial\text{;}\) hence a parallel form is holomorphic.
🔗
Every vector of type \((1,0)\) has the form \(z=u-iIu\) for a real vector \(u\text{.}\) If \(\sigma(z,w)=0\) for all vectors \(w\) of type \((1,0)\text{,}\) type \((2,0)\) implies the same vanishing for arbitrary complexified vectors \(w\text{.}\) Since \(\sigma(z,w)=2\sigma(u,w)\text{,}\) both \(g(Ju,w)\) and \(g(Ku,w)\) vanish for all real \(w\text{.}\) This forces \(u=0\text{,}\) proving nondegeneracy.
🔗
The top form \(\sigma^n\) is a nowhere vanishing parallel section of the canonical bundle. The connection on the canonical bundle is therefore flat. On a Kähler manifold its curvature is, up to the fixed factor \(i\) and sign convention, the Ricci form. The Ricci form and hence the Ricci tensor vanish. This curvature identity is the local Kähler identity \(\rho=-i\partial\bar\partial\log\det(g_{a\bar b})\text{.}\)
