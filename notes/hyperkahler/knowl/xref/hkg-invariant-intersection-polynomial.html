Proposition 11.16.

Let \(0\leq k\leq2n\text{,}\) and let \(\eta\in H^{2k}(X,\C)\) remain of type \((k,k)\) on all small deformations. If \(k\) is odd, then
\begin{equation*} \int_X\eta a^{2n-k}=0\qquad(a\in H^2(X,\C)). \end{equation*}
If \(k=2j\text{,}\) its intersection polynomial is a constant multiple of \(q_X^{n-j}\text{,}\) as in Theorem 2.10. The constant is rational for rational \(\eta\) and real for real \(\eta\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗

Proof.
Let \(m=2n-k\) and \(P_\eta(a)=\int_X\eta a^m\text{.}\) If \(m=0\text{,}\) the polynomial is constant and the assertion follows. Assume \(m>0\text{.}\) By Proposition 11.15, every reduced LLV element annihilates \(\eta\text{.}\) It also acts trivially on \(H^{4n}(X,\C)\text{:}\) that space is one-dimensional, and a semisimple Lie algebra has no nontrivial one-dimensional representation. Because its elements are derivations,
\begin{equation*} \frac{d}{dt}\bigg|_{t=0}P_\eta(a+tDa) =m\int_X\eta a^{m-1}Da =\int_XD(\eta a^m)=0. \end{equation*}
This proves infinitesimal invariance of the polynomial under \(\mathfrak{so}(H^2(X,\C),q_X)\text{,}\) and hence invariance under the connected complex group \(SO(H^2(X,\C),q_X)\text{,}\) acting on its variables in \(H^2(X,\C)\text{.}\)
🔗
Here is the elementary invariant-polynomial calculation when \(m>0\text{.}\) Choose \(e\) with \(q_X(e)=1\text{.}\) The special orthogonal group is transitive on the vectors of square one. Indeed, extend two such vectors to orthonormal bases to obtain an orthogonal transformation between them. If its determinant is \(-1\text{,}\) compose with reflection in another vector of the target orthonormal basis. This reflection fixes the target vector and changes the determinant to one. Here \(\dim H^2(X,\C)\geq3\text{,}\) so another basis vector exists. In particular, \(e\) and \(-e\) are in the same orbit.
🔗
If \(m\) is odd, homogeneity gives \(P_\eta(e)=P_\eta(-e)=-P_\eta(e)\text{,}\) so both values vanish. Every vector \(a\) with \(q_X(a)\ne0\) is a nonzero scalar multiple of a square-one vector; hence \(P_\eta\) vanishes on that dense open set and therefore everywhere. If \(m\) is even, the same argument gives
\begin{equation*} P_\eta(a)=P_\eta(e)q_X(a)^{m/2} \end{equation*}
on the nonisotropic locus and hence everywhere by polynomial identity. Since \(m\) has the parity of \(k\text{,}\) these are precisely the two assertions.
🔗
In the even case, for rational \(\eta\text{,}\) evaluate at any rational \(a\) of nonzero square. Both \(P_\eta(a)\) and the nonzero value \(q_X(a)^{m/2}\) are rational, so their ratio is rational. The same evaluation with real classes proves the real assertion.
