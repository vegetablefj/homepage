Lemma 8.10.

An \(H\)-stable sheaf \(E\) is simple: \(\operatorname{Hom}(E,E)=\C\operatorname{id}_E\text{.}\) If \(E,F\) are nonisomorphic stable sheaves with the same reduced Hilbert polynomial, then \(\operatorname{Hom}(E,F)=0\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗

Proof.
Let \(f:E\to F\) be nonzero. Its image \(I\) has the same dimension as \(E\) and \(F\) by purity. If the kernel is nonzero, stability of \(E\) and additivity of the Hilbert polynomial imply \(p_I>p_E\text{:}\) the polynomial of \(E\) is a positive weighted average of those of its kernel and image. Stability of \(F\) gives \(p_I\leq p_F=p_E\text{,}\) a contradiction. Thus the kernel is zero, and \(p_I=p_E=p_F\text{.}\) Stability of \(F\) forces \(I=F\text{,}\) so \(f\) is an isomorphism.
🔗
In particular every nonzero endomorphism of \(E\) is invertible. The algebra \(\operatorname{End}(E)\) is finite-dimensional over \(\C\text{.}\) For any \(u\) in this algebra, its minimal polynomial has a root \(\lambda\in\C\text{;}\) then \(u-\lambda\operatorname{id}\) cannot be invertible. Hence it is zero, proving simplicity.
