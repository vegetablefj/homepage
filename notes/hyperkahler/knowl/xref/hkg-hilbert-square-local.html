Example 5.8.

Choose local coordinates \((z,w)\) on \(S\) with \(\sigma_S=dz\wedge dw\text{.}\) Near a pair of coincident points, write the two ordered points as \((a+u,b+v)\) and \((a-u,b-v)\text{.}\) Exchanging them sends \((u,v)\) to \((-u,-v)\text{,}\) and
\begin{equation*} dz_1\wedge dw_1+dz_2\wedge dw_2 =2\,da\wedge db+2\,du\wedge dv. \end{equation*}
The quotient in the difference coordinates has invariants \(x=u^2\text{,}\) \(y=v^2\text{,}\) \(z=uv\) satisfying \(xy=z^2\text{.}\) It is the ordinary double point \(\C^2/\{\pm1\}\text{.}\)
🔗
In one chart of its minimal resolution, let \(v=ut\) and \(r=u^2\text{.}\) The quotient coordinates are \((r,t)\text{,}\) and
\begin{equation*} 2\,du\wedge dv =2u\,du\wedge dt=dr\wedge dt. \end{equation*}
Thus the form becomes \(2\,da\wedge db+dr\wedge dt\text{,}\) which is regular and nondegenerate even along \(r=0\text{.}\) The second chart, obtained by exchanging \(u\) and \(v\text{,}\) gives the same form on the overlap because both are extensions of the form on the complement of the exceptional curve. Locally, the Hilbert–Chow resolution is this resolution times the centre coordinates \((a,b)\text{.}\) The calculation explains the symplectic extension at a double collision; collisions of greater length require the general theorem.
