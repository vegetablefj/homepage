Proposition 9.4.

If \(E\) is stable for a Bridgeland stability condition, then
\begin{equation*} \operatorname{Ext}^0(E,E)=\mathbb C,\qquad\operatorname{Ext}^2(E,E)=\mathbb C,\qquad\dim\operatorname{Ext}^1(E,E)=v(E)^2+2. \end{equation*}
Its other self-Ext groups vanish. In particular, \(v(E)^2\geq-2\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗

Proof.
A stable object is simple in its slicing category, so its endomorphisms form a finite-dimensional division algebra over \(\mathbb C\text{,}\) hence equal \(\mathbb C\text{.}\) Serre duality on a K3 surface identifies \(\operatorname{Ext}^2(E,E)\) with the dual of this space. If \(i<0\text{,}\) then \(E[i]\) has smaller phase than \(E\text{,}\) so \(\operatorname{Hom}(E,E[i])=0\text{.}\) Serre duality gives the corresponding vanishing for \(i>2\text{.}\) The Euler formula now gives \(-v(E)^2=2-\dim\operatorname{Ext}^1(E,E)\text{.}\)
