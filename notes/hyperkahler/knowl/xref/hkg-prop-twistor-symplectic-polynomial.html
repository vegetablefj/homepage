Proposition 3.1.

For finite \(\zeta\text{,}\) a holomorphic symplectic form on \((M,I_\zeta)\) is
\begin{equation} \sigma_\zeta=\sigma-2\zeta\omega_I-\zeta^2\bar\sigma.\tag{3.2} \end{equation}
Its coefficients as a differential form on the fixed smooth manifold are holomorphic in \(\zeta\text{.}\) On the chart \(w=1/\zeta\text{,}\) use
\begin{equation*} \widetilde\sigma_w=w^2\sigma_{1/w}=w^2\sigma-2w\omega_I-\bar\sigma. \end{equation*}
At \(w=0\) this is \(-\bar\sigma\text{,}\) a holomorphic symplectic form for \(-I\text{.}\)

🔗

Proof.
Let \(\zeta=x+iy\) and \(r=1+x^2+y^2\text{.}\) Define endomorphisms \(J_\zeta,K_\zeta\) by the same coefficient rows that express the real and imaginary parts of \(\sigma_\zeta/r\text{:}\)
\begin{equation*} \begin{pmatrix}I_\zeta\\J_\zeta\\K_\zeta\end{pmatrix}=\frac1r\begin{pmatrix} 1-x^2-y^2&2x&2y\\ -2x&1-x^2+y^2&-2xy\\ -2y&-2xy&1+x^2-y^2 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}I\\J\\K\end{pmatrix}. \end{equation*}
The row vectors have squared length \(r^2\) before division by \(r\text{,}\) and their pairwise dot products are zero. For example, the first and second have dot product
\begin{equation*} -2x(1-x^2-y^2)+2x(1-x^2+y^2)-4xy^2=0. \end{equation*}
The other two pairings follow by interchanging \(x,y\) or by the same expansion. The matrix is orthogonal, and its determinant is \(1\text{,}\) since the determinant is continuous and equals \(1\) at \(x=y=0\text{.}\) Thus it is a rotation. Formula (1.1) makes \((I_\zeta,J_\zeta,K_\zeta)\) a parallel orthogonal quaternionic triple.
🔗
Expanding the polynomial in (3.2) gives
\begin{equation*} \begin{aligned} \operatorname{Re}\sigma_\zeta&=-2x\omega_I+(1-x^2+y^2)\omega_J-2xy\omega_K,\\ \operatorname{Im}\sigma_\zeta&=-2y\omega_I-2xy\omega_J+(1+x^2-y^2)\omega_K. \end{aligned} \end{equation*}
Hence \(\sigma_\zeta=r(\omega_{J_\zeta}+i\omega_{K_\zeta})\text{.}\) The metric construction of Proposition 1.6 proves that it is holomorphic symplectic for \(I_\zeta\text{.}\) The second chart formula and its value at infinity follow by multiplying the polynomial by \(w^2\text{.}\)
