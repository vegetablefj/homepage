Proposition 2.14.

Let \(L\) be a line bundle with nonzero class \(\lambda=c_1(L)\in\overline{\mathcal K_X}\) and \(q_X(\lambda)=0\text{.}\) For every Kähler class \(\omega\text{,}\)
\begin{equation*} \int_X\lambda^k\omega^{2n-k}=0\quad(n<k\leq2n),\qquad \int_X\lambda^n\omega^n=\frac{2^nc_X}{\binom{2n}{n}}q_X(\lambda,\omega)^n>0. \end{equation*}
Thus its numerical dimension, defined by the largest \(k\) with \(\int_X\lambda^k\omega^{2n-k}>0\text{,}\) is \(n\text{.}\) Also \(\chi(X,L^{\otimes m})=n+1\) for every integer \(m\text{.}\)

🔗

Proof.
Because \(\lambda\) is a nonzero vector in \(\overline{\mathcal C_X}\text{,}\) Lemma 2.8 gives \(q_X(\lambda,\omega)>0\text{.}\) The Fujiki identity becomes
\begin{equation*} \int_X(\omega+t\lambda)^{2n}=c_X\bigl(q_X(\omega)+2tq_X(\lambda,\omega)\bigr)^n. \end{equation*}
The right side has degree \(n\) in \(t\text{.}\) Comparing coefficients yields both intersection assertions. The Euler-characteristic assertion follows by evaluating (2.6) at \(q_X(m\lambda)=0\text{.}\)
