Proposition 4.2.

If \(\mathcal A\) is abelian, then \(\Ch(\mathcal A)\) is abelian, with kernels and cokernels computed degreewise. Every short exact sequence of complexes \(0\to X\to Y\to Z\to0\) gives a natural long exact sequence
\begin{equation*} \cdots\to H^nX\to H^nY\to H^nZ\xrightarrow{\delta^n}H^{n+1}X\to\cdots. \end{equation*}
🔗
🔗

Proof.
Degreewise kernels and cokernels inherit differentials because the structure maps commute with differentials. The abelian category axioms follow degreewise. The snake lemma applied to cycles, boundaries, and the differentials gives the connecting maps and exactness. For modules, the connecting map has the following description: lift a cycle \(z\in Z^n\) to \(y\in Y^n\text{;}\) then \(dy\) is the image of a cycle \(x\in X^{n+1}\text{,}\) and \(\delta^n[z]=[x]\text{.}\) Changing either lift changes \(x\) by a boundary.
