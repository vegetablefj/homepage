Proposition 5.10.

Suppose that \(\mathcal A\) has enough injectives and \(F:\mathcal A\to\mathcal B\) is left exact.
  1. A short exact sequence \(0\to A'\to A\to A''\to0\) gives a natural long exact sequence starting with \(0\to F(A')\to F(A)\to F(A'')\to R^1F(A')\to\cdots\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  2. For injective \(I\text{,}\) one has \(R^nF(I)=0\) for \(n>0\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  3. If \(0\to A\to I\to C\to0\) is exact with \(I\) injective, then \(R^1F(A)\cong\coker(F(I)\to F(C))\) and \(R^{n+1}F(A)\cong R^nF(C)\) for \(n\geq1\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
There are dual statements for left derived functors of right exact functors.
🔗
🔗

Proof.
Use the injective version of Proposition 5.8. Its sequence of resolutions is split in every degree, so applying the additive functor \(F\) preserves its exactness. The cohomology sequence gives the first assertion. The resolution consisting of \(I\) in degree zero proves the second; the first two assertions give the third.
