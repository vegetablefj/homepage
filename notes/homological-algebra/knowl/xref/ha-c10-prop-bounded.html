Proposition 10.3.

A bounded below complex of injective objects is K-injective. A bounded above complex of projective objects is K-projective.
🔗
🔗

Proof.
For the first assertion let \(f:N\to I\) have acyclic source, and let \(I^n=0\) for \(n<a\text{.}\) Construct a homotopy \(h^n:N^n\to I^{n-1}\) with \(f^n=d_Ih^n+h^{n+1}d_N\text{.}\) Start with zero below \(a\text{.}\) If \(h^n\) is constructed, the map \(f^n-d_Ih^n\) kills \(\ker d_N^n=\im d_N^{n-1}\) by the preceding equation and the chain-map identity. It factors through \(\im d_N^n\subset N^{n+1}\text{.}\) Injectivity of \(I^n\) extends that factor to \(h^{n+1}\text{.}\) Induction gives a null-homotopy. Reverse arrows and degrees for the projective assertion.
