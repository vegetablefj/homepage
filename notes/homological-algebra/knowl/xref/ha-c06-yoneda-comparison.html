Proposition 6.4.

In an abelian category with enough projectives or enough injectives, equivalence classes of \(n\)-extensions form an abelian group under Baer sum, naturally isomorphic to \(\Ext^n(A,B)\text{.}\) The zero class in degree one consists of the split short exact sequences. Splicing gives an associative bilinear product, with degree-zero morphisms acting by pullback and pushout.
🔗
🔗

Proof.
For degree one, choose \(0\to K\to P^0\to A\to0\) with \(P^0\) projective. Pulling an extension back to \(P^0\) splits it, and a choice of splitting gives a map \(K\to B\text{.}\) A different splitting changes this map by the restriction of a map \(P^0\to B\text{.}\) Conversely, pushing out \(0\to K\to P^0\to A\to0\) along \(K\to B\) produces an extension. These constructions are inverse and identify the group with \(\coker(\Hom(P^0,B)\to\Hom(K,B))=\Ext^1(A,B)\text{.}\)
🔗
The higher comparison follows by cutting a resolution into short exact sequences and iterating this construction. The compatibility with Baer sum and splicing is the standard Yoneda comparison; see [Wei94, §3.4] and Stacks, Tag 06XP. The injective construction is dual.
