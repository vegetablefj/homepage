Theorem 7.4.

Every filtered complex in an abelian category has a natural spectral sequence with
\begin{equation*} E_0^{p,q}=\operatorname{gr}_F^pC^{p+q},\qquad E_1^{p,q}=H^{p+q}(\operatorname{gr}_F^pC). \end{equation*}
If the filtration is degreewise finite, then each bidegree stabilizes and there are natural isomorphisms \(E_\infty^{p,q}\cong\operatorname{gr}_F^pH^{p+q}(C)\text{.}\) In particular this is a convergent spectral sequence in the sense just defined.
🔗
🔗

Proof.
Here is a construction using only subobjects, finite sums, and quotients. Write \(n=p+q\) and, for \(r\geq0\text{,}\) put
\begin{equation*} Z_r^{p,q}=F^pC^n\cap d^{-1}(F^{p+r}C^{n+1}). \end{equation*}
Since the filtration is by subcomplexes, \(Z_0^{p,q}=F^pC^n\text{.}\)
 2 
Our \(Z_r^{p,q}\) is a subobject of \(C^{p+q}\text{.}\) Some sources use the same symbol for its image in the associated graded object. Their quotients describe the same pages, but the cycle and boundary formulas must be translated together.
For \(r\geq1\text{,}\) define
\begin{equation*} E_r^{p,q}=\frac{Z_r^{p,q}}{Z_{r-1}^{p+1,q-1}+dZ_{r-1}^{p-r+1,q+r-2}}. \end{equation*}
Both summands of the denominator are subobjects of the numerator. The differential of \(C\) induces \(d_r[x]=[dx]\text{,}\) with target bidegree \((p+r,q-r+1)\text{.}\) It is well-defined because changing \(x\) by the first denominator summand changes \(dx\) by a boundary in the target, and changing it by the second changes \(dx\) by zero. Its square is zero.
🔗
To identify the next page, suppose that \([x]\) is a \(d_r\)-cycle. The equation \(d_r[x]=0\) permits subtraction from \(x\) of an element of \(Z_{r-1}^{p+1,q-1}\) so that the resulting differential lies in \(F^{p+r+1}C^{n+1}\text{.}\) Thus the cycle class has a representative in \(Z_{r+1}^{p,q}\text{.}\) Dividing these representatives by the old identifications and the \(d_r\)-boundaries gives exactly
\begin{equation*} \frac{Z_{r+1}^{p,q}}{Z_r^{p+1,q-1}+dZ_r^{p-r,q+r-1}}=E_{r+1}^{p,q}. \end{equation*}
The same argument is an identity of kernels, images, and quotients in an abelian category; the representatives merely describe it for modules. At \(r=1\) the formula is the cohomology of the associated graded complex, as asserted. All constructions commute with filtration-preserving complex morphisms.
🔗
Now assume degreewise finiteness and fix \(p,q\text{.}\) For large \(r\text{,}\) the numerator becomes \(F^pC^n\cap\ker d\text{,}\) since \(F^{p+r}C^{n+1}=0\text{.}\) The first denominator summand becomes \(F^{p+1}C^n\cap\ker d\text{.}\) In the second summand, \(F^{p-r+1}C^{n-1}=C^{n-1}\) for large \(r\text{,}\) and its defining cycle condition is \(dz\in F^pC^n\text{.}\) Consequently the stable value is
\begin{equation*} \frac{F^pC^n\cap\ker d}{F^{p+1}C^n\cap\ker d+F^pC^n\cap\im d}=\operatorname{gr}_F^pH^n(C). \end{equation*}
The induced filtration on \(H^n(C)\) is finite, so these graded pieces account for the whole cohomology object. This proves convergence.
