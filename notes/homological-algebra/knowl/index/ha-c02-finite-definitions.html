Definition 2.6 Finite objects and local finiteness conditions.

Let \(\mathcal A\) be Grothendieck.
🔗
  1. An object \(X\) is finitely generated if, whenever it is the directed union of subobjects \(X_i\text{,}\) one of those subobjects is \(X\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  2. It is finitely presentable if \(\Hom(X,-)\) commutes with all small filtered colimits. Thus the canonical map \(\colim_i\Hom(X,Y_i)\to\Hom(X,\colim_iY_i)\) must be bijective for every filtered diagram.
    🔗
    🔗
  3. The category is locally finitely generated if it has a small generating family of finitely generated objects. It is locally finitely presentable if a small family of finitely presentable objects supplies every object as a filtered colimit. It is locally coherent if it is locally finitely presentable and its full subcategory of finitely presentable objects is abelian with exact inclusion. It is locally noetherian if it has a small generating family of noetherian objects.
