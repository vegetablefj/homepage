Definition 4.1 Complexes and their cohomology.

Let \(\mathcal A\) be an additive category.
  1. A cochain complex \(X\) consists of objects \(X^n\) and morphisms \(d_X^n:X^n\to X^{n+1}\text{,}\) indexed by \(n\in\Z\text{,}\) such that \(d_X^{n+1}d_X^n=0\text{.}\) A morphism \(f:X\to Y\) satisfies \(d_Y^nf^n=f^{n+1}d_X^n\text{.}\) These form the category \(\Ch(\mathcal A)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  2. Suppose that \(\mathcal A\) is abelian. Write \(Z^nX=\ker d_X^n\text{,}\) \(B^nX=\im d_X^{n-1}\text{,}\) and \(H^nX=Z^nX/B^nX\text{.}\) The complex is acyclic if every \(H^nX\) is zero. A morphism is a quasi-isomorphism if it induces an isomorphism on each cohomology object.
    🔗
    🔗
  3. The complex is bounded below if \(X^n=0\) for all sufficiently negative \(n\text{,}\) bounded above if \(X^n=0\) for all sufficiently positive \(n\text{,}\) and bounded if both hold. The corresponding full subcategories carry superscripts \(+\text{,}\) \(-\text{,}\) and \(b\text{.}\)
