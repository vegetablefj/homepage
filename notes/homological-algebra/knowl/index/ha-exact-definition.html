Definition A.1 Exact structures and admissible morphisms.

Let \(\mathcal E\) be additive. A kernel–cokernel pair is a pair \(A\xrightarrow{i}B\xrightarrow{p}C\) with \(i=\ker p\) and \(p=\coker i\text{,}\) in the sense of their universal properties. An exact structure is a class of these pairs, closed under isomorphism, satisfying:
  1. The identity of zero is admissible both as an epimorphism and as a monomorphism.
    🔗
    🔗
  2. Composites of admissible monomorphisms are admissible monomorphisms, and composites of admissible epimorphisms are admissible epimorphisms.
    🔗
    🔗
  3. The pullback of an admissible epimorphism along any morphism exists and is an admissible epimorphism.
    🔗
    🔗
  4. The pushout of an admissible monomorphism along any morphism exists and is an admissible monomorphism.
    🔗
    🔗
Here a morphism is an admissible monomorphism, or inflation, if it is the first map of a chosen pair, and an admissible epimorphism, or deflation, if it is the second. A chosen pair, written \(0\to A\to B\to C\to0\text{,}\) is a conflation. The category together with this structure is an exact category.
