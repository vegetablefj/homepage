    Chapter 7 Filtered complexes and spectral sequences

    A filtration separates cohomology into successive approximation problems. The associated spectral sequence records which approximate cycles extend further through the filtration and which become boundaries. Construction and convergence are different questions: the pages can exist without determining the cohomology of the total complex. We prove convergence for degreewise finite filtrations and use that case for double complexes. Main references are [LiII, Chapter 5] and [Wei94, Chapter 5].

    Section 7.1 Filtrations and the abutment

    Definition 7.1 Filtered complexes.

    Let \(\mathcal A\) be abelian. A decreasing filtration on a complex \(C\) consists of subcomplexes \(F^pC\text{,}\) indexed by \(p\in\Z\text{,}\) with \(F^{p+1}C\subseteq F^pC\text{.}\) Its associated graded complexes are \(\operatorname{gr}_F^pC=F^pC/F^{p+1}C\text{.}\) The induced filtration on cohomology is
    \begin{equation*} F^pH^n(C)=\im\bigl(H^n(F^pC)\to H^n(C)\bigr). \end{equation*}
    We call the filtration degreewise finite if for each \(n\) there are integers \(a(n)<b(n)\) with \(F^{a(n)}C^n=C^n\) and \(F^{b(n)}C^n=0\text{.}\)
    The bounds may depend on the degree. There need not be one finite interval working for the whole complex. In modules, an arbitrary decreasing filtration is called exhaustive if \(\bigcup_pF^pC^n=C^n\text{,}\) separated if \(\bigcap_pF^pC^n=0\text{,}\) and complete if \(C^n\to\lim_pC^n/F^pC^n\) is an isomorphism. Degreewise finiteness implies all three conditions. These conditions alone should not be substituted for a convergence theorem.

    Definition 7.2 Filtered morphisms and strictness.

    A filtered morphism \(f:(C,F)\to(C',F)\) is a complex morphism satisfying \(f(F^pC^n)\subseteq F^pC'^n\text{.}\) It is strict if \(f(F^pC^n)=\im(f^n)\cap F^pC'^n\) for all \(p,n\text{.}\) A short exact sequence of filtered complexes is strict exact if its underlying sequence is exact and both maps are strict. A filtered quasi-isomorphism is a filtered morphism inducing quasi-isomorphisms on all associated graded complexes.
    Strict exactness gives short exact sequences on associated graded complexes: use the induced filtration on the subcomplex and the quotient filtration on the quotient complex. An exact sequence of underlying complexes with unrelated filtrations need not have this property. For degreewise finite filtrations, a filtered quasi-isomorphism is an ordinary quasi-isomorphism by Corollary 7.5. The converse fails: the identity of \(k[0]\text{,}\) from the filtration with \(F^0=k,F^1=0\) to the filtration with \(F^0=F^1=k,F^2=0\text{,}\) is a filtered underlying isomorphism but is not an isomorphism on associated graded objects.

    Definition 7.3 Cohomological spectral sequences.

    A cohomological spectral sequence consists of bigraded objects \(E_r^{p,q}\text{,}\) for \(r\geq r_0\text{,}\) and differentials
    \begin{equation*} d_r:E_r^{p,q}\to E_r^{p+r,q-r+1} \end{equation*}
    with \(d_r^2=0\text{,}\) together with specified isomorphisms \(E_{r+1}^{p,q}\cong H^{p,q}(E_r,d_r)\text{.}\) It degenerates at \(E_r\) if all differentials from that page onward are zero. In the finite situations below, \(E_r^{p,q}\Rightarrow H^{p+q}\) means that \(H^n\) has a finite filtration with \(\operatorname{gr}_F^pH^n\cong E_\infty^{p,n-p}\text{,}\) where \(E_\infty^{p,q}\) is the eventually stable page in that bidegree.
    Outside finite filtrations we will specify the convergence statement, rather than use the arrow alone. “Weak convergence,” “abutment,” and “strong convergence” do not have uniform meanings across the literature; see Stacks, Tag 012K.1

    Section 7.2 Construction from a filtered complex

    Theorem 7.4.

    Every filtered complex in an abelian category has a natural spectral sequence with
    \begin{equation*} E_0^{p,q}=\operatorname{gr}_F^pC^{p+q},\qquad E_1^{p,q}=H^{p+q}(\operatorname{gr}_F^pC). \end{equation*}
    If the filtration is degreewise finite, then each bidegree stabilizes and there are natural isomorphisms \(E_\infty^{p,q}\cong\operatorname{gr}_F^pH^{p+q}(C)\text{.}\) In particular this is a convergent spectral sequence in the sense just defined.

    Proof.
    Here is a construction using only subobjects, finite sums, and quotients. Write \(n=p+q\) and, for \(r\geq0\text{,}\) put
    \begin{equation*} Z_r^{p,q}=F^pC^n\cap d^{-1}(F^{p+r}C^{n+1}). \end{equation*}
    Since the filtration is by subcomplexes, \(Z_0^{p,q}=F^pC^n\text{.}\)2 For \(r\geq1\text{,}\) define
    \begin{equation*} E_r^{p,q}=\frac{Z_r^{p,q}}{Z_{r-1}^{p+1,q-1}+dZ_{r-1}^{p-r+1,q+r-2}}. \end{equation*}
    Both summands of the denominator are subobjects of the numerator. The differential of \(C\) induces \(d_r[x]=[dx]\text{,}\) with target bidegree \((p+r,q-r+1)\text{.}\) It is well-defined because changing \(x\) by the first denominator summand changes \(dx\) by a boundary in the target, and changing it by the second changes \(dx\) by zero. Its square is zero.
    To identify the next page, suppose that \([x]\) is a \(d_r\)-cycle. The equation \(d_r[x]=0\) permits subtraction from \(x\) of an element of \(Z_{r-1}^{p+1,q-1}\) so that the resulting differential lies in \(F^{p+r+1}C^{n+1}\text{.}\) Thus the cycle class has a representative in \(Z_{r+1}^{p,q}\text{.}\) Dividing these representatives by the old identifications and the \(d_r\)-boundaries gives exactly
    \begin{equation*} \frac{Z_{r+1}^{p,q}}{Z_r^{p+1,q-1}+dZ_r^{p-r,q+r-1}}=E_{r+1}^{p,q}. \end{equation*}
    The same argument is an identity of kernels, images, and quotients in an abelian category; the representatives merely describe it for modules. At \(r=1\) the formula is the cohomology of the associated graded complex, as asserted. All constructions commute with filtration-preserving complex morphisms.
    Now assume degreewise finiteness and fix \(p,q\text{.}\) For large \(r\text{,}\) the numerator becomes \(F^pC^n\cap\ker d\text{,}\) since \(F^{p+r}C^{n+1}=0\text{.}\) The first denominator summand becomes \(F^{p+1}C^n\cap\ker d\text{.}\) In the second summand, \(F^{p-r+1}C^{n-1}=C^{n-1}\) for large \(r\text{,}\) and its defining cycle condition is \(dz\in F^pC^n\text{.}\) Consequently the stable value is
    \begin{equation*} \frac{F^pC^n\cap\ker d}{F^{p+1}C^n\cap\ker d+F^pC^n\cap\im d}=\operatorname{gr}_F^pH^n(C). \end{equation*}
    The induced filtration on \(H^n(C)\) is finite, so these graded pieces account for the whole cohomology object. This proves convergence.
    A class on page \(r\) can therefore be represented by \(x\in F^pC^{p+q}\) whose differential has moved at least \(r\) steps into the filtration. The next differential is that remaining obstruction. This description also computes higher differentials in examples: one corrects a representative by terms of deeper filtration until the first obstruction that cannot be removed.
    The direct construction makes higher differentials explicit. The exact-couple construction below instead organizes the long exact sequences already present in the filtration. Both descriptions will be used: cycle representatives are convenient for calculation, while exact couples apply to towers of distinguished triangles.

    Corollary 7.5.

    Let \(f:(C,F)\to(C',F)\) preserve filtrations which are degreewise finite. If its map on one page \(E_r^{p,q}\) is an isomorphism for every bidegree, then \(f\) is a quasi-isomorphism.

    Proof.
    Passing to cohomology of pages preserves the isomorphisms. The theorem identifies the stable maps with the associated graded maps on cohomology. A morphism between finite filtered objects which is an isomorphism on associated graded objects is an isomorphism, by induction on the filtration length and the short five lemma.

    Section 7.3 Exact couples

    Definition 7.6 Exact and derived couples.

    An exact couple in an abelian category consists of objects \(D,E\) and maps \(i:D\to D\text{,}\) \(j:D\to E\text{,}\) and \(k:E\to D\) such that the sequence around the triangle is exact at all three objects. Then \(d=jk\) satisfies \(d^2=0\text{.}\) Its derived couple has objects \(D'=\im i\) and \(E'=H(E,d)\text{,}\) with maps described by
    \begin{equation*} i'(ix)=i^2x,\qquad j'(ix)=[jx],\qquad k'([e])=ke. \end{equation*}
    In the last expression \(e\) is a \(d\)-cycle, so \(ke\in\ker j=\im i\text{.}\) For graded couples these maps retain their specified degrees.

    Proposition 7.7.

    The derived-couple maps are well-defined and form an exact couple. Iterating gives a spectral sequence.

    Proof.
    Changing \(x\) with \(ix\) fixed changes \(jx\) by \(jky=dy\text{,}\) so \(j'\) is well-defined. Changing \(e\) by a boundary does not change \(ke\text{,}\) since \(kj=0\text{.}\) At \(D'\text{,}\) if \(j'(ix)=0\text{,}\) write \(jx=jke\text{;}\) then \(x-ke\in\ker j=\im i\text{,}\) so \(ix\in\im i'\text{.}\) If \(i'(ix)=0\text{,}\) then \(ix=ke\) for some \(e\text{,}\) and \(de=jix=0\text{;}\) hence \(ix\in\im k'\text{.}\) Finally, if \(k'[e]=0\text{,}\) then \(e=jx\) for some \(x\text{,}\) so \([e]=j'(ix)\text{.}\) The reverse inclusions follow from the zero consecutive composites. Repetition replaces \(E\) by its differential cohomology at every step.
    These formulas are written with elements to display the mechanism; the same kernel and image identities define the construction in an abelian category. Applied to a filtered complex, use the families
    \begin{equation*} D^{p,n}=H^n(F^pC),\qquad E^{p,n}=H^n(\operatorname{gr}_F^pC). \end{equation*}
    Inclusion gives \(i:D^{p+1,n}\to D^{p,n}\text{,}\) the quotient gives \(j:D^{p,n}\to E^{p,n}\text{,}\) and the connecting map gives \(k:E^{p,n}\to D^{p+1,n+1}\text{.}\) These are families of objects; no infinite direct sum in the abelian category is required. Setting \(q=n-p\) gives the first page and differential of Theorem 7.4. Tracking these degrees through derived couples gives \(d_r\) of bidegree \((r,1-r)\text{.}\) See [LiII, §§5.2–5.5].
    A tower of distinguished triangles has the same construction after applying a cohomological functor. This is why spectral sequences can be obtained from truncation towers without first choosing a double complex. The exact couple constructs the pages; convergence still depends on how the tower relates to the proposed abutment.

    Section 7.4 Convergence beyond finite filtrations

    For this paragraph work with modules, so all the following intersections and unions exist. For \(r\geq1\text{,}\) let \(\overline Z_r^{p,q}\) be the image of \(Z_r^{p,q}\) in \(\operatorname{gr}_F^pC^{p+q}\text{,}\) and let \(\overline B_r^{p,q}\) be the image there of \(F^pC^{p+q}\cap d(F^{p-r+1}C^{p+q-1})\text{.}\) Then
    \begin{equation*} E_r^{p,q}=\overline Z_r^{p,q}/\overline B_r^{p,q},\qquad E_\infty^{p,q}:=\frac{\bigcap_r\overline Z_r^{p,q}}{\bigcup_r\overline B_r^{p,q}}. \end{equation*}
    The cycles decrease and boundaries increase inside the fixed associated graded object. This defines a limiting term even when there is no eventually constant page. It does not itself identify that term with graded cohomology.

    Proposition 7.8.

    Suppose a filtered complex of modules is exhaustive in every degree and satisfies \(F^pC^n=0\) for all sufficiently large \(p\text{,}\) with the bound allowed to depend on \(n\text{.}\) Then \(E_\infty^{p,q}\cong\operatorname{gr}_F^pH^{p+q}(C)\text{.}\) The filtration on each \(H^n(C)\) is exhaustive, separated, and complete, although it need not be finite.

    Proof.
    For fixed \(p,n\text{,}\) the numerator cycles stabilize to the image of \(F^pC^n\cap\ker d\text{,}\) because \(F^{p+r}C^{n+1}=0\) for large \(r\text{.}\) Every boundary in \(F^pC^n\) has a preimage belonging to some \(F^aC^{n-1}\) by exhaustiveness. Thus the union of the displayed boundaries is the image of \(F^pC^n\cap\im d\text{.}\) Their quotient is the asserted graded cohomology. Every cohomology class has a cycle representative in some filtration step, giving exhaustiveness. For large \(p\) the filtration step in degree \(n\) is zero, so the cohomology filtration is separated and complete.
    This is a common convergence version of [LiII, §5.5]. Degreewise finite filtrations imply its hypotheses, but its boundary subobjects need not stabilize at a common finite page. This is one reason to distinguish a limiting term from an eventually stable one.

    Proposition 7.9.

    For a filtered complex in an abelian category, suppose that for each \(n\) there are bounds such that \(H^n(F^pC)=0\) for all sufficiently large \(p\text{,}\) and \(H^n(F^pC)\to H^n(C)\) is an isomorphism for all sufficiently small \(p\text{.}\) Then its spectral sequence has a finite filtration on the abutment and converges to \(H^*(C)\text{.}\)
    This cohomological criterion is Stacks, Tag 0BK5. The exact sequence of consecutive filtration steps bounds the nonzero first-page terms on each total diagonal. The eventual vanishing and eventual isomorphism in the statement supply the cycle and boundary limits needed for convergence. The hypotheses concern the complexes \(F^pC\text{,}\) so they can hold even when the filtrations of their individual terms are not finite. A finite support pattern on a displayed page, without these links to the abutment, is not a replacement for this criterion.

    Section 7.5 The spectral sequences of a double complex

    Proposition 7.10.

    Let \(C^{p,q}\) be a double complex with commuting differentials. Suppose that on each diagonal \(p+q=n\) only finitely many entries are nonzero. Its total complex \(T\) has two convergent spectral sequences. Filtering by columns gives
    \begin{equation*} E_1^{p,q}=H_v^q(C^{p,\bullet}),\qquad E_2^{p,q}=H_h^p(H_v^q(C))\Rightarrow H^{p+q}(T). \end{equation*}
    Filtering by rows gives the analogous sequence with horizontal cohomology taken first. The usual sign adjustment identifies its first-page differentials with the vertical maps. If all columns are acyclic, or all rows are acyclic, then \(T\) is acyclic.

    Proof.
    Give \(T^n\) the column filtration \(F^aT^n=\bigoplus_{p+q=n,\ p\geq a}C^{p,q}\text{.}\) It is a subcomplex filtration because the horizontal differential raises the column and the vertical differential retains it. Its associated graded differential in column \(p\) is \((-1)^p d_v\text{,}\) so its cohomology is vertical cohomology. The next differential is induced by \(d_h\text{.}\) Finiteness on each diagonal makes the filtration degreewise finite. Apply Theorem 7.4. Interchanging the two indices gives the second sequence.
    This proves the totalization comparisons used for Ext and Tor: resolve each variable, augment the double complex in one direction, and apply the proposition to the cone of the augmentation. First-quadrant and third-quadrant double complexes have finite diagonals. A one-sided bound on only one coordinate need not make the diagonals finite.

    Example 7.11.

    The second differential can be computed by two steps in the double complex. Let \(x\in C^{p,q}\) be a vertical cycle representing an \(E_2\)-class. Horizontal closedness on \(E_1\) says that \(d_hx=d_vy\) for some \(y\in C^{p+1,q-1}\text{.}\) The element \(x+(-1)^py\) in the total complex has differential \((-1)^pd_hy\text{:}\) the terms in column \(p+1\) cancel. Therefore
    \begin{equation*} d_2[x]=[(-1)^pd_hy]\in E_2^{p+2,q-1}. \end{equation*}
    Commutativity of the two differentials implies that \(d_hy\) is a vertical cycle. Changing \(y\) or the representative \(x\) changes this result by the relations defining \(E_2\text{,}\) as follows from the filtered-complex construction. Thus the zigzag computation uses a choice of intermediate lift, but the resulting page differential is independent of that choice. Its sign depends on the chosen totalization convention.

    Example 7.12.

    A first-quadrant example already has a nonzero second differential. Over a field, put basis elements \(x,y,z,w\) in bidegrees \((0,1),(1,0),(1,1),(2,0)\text{.}\) Set \(d_hx=z\text{,}\) \(d_vy=z\text{,}\) and \(d_hy=w\text{,}\) and set all other differentials to zero. The commuting-square condition holds. Vertical cohomology leaves only \([x]\) and \([w]\text{,}\) and the first differential is zero. But \(d_2[x]=[w]\text{,}\) by the preceding calculation, so the third page is zero. The total complex is indeed acyclic: its differential sends \(x\) to \(z\) and \(y\) to \(-z+w\text{,}\) an invertible map between its two nonzero terms. The second page can therefore contain nonzero classes even when total cohomology is zero.

    Example 7.13.

    Let \(k\) be a field. Put \(C^{p,-p}=k\) and \(C^{p,-p-1}=k\) for every integer \(p\text{.}\) The vertical and horizontal maps out of \(C^{p,-p-1}\) to the two adjacent entries on the other diagonal are identities; all remaining maps are zero. The squares commute, and every row and every column is acyclic.
    The sum total has only two nonzero degrees. Its differential sends the basis vector indexed by \(p\) in degree \(-1\) to \(e_{p+1}+(-1)^pe_p\) in degree zero. This map on \(\bigoplus_{p\in\Z}k\) is injective, by looking at the smallest index in a nonzero finite support. Its cokernel is one-dimensional, since the relations express every basis vector as a nonzero scalar multiple of \(e_0\text{.}\) Thus \(H^0\Tot_\oplus(C)\cong k\text{,}\) despite both first pages being zero. The column filtration is not degreewise finite, so the proposition does not apply.3

    Section 7.6 Edges, extensions, and products

    In a first-quadrant spectral sequence, the filtration of \(H^n(T)\) runs from \(F^0H^n=H^n\) to \(F^{n+1}H^n=0\text{.}\) On the bottom row no differential leaves \(E_r^{n,0}\) for \(r\geq2\text{,}\) so \(E_2^{n,0}\) surjects onto \(E_\infty^{n,0}=F^nH^n\text{.}\) On the left edge no differential enters \(E_r^{0,n}\text{,}\) so \(E_\infty^{0,n}\) embeds in \(E_2^{0,n}\text{.}\) These maps give the edge morphisms
    \begin{equation*} E_2^{n,0}\longrightarrow H^n(T),\qquad H^n(T)\longrightarrow E_2^{0,n}. \end{equation*}
    They are respectively an inclusion after quotienting incoming boundaries and a quotient followed by inclusion. Neither edge map is generally an isomorphism.
    For a first-quadrant cohomological spectral sequence obtained above, the edge maps come from the extreme filtration pieces. Its first low-degree terms give the exact sequence
    \begin{equation*} 0\to E_2^{1,0}\to H^1(T)\to E_2^{0,1}\xrightarrow{d_2}E_2^{2,0}\to H^2(T). \end{equation*}
    Indeed \(E_\infty^{1,0}=E_2^{1,0}\text{,}\) \(E_\infty^{0,1}=\ker d_2^{0,1}\text{,}\) and \(E_\infty^{2,0}=\coker d_2^{0,1}\text{.}\) The last term embeds in \(H^2(T)\) as its filtration-two piece. The first-quadrant condition rules out all other differentials affecting these identifications.
    If vertical cohomology of a first-quadrant double complex is concentrated in row zero, the column spectral sequence has just that row on its first page and degenerates on its second page. Its edge map identifies \(H^n(T)\) with \(H_h^n(H_v^0(C))\text{.}\) If vertical cohomology vanishes altogether, total cohomology vanishes. Thus the familiar single-resolution computations are collapse cases with a specified edge isomorphism, rather than just dimension counts.
    Degeneration determines associated graded objects, not a splitting of the resulting filtration. A complex concentrated in degree zero with value \(\Z/4\) and filtration \(F^0=\Z/4\text{,}\) \(F^1=2\Z/4\text{,}\) \(F^2=0\) has the same graded pieces and zero spectral-sequence differentials as \((\Z/2)\oplus(\Z/2)\) filtered by one summand. Their abutments are not isomorphic. Even when all filtration extensions split, a canonical splitting requires further data.

    Proposition 7.14.

    Let \(C,D,E\) be filtered complexes of modules over a commutative ring, and let \(\mu:C\otimes D\to E\) be a complex morphism satisfying \(\mu(F^pC\otimes F^aD)\subseteq F^{p+a}E\text{.}\) It induces pairings
    \begin{equation*} E_r^{p,q}(C)\otimes E_r^{a,b}(D)\longrightarrow E_r^{p+a,q+b}(E) \end{equation*}
    satisfying \(d_r\mu(x,y)=\mu(d_rx,y)+(-1)^{p+q}\mu(x,d_ry)\text{.}\) Passing from one page to the next takes the induced cohomology pairing. In the convergent cases established above, the limiting pairing is the associated graded pairing on cohomology.

    Proof.
    If \(x,y\) represent page-\(r\) cycles, their differentials lie \(r\) steps deeper in their filtrations. The tensor differential shows that \(\mu(x,y)\) satisfies the same condition in filtration \(p+a\text{.}\) Changing either representative by a deeper cycle or an allowed boundary changes the product by the corresponding denominator in the target page. The Leibniz rule on the complexes therefore descends with the displayed total-degree sign. The construction of the next page is by cohomology, which gives its compatibility. Actual cycles and boundaries give the assertion on the abutment.
    A filtered differential graded algebra is the case \(C=D=E\text{.}\) Associativity descends to all pages. For the unital assertion, require also that the unit lie in \(F^0C^0\text{,}\) so that the unit map from the coefficient ring with its standard filtration preserves filtration; it then supplies the unit on each page. Graded commutativity on the complex gives graded commutativity on the pages with respect to total degree \(p+q\text{.}\) Without that hypothesis, a multiplicative spectral sequence need not be commutative. Nor does the existence of the displayed pairings assert a Künneth isomorphism for tensor products of page terms.4
    For a finite spectral sequence of finite-dimensional vector spaces, taking cohomology preserves the alternating sum in total degree. Hence
    \begin{equation*} \sum_n(-1)^n\dim H^n(C)=\sum_{p,q}(-1)^{p+q}\dim E_r^{p,q} \end{equation*}
    for every page, provided only finitely many terms under discussion are nonzero. This follows by pairing each boundary with its source contribution on a page, then using the finite filtration on the abutment. Without a finiteness or summability condition, the displayed alternating sums need not be defined.
    Grothendieck’s spectral sequence for a composite of left exact functors also requires an acyclicity hypothesis connecting the two functors. We postpone its statement to Chapter 11, where the derived comparison for a composite is available; a spectral sequence cannot replace a missing hypothesis on that comparison.
