Proposition 5.10.

Transport is an isomorphism and satisfies
\begin{equation*} T_{\text{constant path}}=\id,\qquad T_{\overline\gamma}=T_\gamma^{-1},\qquad T_{\delta\gamma}=T_\delta T_\gamma. \end{equation*}
Here \(\delta\gamma\) means first traverse \(\gamma\text{,}\) then \(\delta\text{.}\) Transport depends only on the homotopy class relative to endpoints. A morphism of local systems commutes with every transport map.
🔗
🔗

Proof.
The first three assertions follow by reversing or adjoining subdivisions. For homotopy invariance, pull back trivializing opens to a homotopy square. By compactness, subdivide the square into rectangles whose images lie in trivializing opens. Transport around each rectangle is the identity. Cancelling the interior edges identifies transport along the two opposite boundary paths; the other boundary paths are constant when endpoints are fixed. Compatibility with a sheaf morphism follows on each subinterval from the description of maps between constant sheaves on a connected interval.
