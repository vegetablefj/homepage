Proposition 7.20.

The derived tensor product is associative and symmetric, with unit \(\Lambda_X\) and symmetry sign \((-1)^{pq}\) on degrees \(p,q\text{.}\) There is a natural adjunction
\begin{equation*} \Hom_{\D(X;\Lambda)}(A\Ltensor_\Lambda K,L)\simeq\Hom_{\D(X;\Lambda)}(A,\RsheafHom_\Lambda(K,L)). \end{equation*}
Restriction to an open set commutes with both bifunctors. Inverse image commutes with derived tensor products.
🔗
🔗

Proof.
K-flatness makes tensoring preserve quasi-isomorphisms, by applying tensor to their acyclic cones. For internal Hom, restriction of a K-injective complex is K-injective. Thus, if \(K'\to K\) is a quasi-isomorphism, the complex \(\Gamma(U,\sheafHom^\bullet_\Lambda(\Cone(K'\to K),I))\) is acyclic for every open \(U\text{.}\) It follows that internal Hom takes the quasi-isomorphism to a quasi-isomorphism. A quasi-isomorphism between K-injective targets is a homotopy equivalence, which proves independence in the second variable.
🔗
Choose \(P\to K\) K-flat and \(L\to I\) K-injective. The complex \(\sheafHom^\bullet_\Lambda(P,I)\) is K-injective: a map into it from an acyclic \(B\) corresponds, by the tensor–Hom adjunction on complexes, to a map \(B\otimes_\Lambda P\to I\text{,}\) which is null-homotopic. Consequently the same adjunction on complexes computes both sides of the derived adjunction. Associativity and symmetry follow by choosing K-flat resolutions of the factors; tensor products of K-flat complexes are K-flat. Restriction preserves the chosen types of resolutions, and inverse image preserves K-flat complexes and ordinary tensor products.
