Example 4.9.

Inverse image need not preserve infinite products. Let \(X=\{0\}\cup\{1/n:n\geq1\}\) and let \(F=\prod_{r\geq1}\Bbbk_X\text{.}\) A section of \(F\) is a family of locally constant scalar functions. Let its \(r\)-th component be the characteristic function of \(\{1/r\}\text{.}\) This singleton is open and closed in \(X\text{,}\) so the function is locally constant. Each component has zero germ at \(0\text{,}\) but the entire section has a nonzero germ there: every neighbourhood contains some \(1/r\text{,}\) where one component is nonzero. Hence
\begin{equation*} F_0\longrightarrow\prod_{r\geq1}(\Bbbk_X)_0 \end{equation*}
has a nonzero kernel. Since the stalk functor is inverse image by the point map, finite-limit preservation cannot be strengthened to arbitrary-limit preservation.
