Lemma 11.5.

Suppose that every open subspace of \(X\) is paracompact Hausdorff. Then for every open \(U\subset X\) and every \(n\geq0\text{,}\) the natural map
\begin{equation*} C^n_{\mathrm{sing}}(U;L|_U)\longrightarrow\Gamma(U,\mathcal S^n(L)) \end{equation*}
is surjective. Each sheaf \(\mathcal S^n(L)\) is flabby.
🔗
🔗

Proof.
Let \(s\in\Gamma(U,\mathcal S^n(L))\text{.}\) Choose open sets \(U_i\) covering \(U\) and cochains \(c_i\) on \(U_i\) representing \(s\text{.}\) The shrinking property of paracompact Hausdorff spaces gives a locally finite open refinement \((V_j)\) and indices \(i(j)\) with \(\overline{V_j}\subset U_{i(j)}\text{,}\) where closure is taken in \(U\text{.}\) This is the point-set topology fact used in the argument.
🔗
Define a cochain \(c\) on \(U\) as follows. For a simplex whose image lies in some \(V_j\text{,}\) choose one such \(j\) and take its value from \(c_{i(j)}\text{.}\) For every other simplex take zero. We show that the germ of \(c\) represents \(s\) at every \(x\in U\text{.}\)
🔗
Choose a neighborhood of \(x\) meeting only finitely many \(V_j\text{.}\) After shrinking it, every remaining such \(j\) satisfies \(x\in\overline{V_j}\text{:}\) the other finitely many closures can be avoided. Hence all the remaining \(U_{i(j)}\) contain \(x\text{.}\) Choose one index \(j_0\) with \(x\in V_{j_0}\text{.}\) The cochains \(c_{i(j)}\) and \(c_{i(j_0)}\) have the same sheaf germ at \(x\text{,}\) so on a sufficiently small neighborhood they agree on all simplices. Only finitely many agreements are needed. Choose one neighborhood \(W\subset V_{j_0}\) on which all of them hold. Every simplex in \(W\) is eligible for at least one \(V_j\text{,}\) and whichever eligible index was chosen, its value equals the value from \(c_{i(j_0)}\text{.}\) Thus \(c\) has the required germ at \(x\text{.}\)
🔗
This proves surjectivity. A section of \(\mathcal S^n(L)\) on an open set can now be represented by one cochain there, and that cochain can be extended by zero to the ambient open set. Sheafifying gives an extension of the original section. Therefore \(\mathcal S^n(L)\) is flabby.
