Theorem 10.6.

For any Abelian sheaf \(F\) on any topological space \(X\text{,}\) there is a natural bijection
\begin{equation*} \{\text{isomorphism classes of }F\text{-torsors}\}\xrightarrow{\sim}H^1(X,F). \end{equation*}
The trivial torsor corresponds to zero. If an extension of \(\Z_X\) by \(F\) represents a class \(\xi\text{,}\) its fiber over the global section \(1\in\Z_X(X)\) is a torsor representing \(\xi\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗

Proof.
For an extension \(E\text{,}\) let \(P=q^{-1}(1)\text{,}\) taking the fiber in sheaves of sets. Since \(q\) is an epimorphism, \(1\) has local lifts. Their differences are unique sections of \(F\text{,}\) so \(P\) is a torsor. This construction respects equivalences of extensions.
🔗
Conversely, choose local sections \(p_i\) of a torsor \(P\) and write \(p_j=p_i+c_{ij}\text{.}\) On \(U_i\text{,}\) take \(E_i=F|_{U_i}\oplus\Z_{U_i}\text{.}\) Glue the copy on \(U_j\) to the copy on \(U_i\) by
\begin{equation*} (a,n)\longmapsto(a+n c_{ij},n). \end{equation*}
The cocycle relation is exactly the compatibility required for these isomorphisms on triple intersections. Their first component fixes \(F\text{,}\) and their second component fixes \(\Z\text{.}\) They therefore glue to an extension of \(\Z_X\) by \(F\text{.}\) Its fiber over \(1\) has the same transitions as \(P\text{,}\) and is isomorphic to \(P\text{.}\)
🔗
Changing the sections to \(p_i+b_i\) gives an equivalent extension, using the local coordinate change \((a,n)\mapsto(a+n b_i,n)\) in the old coordinates. Any two trivializing coverings have a common refinement, so the extension class is independent of the covering. Starting instead with an extension, choose lifts \(e_i\) of \(1\text{.}\) The maps \((a,n)\mapsto a+n e_i\) identify it with the extension just constructed from its fiber torsor. Thus the two constructions are inverse on isomorphism classes.
🔗
The connecting homomorphism for an extension sends \(1\) to its Yoneda class. Its local lifts are the sections of the fiber torsor, whose differences are \(c_{ij}\text{.}\) The bijection therefore agrees with the Čech-to-derived comparison and with Proposition 10.1. Pullback and morphisms of Abelian coefficient sheaves preserve these descriptions, proving naturality.
