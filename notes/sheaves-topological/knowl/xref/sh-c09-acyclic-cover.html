Theorem 9.10.

If \(\mathcal U\) is acyclic for \(F\text{,}\) the comparison maps
\begin{equation*} \check H^n(\mathcal U,F)\xrightarrow{\sim}H^n(X,F) \end{equation*}
are isomorphisms for all \(n\text{.}\) More generally, if \(K\) is bounded below and every term \(K^a\) is acyclic for sections on every finite intersection, then
\begin{equation*} \Tot\check C^\bullet(\mathcal U,K^\bullet)\longrightarrow R\Gamma(X,K) \end{equation*}
is a quasi-isomorphism.
🔗
🔗

Proof.
For a sheaf, the positive rows of the \(E_1\) page in Theorem 9.7 vanish. The remaining row is \(\check C^\bullet(\mathcal U,F)\text{,}\) which proves the assertion. For a complex, apply a functorial Godement resolution termwise. Compare the total complex of the resulting Čech double complexes with the Čech complex of \(K\text{.}\) On each intersection, the acyclic resolution theorem identifies the section complex of \(K\) with its derived sections. Taking the Čech products preserves quasi-isomorphisms of these module complexes. The remaining totalizations converge because the Čech and resolution degrees are nonnegative and \(K\) is bounded below. This proves the comparison. The sheaf version is also Stacks, Lemma 20.11.6.
