Proposition 8.6.

For \(K\in\D(X;\Lambda)\text{,}\) the sheaf \(\mathcal H^q(Rf_*K)\) is the sheaf associated to the presheaf
\begin{equation*} V\longmapsto H^q(f^{-1}V,K),\qquad V\subseteq Y\text{ open}. \end{equation*}
Consequently, for \(y\in Y\text{,}\)
\begin{equation*} \mathcal H^q(Rf_*K)_y=\colim_{V\ni y}H^q(f^{-1}V,K). \end{equation*}
In particular, for a sheaf \(F\) and \(q>0\text{,}\) the presheaf \(U\mapsto H^q(U,F)\) has zero associated sheaf.
🔗
🔗

Proof.
Take a K-injective resolution \(K\to I\text{.}\) The complex of sections of \(f_*I\) on \(V\) is \(\Gamma(f^{-1}V,I)\text{,}\) which computes the stated hypercohomology because restriction preserves K-injectives. Cohomology of a complex of sheaves is the sheafification of its cohomology presheaf. Taking stalks gives the formula, since filtered colimits of modules are exact. For \(f=\id_X\) and a sheaf \(F\text{,}\) the positive cohomology sheaves are zero.
