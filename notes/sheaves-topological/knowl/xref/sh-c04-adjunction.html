Theorem 4.3.

The constructions above give functors
\begin{equation*} f^{-1}:\Sh(Y)\rightleftarrows\Sh(X):f_* \end{equation*}
and a natural bijection
\begin{equation*} \Hom_X(f^{-1}G,F)\simeq\Hom_Y(G,f_*F). \end{equation*}
The same assertion holds for sheaves of \(\Lambda\)-modules, with linear morphisms.
🔗
🔗

Proof.
A morphism \(a:G\to f_*F\) gives, whenever \(f(U)\subset V\text{,}\) a composite
\begin{equation*} G(V)\xrightarrow{a_V}F(f^{-1}V)\longrightarrow F(U). \end{equation*}
These maps are compatible with shrinking \(V\) and with restricting \(U\text{.}\) They give a morphism of presheaves \(f^\dagger G\to F\text{,}\) which factors uniquely through \(f^{-1}G\text{.}\)
🔗
Conversely, a morphism \(b:f^{-1}G\to F\) gives, for each open \(V\subset Y\text{,}\) the map
\begin{equation*} G(V)\longrightarrow (f^\dagger G)(f^{-1}V)\longrightarrow (f^{-1}G)(f^{-1}V)\xrightarrow{b}F(f^{-1}V). \end{equation*}
The first arrow is defined because \(f(f^{-1}V)\subset V\text{.}\) The composites commute with restrictions. Starting from \(a\) recovers \(a_V\text{;}\) starting from \(b\) recovers its maps on the local representatives defining \(f^\dagger G\text{,}\) and hence recovers \(b\text{.}\) All maps used are linear in the module case.
