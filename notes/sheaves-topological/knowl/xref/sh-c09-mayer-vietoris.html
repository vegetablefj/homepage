Theorem 9.13 Mayer–Vietoris.

Let \(X=U\cup V\) be a union of two open sets. For every \(K\in\D(X;\Lambda)\) there is a natural distinguished triangle
\begin{equation*} R\Gamma(X,K)\longrightarrow R\Gamma(U,K)\oplus R\Gamma(V,K)\longrightarrow R\Gamma(U\cap V,K)\longrightarrow R\Gamma(X,K)[1], \end{equation*}
where the second map is restriction from \(V\) minus restriction from \(U\text{.}\) Its long exact sequence includes
\begin{equation*} \cdots\longrightarrow H^n(X,K)\longrightarrow H^n(U,K)\oplus H^n(V,K)\longrightarrow H^n(U\cap V,K)\xrightarrow{\partial}H^{n+1}(X,K)\longrightarrow\cdots. \end{equation*}
🔗
🔗

Proof.
Choose a K-injective resolution \(K\to I\) with injective terms. In each degree, the sheaf axiom gives exactness at the first two positions of
\begin{equation*} 0\longrightarrow\Gamma(X,I)\longrightarrow\Gamma(U,I)\oplus\Gamma(V,I)\longrightarrow\Gamma(U\cap V,I)\longrightarrow0. \end{equation*}
The last map is surjective because every term of \(I\) is flabby: a section on the intersection extends to \(V\text{.}\) This is a short exact sequence of complexes, even when \(I\) is unbounded. Restriction preserves K-injectives, so its three complexes represent the derived sections stated in the theorem. The triangle and long exact sequence follow.
