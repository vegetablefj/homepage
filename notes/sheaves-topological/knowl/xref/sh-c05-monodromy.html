Theorem 5.12.

If \(X\) is locally path connected and semilocally simply connected, monodromy is an equivalence
\begin{equation*} \Loc(X;\Lambda)\simeq \operatorname{Fun}(\Pi_1(X),\Mod(\Lambda)). \end{equation*}
The analogous equivalence holds for locally constant sheaves of sets. If \(X\) is also path connected and \(x\in X\text{,}\) evaluation at \(x\) gives an equivalence with modules equipped with a left action of \(\pi_1(X,x)\) by linear automorphisms.
🔗
🔗

Proof.
Let \(V:\Pi_1(X)\to\Mod(\Lambda)\) be a functor. Form the set \(E=\coprod_{x\in X}V_x\) over \(X\text{.}\) Choose a path-connected open neighbourhood \(U\) such that its loops become null-homotopic in \(X\text{,}\) and choose \(a\in U\text{.}\) Transport along paths in \(U\) gives bijections \(V_a\to V_y\) for every \(y\in U\text{,}\) independent of the chosen path: two paths differ by a loop that is trivial in \(\Pi_1(X)\text{.}\) They define a bijection \(U\times V_a\to E|_U\text{.}\)
🔗
These bijections define compatible covering charts. On an overlap, choose a path-connected neighbourhood of a point contained in the overlap. Following paths in this smaller neighbourhood shows that the transition map between the two fixed modules is constant there. Hence the transition is continuous for discrete fibres. The resulting topology on \(E\) makes it a covering, with locally constant linear transition maps. Its sheaf of sections is a local system with monodromy \(V\text{.}\)
🔗
A natural transformation between two such functors gives fibre maps that commute with transport. In each path-connected trivializing neighbourhood, they are one fixed homomorphism, and hence define a map of local systems. Conversely, every sheaf morphism gives those fibre maps. These constructions are inverse, proving full faithfulness as well as essential surjectivity.
🔗
When \(X\) is path connected, its fundamental groupoid is connected and its automorphism group at \(x\) is \(\pi_1(X,x)\text{.}\) Restriction of functors to this one-object groupoid is an equivalence: paths from \(x\) to the other points reconstruct all other fibres, and changing these paths gives a naturally isomorphic functor.
