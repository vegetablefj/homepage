Proposition 3.1.

Let \((\mathcal F_i)\) be a small diagram of sheaves of sets or modules. Its limit and colimit exist. The limit is computed sectionwise, and the colimit is the associated sheaf of the sectionwise presheaf colimit:
\begin{equation*} \left(\lim_i\mathcal F_i\right)(U)=\lim_i\mathcal F_i(U), \end{equation*}
\begin{equation*} \colim_i^{\Sh}\mathcal F_i=a\left(U\longmapsto\colim_i\mathcal F_i(U)\right). \end{equation*}
For a point \(x\text{,}\) stalks commute with these colimits and with finite limits. The natural maps are
\begin{equation*} \left(\colim_i^{\Sh}\mathcal F_i\right)_x\simeq\colim_i\mathcal F_{i,x}, \end{equation*}
\begin{equation*} \left(\lim_i\mathcal F_i\right)_x\longrightarrow\lim_i\mathcal F_{i,x}, \end{equation*}
the latter being an isomorphism when the indexing category is finite.

Proof.
The sectionwise limit is a sheaf because a compatible local family can be glued in every component, and the unique gluings respect the diagram maps. This also verifies its universal property. The colimit formula follows from the adjunction between sheafification and inclusion: a morphism from that associated sheaf to a sheaf \(\mathcal G\) is exactly a compatible family of morphisms \(\mathcal F_i\to\mathcal G\text{.}\)
🔗
Sheafification preserves stalks, and a stalk is itself a colimit of sections. Interchanging two colimits proves the stalk-colimit formula. For a finite limit, filtered colimits over neighbourhoods commute with the finite limit in sets or modules. This proves the remaining comparison.
