Theorem 9.7.

For every sheaf \(F\text{,}\) there are natural comparison maps
\begin{equation*} \check C^\bullet(\mathcal U,F)\longrightarrow R\Gamma(X,F),\qquad\check H^n(\mathcal U,F)\longrightarrow H^n(X,F). \end{equation*}
The first is a morphism in \(\D^+(\Lambda)\text{.}\) There is a first-quadrant spectral sequence
\begin{equation*} E_1^{p,q}=\prod_{i_0,\ldots,i_p}H^q(U_{i_0\ldots i_p},F),\qquad E_2^{p,q}=\check H^p(\mathcal U,\underline H^q_F)\ \Longrightarrow\ H^{p+q}(X,F), \end{equation*}
where \(\underline H^q_F\) is the presheaf \(W\mapsto H^q(W,F)\text{.}\) Its differential has bidegree \((r,1-r)\text{.}\) The comparison is compatible with morphisms of sheaves, refinements, and pullback together with a coefficient morphism.

Proof.
Choose an injective resolution \(F\to I^\bullet\) and use the double complex
\begin{equation*} B^{p,q}=\check C^p(\mathcal U,I^q),\qquad d=\delta+(-1)^p d_I. \end{equation*}
By Lemma 9.6, every augmented horizontal row is exact. Hence the augmentation \(\Gamma(X,I^\bullet)\to\Tot B\) is a quasi-isomorphism. There is also a map \(\check C^\bullet(\mathcal U,F)\to\Tot B\text{.}\) Composing it in the derived category with the inverse of the augmentation gives the comparison.
🔗
Restriction of an injective sheaf to an open set is injective. Taking vertical cohomology in \(B\) therefore gives the displayed \(E_1\) page. Products of modules are exact, so vertical cohomology commutes with the products. The next differential is the Čech differential of the cohomology presheaf. Every total degree has finitely many possible \((p,q)\text{,}\) which gives convergence with a finite filtration. Comparison of injective resolutions proves independence of the choice. Refinement maps commute with the augmentations, and the same construction with a map of resolutions proves the functoriality assertions. See Stacks, Lemmas 20.11.2 and 20.11.5 and Lemma 20.15.1.
