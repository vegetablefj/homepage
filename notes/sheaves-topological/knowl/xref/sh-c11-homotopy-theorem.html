Theorem 11.14.

Let \(h:X\times I\to Y\) be a homotopy from \(f_0\) to \(f_1\text{,}\) and let \(L\) be a locally constant \(\Lambda\)-module sheaf on \(Y\text{.}\) Transport along this homotopy gives an isomorphism
\begin{equation*} \tau_h:f_0^{-1}L\xrightarrow{\sim}f_1^{-1}L \end{equation*}
such that the induced pullback maps satisfy
\begin{equation*} H^n(\tau_h)\circ f_0^\sharp=f_1^\sharp:\ H^n(Y,L)\longrightarrow H^n(X,f_1^{-1}L). \end{equation*}
If \(L=M_Y\) is constant, the standard coefficient identifications make \(\tau_h\) the identity, so the two pullback maps are equal.
🔗
🔗

Proof.
Apply Lemma 11.13 to \(H=h^{-1}L\text{.}\) Evaluation at the two endpoints identifies \(p_*H\) with \(f_0^{-1}L\) and \(f_1^{-1}L\text{;}\) their composite is \(\tau_h\text{.}\) Equivalently, its stalk at \(x\) is parallel transport along \(t\mapsto h(x,t)\text{.}\)
🔗
The two maps \(f_t^\sharp\) factor as pullback by \(h\) followed by restriction to the endpoint. The endpoint restriction maps become equal after the displayed transport, since under the counit they are both the inverse of pullback along \(p\text{.}\) This proves the cohomology identity. For constant coefficients, the transported constant section has the same value at both endpoints, giving the last assertion.
