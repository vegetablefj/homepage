Definition 10.3 Torsors.

Let \(G\) be a sheaf of groups on \(X\text{.}\) A right \(G\)-torsor is a sheaf of sets \(P\) with a right \(G\)-action such that every point has a neighborhood on which \(P\) has a section, and the morphism
\begin{equation*} P\times G\longrightarrow P\times P,\qquad (p,g)\longmapsto(p,p\cdot g) \end{equation*}
is an isomorphism. A morphism of torsors is a \(G\)-equivariant morphism of sheaves. The trivial torsor is \(G\) with right multiplication. The set of isomorphism classes, pointed by this trivial torsor, is denoted \(H^1(X,G)\) when \(G\) is not assumed Abelian.
🔗
For an Abelian sheaf \(F\text{,}\) we write the action additively. The torsor is a sheaf of sets acted on by \(F\text{;}\) it does not come with a chosen zero section or an Abelian group structure.
