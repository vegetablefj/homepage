    Chapter 7 Uniform estimates for restriction spaces

    Section 7.1 Restriction spaces with uniform estimates

    Fix a positive integer \(k\) for which \(kL\) is very ample, and embed \(X\) by its complete linear system. For an integral closed subvariety \(W\subset X\) of dimension \(d\text{,}\) define the dimension of the restriction image by1
    \begin{equation*} I_W(h)=\dim_{\C}\operatorname{im}\bigl(R_h\longrightarrow H^0(W,hL|_W)\bigr). \end{equation*}

    Lemma 7.1.

    For every \(1\le d\le n\text{,}\) there are constants \(C_d\) and \(H_d\text{,}\) depending only on \((X,L,k,d)\text{,}\) such that
    \begin{equation} \bigl(d!I_W(h)\bigr)^{1/d}\ge h-C_d \qquad(h\ge H_d)\tag{7.1} \end{equation}
    for every integral \(d\)-dimensional closed subvariety \(W\subset X\text{.}\)

    Proof.
    Let \(D_0=k^d\text{.}\) The degree of \(W\) in the chosen embedding satisfies
    \begin{equation*} D=(kL)^d\cdot W=k^d(L^d\cdot W)\ge D_0, \end{equation*}
    since \(L^d\cdot W\) is a positive integer. A general linear projection restricts to a finite surjective morphism \(\pi:W\longrightarrow\mathbb P^d\text{.}\) One can construct it by successive projections from points outside the current image; each projection is finite by [Stacks, Tag 0B1N]. The pullback of \(\cO_{\mathbb P^d}(1)\) is \(kL|_W\text{,}\) so \(\deg(\pi)=D\text{.}\)
    Let \(K=\C(\mathbb P^d)\) and \(E=\C(W)\text{.}\) The extension \(E/K\) has degree \(D\) and is separable. Consequently the geometric generic fiber consists of \(D\) distinct points. Choose one of the coordinates \(s_0\) defining the projection. It is nonzero at the generic point of \(\mathbb P^d\text{,}\) so all these points belong to the affine chart \(s_0\ne0\text{.}\)
    Select \(D_0\) points in this fiber. Over an algebraic closure \(\overline K\text{,}\) affine linear functions in the ambient coordinates separate every pair of selected points. For a selected point \(a\text{,}\) choose, for each other selected point \(b\text{,}\) a linear function that vanishes at \(b\) and is nonzero at \(a\text{.}\) Their product has degree at most \(D_0-1\text{,}\) vanishes at all the selected points except \(a\text{,}\) and is nonzero at \(a\text{.}\) Homogenizing and multiplying by a power of \(s_0\) gives a homogeneous ambient form of degree \(D_0\) with the same normalized values. These forms show that evaluation of degree-\(D_0\) forms on the selected points has rank \(D_0\text{.}\)
    Write \(V_{D_0}\) for the complex vector space of ambient homogeneous forms of degree \(D_0\text{.}\) Restriction and division by \(s_0^{D_0}\) define a \(K\)-linear map
    \begin{equation*} V_{D_0}\otimes_{\C}K\longrightarrow E. \end{equation*}
    Its rank is at least \(D_0\text{,}\) since rank is preserved by extension to \(\overline K\) and the preceding evaluation map factors through it. A monomial basis of \(V_{D_0}\) spans its image over \(K\text{.}\) We may therefore select ambient forms \(F_1,\ldots,F_{D_0}\) from this basis such that the elements \(F_i/s_0^{D_0}\) are linearly independent over \(K\text{.}\)2
    For \(q\ge D_0\text{,}\) multiply each \(F_i\) by every monomial of degree \(q-D_0\) in the \(d+1\) coordinates of the projection. The resulting sections of \(qkL|_W\) are linearly independent. A relation between them, divided by \(s_0^q\text{,}\) would be a relation over \(K\) between the \(F_i/s_0^{D_0}\text{.}\) Each coefficient would vanish as a rational function on \(\mathbb P^d\text{,}\) and hence the homogeneous polynomial giving that coefficient would vanish identically. All these products come from \(R_{qk}\text{.}\) It follows that
    \begin{equation} I_W(qk)\ge D_0\binom{q-D_0+d}{d}.\tag{7.2} \end{equation}
    Taking \(d\)th roots and using \(D_0^{1/d}=k\) gives
    \begin{equation} \bigl(d!I_W(qk)\bigr)^{1/d} \ge k(q-D_0)=qk-k^{d+1}.\tag{7.3} \end{equation}
    For every residue class \(r\) modulo \(k\text{,}\) choose once and for all an integer \(h_r\) in that class such that \(h_rL\) is globally generated. For any given integral \(W\text{,}\) there is a section of \(h_rL\) whose restriction to \(W\) is nonzero. Multiplication by its restriction injects the image of \(R_{qk}\) on \(W\) into the image of \(R_{qk+h_r}\text{,}\) since a product of two nonzero sections on an integral variety is nonzero. If \(h=qk+h_r\) and \(q\ge D_0\text{,}\) then (7.3) gives
    \begin{equation*} \bigl(d!I_W(h)\bigr)^{1/d}\ge h-(h_r+k^{d+1}). \end{equation*}
    We may take
    \begin{equation*} C_d=H_d=\max_r h_r+k^{d+1}. \end{equation*}
    These choices work for every \(W\text{,}\) independently of its degree and singularities.
