    Chapter 3 A local criterion for adjoint generation

    The final step of the proof is a local criterion for generation of \(P=K_X+(n+1)L\) at a closed point \(x\text{.}\) Its hypotheses compare the coefficients of a rational divisor with log discrepancies, both on a reduced divisor over \(x\) and on the other prime divisors meeting it. Global nefness and bigness give vanishing, while the coefficient comparisons give a section on the reduced divisor that can be lifted.

    Section 3.1 Vanishing and descent

    We use Kawamata–Viehweg vanishing, proved in [Kaw82] and [Vie82], in the rounding form of [Fuj09, Theorem 0.1].

    Theorem 3.1 Kawamata–Viehweg vanishing.

    Let \(T\) be smooth and projective over \(\C\text{.}\) Let \(\Delta\) be an effective rational divisor with simple normal crossing support and coefficients in \([0,1)\text{,}\) and let \(D\) be an integral divisor. If \(D-(K_T+\Delta)\) is nef and big, then
    \begin{equation*} H^i(T,\cO_T(D))=0\qquad(i>0). \end{equation*}
    To obtain this statement from the rounding form, take \(Q=D-K_T-\Delta\text{.}\) The support of its fractional part is simple normal crossing and \(K_T+\ceil{Q}=D\text{.}\) In particular, if \(M\) is an integral divisor, \(B\) is an effective rational divisor with simple normal crossing support, and \(M-B\) is nef and big, then
    \begin{equation*} H^i(T,\cO_T(K_T+M-\floor{B}))=0\qquad(i>0). \end{equation*}
    For this application the boundary is \(\{B\}=B-\floor{B}\text{,}\) and the required nef and big divisor is \(M-B\text{.}\)

    Lemma 3.2.

    Let \(\pi:T\to V\) be a proper birational morphism of smooth varieties, let \(P_V\) be a line bundle on \(V\text{,}\) and let \(C\) be an integral divisor on \(T\) whose positive part is exceptional over \(V\text{.}\) A section of \(\pi^*P_V+C\) defines a regular section of \(P_V\) on \(V\text{.}\)
    Suppose that a prime divisor \(F\) on \(T\) maps to a point \(x\text{,}\) that the coefficient of \(C\) on \(F\) is zero, and that the section is nonzero generically on \(F\text{.}\) Then its descended section is nonzero at \(x\text{.}\)

    Proof.
    Regard the section as a rational section of \(P_V\) in the common function field. On each nonexceptional prime of \(T\text{,}\) the order of its pullback is at least the negative of the coefficient of \(C\text{,}\) and this coefficient is nonpositive. Every prime divisor on \(V\) has a nonexceptional strict transform on \(T\text{,}\) so the rational section has no pole along any prime of \(V\text{.}\) Normality of \(V\) extends it from codimension one to a regular section everywhere.
    At \(F\text{,}\) the coefficient of \(C\) is zero, so nonvanishing at its generic point means that the pullback of the descended section has order zero. A local function vanishing at \(x\) has positive order along every prime centered at \(x\text{.}\) In a local frame of \(P_V\) near \(x\text{,}\) the coefficient of the descended section therefore cannot vanish at \(x\text{.}\)

    Section 3.2 The local generation criterion

    Fujita’s argument in [Fuj93, §1(1.5)–(1.6)] uses equality on a divisor over \(x\) and strict discrepancy inequalities on the components meeting it. Kawamata–Viehweg vanishing lifts a section from that divisor, and a correction whose positive part is exceptional permits descent. The following lemma gives this argument for a possibly reducible reduced divisor \(S\text{.}\)

    Lemma 3.3.

    Let \(X\) be a smooth projective variety over \(\C\) of dimension \(n\geq1\text{,}\) let \(L\) be an ample line bundle, and let \(x\in X\) be a closed point. Let \(\pi:T\to X\) be a projective birational morphism with \(T\) smooth, and let \(B\) be an effective rational divisor with SNC support. For a prime divisor \(F\) on \(T\text{,}\) write
    \begin{equation*} a_F=\A(F)=1+\ord_F(K_{T/X}). \end{equation*}
    Assume that \((n+1)\pi^*L-B\) is nef and big. Suppose that \(S\) is a nonempty effective reduced divisor on \(T\) such that
    1. every component \(F\) of \(S\) satisfies \(\pi(F)=\{x\}\) and \(\operatorname{coeff}_F B=a_F\text{;}\)
    2. every other prime divisor \(F\) on \(T\) meeting \(S\) satisfies \(\operatorname{coeff}_F B<a_F\text{.}\)
    Then there is a section \(s\in H^0(X,P)\) with \(s(x)\ne0\text{,}\) where \(P=K_X+(n+1)L\text{.}\)

    Proof.
    Define the integral Cartier divisor
    \begin{equation} D=K_T+(n+1)\pi^*L-\lfloor B\rfloor.\tag{3.1} \end{equation}
    Since the fractional part \(\{B\}=B-\lfloor B\rfloor\) is an effective rational SNC divisor with coefficients in \([0,1)\text{,}\)
    \begin{equation*} D-(K_T+\{B\})=(n+1)\pi^*L-B. \end{equation*}
    The right side is nef and big by hypothesis. Kawamata–Viehweg vanishing in Theorem 3.1, equivalently the rounding formulation of [Fuj09, Theorem 0.1], therefore gives \(H^1(T,\cO_T(D))=0\text{.}\) Since \(T\) is smooth, \(S\) is Cartier. Its restriction sequence is
    \begin{equation*} 0\longrightarrow\cO_T(D)\longrightarrow\cO_T(D+S) \longrightarrow\cO_S(D+S)\longrightarrow0. \end{equation*}
    The vanishing gives a surjection
    \begin{equation} H^0(T,\cO_T(D+S))\longrightarrow H^0(S,\cO_S(D+S)).\tag{3.2} \end{equation}
    To construct a section on \(S\text{,}\) compare \(D+S\) with the pullback of \(P\text{:}\)
    \begin{equation} C:=D+S-\pi^*P=K_{T/X}-\lfloor B\rfloor+S.\tag{3.3} \end{equation}
    Smoothness of \(X\) and \(T\) implies that every \(a_F\) is a positive integer. For a component \(F\) of \(S\text{,}\) the coefficient of \(K_{T/X}\) is \(a_F-1\text{,}\) and the first hypothesis gives \(\operatorname{coeff}_F B=a_F\text{.}\) Hence
    \begin{equation*} \operatorname{coeff}_F C=(a_F-1)-a_F+1=0. \end{equation*}
    For any other prime \(F\) meeting \(S\text{,}\) the second hypothesis gives \(\lfloor\operatorname{coeff}_F B\rfloor\leq a_F-1\text{.}\) Its coefficient in \(S\) is zero, so its coefficient in \(C\) is nonnegative. A prime outside \(\Supp(B)\) and the exceptional locus of \(\pi\) has coefficient zero in \(C\text{:}\) its coefficient in \(K_{T/X}\) is zero, and it cannot be a component of \(S\) because every component of \(S\) has positive coefficient in \(B\text{.}\)
    It follows that \(C\) is effective on a neighborhood of \(S\) and that no component of \(S\) occurs in its support. More explicitly, every negative component of \(C\) is disjoint from \(S\text{.}\) Remove the supports of these finitely many components to obtain an open neighborhood of \(S\) on which \(C\) is effective. This local effectivity permits restriction of the canonical rational section of \(\cO_T(C)\) to \(S\text{.}\)
    Every component of \(S\) maps to \(x\text{,}\) and \(S\) is reduced. Therefore \(\pi|_S\) factors scheme-theoretically through the reduced point \(x\text{.}\) To check this, take a local function in the maximal ideal of \(x\text{.}\) Its pullback restricts to zero on each irreducible component of \(S\) and hence to zero on \(S\text{,}\) because the intersection of the minimal prime ideals of a reduced ring is zero. It follows that
    \begin{equation*} \pi^*P|_S\simeq P|_x\otimes_{\C}\cO_S. \end{equation*}
    Choose a nonzero vector in the one-dimensional fiber \(P|_x\text{.}\) Under this isomorphism it gives a constant section of \(\pi^*P|_S\text{.}\) Multiply that constant section by the restriction to \(S\) of the canonical rational section of \(\cO_T(C)\text{.}\) The canonical section is regular on the neighborhood just constructed and is nonzero at the generic point of each component of \(S\text{,}\) because the coefficient of \(C\) there is zero. The product is a global section of \(\cO_S(D+S)\) nonzero at the generic point of every component. This construction uses effectivity of \(C\) only near \(S\text{,}\) since its negative components elsewhere are disjoint from \(S\text{.}\)
    Lift this section to \(\widetilde s\in H^0(T,\cO_T(D+S))\) using (3.2). View it as a section of \(\pi^*P+C\text{.}\) On any nonexceptional prime outside \(S\text{,}\) the coefficient of \(K_{T/X}\) is zero, and therefore
    \begin{equation*} \operatorname{coeff}_F C=-\lfloor\operatorname{coeff}_F B\rfloor\leq0. \end{equation*}
    On a component of \(S\text{,}\) the coefficient of \(C\) is zero. Thus the positive part of \(C\) is exceptional over \(X\text{.}\) The divisor \(S\) is nonempty, and each of its components maps to \(x\text{,}\) has coefficient zero in \(C\text{,}\) and has \(\widetilde s\) nonzero at its generic point. Lemma 3.2 gives a regular section \(s\in H^0(X,P)\) with \(s(x)\ne0\text{.}\)
    The proof includes \(n=1\text{.}\) A component of \(S\) can then be the nonexceptional point \(x\) itself. Its coefficient in \(C\) is still zero, the restriction sequence is the sequence for this effective Cartier divisor on the smooth curve, and the same descent argument gives \(s(x)\ne0\text{.}\)
