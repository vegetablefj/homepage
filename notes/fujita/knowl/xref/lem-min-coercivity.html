Lemma 5.3.

Suppose that \(P\) is not generated at \(x\text{.}\) There are constants \(0<t_0<n+1\text{,}\) \(c>0\text{,}\) \(0<\delta<1\text{,}\) \(0<a_-\leq a_+<\infty\text{,}\) and an integer \(m_0\) such that, for every \(m\geq m_0\) and every \(t\in[t_0,n+1]\text{,}\)
\begin{equation} c\leq\lambda_{m,t}(x)\leq1-\delta.\tag{5.4} \end{equation}
Every admissible pair with \(x\in c_X(v)\) and \(\Phi_{m,t}(v,\mathbf s)\leq1-\delta/2\) satisfies
\begin{equation} a_-\leq\A(v)\leq a_+.\tag{5.5} \end{equation}
All constants are independent of \(m\) and \(t\) in the stated ranges.
🔗
🔗

Proof.
By Proposition 4.6, choose \(\epsilon>0\) such that for all sufficiently large \(m\text{,}\)
\begin{equation*} \frac1{mN_m}\sum_{\gamma\in H_m}|\gamma|_1 \geq\frac n{n+1}+3\epsilon. \end{equation*}
Choose \(t_0<n+1\) sufficiently close to \(n+1\) that \(n/t_0\leq n/(n+1)+\epsilon\text{.}\) Take a basis with distinct ordinary initials and use \(v=\rho\ord_x\text{,}\) where \(\rho>0\) is a scaling factor. Here \(\ord_x\) is the order of the exceptional divisor of the point blowup, with discrepancy \(n\text{;}\) in dimension one it is the order at the point. Set \(b_i=n/t-\mult_x(s_i)/m\text{.}\) The objective is
\begin{equation*} \Phi_{m,t}(\rho\ord_x,\mathbf s) =\frac1{N_m}\sum_i e^{\rho b_i}. \end{equation*}
Its linear coefficient at \(\rho=0\) is at most \(-2\epsilon\text{.}\) The numbers \(b_i\) are uniformly bounded by (4.1) and \(t\geq t_0\text{.}\) The remainder after the linear term is therefore uniformly \(O(\rho^2)\text{.}\) One fixed sufficiently small \(\rho>0\) gives an upper bound \(1-\delta\text{,}\) with fixed \(\delta\in(0,1)\text{,}\) for every \(m\) and \(t\) under consideration.
🔗
Apply Lemma 5.2 for this \(t_0\text{,}\) and let \(a=\A(v)\text{.}\) By Lemma 5.1,
\begin{equation*} \{s:v(s)>ma/(2t)\} \subset\{s:\mult_x(s)>m/(2t)\}. \end{equation*}
The subspace on the left consequently has codimension at least \(\kappa N_m\text{.}\) At least that many vectors of any basis lie outside it, since the vectors inside are linearly independent and their number is at most the dimension of the subspace.
 2 
The basis need not be adapted to \(v\text{.}\)
Each vector outside contributes at least \(e^{a/(2t)}\) to the exponential sum, so
\begin{equation} \Phi_{m,t}(v,\mathbf s) \geq\kappa\exp\!\left(\frac a{2t}\right) \geq\kappa\exp\!\left(\frac a{2(n+1)}\right).\tag{5.6} \end{equation}
This gives the lower bound in (5.4), with \(c=\kappa\text{,}\) and bounds \(a\) above on the sublevel set \(\Phi_{m,t}\leq1-\delta/2\text{.}\)
🔗
Finally, (5.2) and (4.1) imply \(v(s_i)/m\leq Ca\text{,}\) and hence \(\Phi_{m,t}(v,\mathbf s)\geq e^{-Ca}\text{.}\) Choose \(a_->0\) so small that \(e^{-Ca_-}>1-\delta/2\text{.}\) Every pair in the stated sublevel set has \(a\geq a_-\text{.}\) Enlarge the upper bound if necessary so that \(a_+\geq a_-\text{.}\) Taking \(m_0\) large enough for both the ordinary-order estimate and the jet estimate completes the proof.
