Lemma 9.3.

There is a prime divisor \(F_0\) over \(X\) such that its center on \(X\) is \(\{x\}\text{,}\)
\begin{equation*} \begin{aligned} \left(\frac{\ord_{F_0}(D_i)}{\A(F_0)}\right)_{i=1}^{N_m}&=q^0,\\ \sum_j\ord_{F_0}(E_j)a_j&=\A(F_0). \end{aligned} \end{equation*}
The divisor \(F_0\) can be retained by its strict transform on every common resolution dominating a model on which it appears.
🔗
🔗

Proof.
Choose an indexed polytope containing \(q^0\text{.}\) Since its vertices and \(q^0\) are rational, the linear system giving a convex representation has a rational solution. Write \(q^0=\sum_j\theta_jz_j\) with rational \(\theta_j\geq0\) and \(\sum_j\theta_j=1\text{.}\) The weights \(\theta_j/a_j\) are rational. Discard the zero weights and take the component of the remaining intersection containing the chosen stratum. These weights define a monomial test with discrepancy one whose orders on the \(D_i\) form the vector \(q^0\text{.}\)
🔗
As in Chapter 2, clear the denominators of the positive weights and divide the resulting integers by their greatest common divisor. The resulting positive integral weights define a primitive ray in the local toroidal cone. Extracting this ray gives a prime divisor \(F_0\text{.}\) Its divisorial order is a positive multiple of the monomial test, so division by discrepancy gives the same vector \(q^0\text{.}\) The monomial discrepancy formula gives the displayed equality with the discrepancy of its retraction to \(Y\text{.}\)
🔗
The center on \(Y\) of this monomial test has image in \(X\) containing \(x\text{.}\) Its normalized vector belongs to the polytope of its own positive-support stratum. If that image were positive-dimensional, it would give an indexed polytope with positive-dimensional image containing \(q^0\text{,}\) which has been excluded. Its center on \(X\) is therefore \(\{x\}\text{.}\) Resolution gives a smooth projective model on which the extracted divisor remains a prime divisor. On higher common resolutions, use its strict transform. With \(Y\) fixed, the section orders along \(F_0\text{,}\) its log discrepancy, and the discrepancy of its retraction to \(Y\) do not change under this replacement. The equality of discrepancies therefore persists on these models.
