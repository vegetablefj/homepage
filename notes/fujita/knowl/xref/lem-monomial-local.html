Lemma 2.1.

A monomial order with fixed positive support can be computed in the Taylor ring at any smooth point of its open stratum. Multiplying its positive-weight coordinates by units, or changing its complementary zero-weight coordinates, does not change the order.
🔗
🔗

Proof.
For \(c>0\text{,}\) let \(I_c\) be the ideal generated by monomials in the positive-weight coordinates whose weight is at least \(c\text{.}\) The stated coordinate changes preserve this ideal: a monomial in the positive-weight coordinates changes by a unit factor, and no generator depends on a choice of complementary coordinates.
🔗
In the completed local ring, \(I_c\) is the extension of a monomial ideal of finite colength in the positive-weight variables. The ideal \(I_c\) is primary to the ideal defining their intersection. Consequently, localization at the generic point of the stratum followed by contraction does not change \(I_c\text{.}\) Faithful flatness of completion gives the same assertion in the regular local ring. A coefficient that vanishes at the chosen closed point but is not identically zero on the stratum remains a nonzero series in zero-weight variables. Its vanishing at that point therefore does not increase the monomial order.
