Lemma 2.3.

Let \(V\) be a smooth complex projective variety, let \(S\) be a complex algebraic variety, and let \(\mathfrak a\) be an ideal on \(V\times S\) locally generated by algebraic families of functions. For each real \(c\text{,}\) the set
\begin{equation*} \{(x,s)\in V\times S:\lct_x(\mathfrak a_s)\leq c\} \end{equation*}
is Zariski closed. The fiber ideal \(\mathfrak a_s\) is generated by restriction of these generators; no flatness of its zero scheme is required.
🔗
🔗

Proof.
We first prove closedness in the classical topology. Locally embed \(S\) in a smooth ambient parameter space and extend the finite list of generators holomorphically. Apply the semicontinuity of complex singularity exponents to
\begin{equation*} \varphi=\frac12\log\sum_i|g_i|^2. \end{equation*}
The function \(e^{\varphi}\) is locally Hölder continuous, so [DK01, Lemma 3.2] applies to this family. Translating the local coordinates on \(V\) reduces a moving marked point to a fixed marked point. Restriction to \(S\) proves that the displayed sublevel set is closed in the classical topology, including fibers where all generators vanish identically.
🔗
Next we prove algebraic constructibility. Over the generic point of each irreducible component of \(S\text{,}\) resolve the ideal, and spread this resolution over a dense open subset of that component. After shrinking the open set, generic smoothness makes the resolution, its boundary components, and their intersections smooth over the base. The fiber morphisms are then log resolutions, with fixed orders of the ideal and fixed discrepancy coefficients. Over this open subset, (2.4) expresses the threshold condition as the union of the images under the proper resolution morphism of those finitely many divisors whose discrepancy-to-order ratio is at most \(c\text{.}\) These images are algebraic. If components must first be separated by a finite cover, their images remain constructible after descending the cover. The fibers on which the ideal is zero are separated by an algebraic condition.
🔗
Repeat the argument on the closed complement. Noetherian induction gives a finite stratification, and hence constructibility of the threshold locus on \(V\times S\text{.}\) This is the generic-resolution argument used for [DK01, Theorem 3.1], applied here to ideals. In dimension zero, constructibility and closedness follow directly from the vanishing of the generators.
🔗
A constructible subset of a complex algebraic variety that is closed in the classical topology is Zariski closed. On each irreducible component of its Zariski closure, the constructible subset contains a nonempty Zariski open subset, which is classically dense in that component. Classical closedness therefore forces the whole component to belong to the subset. This proves the lemma.
