Lemma 4.4.

For the sections of Lemma 4.3, let \(p:B=\Bl_xX\to X\) and \(E\simeq\mathbb P^{n-1}\) be the exceptional divisor. If \(n=1\text{,}\) take \(p\) to be the identity and \(E=x\text{.}\) Remove the common order \(nh\) from the pullbacks of the sections \(r_\ell\text{,}\) and let \(\mathfrak b\) be the base ideal of the resulting subsystem of
\begin{equation*} H=p^*(ht'L)-nhE. \end{equation*}
Then
\begin{equation} \lct_z(E,\mathfrak b|_E)>1/h\qquad(z\in E),\tag{4.4} \end{equation}
where \(\mathfrak b|_E\) denotes its image in \(\cO_E\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗

Proof.
Every \(r_\ell\) has ordinary order exactly \(nh\text{.}\) The restricted subsystem on \(E\) is generated by the leading homogeneous forms \(g_\ell\) of the \(r_\ell\text{,}\) each of degree \(nh\text{.}\) For \(n=1\text{,}\) these restrictions are nonzero at the point \(E\text{,}\) so the restricted ideal is the unit ideal and its threshold is infinite. Assume \(n>1\text{.}\)
🔗
First consider the monomial forms \(z^{\beta_\ell}\text{.}\) On the chart \(z_k\ne0\) of \(E\text{,}\) the exponent vectors are \(\beta_\ell^{(k)}\text{,}\) obtained by deleting the \(k\)th coordinate. Projection from the hyperplane \(|u|_1=n\) to the remaining coordinates is an affine isomorphism. The hypothesis on the convex hull therefore puts the all-ones vector in the ordinary interior of the convex hull of the \(\beta_\ell^{(k)}/h\text{.}\) Every nonzero linear functional given by \(u\in\R_{\geq0}^{n-1}\) takes a value at some point of that polytope strictly smaller than its value at \(\mathbf1\text{.}\) Consequently
\begin{equation} \frac1h\min_\ell\langle u,\beta_\ell^{(k)}\rangle <\sum_i u_i\qquad(0\ne u\in\R_{\geq0}^{n-1}).\tag{4.5} \end{equation}
🔗
For a prime-divisor order over this chart, take \(u_i\) to be its values on the coordinate functions. The left side of (4.5) is the order of the monomial ideal divided by \(h\text{.}\) The simple normal crossing inequality of Lemma 2.2, applied on this chart, gives the lower bound \(\sum_i u_i\) for the log discrepancy of this prime-divisor order. If all \(u_i\) are zero, the ideal has order zero and the discrepancy is positive. Thus the monomial ideal is klt at coefficient \(1/h\text{.}\) Its threshold at each point is computed by a finite list of divisors on a log resolution, so the threshold is strictly greater than \(1/h\text{.}\)
 1 
The monomial condition (4.5) is also the criterion in [How01, Main Theorem] for the multiplier ideal at coefficient \(1/h\) to be the unit ideal. The preceding argument proves the required strict inequality directly.
🔗
We transfer this conclusion to the forms \(g_\ell\) by degeneration. Because their monomial supports are finite, choose integral weights \(w_1,\ldots,w_n\) for which the lexicographic initial monomial of each \(g_\ell\) is its unique term of smallest weight. For example, successive sufficiently large powers of one integer realize lexicographic order on these finite supports. The forms
\begin{equation*} g_{\ell,\tau}(z)= \tau^{-\langle w,\beta_\ell\rangle} g_\ell(\tau^{w_1}z_1,\ldots,\tau^{w_n}z_n) \end{equation*}
have polynomial coefficients in \(\tau\text{.}\) At \(\tau=0\) they are nonzero scalar multiples of \(z^{\beta_\ell}\text{.}\) Viewed as sections of \(\cO_E(nh)\text{,}\) they define an algebraic family of base ideals on \(E\times\mathbb A^1\text{.}\)
🔗
By Lemma 2.3, the set of pairs where the log canonical threshold of the fiber ideal is at most \(1/h\) is closed. Its projection to \(\mathbb A^1\) is closed because \(E\) is projective. This projection omits \(0\text{,}\) by the monomial computation, so there is a nonzero parameter at which the threshold is greater than \(1/h\) at every point of \(E\text{.}\) Every nonzero fiber is obtained from the original forms by a diagonal projective automorphism and nonzero scalar changes of generators. The threshold inequality therefore holds for the original restricted ideal.
 2 
Lemma 2.3 uses the family semicontinuity furnished by [DK01, Lemma 3.2].
