Lemma 4.2.

Let \(V=L^n\text{.}\) Then
\begin{equation} \liminf_{j\to\infty}\frac1{jN_j} \sum_{\gamma\in H_j}|\gamma|_1 \geq V^{1/n}\frac n{n+1}.\tag{4.2} \end{equation}
If the left side equals \(n/(n+1)\text{,}\) then \(V=1\text{.}\) Along any subsequence on which this normalized mean tends to \(n/(n+1)\text{,}\) every open ball whose closure lies in the interior of
\begin{equation*} \Delta=\{u\in\R_{\geq0}^n:|u|_1\leq1\} \end{equation*}
meets \(j^{-1}H_j\) for all sufficiently large \(j\) in the subsequence.
🔗
🔗

Proof.
Among all sets of \(N_j\) nonnegative lattice points, the sum of total degrees is smallest for a set \(T_j\) obtained by filling successive total degrees. There are \(\binom{r+n}{n}\) points of degree at most \(r\text{,}\) and their degree sum is
\begin{equation*} \sum_{|\gamma|_1\leq r}|\gamma|_1 =n\binom{r+n}{n+1}. \end{equation*}
By (2.1), the last degree occupied by \(T_j\) is \(V^{1/n}j+O(1)\text{.}\) The possible partial last layer contains \(O(j^{n-1})\) points. Its contribution changes the degree sum by at most \(O(j^n)\text{,}\) and hence changes the average total degree by \(O(1)\text{.}\) Therefore
\begin{equation*} \frac1{N_j}\sum_{\gamma\in T_j}|\gamma|_1 =V^{1/n}\frac n{n+1}j+O(1). \end{equation*}
The minimality of \(T_j\) gives (4.2). Since \(V\) is a positive integer, equality with \(n/(n+1)\) forces \(V=1\text{.}\)
🔗
Now work along a subsequence with \(V=1\) and limiting normalized mean \(n/(n+1)\text{.}\) If a fixed ball in the interior of \(\Delta\) were missed for infinitely many \(j\text{,}\) then at least \(c_1j^n\) lattice points of \(T_j\) would be absent from \(H_j\text{,}\) all of degree at most \((1-c_2)j\text{,}\) for constants \(c_1,c_2>0\text{.}\) Each must be replaced by a point outside \(T_j\text{,}\) of degree at least \(j-O(1)\text{.}\) Pair the remaining points of \(T_j\setminus H_j\) with the remaining points of \(H_j\setminus T_j\text{.}\) These replacements cannot decrease total degree. The missed ball would thus increase the degree sum by at least a fixed positive multiple of \(j^{n+1}\text{.}\) After division by \(jN_j\text{,}\) this contradicts convergence to the lattice minimum.
