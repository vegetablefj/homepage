Corollary 9.5.

Under the hypotheses of Theorem 1.1, the line bundle \(K_X+mL\) is globally generated for every integer \(m\geq n+1\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗

Proof.
Let \(r=m-n-1\text{.}\) If \(r=0\text{,}\) then \(m=n+1\) and the conclusion is Theorem 1.1. If \(r>0\text{,}\) apply that theorem to the smooth connected variety \(X\times\mathbb P^r\) of dimension \(n+r\) and the ample line bundle \(L\boxtimes\cO_{\mathbb P^r}(1)\text{.}\)
 1 
An external product is the tensor product of the pullbacks under the two projections.
The canonical bundle of the product is \(K_X\boxtimes\cO_{\mathbb P^r}(-r-1)\text{,}\) so the adjoint bundle in dimension \(n+r\) is
\begin{equation*} (K_X+(n+r+1)L)\boxtimes\cO_{\mathbb P^r}(n) =(K_X+mL)\boxtimes\cO_{\mathbb P^r}(n). \end{equation*}
This adjoint bundle is globally generated. Fix a point \(z\in\mathbb P^r\text{.}\) Restriction to \(X\times\{z\}\) gives generating sections of \(K_X+mL\) tensored with the one-dimensional fiber of \(\cO_{\mathbb P^r}(n)\) at \(z\text{.}\) Choosing a nonzero vector in that fiber identifies the restricted bundle with \(K_X+mL\text{,}\) so \(K_X+mL\) is globally generated.
