    Chapter 2 Valuations, discrepancies, and thresholds

    Let \(X\) be a smooth connected complex projective variety of dimension \(n\geq1\text{,}\) and let \(L\) be ample, as in Theorem 1.1. We use additive notation for line bundles and write \(P=K_X+(n+1)L\text{.}\) For each positive integer \(j\text{,}\) set \(R_j=H^0(X,jL)\) and \(N_j=\dim R_j\text{.}\) Serre vanishing and the Hilbert polynomial give
    \begin{equation} N_j=\frac{L^n}{n!}j^n+O(j^{n-1}).\tag{2.1} \end{equation}
    The intersection number \(L^n\) is a positive integer, so \(L^n\geq1\text{.}\) Throughout, \(c_X(v)\) denotes the closed center of an order \(v\text{.}\) A test is admissible at \(x\) when \(x\in c_X(v)\text{.}\) Its center may have positive dimension.

    Section 2.1 Monomial orders and discrepancy

    We use resolution and principalization in characteristic zero, with centers having normal crossings with a prescribed simple normal crossing divisor. These forms of resolution follow from [Wlo05, Theorems 1.0.1 and 2.4.1]. All models used in cohomological arguments are projective. Resolution may be carried out above a previously chosen smooth model, and an extracted prime divisor is retained by its strict transform.
    For a prime divisor \(F\) on a smooth birational model \(\pi:Y\to X\text{,}\) our discrepancy convention is
    \begin{equation*} \A(F)=1+\ord_F(K_{Y/X}). \end{equation*}
    If \(v=a\ord_F\) for \(a>0\text{,}\) define \(\A(v)=a\A(F)\text{.}\) For a nonzero section \(s\) of a line bundle, \(v(s)\) means the order of its effective divisor of zeros.1 The order of an ideal is the minimum of the orders of its local generators.
    The allowed real orders are monomial on a smooth projective birational model \(Y\to X\text{.}\) Choose local regular coordinates for which every component of \(K_{Y/X}\) meeting the neighborhood is a coordinate hyperplane. Any further coordinate divisors used in the monomial order must have simple normal crossings together with this entire support. Assign positive real weights \(u_1,\ldots,u_r\) to coordinate divisors \(E_1,\ldots,E_r\) and weight zero to the complementary coordinates. The value of a germ is the smallest weight of a nonzero monomial in the equations of the \(E_j\text{,}\) with coefficients in the zero-weight coordinates. Define
    \begin{equation} \A(v)=\sum_{j=1}^r u_j\A(E_j).\tag{2.2} \end{equation}
    A coordinate divisor not occurring in \(K_{Y/X}\) has discrepancy one. We choose an irreducible component of the intersection of the positive-weight divisors and compute at its generic point, away from the zero-weight coordinate divisors. The image in \(X\) of the closure of this component in \(Y\) is \(c_X(v)\text{.}\) The order with every weight zero is excluded. We call such a nonzero monomial order a test. A test is admissible at \(x\) if \(x\in c_X(v)\text{.}\)
    The requirement that the coordinates include every component of \(K_{Y/X}\) meeting the neighborhood makes (2.2) agree with divisorial discrepancy; see [JM12, Proposition 5.1]. For rational weights, clear denominators and use a toroidal modification to extract the primitive ray determined by the resulting integral weights. The relative canonical coefficient of the extracted prime over \(Y\) is the sum of the primitive weights minus one. The pullback of \(K_{Y/X}\) adds the weighted canonical coefficients, giving (2.2). Further resolution realizes this extraction on a smooth projective model. Thus rational monomial weights give positive multiples of prime-divisor orders.
    On a fixed positive support, approximation by positive rational weights preserves the center and approximates the discrepancy and the orders of any finite collection of sections. Indeed, for each Taylor expansion only the coordinatewise minimal exponents can contribute a minimum. There are finitely many such exponents, so the order is the minimum of finitely many linear functions of the weights.

    Lemma 2.1.

    A monomial order with fixed positive support can be computed in the Taylor ring at any smooth point of its open stratum. Multiplying its positive-weight coordinates by units, or changing its complementary zero-weight coordinates, does not change the order.

    Proof.
    For \(c>0\text{,}\) let \(I_c\) be the ideal generated by monomials in the positive-weight coordinates whose weight is at least \(c\text{.}\) The stated coordinate changes preserve this ideal: a monomial in the positive-weight coordinates changes by a unit factor, and no generator depends on a choice of complementary coordinates.
    In the completed local ring, \(I_c\) is the extension of a monomial ideal of finite colength in the positive-weight variables. The ideal \(I_c\) is primary to the ideal defining their intersection. Consequently, localization at the generic point of the stratum followed by contraction does not change \(I_c\text{.}\) Faithful flatness of completion gives the same assertion in the regular local ring. A coefficient that vanishes at the chosen closed point but is not identically zero on the stratum remains a nonzero series in zero-weight variables. Its vanishing at that point therefore does not increase the monomial order.
    The following calculation is the divisorial form of the retraction in [JM12, §4.3 and Corollary 5.4]. We include the calculation because preservation of the chosen section orders will be used in the minimization argument.

    Lemma 2.2.

    Let \(D_1,\ldots,D_N\) be effective divisors on \(X\text{,}\) and let \(Y\to X\) be a log resolution of their union. Include all exceptional divisors in the simple normal crossing divisor, and denote its components by \(E_j\text{.}\) For a positive multiple \(v\) of a prime-divisor order over \(Y\text{,}\) let \(u_j=v(E_j)\text{.}\) Then
    \begin{equation} \begin{aligned} v(D_i)&=\sum_j u_j\ord_{E_j}(D_i),\\ \A(v)&\geq\sum_j u_j\A(E_j). \end{aligned}\tag{2.3} \end{equation}
    If at least one \(u_j\) is positive, the monomial order determined by these weights at the stratum containing the generic center of \(v\) has the same values on the \(D_i\text{,}\) no larger discrepancy, and a closed center on \(X\) containing \(c_X(v)\text{.}\)

    Proof.
    Locally at the generic center on \(Y\text{,}\) the pullback of \(D_i\) is a monomial in the equations of the \(E_j\) times a unit. Taking \(v\) gives the equality in (2.3).
    The reduced simple normal crossing divisor \(\sum_j E_j\) is log canonical on \(Y\text{.}\) Hence the log discrepancy of \(v\) relative to \(Y\) is at least \(\sum_j u_j\text{.}\) Equivalently, a logarithmic volume form has at most a simple pole along any prime on a higher smooth model. The discrepancy relative to \(X\) is obtained by adding \(v(K_{Y/X})\text{.}\) Since every exceptional component was included among the \(E_j\text{,}\) this gives
    \begin{equation*} \A(v)\geq\sum_j u_j+ \sum_j u_j\ord_{E_j}(K_{Y/X}) =\sum_j u_j\A(E_j). \end{equation*}
    The last expression is the discrepancy of the retracted monomial order. The closure of the stratum defining that order contains the generic center of \(v\) on \(Y\text{.}\) Properness then gives the asserted inclusion of closed centers on \(X\text{.}\)

    Section 2.2 Log canonical thresholds in families

    For a nonzero coherent ideal \(\mathfrak a\) on a smooth variety, a log resolution \(\pi:Y\to X\) computes the threshold at a closed point \(x\) by
    \begin{equation} \lct_x(\mathfrak a)= \min_{\substack{x\in\pi(F)\\\ord_F(\mathfrak a)>0}} \frac{\A(F)}{\ord_F(\mathfrak a)}.\tag{2.4} \end{equation}
    The minimum may be taken over the finitely many prime components of the divisor of the resolved ideal and of the exceptional divisor. The threshold is \(+\infty\) for the unit ideal and zero for the zero ideal. For generators \(g_i\text{,}\) the same threshold is the supremum of the \(c\) for which \((\sum_i|g_i|^2)^{-c}\) is locally integrable near \(x\text{.}\) This description follows by change of variables on the resolution.

    Lemma 2.3.

    Let \(V\) be a smooth complex projective variety, let \(S\) be a complex algebraic variety, and let \(\mathfrak a\) be an ideal on \(V\times S\) locally generated by algebraic families of functions. For each real \(c\text{,}\) the set
    \begin{equation*} \{(x,s)\in V\times S:\lct_x(\mathfrak a_s)\leq c\} \end{equation*}
    is Zariski closed. The fiber ideal \(\mathfrak a_s\) is generated by restriction of these generators; no flatness of its zero scheme is required.

    Proof.
    We first prove closedness in the classical topology. Locally embed \(S\) in a smooth ambient parameter space and extend the finite list of generators holomorphically. Apply the semicontinuity of complex singularity exponents to
    \begin{equation*} \varphi=\frac12\log\sum_i|g_i|^2. \end{equation*}
    The function \(e^{\varphi}\) is locally Hölder continuous, so [DK01, Lemma 3.2] applies to this family. Translating the local coordinates on \(V\) reduces a moving marked point to a fixed marked point. Restriction to \(S\) proves that the displayed sublevel set is closed in the classical topology, including fibers where all generators vanish identically.
    Next we prove algebraic constructibility. Over the generic point of each irreducible component of \(S\text{,}\) resolve the ideal, and spread this resolution over a dense open subset of that component. After shrinking the open set, generic smoothness makes the resolution, its boundary components, and their intersections smooth over the base. The fiber morphisms are then log resolutions, with fixed orders of the ideal and fixed discrepancy coefficients. Over this open subset, (2.4) expresses the threshold condition as the union of the images under the proper resolution morphism of those finitely many divisors whose discrepancy-to-order ratio is at most \(c\text{.}\) These images are algebraic. If components must first be separated by a finite cover, their images remain constructible after descending the cover. The fibers on which the ideal is zero are separated by an algebraic condition.
    Repeat the argument on the closed complement. Noetherian induction gives a finite stratification, and hence constructibility of the threshold locus on \(V\times S\text{.}\) This is the generic-resolution argument used for [DK01, Theorem 3.1], applied here to ideals. In dimension zero, constructibility and closedness follow directly from the vanishing of the generators.
    A constructible subset of a complex algebraic variety that is closed in the classical topology is Zariski closed. On each irreducible component of its Zariski closure, the constructible subset contains a nonempty Zariski open subset, which is classically dense in that component. Classical closedness therefore forces the whole component to belong to the subset. This proves the lemma.
