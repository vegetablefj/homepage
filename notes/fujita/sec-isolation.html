    Chapter 9 Boundary construction and adjoint generation

    Fix a closed point \(x\in X\) and suppose, for contradiction, that \(P=K_X+(n+1)L\) is not generated at \(x\text{.}\) Propositions 6.2 and 8.1 give a positive real number \(t<n+1\) and an integer \(m\) such that \(\lambda_{m,t}(x)\) is attained and every minimizing monomial pair in this degree has center \(\{x\}\text{.}\) Keep \(t\) and \(m\) fixed throughout the section. We will construct an effective rational divisor \(B\) on a smooth projective model \(\rho:T\to X\) and a nonempty reduced divisor \(S\) whose components map to \(x\text{.}\) For each component \(F\) of \(S\text{,}\) the coefficient of \(B\) along \(F\) will equal \(\A(F)\text{.}\) For each other component of the chosen SNC divisor that meets \(S\text{,}\) this coefficient will be strictly smaller than its log discrepancy. These coefficient comparisons, together with nefness and bigness of \((n+1)\rho^*L-B\text{,}\) will produce a section of \(P\) nonzero at \(x\text{.}\)

    Section 9.1 Rational supporting data

    Choose a minimizing pair \((s_1,\ldots,s_{N_m};v)\) as in Proposition 6.2. Thus \(s_1,\ldots,s_{N_m}\) is a basis of \(R_m\text{,}\) and \(v\) is a nonzero monomial test on a projective log resolution \(\pi:Y\to X\) of the divisors \(D_i=\operatorname{div}(s_i)\text{.}\)1 Let \(E_j\) be the components of the SNC divisor consisting of the supports of \(\pi^*D_i\) and all exceptional divisors. For each component, set \(a_j=\A(E_j)\) and define
    \begin{equation*} z_j=\left(\frac{\ord_{E_j}(D_i)}{a_j}\right)_{i=1}^{N_m}. \end{equation*}
    Smoothness of \(X\) implies that every \(a_j\) is a positive integer. Consequently every \(z_j\) is a rational vector with nonnegative entries.
    Let \(Z\) be an irreducible component of an SNC stratum whose closure has image containing \(x\text{,}\) and let \(J(Z)\) index the components \(E_j\) containing \(Z\text{.}\) For \(J(Z)\ne\varnothing\text{,}\) define
    \begin{equation} C_Z=\conv\{z_j:j\in J(Z)\}.\tag{9.1} \end{equation}
    There are finitely many such indexed polytopes. The indexing matters even when two polytopes are equal, since their strata may have different images on \(X\text{.}\)
    Points of \(C_Z\) give the orders divided by discrepancy for monomial tests supported on the crossing components indexed by \(J(Z)\text{.}\) Indeed, if \(z=\sum_{j\in J(Z)}\theta_jz_j\text{,}\) where \(\theta_j\geq0\) and \(\sum_j\theta_j=1\text{,}\) assign weight \(\theta_j/a_j\) to \(E_j\text{.}\) The monomial discrepancy formula gives discrepancy one, and the order of \(D_i\) is \(z_i\text{.}\) When some \(\theta_j\) vanish, take the component of the intersection of the remaining positive-weight divisors containing \(Z\text{.}\) The closure of this component contains the closure of \(Z\text{,}\) so its image on \(X\) still contains \(x\text{.}\) Rescaling the weights realizes the same normalized vector \(z\) with any prescribed positive discrepancy.
    For the chosen minimizing test, set \(a=\A(v)>0\) and write
    \begin{equation*} q=\left(\frac{v(s_i)}{a}\right)_{i=1}^{N_m}. \end{equation*}
    The vector \(q\) belongs to the union of the polytopes (9.1). It belongs to no polytope indexed by a stratum with positive-dimensional image. To prove this, suppose that \(q\in C_Z\) for such a stratum. Realize \(q\) by the preceding monomial construction and rescale that test to discrepancy \(a\text{.}\) The constructed test has the same discrepancy and the same orders on the sections \(s_i\) as \(v\text{,}\) so its value of \(\Phi_{m,t}\) is also \(\lambda_{m,t}(x)\text{.}\) The center of this test contains the positive-dimensional image of the closure of \(Z\text{.}\) This contradicts the assertion in Proposition 8.1 that every minimizing pair has center \(\{x\}\text{.}\)

    Lemma 9.1.

    Define
    \begin{equation} b_i=\frac{t}{m} \frac{\exp(-v(s_i)/m)}{\sum_{h=1}^{N_m}\exp(-v(s_h)/m)}.\tag{9.2} \end{equation}
    Then
    \begin{equation} \begin{aligned} b\cdot q&=1,\\ m\sum_i b_i&=t<n+1. \end{aligned}\tag{9.3} \end{equation}
    Every coefficient \(b_i\) is strictly positive. For each indexed polytope containing \(q\text{,}\) one also has
    \begin{equation} b\cdot q'\leq1 \quad\text{for every }q'\in C_Z\text{ whenever }q\in C_Z.\tag{9.4} \end{equation}

    Proof.
    Positive rescaling of \(v\) preserves its center and remains an admissible test at \(x\text{.}\) Therefore the function \(c\mapsto\Phi_{m,t}(cv,(s_i))\text{,}\) for \(c>0\text{,}\) has a minimum at \(c=1\text{.}\) Since this function is positive, its logarithmic derivative also vanishes there. Using \(v(s_i)=a q_i\text{,}\) we obtain
    \begin{equation*} 0=\left.\frac{d}{dc}\log\Phi_{m,t}(cv,(s_i))\right|_{c=1} =\frac{a}{t} -\frac{a}{m} \frac{\sum_i q_i\exp(-v(s_i)/m)}{\sum_i\exp(-v(s_i)/m)} =\frac{a}{t}(1-b\cdot q). \end{equation*}
    This proves \(b\cdot q=1\text{.}\) The formula for \(b_i\) also gives strict positivity and \(m\sum_i b_i=t\text{.}\)
    Now fix \(C_Z\) containing \(q\) and fix \(q'\in C_Z\text{.}\) For \(0\leq\epsilon\leq1\text{,}\) the segment \(q_\epsilon=(1-\epsilon)q+\epsilon q'\) lies in \(C_Z\text{.}\) Choose convex representations of its endpoints and interpolate their coefficients. The resulting monomial tests have discrepancy one, and their orders on the \(D_i\) form the vector \(q_\epsilon\text{.}\) Their centers all contain \(x\text{,}\) including when some weights are zero. After rescaling to discrepancy \(a\text{,}\) their objective values are
    \begin{equation*} \Psi(\epsilon)=\frac{\exp(a/t)}{N_m} \sum_i\exp\left(-\frac{a}{m}\bigl((1-\epsilon)q_i+\epsilon q'_i\bigr)\right). \end{equation*}
    At \(\epsilon=0\) this equals the global minimum \(\lambda_{m,t}(x)\text{.}\) Thus \(\Psi'(0)\geq0\text{.}\) Dividing by \(\Psi(0)>0\) gives
    \begin{equation*} 0\leq\frac{\Psi'(0)}{\Psi(0)} =-\frac{a}{t}\,b\cdot(q'-q). \end{equation*}
    Since \(b\cdot q=1\text{,}\) this is precisely (9.4).
    The coefficients of the divisor used for vanishing must be rational. An arbitrary rational approximation to \(q\) need not preserve the supporting inequalities, and it may enter a polytope indexed by a stratum with positive-dimensional image. We therefore approximate \(q\) inside the faces on which the functional \(b\) is constant, and then approximate \(b\) inside the polyhedron of supporting functionals.

    Lemma 9.2.

    Let \(\mathcal C\) be a finite indexed family of rational polytopes in \(\R^N\text{,}\) and let \(q\) belong to their union.2 Suppose that \(b\in\R_{>0}^N\) satisfies
    \begin{equation*} \begin{aligned} b\cdot q&=1,\\ b\cdot z&\leq1 \quad\text{for every }z\in C\text{ whenever }q\in C\in\mathcal C. \end{aligned} \end{equation*}
    For every open neighborhood \(V\) of \(b\text{,}\) there are rational vectors \(q^0\) arbitrarily close to \(q\) and \(b^0\in V\cap\Q_{>0}^N\) such that
    1. \(q^0\in C\) if and only if \(q\in C\text{,}\) for each indexed member \(C\) of \(\mathcal C\text{;}\)
    2. \(b^0\cdot q^0=1\text{,}\) and \(b^0\cdot z\leq1\) on every \(C\in\mathcal C\) containing \(q^0\text{.}\)

    Proof.
    For every \(C\) containing \(q\text{,}\) let \(F_C\) be the smallest face of \(C\) containing \(q\text{.}\) Then \(q\) lies in the relative interior of \(F_C\text{.}\) The inequality on \(C\) and the equality \(b\cdot q=1\) imply that \(F_C\) lies in the face where \(b\) has value one. Hence \(b\cdot z=1\) for all \(z\in F_C\text{.}\)
    The intersection
    \begin{equation*} F=\bigcap_{C\ni q}F_C \end{equation*}
    is a nonempty rational polytope. The point \(q\) lies in its relative interior: a sufficiently small neighborhood of \(q\) in the intersection of the affine hulls of the \(F_C\) is contained in every \(F_C\text{.}\) Rational points are dense in a rational polytope, so we may choose \(q^0\in F\cap\Q^N\) arbitrarily close to \(q\text{.}\) Choose it close enough to avoid every member of \(\mathcal C\) not containing \(q\text{.}\) Such a choice is possible because those members form a finite collection of closed sets disjoint from \(q\text{.}\) The first assertion follows, and \(b\cdot q^0=1\) because \(q^0\in F\text{.}\)
    With this rational point \(q^0\) fixed, consider all functionals \(c\) satisfying \(c\cdot q^0=1\) and \(c\cdot z\leq1\) at every vertex of every polytope containing \(q^0\text{.}\) These are finitely many rational linear constraints, so their solution set is a rational polyhedron. It contains \(b\text{.}\) The smallest face of this polyhedron containing \(b\) has rational points dense in it. Choose a rational point \(b^0\) in this face sufficiently close to \(b\) that it belongs to \(V\) and every coordinate remains positive. The vertex inequalities imply the inequalities on the entire polytopes, proving the second assertion.
    Apply Lemma 9.2 to the indexed family (9.1). Choose \(V\) small enough that \(m\sum_i c_i<n+1\) for every \(c\in V\text{.}\) This is possible by the strict inequality in (9.3). We obtain rational vectors \(q^0\in\Q_{\geq0}^{N_m}\) and \(b^0\in\Q_{>0}^{N_m}\) satisfying
    \begin{equation} \begin{aligned} b^0\cdot q^0&=1,\\ m\sum_i b_i^0&<n+1,\\ b^0\cdot q'&\leq1 \quad\text{for every }q'\in C_Z\text{ whenever }q^0\in C_Z. \end{aligned}\tag{9.5} \end{equation}
    The first conclusion of Lemma 9.2 shows that \(q^0\) belongs to no polytope indexed by a stratum with positive-dimensional image. The equality \(b^0\cdot q^0=1\) also gives \(q^0\ne0\text{.}\)

    Lemma 9.3.

    There is a prime divisor \(F_0\) over \(X\) such that its center on \(X\) is \(\{x\}\text{,}\)
    \begin{equation*} \begin{aligned} \left(\frac{\ord_{F_0}(D_i)}{\A(F_0)}\right)_{i=1}^{N_m}&=q^0,\\ \sum_j\ord_{F_0}(E_j)a_j&=\A(F_0). \end{aligned} \end{equation*}
    The divisor \(F_0\) can be retained by its strict transform on every common resolution dominating a model on which it appears.

    Proof.
    Choose an indexed polytope containing \(q^0\text{.}\) Since its vertices and \(q^0\) are rational, the linear system giving a convex representation has a rational solution. Write \(q^0=\sum_j\theta_jz_j\) with rational \(\theta_j\geq0\) and \(\sum_j\theta_j=1\text{.}\) The weights \(\theta_j/a_j\) are rational. Discard the zero weights and take the component of the remaining intersection containing the chosen stratum. These weights define a monomial test with discrepancy one whose orders on the \(D_i\) form the vector \(q^0\text{.}\)
    As in Chapter 2, clear the denominators of the positive weights and divide the resulting integers by their greatest common divisor. The resulting positive integral weights define a primitive ray in the local toroidal cone. Extracting this ray gives a prime divisor \(F_0\text{.}\) Its divisorial order is a positive multiple of the monomial test, so division by discrepancy gives the same vector \(q^0\text{.}\) The monomial discrepancy formula gives the displayed equality with the discrepancy of its retraction to \(Y\text{.}\)
    The center on \(Y\) of this monomial test has image in \(X\) containing \(x\text{.}\) Its normalized vector belongs to the polytope of its own positive-support stratum. If that image were positive-dimensional, it would give an indexed polytope with positive-dimensional image containing \(q^0\text{,}\) which has been excluded. Its center on \(X\) is therefore \(\{x\}\text{.}\) Resolution gives a smooth projective model on which the extracted divisor remains a prime divisor. On higher common resolutions, use its strict transform. With \(Y\) fixed, the section orders along \(F_0\text{,}\) its log discrepancy, and the discrepancy of its retraction to \(Y\) do not change under this replacement. The equality of discrepancies therefore persists on these models.

    Section 9.2 Equality in the discrepancy bound

    To construct \(B\text{,}\) let \(I=\{i:q_i^0>0\}\text{.}\) This set is nonempty. Choose an integer \(M>0\) such that \(M/q_i^0\) is an integer for every \(i\in I\text{,}\) and define the coherent ideal
    \begin{equation} \mathfrak c=\sum_{i\in I}\cO_X\bigl(-(M/q_i^0)D_i\bigr) \subseteq\cO_X.\tag{9.6} \end{equation}
    Since each \(D_i\) is effective, every summand is an ideal. For any prime divisor \(F\) over \(X\text{,}\) the order of this sum is the minimum of the orders of its summands. Thus the order of \(\mathfrak c\) divided by \(M\) is the minimum of the ratios \(\ord_F(D_i)/q_i^0\text{,}\) for \(i\in I\text{.}\) At \(F_0\text{,}\) each ratio equals \(\A(F_0)\text{.}\) The convex combination in (9.7) will preserve this equality. Equality in the coefficient bound will require all these ratios to be equal. The positivity of \(b_i^0\) for \(i\notin I\) will also determine the zero coordinates of the normalized vector.
    Ampleness of \(L\) gives an integer \(u\) for which \(\mathfrak c(uL)\) is globally generated; see [Stacks, Tag 01Q3, (7)]. After fixing \(u\text{,}\) take a smooth projective resolution \(\rho:T\to X\) dominating \(Y\text{,}\) retaining \(F_0\text{,}\) and principalizing \(\mathfrak c\text{.}\) Write \(\rho=\pi\circ\tau\text{,}\) where \(\tau:T\to Y\text{,}\) and write
    \begin{equation*} \mathfrak c\cO_T=\cO_T(-G). \end{equation*}
    Choose the resolution so that \(G\text{,}\) all pullbacks of the \(D_i\text{,}\) and all exceptional divisors have SNC support. Pull back the evaluation map for \(\mathfrak c(uL)\) and compose it with the map to \(\cO_T(u\rho^*L)\text{.}\) Its image is \(\cO_T(u\rho^*L-G)\text{,}\) so this line bundle is globally generated. Hence \(u\rho^*L-G\) is nef.
    For a rational number \(0<\eta<1\text{,}\) define
    \begin{equation} B=(1-\eta)\rho^*\left(\sum_i b_i^0D_i\right)+\frac{\eta}{M}G.\tag{9.7} \end{equation}
    This is an effective rational divisor with SNC support. Since \(D_i\sim mL\text{,}\) one has
    \begin{equation} \begin{aligned} (n+1)\rho^*L-B &\equiv \left(n+1-(1-\eta)m\sum_i b_i^0-\frac{\eta u}{M}\right)\rho^*L \\ &\hspace{2em}+\frac{\eta}{M}(u\rho^*L-G). \end{aligned}\tag{9.8} \end{equation}
    At \(\eta=0\text{,}\) the coefficient of \(\rho^*L\) on the right is \(n+1-m\sum_i b_i^0>0\text{.}\) Fix \(\eta\) rational and sufficiently small that this coefficient remains positive. The pullback \(\rho^*L\) is nef and big, while the second summand is nef. It follows that \((n+1)\rho^*L-B\) is nef and big. Here \(\eta\) is chosen after \(\mathfrak c\) and \(u\) have been fixed.
    For a prime divisor \(F\) on \(T\text{,}\) set \(a_F=\A(F)\) and let
    \begin{equation*} r_F=\sum_j\ord_F(E_j)a_j. \end{equation*}
    The divisors \(E_j\) in this formula are on \(Y\text{,}\) and their orders along \(F\) are computed after pullback by \(\tau\text{.}\) Lemma 2.2 gives \(0\leq r_F\leq a_F\text{.}\) When \(r_F>0\text{,}\) the number \(r_F\) is the discrepancy of the monomial retraction to \(Y\text{,}\) before rescaling. The number \(a_F\) is always a positive integer.
    Let \(S\) be the reduced sum of the primes in the chosen SNC support satisfying
    \begin{equation} \begin{aligned} x&\in\rho(F),\\ \left(\frac{\ord_F(D_i)}{a_F}\right)_{i=1}^{N_m}&=q^0,\\ r_F&=a_F. \end{aligned}\tag{9.9} \end{equation}
    The retained divisor \(F_0\) satisfies all three conditions by Lemma 9.3. It belongs to the SNC support because \(q^0\ne0\text{,}\) so at least one \(D_i\) has positive order along it. Therefore \(S\ne0\text{.}\)

    Lemma 9.4.

    Every component \(F\) of \(S\) maps to \(x\) and has coefficient \(a_F\) in \(B\text{.}\) For any prime divisor \(F\) in the chosen SNC support, if \(F\) meets \(S\) and is not a component of \(S\text{,}\) its coefficient in \(B\) is strictly smaller than its log discrepancy.

    Proof.
    Let \(F\) be a component of \(S\text{.}\) Its retraction to \(Y\) is nonzero since \(r_F=a_F>0\text{.}\) The orders of the \(D_i\) are preserved under retraction, and the equality of discrepancies shows that its normalized vector is \(q^0\text{.}\) The image of its retraction center contains \(\rho(F)\) and hence contains \(x\text{.}\) A positive-dimensional image would give a polytope (9.1) of that stratum containing \(q^0\text{,}\) contrary to its choice. Thus the image of the retraction center is \(\{x\}\text{,}\) and \(\rho(F)=\{x\}\) as well.
    Principalization and the formula for the order of a sum of ideals give, for every prime \(F\) on \(T\text{,}\)
    \begin{equation} \frac{\ord_F(G)}{M}=\min_{i\in I}\frac{\ord_F(D_i)}{q_i^0}.\tag{9.10} \end{equation}
    If \(F\) is a component of \(S\text{,}\) every ratio on the right is \(a_F\text{.}\) Moreover,
    \begin{equation*} \sum_i b_i^0\ord_F(D_i)=a_F\,b^0\cdot q^0=a_F. \end{equation*}
    Both summands of (9.7), before multiplication by \(1-\eta\) and \(\eta\text{,}\) consequently have coefficient \(a_F\text{.}\) Hence \(\operatorname{coeff}_F B=a_F\text{.}\)
    Now let \(F\) be any prime divisor in the SNC support meeting a component \(F_S\) of \(S\text{.}\) Choose a closed point in \(F\cap F_S\) and let \(y\) be its image on \(Y\text{.}\) Since \(\rho(F_S)=\{x\}\text{,}\) the point \(y\) lies over \(x\text{.}\) Let \(Z\) be the component of the stratum through \(y\) obtained from all the \(E_j\) passing through \(y\text{,}\) and let \(C=C_Z\text{.}\) The image of the closure of \(Z\) under \(\pi\) contains \(x\text{,}\) so \(C\) belongs to the indexed family (9.1).
    If \(\ord_F(E_j)>0\text{,}\) then the closed center of \(F\) on \(Y\) is contained in \(E_j\text{.}\) Since this center contains \(y\text{,}\) the divisor \(E_j\) passes through \(y\text{.}\) The same argument applies to \(F_S\text{.}\) Whenever \(r_F>0\text{,}\) the retraction formula therefore expresses
    \begin{equation*} \left(\frac{\ord_F(D_i)}{r_F}\right)_i =\sum_j\frac{a_j\ord_F(E_j)}{r_F}\,z_j \end{equation*}
    as a convex combination of vertices of \(C\text{.}\) For \(F_S\text{,}\) the discrepancy of its retraction equals \(a_{F_S}\) and its normalized vector is \(q^0\text{.}\) Thus \(q^0\in C\text{,}\) and the supporting inequality in (9.5) applies on this same polytope.
    If \(r_F=0\text{,}\) the retraction formula gives \(\ord_F(D_i)=0\) for all \(i\text{,}\) and (9.10) gives \(\ord_F(G)=0\text{.}\) Therefore \(\operatorname{coeff}_F B=0<a_F\text{.}\) Suppose now that \(r_F>0\text{,}\) and let
    \begin{equation*} \begin{aligned} q'&=\left(\frac{\ord_F(D_i)}{r_F}\right)_{i=1}^{N_m}\in C,\\ \mu&=\min_{i\in I}\frac{q'_i}{q_i^0}. \end{aligned} \end{equation*}
    The positive numbers \(b_i^0q_i^0\text{,}\) for \(i\in I\text{,}\) sum to one by (9.5). Using these numbers as weights for the ratios defining \(\mu\) gives
    \begin{equation} \mu\leq\sum_{i\in I}b_i^0q'_i\leq b^0\cdot q'\leq1.\tag{9.11} \end{equation}
    The middle inequality uses \(q'_i\geq0\) and \(b_i^0>0\) for all \(i\text{.}\) The last is the supporting inequality on \(C\text{,}\) which contains \(q^0\text{.}\)
    The coefficient in the first summand of (9.7) is \((1-\eta)r_F b^0\cdot q'\text{.}\) By (9.10), the coefficient in the second summand is \(\eta r_F\mu\text{.}\) Hence
    \begin{equation} \frac{\operatorname{coeff}_F B}{a_F} =\frac{r_F}{a_F}\bigl((1-\eta)b^0\cdot q'+\eta\mu\bigr) \leq1.\tag{9.12} \end{equation}
    All factors here are nonnegative, \(r_F/a_F\leq1\text{,}\) and both \(b^0\cdot q'\) and \(\mu\) are at most one.
    If equality holds in (9.12), then \(r_F=a_F\text{.}\) Since \(0<\eta<1\text{,}\) equality also requires \(b^0\cdot q'=\mu=1\text{.}\) Every inequality in (9.11) is then an equality. A weighted average of the ratios \(q'_i/q_i^0\text{,}\) with every weight \(b_i^0q_i^0\) positive, can equal their minimum only if every ratio equals that minimum. Thus \(q'_i/q_i^0=1\) for all \(i\in I\text{.}\) Equality in the middle inequality gives \(\sum_{i\notin I}b_i^0q'_i=0\text{,}\) so strict positivity of the \(b_i^0\) implies \(q'_i=0\) for \(i\notin I\text{.}\) Therefore \(q'=q^0\text{,}\) including the zero coordinates.
    The intersection with \(F_S\) also gives \(x\in\rho(F)\text{.}\) Together with \(r_F=a_F\) and \(q'=q^0\text{,}\) this says that \(F\) satisfies all conditions in (9.9). Hence \(F\) is a component of \(S\text{.}\) Equality in the coefficient bound therefore occurs only for components of \(S\text{,}\) proving the required strict inequality for every other component of the chosen SNC divisor that meets \(S\text{.}\)

    Section 9.3 Application of the local criterion

    Apply Lemma 3.3 to the morphism \(\rho:T\to X\) and the divisors \(B\) and \(S\) just constructed. The morphism is projective and birational, with \(T\) smooth. The divisor \(B\) is effective and rational with SNC support by (9.7), and \((n+1)\rho^*L-B\) is nef and big by (9.8). The divisor \(S\) is nonempty and reduced by its definition and the presence of \(F_0\text{.}\) Lemma 9.4 shows that every component \(F\) of \(S\) maps to \(x\) and has coefficient \(a_F\) in \(B\text{.}\) It also gives the strict coefficient inequality for every other prime in the chosen SNC support meeting \(S\text{.}\) For a prime outside that support, the coefficient in \(B\) is zero and \(a_F>0\text{,}\) so the required strict inequality holds there as well. All hypotheses of the local criterion are therefore satisfied.
    The criterion gives a section \(s\in H^0(X,P)\) with \(s(x)\ne0\text{,}\) contradicting the assumed failure of generation at \(x\text{.}\) The application also includes \(n=1\text{:}\) a component of \(S\) may be the nonexceptional point \(x\) itself, and the coefficient and descent calculations in Lemma 3.3 still apply. Since any failure of global generation occurs at a closed point, this proves Theorem 1.1.

    Section 9.4 All higher adjoint powers

    Corollary 9.5.

    Under the hypotheses of Theorem 1.1, the line bundle \(K_X+mL\) is globally generated for every integer \(m\geq n+1\text{.}\)

    Proof.
    Let \(r=m-n-1\text{.}\) If \(r=0\text{,}\) then \(m=n+1\) and the conclusion is Theorem 1.1. If \(r>0\text{,}\) apply that theorem to the smooth connected variety \(X\times\mathbb P^r\) of dimension \(n+r\) and the ample line bundle \(L\boxtimes\cO_{\mathbb P^r}(1)\text{.}\)1 The canonical bundle of the product is \(K_X\boxtimes\cO_{\mathbb P^r}(-r-1)\text{,}\) so the adjoint bundle in dimension \(n+r\) is
    \begin{equation*} (K_X+(n+r+1)L)\boxtimes\cO_{\mathbb P^r}(n) =(K_X+mL)\boxtimes\cO_{\mathbb P^r}(n). \end{equation*}
    This adjoint bundle is globally generated. Fix a point \(z\in\mathbb P^r\text{.}\) Restriction to \(X\times\{z\}\) gives generating sections of \(K_X+mL\) tensored with the one-dimensional fiber of \(\cO_{\mathbb P^r}(n)\) at \(z\text{.}\) Choosing a nonzero vector in that fiber identifies the restricted bundle with \(K_X+mL\text{,}\) so \(K_X+mL\) is globally generated.
