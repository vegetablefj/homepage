    Chapter 1 Fujita’s freeness conjecture

    These notes present OpenAI’s argument.1
    Let \(X\) be a smooth complex projective variety of dimension \(n\text{,}\) and let \(L\) be an ample line bundle. We prove that \(K_X+mL\) is globally generated for every integer \(m\geq n+1\text{.}\) At a hypothetical base point \(x\) of \(K_X+(n+1)L\text{,}\) the proof minimizes an exponential functional of section orders and log discrepancies at a fixed section degree. A uniform estimate for the images of restriction maps excludes positive-dimensional centers of minimizing valuations. A minimizing pair whose valuation has center \(\{x\}\) determines a rational boundary to which Kawamata–Viehweg vanishing applies.
    We use additive notation for line bundles.2 Fujita’s freeness conjecture predicts that \(K_X+(n+1)L\) is globally generated whenever \(X\) is a smooth projective variety of dimension \(n\) and \(L\) is ample [Fuj87]. No global generation or nonvanishing assumption is imposed on \(L\) itself.

    Theorem 1.1.

    Let \(X\) be a smooth connected projective variety over \(\C\) of dimension \(n\geq1\text{,}\) and let \(L\) be an ample line bundle on \(X\text{.}\) Then \(K_X+(n+1)L\) is generated by its global sections.
    Connectedness only fixes the notation: one applies the assertion separately to each connected component.3 The bound is sharp, since \(K_{\mathbb P^n}=-(n+1)\cO_{\mathbb P^n}(1)\text{.}\) Thus the adjoint bundle is trivial at \(m=n+1\text{,}\) whereas at \(m=n\) it has no nonzero sections.
    For global generation it suffices to find, at each closed point \(x\text{,}\) a global section with nonzero value in the fiber at \(x\text{.}\)4 Theorem 1.1 also gives generation for every \(m\geq n+1\text{.}\) Corollary 9.5 deduces this by applying the theorem to a product with projective space. The argument applies even when \(L\) is not globally generated.

    Section 1.1 Previous results

    Riemann–Roch proves the assertion for curves. Reider proved the surface case [Rei88], Ein–Lazarsfeld the threefold case [EL93], Kawamata the fourfold case [Kaw97], and Ye–Zhu the fivefold case [YZ20]. In arbitrary dimension, Angehrn–Siu obtained the bound \(m\geq(n^2+n+2)/2\) [AS95]. Helmke established multiplicity estimates for the centers occurring in the vanishing argument [Hel97], [Hel99]. Heier used these estimates together with the Angehrn–Siu method to obtain a bound of order \(n^{4/3}\) [Hei02]. Ghidelli–Lacini proved generation for \(m\geq n(\log\log n+2.34)\) when \(n\geq2\) [GL24, Theorem 1.1]. Han’s preprint gives \(m\geq\ceil{C_0n}\text{,}\) where \(C_0=1.77629988\ldots\) [Han26, Theorem 1.1].
    Chan announced a proof of the stronger intersection-number formulation [Cha24, Theorem 1.3].1
    The vanishing arguments of Ein–Lazarsfeld [EL93] and Kawamata [Kaw97] construct singularities at a prescribed point, reduce a center to that point, and lift a nonzero fiber value. A positive-dimensional center may be singular, and reducing its dimension uses positivity. Angehrn–Siu used a smooth moving point and semicontinuity [AS95]. Fujita estimated the ranks of restrictions to the exceptional divisor of the point blowup [Fuj93]. Helmke instead controlled the multiplicities of log canonical centers [Hel97], [Hel99].
    Here the center is constrained by a minimizing condition. The growth of restrictions to the center gives a lower bound for a tangential moment, while the first variation at a minimizer gives an incompatible upper bound. The restriction estimate is uniform over all integral subvarieties of a fixed dimension, including singular subvarieties of unbounded degree. The final section is obtained by the local discrepancy and vanishing argument of Fujita [Fuj93, §1(1.6)].
    The convex constructions are related to the initial-exponent semigroups studied by Okounkov [Oko03], Lazarsfeld–Mustaţă [LM09], and Kaveh–Khovanskii [KK12]. Boucksom–Chen study distributions of filtration jumps [BC11]. Blum–Jonsson prove attainment for valuative log canonical and stability thresholds of ample line bundles [BJ20, Theorem E]. We establish attainment separately for the exponential functional used here.

    Section 1.2 Notation and the argument

    Throughout, \(X\text{,}\) \(L\text{,}\) and \(n\) satisfy Theorem 1.1. We denote the adjoint bundle by \(P=K_X+(n+1)L\text{,}\) and write \(R_j=H^0(X,jL)\) and \(N_j=\dim R_j\text{.}\) Since \(X\) is smooth and connected, it is integral. All centers of valuations are closed centers: a valuation centered along a subvariety \(W\) is admissible at every point of \(W\text{.}\)
    The local generation criterion in Chapter 3 states the divisor conditions needed for the final lift. On a smooth model \(\rho:T\to X\text{,}\) it is enough to construct an effective rational divisor \(B\) with simple normal crossing (SNC) support such that \((n+1)\rho^*L-B\) is nef and big. There must also be a nonempty reduced divisor \(S\) whose components map to \(x\) and have coefficients in \(B\) equal to their log discrepancies. Every other prime divisor meeting \(S\) must have coefficient in \(B\) strictly less than its log discrepancy.
    Suppose that \(P\) is not generated at \(x\text{.}\) The mean of the total degrees of the ordinary initial exponents, divided by the section degree, then has lower limit strictly greater than \(n/(n+1)\text{,}\) by Proposition 4.6. This strictness is proved by analyzing the lattice equality case and lifting from the exceptional divisor of the blowup of \(x\text{.}\)
    For a positive integer \(m\) with \(N_m>0\text{,}\) a basis \(\mathbf s=(s_i)\) of \(R_m\text{,}\) and a nonzero monomial valuation \(v\text{,}\) write \(\A(v)\) for its log discrepancy, scaled in the same way as \(v\text{,}\) and consider
    \begin{equation*} \Phi_{m,t}(v,\mathbf s)=\frac1{N_m}\sum_{i=1}^{N_m}\exp\!\left(\frac{\A(v)}t-\frac{v(s_i)}m\right). \end{equation*}
    The parameter \(t\) will be fixed below \(n+1\text{.}\) The infimum of \(\Phi_{m,t}\) over bases and valuations whose closed centers contain \(x\) is denoted by \(\lambda_{m,t}(x)\text{.}\) The strict ordinary-order inequality gives \(\lambda_{m,t}(x)\leq1-\delta\) for some fixed \(\delta>0\text{,}\) uniformly in sufficiently large \(m\) and in \(t\) near \(n+1\text{.}\) Every admissible pair \((v,\mathbf s)\) satisfying \(\Phi_{m,t}\leq1-\delta/2\) has \(\A(v)\) in a fixed interval \([a_-,a_+]\subset(0,\infty)\text{,}\) uniformly in the same ranges. These bounds therefore apply to every minimizing pair and to all sufficiently late terms of every minimizing sequence.
    To obtain a minimum, Chapter 5–6 expresses simultaneous inequalities on section orders as threshold conditions on a complete flag variety. Properness of the flag variety and closedness of these loci give a single flag and an adapted basis. Retraction to a log resolution of the divisors of this basis then reduces the remaining limit to a compact simplex of monomial weights.
    In Chapter 7–8, a tangential variation at a positive-dimensional center bounds a weighted first moment from above. Sections restricted to the center give pure tangential initial exponents. Their growth, estimated uniformly and combined with the Brunn–Minkowski inequality, gives a lower bound incompatible with the variation inequality. One fixed choice of \(t<n+1\) therefore forces every minimizing center to be \(\{x\}\) in arbitrarily large degrees.
    A minimizing pair with \(c_X(v)=\{x\}\) determines a supporting functional on finitely many rational polytopes of normalized section orders. Its coefficients are proportional to the negative gradient of \(\Phi_{m,t}\) viewed as a function of these normalized orders with the discrepancy held fixed. The vanishing derivative under positive rescaling determines their normalization. Chapter 9 selects rational supporting data and perturbs them by a coherent ideal. The perturbation makes the boundary coefficient equal to the log discrepancy on each component of a reduced divisor \(S\) and gives the local conditions of Chapter 3. Kawamata–Viehweg vanishing then produces a section of \(P\) nonzero at \(x\text{.}\) This construction requires control of the boundary near \(S\) and allows the correction divisor used in descent to have negative components away from \(S\text{.}\)
