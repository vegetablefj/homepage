    Preface Preface

    Let \(X\subset\mathbb P^5_{\mathbb C}\) be a smooth cubic fourfold and let \(h=c_1(\mathcal O_X(1))\text{.}\) We have \(h^{3,1}(X)=1\text{,}\) and the Tate-twisted primitive Hodge structure \(H^4(X,\mathbb Z)_{\mathrm{prim}}(1)\) has Hodge numbers \((1,20,1)\text{.}\) We study when additional algebraic classes on \(X\) give an association with a polarized K3 surface, and how this association relates to the geometry and rationality of \(X\text{.}\)
    The variety of lines \(F(X)\) is an irreducible holomorphic symplectic fourfold of \(K3^{[2]}\) deformation type, by Beauville and Donagi [BD85]. Their incidence correspondence identifies primitive cubic cohomology, after a Tate twist and a change of sign in the pairing, with primitive second cohomology of \(F(X)\) relative to its Plücker class. Kuznetsov’s component
    \begin{equation*} \Ku(X)=\langle\mathcal O_X,\mathcal O_X(1),\mathcal O_X(2)\rangle^\perp\subset D^b(X) \end{equation*}
    is a 2-Calabi–Yau category (i.e., its Serre functor is isomorphic to \([2]\)) [Kuz10]. We also ask when \(\Ku(X)\simeq D^b(S,\alpha)\) for a projective K3 surface \(S\) and a Brauer class \(\alpha\in\operatorname{Br}(S)\text{,}\) and when such an equivalence can be chosen with \(\alpha=0\text{.}\)
    By Voisin’s Torelli theorem, the integral Hodge structure on \(H^4(X,\mathbb Z)\text{,}\) together with the cup-product pairing and the distinguished class \(h^2\text{,}\) determines \(X\) up to isomorphism [Voi86], [Voi08]. Laza [Laz10] and Looijenga [Loo09] independently described the image of the period map by excluding two arithmetic hyperplane arrangements.
    Hassett’s loci \(\mathcal C_d\) consist of cubics admitting a saturated rank-two lattice of algebraic classes containing \(h^2\text{,}\) with discriminant \(d\text{.}\) They are nonempty divisors exactly when \(d>6\) and \(d\equiv0,2\pmod6\text{.}\) His association theorem determines when the orthogonal complement of a specified label comes from the primitive cohomology of a polarized K3 surface [Has00]. Comparisons of integral Mukai lattices [AT14], [Huy17] and moduli of stable objects [BLMNPS21] give criteria for these derived equivalences. These notes explain the comparisons and their arithmetic conditions, then study the geometric rationality proofs for \(\mathcal C_{14},\mathcal C_{26},\mathcal C_{38},\mathcal C_{42}\) and the quadric-fibration criterion for cubics containing a plane.
    The intended reader knows complex projective algebraic geometry, intersection theory, Hodge structures, bounded derived categories, and integral lattice theory. Residue and Chern-class calculations, mutations, and residual-intersection arguments are given in detail. The proofs of Torelli and of the period image are explained through their principal reductions. Results from holomorphic symplectic geometry, degeneration theory, homological projective duality, and stability theory are stated in the forms used here, with their hypotheses and references.
    Chapter 1 establishes the cohomological conventions. Chapter 2 constructs \(F(X)\) and its polarization, which enter the Torelli proof in Chapter 3. The period image in Chapter 4 supplies the existence argument for special cubics and K3 associations in Chapter 5.
    Chapter 6 constructs \(\Ku(X)\) and its geometric examples. Chapter 7 combines this category with the Hassett conditions through the Mukai lattice and moduli spaces, then studies stability spaces, autoequivalences, and Fourier–Mukai partners. The geometric continuation from Chapter 5 to Chapter 8 gives the birational constructions and specialization arguments, followed by recent necessary K3 conditions for rationality and a preprint on irrationality with geometric K3 categories. Chapter 9 brings together questions about Hassett intersections, stability spaces, categorical equivalence, L-equivalence, and rationality. Appendix A contains the mutation and Serre-functor calculations.
    For rationality obstructions and further work on cubic fourfolds, see the companion Notes on rationality of cubic fourfolds. For further reading, Huybrechts’ monograph [Huy23] treats cubic hypersurfaces more broadly, Hassett’s survey [Has17] develops the connections with rationality, and Huybrechts’ update [Huy25] explains these derived equivalences and their consequences.
