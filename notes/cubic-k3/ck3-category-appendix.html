    Appendix A. Calculations with mutations and Serre functors

    The projection and rotation functors are constructed in Chapter 6. This appendix proves the projection and Serre identities used there, then computes the degree-three periodicity in the matrix-factorization description.

    Section A.1 The projection functor

    Let \(E\) be an exceptional object of \(D^b(Y)\) for a smooth projective complex variety \(Y\text{.}\) Identify \(\langle E\rangle\) with \(D^b(\C)\) by \(V\mapsto V\otimes E\text{.}\) Under this identification, the right adjoint of the inclusion \(j:\langle E\rangle\hookrightarrow D^b(Y)\) sends \(F\) to \(\operatorname{RHom}(E,F)\text{.}\) Evaluation is its adjunction counit. The triangle
    \begin{equation*} \operatorname{RHom}(E,F)\otimes E\longrightarrow F\longrightarrow L_E(F) \end{equation*}
    defines left mutation. Applying \(\operatorname{RHom}(E,-)\text{,}\) the first arrow is an isomorphism because \(\operatorname{RHom}(E,E)=\C\text{;}\) hence the last term vanishes. Thus \(L_E(F)\in E^\perp\text{.}\)
    For \(A\in E^\perp\text{,}\) apply \(\operatorname{Hom}(-,A[k])\) to the triangle. The groups from its first term vanish for all \(k\text{,}\) giving functorial isomorphisms \(\operatorname{Hom}(L_EF,A[k])\simeq\operatorname{Hom}(F,A[k])\text{.}\) This proves that mutation is the left adjoint projection to \(E^\perp\text{.}\) The functor can be constructed using the adjunction, or with the Fourier–Mukai kernel given by the cone of the evaluation map to the diagonal kernel; see [Huy23, Chapter 7, Section 1.3].1
    For the cubic collection, apply \(L_{\cO_X(2)}\text{,}\) then \(L_{\cO_X(1)}\text{,}\) then \(L_{\cO_X}\text{.}\) The second step preserves orthogonality to \(\cO_X(2)\text{,}\) since \(\operatorname{Ext}^*(\cO_X(2),\cO_X(1))=0\text{.}\) The last step preserves both previous vanishings for the same reason. The composite therefore lies in \(\Ku(X)\) and is
    \begin{equation*} \operatorname{pr}=L_{\cO_X}L_{\cO_X(1)}L_{\cO_X(2)}. \end{equation*}
    For \(A\in\Ku(X)\text{,}\) successive use of the preceding adjunction gives \(\operatorname{Hom}(\operatorname{pr}F,A)\simeq\operatorname{Hom}(F,A)\text{.}\) Thus \(\operatorname{pr}=i^*\) for the inclusion \(i:\Ku(X)\hookrightarrow D^b(X)\text{.}\) This proves the projection assertion in Section 6.1.
    The line construction there gives a useful check. The object \(I_\ell(1)\) has four global sections and is not in \(\Ku(X)\text{.}\) Mutation through \(\cO_X\) replaces it by \(F_\ell[1]\text{.}\) Applying all three mutations to \(\cO_\ell(1)\) gives \(F_\ell[2]\text{.}\) The ideal sheaf, its mutation, and its K-class have distinct roles.

    Section A.2 Restriction of Serre duality and rotation

    Proposition A.1.

    Let \(i:\mathcal A\hookrightarrow\mathcal D\) be an admissible inclusion of Hom-finite triangulated categories, and suppose \(\mathcal D\) has a Serre functor. With \(i^*\) its left adjoint, the inverse Serre functor on \(\mathcal A\) is
    \begin{equation*} S_{\mathcal A}^{-1}\simeq i^*S_{\mathcal D}^{-1}i. \end{equation*}

    Proof.
    For \(A,B\in\mathcal A\text{,}\) adjunction and Serre duality give
    \begin{equation*} \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal A}(i^*S_{\mathcal D}^{-1}iB,A)\simeq\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal D}(S_{\mathcal D}^{-1}iB,iA)\simeq\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal A}(A,B)^\vee. \end{equation*}
    These functorial isomorphisms characterize \(S_{\mathcal A}^{-1}B\text{.}\) The admissible projection gives the inverse Serre equivalence; equivalently the Serre functor itself is \(i^!S_{\mathcal D}i\text{.}\) This is the restriction theorem of [Kuz10, Section 2], applied with the specified adjoints.
    For \(F\in\Ku(X)\text{,}\) the object \(F(1)\) is right orthogonal to \(\cO_X(1)\) and \(\cO_X(2)\text{.}\) Indeed, the derived Hom complexes from those line bundles to \(F(1)\) are those from \(\cO_X\) and \(\cO_X(1)\) to \(F\text{.}\) Left mutation through \(\cO_X\) preserves these vanishings because \(\operatorname{RHom}(\cO_X(1),\cO_X)=\operatorname{RHom}(\cO_X(2),\cO_X)=0\text{.}\) Therefore \(R(F)=L_{\cO_X}(F(1))\) belongs to \(\Ku(X)\text{.}\)
    An exact autoequivalence takes the evaluation triangle for \(E\) to the evaluation triangle for its image. For tensor product \(T=(-\otimes\cO_X(1))\text{,}\) this gives \(TL_E\simeq L_{E(1)}T\text{.}\) Consequently
    \begin{equation*} R^3=L_{\cO_X}L_{\cO_X(1)}L_{\cO_X(2)}T^3=\operatorname{pr}(-\otimes\cO_X(3)) \end{equation*}
    on \(\Ku(X)\text{.}\) Since \(S_{D^b(X)}^{-1}=(-\otimes\cO_X(3))[-4]\text{,}\) the proposition proves
    \begin{equation*} S_{\Ku(X)}^{-1}\simeq R^3[-4]. \end{equation*}
    This supplies the adjunction and mutation identities used in Section 6.2.

    Section A.3 The grading relation

    For the cubic equation \(f\in\C[x_0,\ldots,x_5]\text{,}\) write a graded matrix factorization as
    \begin{equation*} P_0\xrightarrow{a}P_1\xrightarrow{b}P_0(3). \end{equation*}
    Its homological shift is
    \begin{equation*} P_1\xrightarrow{-b}P_0(3)\xrightarrow{-a(3)}P_1(3). \end{equation*}
    Applying this rule twice gives \(P_0(3)\xrightarrow{a(3)}P_1(3)\xrightarrow{b(3)}P_0(6)\text{.}\) Thus the internal shift and homological shift satisfy \((3)\simeq[2]\text{.}\)
    We use the specialization of Orlov’s equivalence stated in Section 6.2: for the smooth cubic, internal shift corresponds to rotation on the residual category. Substitution in the preceding identity gives \(S_{\Ku(X)}^{-1}\simeq[-2]\) and \(S_{\Ku(X)}\simeq[2]\text{.}\) The equivalence and the correspondence of shifts are discussed in [Orl09, Section 3] and [Huy23, Chapter 7, Sections 1.6–1.7].2
