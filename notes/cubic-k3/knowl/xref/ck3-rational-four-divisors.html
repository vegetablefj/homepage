Theorem 8.4.

Every smooth complex cubic fourfold \(X\) with \([X]\in\mathcal C_{14}\cup\mathcal C_{26}\cup\mathcal C_{38}\cup\mathcal C_{42}\) is rational.
🔗
🔗

Proof.
For each of these divisors, the incidence of the specified surfaces and cubics has a dense image, and the pairs on a nonempty open subset admit a rational parametrization. Its image therefore contains a nonempty open subset of the divisor. Fix a smooth cubic \(X\) in that divisor. In the inverse image of that divisor in the parameter space of smooth cubic equations, choose an integral curve through \(X\) whose generic point lies in this open subset. Restrict the universal cubic family to the normalization of the curve.
🔗
The generic fiber of the incidence of surfaces and auxiliary construction data is nonempty. A closed point of this fiber is defined over a finite extension of the curve’s function field. Include any choices used to parametrize the target, such as a point on the target quadric, among these auxiliary data. After normalizing the curve in that extension, the surface and the rational parametrization are defined over the new function field. The base-changed cubic family is still smooth and projective, and its generic fiber is rational over that field. The specialization theorem applies. A point above the chosen complex cubic has residue field \(\mathbb C\text{,}\) and its fiber is \(X\text{.}\) Hence \(X\) is rational.
🔗
The whole-divisor conclusions for \(d=26,38\) are [RS19a, Theorems 4 and 7], and for \(d=42\) are [RS23, Theorem 5.12]. The same specialization argument applies to \(\mathcal C_{14}\) [RS19a, Theorem 2].
