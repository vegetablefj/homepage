    Chapter 9 Further problems

    The questions below concern the components of the common Hassett intersection, stability spaces, birationality of Fourier–Mukai partners, L-equivalence, and the K3 conditions forced by rationality.

    Section 9.1 Components of the common Hassett intersection

    For the locus \(\mathcal Z\) of Section 5.5, the dimension and one geometric component are known. Gal and Nuer ask the following questions in [GN25, Introduction, p. 3].

    Question 9.1.

    Is \(\mathcal Z\) equidimensional? How many irreducible components does it have, and which algebraic lattices occur at their very general points?
    The dimension theorem specifies the largest component dimension. Gal and Nuer find at least two sixteen-dimensional components [GN25, Introduction, p. 3]; their 2026 equations describe the general member of one component [GN26, Theorem 1.1]. A classification would have to account for any components of larger codimension as well. Yang and Yu’s finite-intersection algorithm in Section 5.5 provides a lattice-theoretic approach, but the four-plane construction alone does not describe all of \(\mathcal Z\text{.}\)

    Section 9.2 Beyond the minimal algebraic Mukai lattice

    Bridgeland’s conjecture for a projective K3 surface asks whether every exact autoequivalence preserves the distinguished component and whether that component is simply connected [Bri08, Conjecture 1.2]. The Picard-rank-one theorem in Section 7.8 answers these two questions in that case. For cubic K3 categories, the following formulation separates the entire stability space from the topology of its distinguished component.

    Question 9.2.

    For every smooth complex cubic fourfold \(X\) with \(N_X\supsetneq A_2\text{,}\) is \(\operatorname{Stab}(\Ku(X))=\operatorname{Stab}^{\dagger}(\Ku(X))\text{?}\) Is the central-charge covering in the covering theorem universal?
    These are questions posed here for cubic components, motivated by the K3 conjecture. Feyzbakhsh–Pertusi’s theorem answers the whole-space question when \(N_X=A_2\text{;}\) it does not apply to the lattices specified in the question. A universal-cover description would also help determine the cohomologically trivial autoequivalences appearing in the Mukai-lattice action. Invariance of one component under all autoequivalences is weaker than connectedness of the entire space.

    Section 9.3 Equivalence and birationality

    Rotation compatibility gives projective reconstruction in Theorem 7.8. After it is forgotten, Huybrechts proposes the following birational statement [Huy25, Conjecture 2.5], retained as [BvBM26, Conjecture 1.1] in the 2026 counting paper.

    Conjecture 9.3.

    If two smooth complex cubic fourfolds have equivalent Kuznetsov components, then they are birational.
    The nonspecial case, the symplectic-involution examples, and the very general \(\mathcal C_{44}\) construction in Section 7.9 satisfy this conclusion. The conjecture imposes no compatibility with rotation.
    Brooke, Frei, and Marquand propose a related geometric assertion [BFM24, Conjecture 1.2].

    Conjecture 9.4 Brooke–Frei–Marquand.

    Let \(X,Y\) be smooth complex cubic fourfolds. If \(F(X)\) and \(F(Y)\) are birational, then \(X\) and \(Y\) are birational.
    No compatibility with the Plücker polarizations is assumed. When \(X\) is very general in a Hassett divisor, the known implication from birationality of the varieties of lines to Fourier–Mukai partnership makes this a consequence of Huybrechts’ conjecture. The \(\mathcal C_{44}\) pairs in the Enriques construction satisfy both conjectures.

    Section 9.4 L-equivalence

    Let \(\mathbb L=[\mathbb A^1]\in K_0(\operatorname{Var}_{\mathbb C})\text{.}\) We write \(X\sim_{\mathbb L}Y\) if
    \begin{equation*} \mathbb L^n([X]-[Y])=0 \end{equation*}
    for some integer \(n\geq0\) [MM26, Definition 1.1]. Meinsma and Moschetti propose the following conjecture [MM26, Conjecture 1.3], also formulated independently by Billi and Li Bassi [BLB26, Conjecture 1.6].

    Conjecture 9.5.

    If smooth complex cubic fourfolds \(X,Y\) are L-equivalent, then \(\Ku(X)\simeq\Ku(Y)\text{.}\)
    Billi and Li Bassi prove this implication when \(X\) is very general in a nonempty \(\mathcal C_d\) with \(9\nmid d\) [BLB26, Theorem 1.5]. Meinsma and Moschetti prove that every smooth cubic fourfold has only finitely many L-equivalent cubic fourfolds up to isomorphism [MM26, Theorem 5.3]. Together with Conjecture 9.3, the conjecture would imply that L-equivalent smooth cubics are birational.
    Meinsma and Moschetti also ask the following question [MM26, Question 1.6].

    Question 9.6.

    Do there exist L-equivalent smooth complex cubic fourfolds \(X,Y\) with \([X]\ne[Y]\) in \(K_0(\operatorname{Var}_{\mathbb C})\text{?}\)
    Nonisomorphic cubics in \(\mathcal C_{20}\) related by a Veronese Cremona transformation can already have equal classes in this ring [BLB26, proof of Proposition 3.5]. The question asks for a difference annihilated by a power of \(\mathbb L\) that is itself nonzero. For the very general \(\mathcal C_{44}\) pairs in the Enriques construction, L-equivalence is equivalent to \([X]=[Y]\) [BM26, Corollary 5.7 and Remark 5.8]. That family therefore cannot supply the examples sought in the question.

    Section 9.5 Which K3 conditions can rationality imply?

    The necessity direction of the Hassett–Kuznetsov prediction is recorded in [Huy25, Conjecture 2.2]. The results of Subsection 8.8.1 give partial answers to this assertion.

    Conjecture 9.7.

    If a smooth complex cubic fourfold \(X\) is rational, then \(\Ku(X)\simeq D^b(S)\) for some smooth projective K3 surface \(S\text{.}\)
    By Theorem 7.3 and the discriminant criteria, the conjecture asks whether rationality forces an admissible label. Guéré’s theorem excludes rational cubics with \(\operatorname{rank}A_X=1\text{.}\) Under the integral decomposition asserted in the OpenAI preprint, Corollary 8.7 proves the conjecture when \(\operatorname{rank}A_X=2\text{.}\) Assuming this decomposition, the conjecture reduces to the case \(\operatorname{rank}A_X\geq3\text{.}\)
    In that case \(\operatorname{rank}T_X\leq20\text{,}\) so the surface center supplied by the integral decomposition is not forced to have K3 minimal model by the classification for transcendental rank twenty-one. Guéré finds a K3 center, but his rational Hodge comparison does not identify it with this surviving integral summand. Comparing the two constructions, or finding another integral argument, is the additional step required for this approach. The rational divisors in Theorem 8.4 satisfy the conjecture at their higher-rank points as well. The irrational examples in Theorem 8.8 concern the opposite implication, from an admissible label to rationality.

    Question 9.8.

    For the very general cubics in the large-discriminant families considered in Theorem 8.8, can \(X\times\mathbb P^m\) be rational for some \(m\geq1\text{?}\)
    This question is posed here. The cited manuscript explicitly makes no assertion about stable rationality [OAI26, Section 1.2]; its irrationality claim concerns birational maps from \(\mathbb P^4\text{.}\)
