    Chapter 8 Rationality constructions

    Every smooth complex cubic fourfold in \(\mathcal C_{14},\mathcal C_{26},\mathcal C_{38}\text{,}\) or \(\mathcal C_{42}\) is birational to \(\mathbb P^4\text{,}\) as stated in Theorem 8.4. The explicit maps arise from residual intersections with secant curves or from kernels of alternating forms. They apply to the geometric pairs specified below; specialization gives the conclusion for every smooth member of the divisor. Projection from a plane gives a separate criterion over \(\mathbb C(\mathbb P^2)\text{.}\)

    Section 8.1 Residual points and congruences

    Let \(\mathcal H\) be an irreducible four-dimensional family of integral curves in \(\mathbb P^5\text{,}\) with universal family \(\mathcal U\) and dominant evaluation map \(\operatorname{ev}:\mathcal U\to\mathbb P^5\text{.}\) This is a congruence; its order is the degree of evaluation. An order-one congruence has birational evaluation, so there is an open set \(\Omega\subset\mathbb P^5\) over which a point determines a unique curve.

    Proposition 8.1.

    Suppose an order-one congruence has general curve \(C\simeq\mathbb P^1\) of degree \(e\text{.}\) Let \(S\subset\mathbb P^5\) meet that curve in a scheme of length \(3e-1\text{.}\) Let \(X\) be an integral cubic containing \(S\text{.}\) Assume a general curve is not contained in \(X\text{,}\) and its residual intersection point with \(X\) lies in \(\Omega\setminus S\text{.}\) Then \(X\) is birational to \(\mathcal H\text{.}\) In particular, it is rational if \(\mathcal H\) is rational.

    Proof.
    On a general curve \(C\text{,}\) the cubic equation cuts an effective divisor of degree \(3e\text{.}\) It contains \(Z=C\cap S\text{,}\) of length \(3e-1\text{,}\) so its residual divisor has length one. Over the function field of \(\mathcal H\text{,}\) this length-one scheme is a rational point. Thus residual intersection defines \(r:\mathcal H\dashrightarrow X\text{.}\) On the open set where \(r(C)\in\Omega\text{,}\) inverse evaluation recovers the unique curve \(C\text{.}\) Hence \(r\) is generically injective. Since both \(\mathcal H\) and \(X\) have dimension four, it is dominant and birational. Its inverse assigns to a general point of \(X\) the unique member of the congruence through that point.
    The geometric theorems below verify the residual-point condition. Russo and Staglianò formulate the argument using transversality to the congruence [RS19a, Theorem 1].
    Write \(\mathcal I_S\) for the ideal sheaf of the surface. Consider the forms
    \begin{equation*} H^0(\mathbb P^5,\mathcal I_S^e(3e-1)), \end{equation*}
    where the power is the ordinary product of ideal sheaves [RS19b, Section 1.1, equation (1)]. On a general curve \(C\simeq\mathbb P^1\) in the congruence, these forms vanish along \(eZ\text{,}\) where \(Z=C\cap S\text{.}\) Since
    \begin{equation*} \deg\mathcal O_C(3e-1)=e(3e-1)=\deg(eZ), \end{equation*}
    removing this fixed divisor leaves sections of a degree-zero line bundle. If some restriction is nonzero, their ratios are constant on \(C\text{,}\) so the linear system contracts \(C\text{.}\) The geometric theorems below identify its general fibers and image and prove that its restriction to a cubic is birational.
    The Pfaffian map in Section 8.3 uses kernels of alternating forms.

    Section 8.2 Quartic scrolls and quintic del Pezzo surfaces

    Let \(\Sigma\subset\mathbb P^5\) be a smooth quartic rational normal scroll. Its ideal is generated by six quadrics. The linear system of these quadrics defines
    \begin{gather*} \phi_\Sigma:\mathbb P^5\dashrightarrow Q^4\subset\mathbb P^5, \end{gather*}
    where \(Q^4\) is a smooth quadric. A general fiber is a secant line of \(\Sigma\text{,}\) and a general point of \(\mathbb P^5\) lies on exactly one such line. These assertions hold for both smooth scroll types \(S(2,2)\) and \(S(1,3)\) [BRS19, Proposition 2.1].
    For the balanced scroll \(\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1\) embedded by \(\mathcal O(1,2)\text{,}\) we can verify the fiber assertion from the minors of
    \begin{align*} M(x)=\begin{pmatrix}x_0&x_1&x_3&x_4\\x_1&x_2&x_4&x_5\end{pmatrix}. \end{align*}
    Rank one defines the scroll. The six minors \(p_{ij}\) satisfy the Plücker equation \(p_{12}p_{34}-p_{13}p_{24}+p_{14}p_{23}=0\text{,}\) giving the quadric target. Fix a general two-dimensional row space \(R\subset\mathbb C^4\text{.}\) Writing both rows in a basis of \(R\) gives four coefficients. The repeated entries impose two independent linear equations for general \(R\text{,}\) leaving a projective line of matrices. For example, independence holds for \(R=\langle(1,0,0,1),(0,1,1,0)\rangle\text{,}\) so it holds on a nonempty open subset of the Grassmannian. Thus the general fiber is a line in \(\mathbb P^5\text{.}\) Rank one occurs at two points on that line, since the determinant of the two row coefficients is quadratic. This proves that the fiber is a secant and that the secant through a general point is unique.
    Suppose that a smooth cubic \(X\) contains \(\Sigma\text{.}\) A general secant line meets \(\Sigma\) in a scheme of length two and meets \(X\) in degree three. The third point gives the inverse of the restriction \(\phi_\Sigma|_X\text{.}\) The blow-up gives a precise degree calculation. Write \(H\) for the pullback of a hyperplane and \(E\) for the exceptional divisor on \(\operatorname{Bl}_\Sigma\mathbb P^5\text{.}\) The map to \(Q^4\) is defined by \(2H-E\text{,}\) while the strict transform of \(X\) has class \(3H-E\text{.}\) On a general secant fiber \(C\simeq\mathbb P^1\text{,}\) these divisors have degrees zero and one. Hence the strict transform of \(X\) maps birationally onto \(Q^4\text{.}\) Projection from a point of the quadric proves that \(X\) is rational [BRS19, Theorem 2.2].
    The target quadric is the parameter space of the contracted secants. Alternatively, a general pair of points of \(\Sigma\) determines its secant, so this parameter space is birational to \(\operatorname{Sym}^2\Sigma\text{.}\)1
    A smooth quintic del Pezzo surface \(D\subset\mathbb P^5\) is the blow-up of \(\mathbb P^2\) at four general points, embedded by \(-K_D\text{.}\) Its ideal is generated by five quadrics. They define a rational map \(\phi_D:\mathbb P^5\dashrightarrow\mathbb P^4\) whose general fibers are secant lines of \(D\text{.}\) For a cubic containing \(D\text{,}\) the same divisor calculation gives a birational map to \(\mathbb P^4\text{.}\) Thus a smooth cubic containing a quintic del Pezzo surface is rational. A presentation of this classical construction is given in [Has17, Proposition 6].
    The rationality of \(\operatorname{Sym}^2D\) can be checked without the map of quadrics. It is birational to \(\operatorname{Sym}^2\mathbb P^2\text{.}\) Send an unordered pair of distinct points to their line in the dual projective plane. Over that plane, the pairs form a \(\mathbb P^2\)-bundle, since \(\operatorname{Sym}^2\mathbb P^1\simeq\mathbb P^2\text{.}\) Its function field is therefore purely transcendental of degree four.2

    Section 8.3 The Pfaffian map and the divisor of discriminant fourteen

    Let \(V\) be a six-dimensional vector space and \(L\subset\bigwedge^2V^*\) a general six-dimensional subspace. The degenerate skew forms define the Pfaffian cubic
    \begin{gather*} X=\mathbb P(L)\cap\{\operatorname{Pf}(\varphi)=0\}\subset\mathbb P(L)\simeq\mathbb P^5. \end{gather*}
    A general form on \(X\) has rank four and a two-dimensional kernel. Choose a general hyperplane \(W\subset V\text{.}\) Intersecting the kernel with \(W\) gives a line in \(W\) and a map
    \begin{gather*} X\dashrightarrow\mathbb P(W). \end{gather*}
    The map sends \([\varphi]\) to \([\ker(\varphi)\cap W]\text{.}\) For general \((L,W)\) this is birational [Has17, Section 1.6].
    The inverse is obtained by solving linear equations. For a point \([v]\in\mathbb P(W)\text{,}\) require \(\varphi(v,-)=0\) for \(\varphi\in L\text{.}\) These are five independent linear conditions on a six-dimensional vector space for general \([v]\text{,}\) so they determine one projective form. Since a skew form on a six-dimensional space has even rank, a form with a nonzero kernel has rank at most four and belongs to \(X\text{.}\) The rank-five evaluation condition is open. It is nonempty by choosing five skew forms with independently prescribed contractions against \(v\text{.}\) On the further open set where the resulting form has rank four and its kernel is not contained in \(W\text{,}\) the kernel meets \(W\) exactly in \(\mathbb Cv\text{.}\) This proves that the two maps are inverse for the general choices under consideration. Huybrechts explains the same map through the incidence variety of kernels and shows that the construction extends to every smooth Pfaffian cubic [Huy23, Chapter 6, Section 2.7].
    The orthogonal space \(L^\perp\subset\bigwedge^2V\) cuts the Grassmannian in a degree-fourteen K3 surface
    \begin{gather*} S=\operatorname{Gr}(2,V)\cap\mathbb P(L^\perp). \end{gather*}
    This is the polarized K3 surface used in the Pfaffian categorical construction. The equivalence between the Pfaffian condition and containing a smooth quintic del Pezzo surface is proved in [Bea00, Proposition 9.2(a)]. These are geometric descriptions of a dense subset of \(\mathcal C_{14}\text{.}\)

    Section 8.4 Five-secant conics for discriminants twenty-six and thirty-eight

    For discriminant twenty-six, take a general three-nodal septic scroll \(S_{26}\subset\mathbb P^5\) in the family studied by Farkas and Verra. It is obtained by projecting a smooth rational normal scroll of degree seven in \(\mathbb P^8\) from a suitable plane, producing exactly three transverse double points. A general cubic of \(\mathcal C_{26}\) contains such a scroll [FV18, Theorem 1.2]. Its degree is seven and its self-intersection in the cubic is twenty-five, giving \(3\cdot25-7^2=26\text{.}\)
    The numerical value twenty-five includes the contribution of the nodes. Let a proper morphism from a smooth surface to a smooth fourfold be injective on tangent spaces and birational onto its image, with only \(\delta\) transverse double points. The double-point formula gives self-intersection of the image class equal to the degree of the second Chern class of the normal bundle plus \(2\delta\) [Ful84, Theorem 9.3]. We apply this formula to the normalization of the scroll. On that surface, \(H^2=7\text{,}\) \(H\cdot K=-9\text{,}\) \(K^2=8\text{,}\) and \(c_2=4\text{.}\) Thus the formula gives \(42-27+8-4+2\cdot3=25\text{.}\)
    For a general scroll in this family, Russo and Staglianò prove that
    \begin{gather*} \dim H^0(\mathbb P^5,\mathcal I_{S_{26}}^2(5))=5. \end{gather*}
    The five quintics define a dominant rational map \(\Phi_{26}:\mathbb P^5\dashrightarrow\mathbb P^4\) whose general fiber is a five-secant conic to \(S_{26}\) [RS19b, Theorem 3]. This theorem supplies both the uniqueness of the conic through a general point and its rational parameter space. Restriction to a general cubic through the scroll is birational because \(3\cdot2-5=1\text{.}\)
    For discriminant thirty-eight, take the blow-up of \(\mathbb P^2\) at ten general points. With \(L\) the pullback of a line and \(E_i\) the exceptional curves, the linear system
    \begin{gather*} H=10L-3\sum_{i=1}^{10}E_i \end{gather*}
    embeds the surface as a smooth \(S_{38}\subset\mathbb P^5\) of degree ten and sectional genus six. Nuer proves that a general member of \(\mathcal C_{38}\) contains a surface in this family [Nue17, Theorem 3.1 and Table 1]. Here \(H^2=10\text{,}\) \(H\cdot K=0\text{,}\) \(K^2=-1\text{,}\) and \(c_2=13\text{.}\) The normal bundle formula gives \([S_{38}]^2=46\text{,}\) so its discriminant is \(3\cdot46-10^2=38\text{.}\)
    For general \(S_{38}\text{,}\) the space \(H^0(\mathbb P^5,\mathcal I_{S_{38}}^2(5))\) again has dimension five. Its map \(\Phi_{38}:\mathbb P^5\dashrightarrow\mathbb P^4\) has general fibers equal to five-secant conics [RS19b, Theorem 4]. The general cubic containing the surface is birational to this target. The proofs of these fiber statements use explicit equations, computations in examples, and deformation arguments.3
    For a general pair \((X,S_{38})\text{,}\) let \(\mu=\Phi_{38}|_X\text{.}\) The base locus of \(\mu^{-1}:\mathbb P^4\dashrightarrow X\) contains a smooth surface \(U\) whose minimal model is a polarized K3 surface \((S_0,\ell)\) with \(\ell^2=38\text{.}\) This description follows from the trisecant flop and the contraction of eleven exceptional curves on \(U\) [RS23, Section 3.3].
    Assume also that \(A_X=\mathbb Z h^2+\mathbb Z[S_{38}]\text{,}\) as holds for a very general \(X\in\mathcal C_{38}\text{.}\) Comparing cohomology across the common blow-up gives an integral Hodge isometry
    \begin{equation*} \langle h^2,[S_{38}]\rangle^\perp\simeq\ell^\perp(-1). \end{equation*}
    Thus \((S_0,\ell)\) is associated to this labelled cubic in the sense of Definition 5.3 [RS23, Section 3.4].

    Section 8.5 Eight-secant twisted cubics for discriminant forty-two

    Let \(V=\operatorname{Gr}(2,5)\cap\mathbb P^8\) be a smooth del Pezzo fivefold. Russo and Staglianò construct a twenty-five-dimensional family of smooth rational surfaces \(T\subset V\text{,}\) each of degree nine and sectional genus two. Abstractly the general surface is the blow-up of \(\mathbb P^2\) at four points with hyperplane class \(5L-2\sum_{i=1}^4E_i\text{.}\) Projection from a general plane of Schubert type \(\sigma_{2,2}\) in \(V\) maps it to a surface \(S_{42}\subset\mathbb P^5\) with five transverse double points [RS23, Proposition 5.6].
    The normalization has \(H^2=9\text{,}\) \(H\cdot K=-7\text{,}\) \(K^2=5\text{,}\) and \(c_2=7\text{.}\) Including the five nodes gives
    \begin{gather*} [S_{42}]^2=54-21+5-7+10=41. \end{gather*}
    The discriminant is therefore \(3\cdot41-9^2=42\text{.}\) The family of these surfaces in \(\mathbb P^5\) has dimension forty-eight. The incidence with smooth cubics dominates \(\mathcal C_{42}\) [RS23, Theorem 5.9].
    For a general surface in this family, the octics with triple vanishing along it define
    \begin{gather*} \Phi_{42}:\mathbb P^5\dashrightarrow W^4\subset\mathbb P^7. \end{gather*}
    The target \(W^4=\operatorname{Gr}(2,5)\cap\mathbb P^7\) is smooth. The general fibers are twisted cubics meeting \(S_{42}\) in length eight, and form an order-one congruence [RS23, Theorem 5.12]. Consequently the restriction to a general cubic through \(S_{42}\) is birational, since its intersection degree with a twisted cubic is nine.
    The target \(W^4\) is rational. Regard its points as lines in \(\mathbb P^4\) satisfying two linear conditions in Plücker coordinates. Fix a general hyperplane \(H\simeq\mathbb P^3\) in \(\mathbb P^4\text{.}\) A general such line meets \(H\) in one point \(p\text{.}\) Lines through \(p\) form a \(\mathbb P^3\text{,}\) and the two Plücker conditions cut out a \(\mathbb P^1\) for general \(p\text{.}\) Over an open subset of \(H\) these kernels form a rank-two vector bundle. Thus \(W^4\) is birational to a \(\mathbb P^1\)-bundle over an open subset of \(\mathbb P^3\text{,}\) proving rationality.

    Section 8.6 Rationality on the four divisors

    The preceding incidence families dominate the corresponding Hassett divisors. Their general pairs have the rational maps just constructed. The passage to an arbitrary smooth member uses the following specialization theorem.

    Theorem 8.3.

    Let \(f:\mathcal X\to B\) be a smooth projective family with geometrically integral fibers over a smooth connected curve over a field of characteristic zero. If the generic fiber is rational over \(k(B)\text{,}\) then every closed fiber is rational over its residue field.
    This is the rationality consequence of Kontsevich–Tschinkel’s birational-specialization theorem [KT19, Theorem 1], applied by comparison with \(\mathbb P^4\times B\text{.}\)

    Theorem 8.4.

    Every smooth complex cubic fourfold \(X\) with \([X]\in\mathcal C_{14}\cup\mathcal C_{26}\cup\mathcal C_{38}\cup\mathcal C_{42}\) is rational.

    Proof.
    For each of these divisors, the incidence of the specified surfaces and cubics has a dense image, and the pairs on a nonempty open subset admit a rational parametrization. Its image therefore contains a nonempty open subset of the divisor. Fix a smooth cubic \(X\) in that divisor. In the inverse image of that divisor in the parameter space of smooth cubic equations, choose an integral curve through \(X\) whose generic point lies in this open subset. Restrict the universal cubic family to the normalization of the curve.
    The generic fiber of the incidence of surfaces and auxiliary construction data is nonempty. A closed point of this fiber is defined over a finite extension of the curve’s function field. Include any choices used to parametrize the target, such as a point on the target quadric, among these auxiliary data. After normalizing the curve in that extension, the surface and the rational parametrization are defined over the new function field. The base-changed cubic family is still smooth and projective, and its generic fiber is rational over that field. The specialization theorem applies. A point above the chosen complex cubic has residue field \(\mathbb C\text{,}\) and its fiber is \(X\text{.}\) Hence \(X\) is rational.
    The whole-divisor conclusions for \(d=26,38\) are [RS19a, Theorems 4 and 7], and for \(d=42\) are [RS23, Theorem 5.12]. The same specialization argument applies to \(\mathcal C_{14}\) [RS19a, Theorem 2].
    There is also a direct geometric proof for every smooth cubic in \(\mathcal C_{14}\text{.}\) Bolognesi, Russo, and Staglianò prove that cubics in \(\mathcal C_{14}\setminus\mathcal C_8\) contain a smooth quartic scroll, and treat the remaining intersection through degenerations to reducible surfaces whose secants still have the required uniqueness [BRS19, Theorems 2.9 and 3.4].4
    The theorem concerns the cubic fourfolds parametrized by \(\mathcal C_d\text{;}\) the birational geometry of the parameter divisor \(\mathcal C_d\) is a different problem.

    Section 8.7 Projection from a plane

    Suppose that the smooth cubic \(X\) contains a plane \(P\text{.}\) Choose coordinates \(x_0,x_1,x_2,y_0,y_1,y_2\) with \(P=\{y_0=y_1=y_2=0\}\text{.}\) The cubic equation has the form \(\sum_i y_iQ_i(x,y)=0\text{.}\) A point \(t=[t_0:t_1:t_2]\) of the target of projection corresponds to the three-space with \(y_i=s t_i\text{.}\) Its intersection with \(X\) is the union of \(P\) and the residual quadric
    \begin{gather*} \sum_{i=0}^2t_iQ_i(x,st)=0. \end{gather*}
    Blowing up the plane resolves projection and gives \(q:\operatorname{Bl}_P X\to\mathbb P^2\) with quadric surface fibers. Its generic fiber is smooth: the total space is smooth, and generic smoothness applies to this dominant morphism in characteristic zero.
    A rational section gives a point on the generic quadric over \(K=\mathbb C(\mathbb P^2)\text{.}\) Projection from that point makes the smooth quadric birational to \(\mathbb P^2_K\text{.}\) Hence \(\mathbb C(X)\simeq K(u,v)\) is purely transcendental of degree four over \(\mathbb C\text{.}\)
    We use Springer’s theorem in the following form: over a field of characteristic different from two, a smooth projective quadric with a point over a finite extension of odd degree already has a point over the original field. Consequently a zero-cycle of odd degree suffices. A zero-cycle of odd degree contains a closed point of odd degree among its terms. For this fibration such a point is the generic point of an odd-degree multisection, obtained by closure. This is the quadratic-form input in Hassett’s criterion [Has99, Theorem 3.1].

    Proposition 8.5.

    Let \(X\) be a smooth complex cubic fourfold containing a plane \(P\text{,}\) and put \(Q=h^2-[P]\text{.}\) If there exists \(\gamma\in A_X\) with \((\gamma,Q)\) odd, then \(X\) is rational.

    Proof.
    The integral Hodge theorem represents \(\gamma\) by an algebraic codimension-two cycle. Hassett’s geometric criterion identifies its odd intersection with \(Q\) with an odd-degree zero-cycle on the generic quadric [Has99, Theorem 3.1]. This comparison includes the change from \(X\) to the blow-up of the plane. Springer’s theorem gives a rational point on the generic quadric, hence a rational section of \(q\text{.}\) The preceding projection then proves rationality.
    A second plane disjoint from \(P\) has intersection one with \(Q\text{.}\) Its strict transform maps birationally to the base and gives a section directly.
    For the very general member of \(\mathcal C_8\text{,}\) the only algebraic classes are generated by \(h^2\) and \([P]\text{.}\) Their pairings with \(Q\) are two and minus two. An odd pairing therefore requires additional classes. Hassett constructs a countable collection of divisors inside \(\mathcal C_8\) satisfying the criterion, hence loci of codimension two in \(\mathcal C\) [Has99, Theorem 4.2].
    If the discriminant curve of the fibration is smooth, it is a plane sextic. Its double cover \(S\to\mathbb P^2\) is a K3 surface: the double-cover formula gives \(K_S=0\text{,}\) and \(H^1(S,\mathcal O_S)=0\text{.}\) A smooth quadric has two rulings. Their variation gives this double cover, and the lines in each ruling form a conic bundle over \(S\text{.}\) Its Brauer class \(\alpha\in\operatorname{Br}(S)[2]\) is the Clifford invariant. Vanishing of \(\alpha\) is equivalent to the existence of a rational section of \(q\text{;}\) the equivalence and its geometric proof via the two rulings are given in [Huy23, Chapter 6, Section 1.2]. See also [Kuz10, Proposition 4.7] for the relation with odd intersections.
    The K3 category of the cubic is equivalent to \(D^b(S,\alpha)\) for this construction. The condition \(\alpha\ne0\) is not an obstruction to rationality of the total fourfold. There are smooth rational Pfaffian cubics containing a plane whose Clifford invariant is nonzero [ABBVA14, Theorem 9]. Their components are also equivalent to the derived category of an untwisted K3 surface.5

    Section 8.8 Recent results on K3 conditions and rationality

    Subsection 8.8.1 K3 conditions forced by rationality

    Guéré’s preprint gives a necessary condition involving the whole primitive cohomology, including its algebraic classes [Gue26, Theorem 63].

    Theorem 8.6 Guéré.

    If a smooth complex cubic fourfold \(X\) is rational, there is a projective K3 surface \(S\) and an isomorphism of rational Hodge structures
    \begin{equation*} H^4(X,\mathbb Q)_{\mathrm{prim}}\simeq H^2(S,\mathbb Q)(-1). \end{equation*}
    The theorem implies that \(X\) is special. Indeed, an ample class on \(S\) gives a nonzero rational \((2,2)\) class in primitive cubic cohomology. Clearing denominators gives an integral Hodge class independent of \(h^2\text{.}\) More precisely, \(\rho(S)=\operatorname{rank}A_X-1\text{.}\) Guéré’s argument retains the nilpotent part of quantum multiplication through a weak factorization and finds a surface center with K3 minimal model. The resulting Hodge isomorphism is not asserted to preserve the cup product or integral lattices. The construction is discussed in the companion note on rational cohomology of a K3 surface.
    For \(\operatorname{rank}A_X=2\) and \(\operatorname{End}_{\mathrm{Hdg}}(T_X\otimes\mathbb Q)=\mathbb Q\text{,}\) Fay deduces from this comparison that rationality forces the twisted-K3 condition \((**')\) of Section 7.5 [Fay26, Theorem 1.1]. Thus Theorem 7.3 gives \(\Ku(X)\simeq D^b(S,\alpha)\) for some projective K3 surface and Brauer class. The stronger untwisted conclusion follows in this rank from the integral decomposition asserted in OpenAI’s September 24, 2026 preprint.

    Corollary 8.7 Consequence of the OpenAI preprint.

    Assume the integral decomposition in [OAI26, Theorem 3.3 and Lemma 3.8]. If a smooth complex cubic fourfold \(X\) is rational and \(\operatorname{rank}A_X=2\text{,}\) then \(d=\det A_X\) is admissible and \(\Ku(X)\simeq D^b(S)\) for some smooth projective K3 surface \(S\text{.}\)

    Proof.
    Choose a smooth weak factorization of a birational map \(\mathbb P^4\dashrightarrow X\text{.}\) Its intermediate varieties are rationally connected. The cited integral decomposition expresses their transcendental lattices as orthogonal sums of entire transcendental lattices of surface centers, shifted by \((-1)\text{,}\) and identifies each summand removed by a blowdown. Each nonzero summand has a nonzero \((3,1)\) part. Since \(h^{3,1}(X)=1\text{,}\) exactly one summand survives at \(X\text{.}\) Thus, for a smooth projective surface center \(Z\text{,}\)
    \begin{equation*} T_X\simeq T_Z(-1) \end{equation*}
    as integral Hodge lattices, with the negative of the surface cup product on the right. Here \(p_g(Z)=1\) and \(\operatorname{rank}T_Z=23-2=21\text{.}\)
    By the first assertion of [OAI26, Lemma 3.9], \(Z\) is birational to a K3 surface of Picard rank one. Point blowups preserve its transcendental lattice. Addington’s integral criterion now gives an admissible label on \(X\) [Add16, Theorem 1]. Its only saturated rank-two label is \(A_X\text{,}\) so that label has discriminant \(d\text{.}\) The categorical conclusion follows from Theorem 7.3 and Section 7.5.
    This deduction requires neither a bound on \(d\) nor a condition on \(\operatorname{End}_{\mathrm{Hdg}}(T_X\otimes\mathbb Q)\text{.}\) The latter condition enters the automorphism and counting assertions of [OAI26, Proposition 3.10], rather than the K3 classification used here.

    Subsection 8.8.2 Irrationality with geometric K3 categories

    The same OpenAI preprint states the following irrationality result [OAI26, Theorem 1.1].

    Theorem 8.8 OpenAI preprint.

    There is an integer \(d_0\) such that, for every admissible discriminant \(d>d_0\text{,}\) a very general smooth complex cubic fourfold \(X\in\mathcal C_d\) is irrational and admits an exact \(\mathbb C\)-linear equivalence
    \begin{equation*} \Ku(X)\simeq D^b(S) \end{equation*}
    for a smooth projective K3 surface \(S\text{.}\)
    The categorical equivalence follows from Theorem 7.3; the irrationality assertion is the new result claimed in the preprint. The irrationality criterion in [OAI26, Proposition 7.1] applies more generally when \(\operatorname{rank}A_X=2\text{,}\) \(d=\det A_X>d_0\text{,}\) and \(\operatorname{End}_{\mathrm{Hdg}}(T_X\otimes\mathbb Q)=\mathbb Q\text{,}\) without assuming admissibility. The threshold is ineffective, and the exceptional countable union is chosen separately in each divisor. The result would disprove sufficiency of an untwisted K3 category for rationality, while allowing rational subloci in these divisors.
    For the unbounded sequence \(d=2(n^2+n+1)\text{,}\) with \(n\geq2\) and \(d>d_0\text{,}\) the manuscript also asserts irrationality together with \(F(X)\) birational to a K3 Hilbert square [OAI26, Corollary 1.3]. Thus even the stronger condition in Addington’s Hilbert-square criterion would not suffice for rationality. The integral surface contributions and uniform Sarkisov argument are discussed in the companion Notes on rationality of cubic fourfolds, Chapters 6–7.
