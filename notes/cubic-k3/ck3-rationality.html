    Chapter 8 Rationality constructions

    Section 8.1 Secant lines and quadrics through a surface

    A birational parametrization requires a rational parameter space and a unique parameter for a general point. An intersection count can establish the second part only after the family of curves and its incidence map have been constructed. Quartic scrolls and quintic del Pezzo surfaces provide cases where both parts can be seen explicitly.
    Let \(\Sigma\subset\mathbb P^5\) be a smooth quartic rational normal scroll. Its ideal is generated by six quadrics. The linear system of these quadrics defines
    \begin{gather*} \phi_\Sigma:\mathbb P^5\dashrightarrow Q^4\subset\mathbb P^5, \end{gather*}
    where \(Q^4\) is a smooth quadric. A general fiber is a secant line of \(\Sigma\text{,}\) and a general point of \(\mathbb P^5\) lies on exactly one such line. These assertions hold for both smooth scroll types \(S(2,2)\) and \(S(1,3)\) [BRS19, Proposition 2.1].
    For the scroll \(\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1\) embedded by \(\mathcal O(1,2)\text{,}\) this map can be written using the minors of
    \begin{align*} M(x)=\begin{pmatrix}x_0&x_1&x_3&x_4\\x_1&x_2&x_4&x_5\end{pmatrix}. \end{align*}
    Rank one defines the scroll. The six minors \(p_{ij}\) satisfy the Plücker equation \(p_{12}p_{34}-p_{13}p_{24}+p_{14}p_{23}=0\text{,}\) giving the quadric target. Fix a general two-dimensional row space \(R\subset\mathbb C^4\text{.}\) Writing both rows in a basis of \(R\) gives four coefficients. The repeated entries impose two independent linear equations, leaving a projective line of matrices. Thus the general fiber is a line in \(\mathbb P^5\text{.}\) Rank one occurs at two points on that line, since the determinant of the two row coefficients is quadratic. This proves that the fiber is a secant and that the secant through a general point is unique.
    Suppose that a smooth cubic \(X\) contains \(\Sigma\text{.}\) A general secant line meets \(\Sigma\) in a scheme of length two and meets \(X\) in degree three. The third point gives the inverse of the restriction \(\phi_\Sigma|_X\text{.}\) The blow-up gives a precise degree calculation. Write \(H\) for the pullback of a hyperplane and \(E\) for the exceptional divisor on \(\operatorname{Bl}_\Sigma\mathbb P^5\text{.}\) The map to \(Q^4\) is defined by \(2H-E\text{,}\) while the strict transform of \(X\) has class \(3H-E\text{.}\) On a general secant fiber \(C\simeq\mathbb P^1\text{,}\) these divisors have degrees zero and one. Hence the strict transform of \(X\) maps birationally onto \(Q^4\text{.}\) Projection from a point of the quadric proves that \(X\) is rational [BRS19, Theorem 2.2].
    The target quadric is the parameter space of the contracted secants. Alternatively, a general pair of points of \(\Sigma\) determines its secant, so this parameter space is birational to \(\operatorname{Sym}^2\Sigma\text{.}\) The uniqueness of the secant and the rationality of the parameter space are separate statements in this proof.1
    A quintic del Pezzo surface \(D\subset\mathbb P^5\) is the blow-up of \(\mathbb P^2\) at four general points, embedded by \(-K_D\text{.}\) Its ideal is generated by five quadrics. They define a rational map \(\phi_D:\mathbb P^5\dashrightarrow\mathbb P^4\) whose general fibers are secant lines of \(D\text{.}\) For a cubic containing \(D\text{,}\) the same divisor calculation gives a birational map to \(\mathbb P^4\text{.}\) Thus a smooth cubic containing a quintic del Pezzo surface is rational. A presentation of this classical construction is given in [Has17, Proposition 6].
    The rationality of \(\operatorname{Sym}^2D\) can be checked without the map of quadrics. It is birational to \(\operatorname{Sym}^2\mathbb P^2\text{.}\) Send an unordered pair of distinct points to their line in the dual projective plane. Over that plane, the pairs form a \(\mathbb P^2\)-bundle, since \(\operatorname{Sym}^2\mathbb P^1\simeq\mathbb P^2\text{.}\) Its function field is therefore purely transcendental of degree four.2

    Section 8.2 The Pfaffian map and the divisor of discriminant fourteen

    Let \(V\) be a six-dimensional vector space and \(L\subset\bigwedge^2V^*\) a general six-dimensional subspace. The degenerate skew forms define the Pfaffian cubic
    \begin{gather*} X=\mathbb P(L)\cap\{\operatorname{Pf}(\varphi)=0\}\subset\mathbb P(L)\simeq\mathbb P^5. \end{gather*}
    A general form on \(X\) has rank four and a two-dimensional kernel. Fix a hyperplane \(W\subset V\text{.}\) Intersecting the kernel with \(W\) gives a line in \(W\) and a map
    \begin{gather*} X\dashrightarrow\mathbb P(W),\qquad[\varphi]\longmapsto[\ker(\varphi)\cap W]. \end{gather*}
    For a general Pfaffian cubic this is birational [Has17, Section 1.6].
    The inverse is obtained by solving linear equations. For a point \([v]\in\mathbb P(W)\text{,}\) require \(\varphi(v,-)=0\) for \(\varphi\in L\text{.}\) These are five independent linear conditions on a six-dimensional vector space for general \([v]\text{,}\) so they determine one projective form. Since a skew form on a six-dimensional space has even rank, a form with a nonzero kernel has rank at most four and belongs to \(X\text{.}\) Generality ensures that its kernel meets \(W\) exactly in \(\mathbb Cv\text{,}\) proving that the two maps are inverse.
    The orthogonal space \(L^\perp\subset\bigwedge^2V\) cuts the Grassmannian in a degree-fourteen K3 surface
    \begin{gather*} S=\operatorname{Gr}(2,V)\cap\mathbb P(L^\perp). \end{gather*}
    The same linear algebra relates this K3 surface to the family of quartic scrolls on the cubic. The equivalence between the Pfaffian condition and containing a smooth quintic del Pezzo surface is proved in [Bea00, Proposition 9.2(a)]. These are geometric descriptions of a dense subset of \(\mathcal C_{14}\text{.}\)
    Rationality extends to every smooth cubic in \(\mathcal C_{14}\text{.}\) Bolognesi, Russo, and Staglianò prove that cubics in \(\mathcal C_{14}\setminus\mathcal C_8\) contain a smooth quartic scroll, and treat the remaining intersection through degenerations to reducible surfaces whose secants still have the required uniqueness [BRS19, Theorems 2.9 and 3.4].3

    Section 8.3 Projection from a plane

    Suppose that \(X\) contains a plane \(P\text{.}\) Choose coordinates \(x_0,x_1,x_2,y_0,y_1,y_2\) with \(P=\{y_0=y_1=y_2=0\}\text{.}\) The cubic equation has the form \(\sum_i y_iQ_i(x,y)=0\text{.}\) A point \(t=[t_0:t_1:t_2]\) of the target of projection corresponds to the three-space with \(y_i=s t_i\text{.}\) Its intersection with \(X\) is the union of \(P\) and the residual quadric
    \begin{gather*} \sum_{i=0}^2t_iQ_i(x,st)=0. \end{gather*}
    Blowing up the plane resolves projection and gives \(q:\operatorname{Bl}_P X\to\mathbb P^2\) with quadric surface fibers.
    A rational section of \(q\) gives a point on its generic fiber over \(K=\mathbb C(\mathbb P^2)\text{.}\) A smooth quadric with a rational point is rational over its field by projection from that point. Hence \(\mathbb C(X)\simeq K(u,v)\text{,}\) which is a rational function field in four variables over \(\mathbb C\text{.}\) Springer’s theorem gives the same conclusion if the generic quadric has a zero-cycle of odd degree, or equivalently if the fibration has an odd-degree multisection.
    Let \(Q=h^2-[P]\) denote the class of a residual quadric in \(X\text{.}\) If an algebraic surface class \(\gamma\) satisfies \(\gamma\cdot Q\) odd, its strict transform gives an odd-degree multisection, or an algebraic cycle containing one. The integral Hodge conjecture lets us use any integral Hodge class with this pairing. Hassett obtains the criterion
    \begin{gather*} \gamma\in A_X,\qquad \gamma\cdot(h^2-[P])\text{ odd}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad X\text{ rational} \end{gather*}
    in [Has99, Theorem 3.1]. A second plane disjoint from \(P\) has intersection one with \(Q\) and gives a section.
    For the very general member of \(\mathcal C_8\text{,}\) the only algebraic classes are generated by \(h^2\) and \([P]\text{.}\) Their pairings with \(Q\) are two and minus two. An odd pairing therefore requires additional classes. Hassett constructs a countable collection of divisors inside \(\mathcal C_8\) satisfying the criterion, hence loci of codimension two in \(\mathcal C\) [Has99, Theorem 4.2].
    If the discriminant curve of the fibration is smooth, it is a plane sextic. Its double cover \(S\to\mathbb P^2\) is a K3 surface: the double-cover formula gives \(K_S=0\text{,}\) and \(H^1(S,\mathcal O_S)=0\text{.}\) A smooth quadric has two rulings. Their variation gives this double cover, and the lines in each ruling form a conic bundle over \(S\text{.}\) Its Brauer class \(\alpha\in\operatorname{Br}(S)[2]\) is the Clifford invariant. Vanishing of \(\alpha\) is equivalent to the existence of a rational section of \(q\text{;}\) the relation with odd intersections is discussed in [Kuz10, Proposition 4.7].
    The K3 category of the cubic is equivalent to \(D^b(S,\alpha)\) for this construction. The condition \(\alpha\ne0\) is not an obstruction to rationality of the total fourfold. There are smooth rational Pfaffian cubics containing a plane whose Clifford invariant is nonzero [ABBVA14, Theorem 9]. Their categories admit an untwisted K3 realization through a different K3 surface.4

    Section 8.4 Five-secant conics for discriminants twenty-six and thirty-eight

    A congruence of curves in \(\mathbb P^5\) is a four-dimensional irreducible parameter space \(\mathcal H\) of curves whose universal family dominates \(\mathbb P^5\) by evaluation. Its order is the degree of that generically finite evaluation map. An order-one congruence therefore has birational evaluation: exactly one curve of the family passes through a general point. Suppose that its general member is a rational curve of degree \(e\) meeting a surface \(S\) in length \(3e-1\text{.}\) A cubic containing \(S\) and transversal to the order-one congruence has one residual intersection point on each general curve. The resulting map \(\mathcal H\dashrightarrow X\) and the map assigning a curve to a general point of \(X\) are inverse. Thus rationality of \(\mathcal H\) proves rationality of \(X\) [RS19a, Theorem 1].
    The degrees of the linear systems below have the same explanation. Hypersurfaces of degree \(3e-1\) vanishing to order \(e\) on \(S\) restrict to a curve of degree \(e\) with total degree \(e(3e-1)\text{.}\) The prescribed zeros already have that degree, so the induced projective map is constant along the curve. Proving that these curves are precisely the general fibers, and that they form a congruence, requires more than this calculation.
    For discriminant twenty-six, take a general three-nodal septic scroll \(S_{26}\subset\mathbb P^5\) in the family studied by Farkas and Verra. It is obtained by projecting a smooth rational normal scroll of degree seven in \(\mathbb P^8\) from a suitable plane, producing exactly three transverse double points. A general cubic of \(\mathcal C_{26}\) contains such a scroll [FV18, Theorem 1.2]. Its degree is seven and its self-intersection in the cubic is twenty-five, giving \(3\cdot25-7^2=26\text{.}\)
    The numerical value twenty-five includes the contribution of the nodes. On the smooth normalization of the septic scroll, \(H^2=7\text{,}\) \(H\cdot K=-9\text{,}\) \(K^2=8\text{,}\) and \(c_2=4\text{.}\) The immersed self-intersection formula adds twice the number of transverse double points to the normal bundle calculation. It gives \(42-27+8-4+2\cdot3=25\text{.}\)
    For a general scroll in this family, Russo and Staglianò prove that
    \begin{gather*} \dim H^0(\mathbb P^5,\mathcal I_{S_{26}}^2(5))=5. \end{gather*}
    The five quintics define a dominant rational map \(\Phi_{26}:\mathbb P^5\dashrightarrow\mathbb P^4\) whose general fiber is a five-secant conic to \(S_{26}\) [RS19b, Theorem 3]. This theorem supplies both the uniqueness of the conic through a general point and its rational parameter space. Restriction to a general cubic through the scroll is birational because \(3\cdot2-5=1\text{.}\)
    For discriminant thirty-eight, take the blow-up of \(\mathbb P^2\) at ten general points. With \(L\) the pullback of a line and \(E_i\) the exceptional curves, the linear system
    \begin{gather*} H=10L-3\sum_{i=1}^{10}E_i \end{gather*}
    embeds the surface as a smooth \(S_{38}\subset\mathbb P^5\) of degree ten and sectional genus six. Nuer proves that a general member of \(\mathcal C_{38}\) contains a surface in this family [Nue17, Theorem 3.1 and Table 1]. Here \(H^2=10\text{,}\) \(H\cdot K=0\text{,}\) \(K^2=-1\text{,}\) and \(c_2=13\text{.}\) The normal bundle formula gives \([S_{38}]^2=46\text{,}\) so its discriminant is \(3\cdot46-10^2=38\text{.}\)
    For general \(S_{38}\text{,}\) the space \(H^0(\mathbb P^5,\mathcal I_{S_{38}}^2(5))\) again has dimension five. Its map \(\Phi_{38}:\mathbb P^5\dashrightarrow\mathbb P^4\) has general fibers equal to five-secant conics [RS19b, Theorem 4]. The general cubic containing the surface is birational to this target. The proofs establish the fiber descriptions through explicit equations and deformation arguments. We use those fiber descriptions as the precise geometric assertions, rather than infer them from the degrees of the surfaces.5

    Section 8.5 Eight-secant twisted cubics and specialization

    The construction for discriminant forty-two uses a different family of surfaces. Let \(V=\operatorname{Gr}(2,5)\cap\mathbb P^8\) be a smooth del Pezzo fivefold. Russo and Staglianò construct a twenty-five-dimensional family of smooth rational surfaces \(T\subset V\text{,}\) each of degree nine and sectional genus two. Abstractly the general surface is the blow-up of \(\mathbb P^2\) at four points with hyperplane class \(5L-2\sum_{i=1}^4E_i\text{.}\) Projection from a general plane of Schubert type \(\sigma_{2,2}\) in \(V\) maps it to a surface \(S_{42}\subset\mathbb P^5\) with five transverse double points [RS23, Proposition 5.6]. The construction of the embedding in \(V\) is part of that proposition; an arbitrary embedding of the abstract surface is not being asserted to suffice.
    The normalization has \(H^2=9\text{,}\) \(H\cdot K=-7\text{,}\) \(K^2=5\text{,}\) and \(c_2=7\text{.}\) Including the five nodes gives
    \begin{gather*} [S_{42}]^2=54-21+5-7+10=41. \end{gather*}
    The discriminant is therefore \(3\cdot41-9^2=42\text{.}\) The family of these surfaces in \(\mathbb P^5\) has dimension forty-eight. The incidence with smooth cubics dominates \(\mathcal C_{42}\) [RS23, Theorem 5.9].
    For a general surface in this family, the octics with triple vanishing along it define
    \begin{gather*} \Phi_{42}:\mathbb P^5\dashrightarrow W^4\subset\mathbb P^7,\qquad W^4=\operatorname{Gr}(2,5)\cap\mathbb P^7. \end{gather*}
    The target is smooth. The general fibers are twisted cubics meeting \(S_{42}\) in length eight, and form an order-one congruence [RS23, Theorem 5.12]. Consequently the restriction to a general cubic through \(S_{42}\) is birational, since its intersection degree with a twisted cubic is nine.
    The target \(W^4\) is rational. Regard its points as lines in \(\mathbb P^4\) satisfying two linear conditions in Plücker coordinates. Fix a general hyperplane \(H\simeq\mathbb P^3\) in \(\mathbb P^4\text{.}\) A general such line meets \(H\) in one point \(p\text{.}\) Lines through \(p\) form a \(\mathbb P^3\text{,}\) and the two Plücker conditions cut out a \(\mathbb P^1\) for general \(p\text{.}\) Over an open subset of \(H\) these kernels form a rank-two vector bundle. Thus \(W^4\) is birational to a \(\mathbb P^1\)-bundle over an open subset of \(\mathbb P^3\text{,}\) proving rationality.
    The general-member constructions must be distinguished from their extensions to whole divisors. Kontsevich and Tschinkel prove that birational types specialize in smooth proper families over a smooth curve in characteristic zero [KT19, Theorem 1]. In particular, a rational generic fiber has rational smooth special fibers. Applying this after a base change when necessary extends the rationality results to every smooth cubic in \(\mathcal C_{26},\mathcal C_{38},\mathcal C_{42}\text{.}\) The resulting statements for the first two divisors are given in [RS19a, Theorems 4 and 7], and for the third in [RS23, Theorem 5.12].
    To apply specialization at a chosen smooth member, take a curve through its moduli point whose general point belongs to the locus of the construction. A finite base change permits a family of labelled cubics and the auxiliary data over the generic point. The birational map supplied by the construction makes that generic fiber rational over the resulting function field. The special fiber is still the chosen complex cubic. This argument extends the rationality conclusion without requiring the same scroll or the same linear system to persist on every special member.
    These proofs establish rationality of the cubic fourfolds by constructing birational maps. The rationality or unirationality of the parameter space \(\mathcal C_d\) is a different question. The common K3 associations of the four divisors describe their cohomology and categories, but do not replace the curve families, their unique-incidence property, or the rational targets used here.6
