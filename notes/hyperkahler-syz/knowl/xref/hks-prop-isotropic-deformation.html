Proposition 7.7.

Let \(X\) be a compact hyperkähler manifold with \(b_2(X)\ge5\text{.}\) There is a deformation \(X_0\) of \(X\) and a nontrivial nef isotropic line bundle \(L_0\) such that \(\Pic(X_0)=\Z L_0\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗

Proof.
Let \(\Lambda=H^2(X,\Z)\text{,}\) with its integral BBF form. Write \(r=\operatorname{rk}\Lambda=b_2(X)\text{.}\) The rational quadratic space \(\Lambda_{\Q}\) has signature \((3,r-3)\text{.}\) It is indefinite and has rank at least five. Meyer’s theorem gives a nonzero rational isotropic vector; see [Ser73, Chapter IV, §3.2, Corollary 2]. Clearing denominators and dividing by the greatest common divisor of its integral coordinates gives a primitive vector \(e\in\Lambda\) with \(q_X(e)=0\text{.}\)
🔗
Consider the period domain inside \(\PP(\Lambda_{\C})\text{,}\) and impose \(q_X(\sigma,e)=0\text{.}\) The quotient
\begin{equation*} e^\perp/\R e \end{equation*}
has signature \((2,r-4)\text{.}\) It contains oriented positive two-planes, so the imposed period locus is nonempty. For each \(a\in\Lambda\setminus\Q e\text{,}\) the additional equation \(q_X(\sigma,a)=0\) cuts out a proper analytic subset of that locus. Indeed, a functional vanishing on all of \(e^\perp\) would be a multiple of \(q_X(e,-)\text{,}\) which would force \(a\in\R e\text{,}\) hence \(a\in\Q e\) since \(a\) is integral and \(e\) is primitive.
🔗
There are countably many integral vectors \(a\text{.}\) The Baire category theorem permits a period outside these proper subsets, within the relevant period component. Period surjectivity gives a marked deformation \(X_0\) of \(X\) with this period. The integral classes of type \((1,1)\) are exactly
\begin{equation*} \NS(X_0)=\Z e. \end{equation*}
Here primitivity of \(e\) is what turns the rational line into the displayed integral lattice. The exponential sequence and \(H^1(X_0,\cO_{X_0})=0\) identify \(\Pic(X_0)\) with this lattice. The period and Picard assertions are reviewed in the deformation theory in General HK.
🔗
No integral \((1,1)\)-class on \(X_0\) has negative square, because every such class is a multiple of \(e\text{.}\) The Kähler cone theorem therefore identifies the Kähler cone with the component of the positive cone containing a Kähler class \(\omega\text{.}\) In the real space \(H^{1,1}(X_0,\R)\text{,}\) the BBF form has signature \((1,r-3)\text{.}\) A nonzero isotropic vector has nonzero pairing with \(\omega\text{,}\) since \(\omega^\perp\) is negative definite. Replace \(e\) by \(-e\) if necessary so that \(q_X(e,\omega)>0\text{.}\)
🔗
The Lorentzian description of the positive cone places this \(e\) in its closure. More explicitly, \(e+\varepsilon\omega\) has positive square and positive pairing with \(\omega\) for every \(\varepsilon>0\text{.}\) It lies in the chosen positive cone and hence in the Kähler cone. Letting \(\varepsilon\to0\) proves that the line bundle \(L_0\) with \(c_1(L_0)=e\) is nef. It is nontrivial and isotropic, and it generates \(\Pic(X_0)\text{.}\)
