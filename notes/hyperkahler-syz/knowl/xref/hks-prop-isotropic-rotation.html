Proposition 2.7.

Let \((M,g,I,J,K)\) be a compact hyperkähler manifold such that \((M,J)\) is IHS. Let \(F\subset(M,J)\) be a compact connected special Lagrangian submanifold. Suppose its Poincaré dual, with the calibration orientation, is \(c\lambda^n\text{,}\) where \(c\in\R\text{,}\) \(\lambda\in H^2(M,\R)\text{,}\) and \(q_M(\lambda)=0\text{.}\) Then \(q_M(\lambda,\sigma_J)\ne0\text{.}\) Write
\begin{equation*} q_M(\lambda,\sigma_J)=\rho e^{i\phi},\qquad \rho>0, \end{equation*}
and put \(I'=\sin\phi\, I+\cos\phi\, K\) and \(K'=I'J\text{.}\) The submanifold \(F\) is \(I'\)-holomorphic Lagrangian. The class \(\lambda\) has type \((1,1)\) for \(I'\) and satisfies \(q_M(\lambda,\omega_{I'})=\rho>0\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗

Proof.
Since \(F\) is calibrated, \(\int_F\sigma_J^n\ne0\text{.}\) Formula (2.1) implies that \(c\ne0\) and \(q_M(\lambda,\sigma_J)\ne0\text{.}\) The restriction of \(\omega_J\) to \(F\) vanishes, so the same formula gives \(q_M(\lambda,\omega_J)=0\text{.}\)
🔗
The quaternionic relations give
\begin{equation*} K'=\sin\phi\,K-\cos\phi\,I. \end{equation*}
If \(\psi=\pi/2-\phi\text{,}\) then the normalized symplectic form for the unchanged complex structure \(J\text{,}\) expressed in the rotated triple, is
\begin{equation*} \sigma'_J=\omega_{K'}+i\omega_{I'}=e^{i\psi}\sigma_J. \end{equation*}
Thus \(F\) is still special Lagrangian for \(J\text{,}\) with its phase changed by \(n\psi\text{.}\) Its volume and orientation do not change.
🔗
The choice of \(\phi\) gives \(q_M(\lambda,\omega_{K'})=0\) and \(q_M(\lambda,\omega_{I'})=\rho\text{.}\) Apply (2.1) to \(\sigma'_J\) and \(\omega_{I'}\text{.}\) Since \(q_M(\lambda,\sigma'_J)=i\rho\text{,}\) it follows that
\begin{equation*} \int_F(\omega_{K'}+i\omega_{I'})^n=i^n\int_F\omega_{I'}^n. \end{equation*}
Condition (iii) of Theorem 2.5, applied to \(I',J,K'\text{,}\) makes \(F\) an \(I'\)-holomorphic Lagrangian submanifold.
🔗
The holomorphic symplectic form for \(I'\) is \(\sigma_{I'}=\omega_J+i\omega_{K'}\text{.}\) We have proved \(q_M(\lambda,\sigma_{I'})=0\text{.}\) The BBF Hodge decomposition identifies the real classes orthogonal to \(\sigma_{I'}\) and its conjugate with \(H^{1,1}(M,I';\R)\text{.}\) Hence \(\lambda\) has the required type and positive Kähler pairing.
