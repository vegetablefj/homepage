Lemma 2.3.

A real oriented \(d\)-plane is calibrated by \(\operatorname{Re}(e^{-i\theta}\Omega)\) if and only if it is Lagrangian and \(\Omega\) has positive phase \(\theta\) on it.
🔗
🔗

Proof.
Choose a real orthonormal basis \(v_1,\ldots,v_d\) of the plane. The Hermitian Gram matrix of these vectors is \(I-iA\text{,}\) where \(A_{jk}=\omega(v_j,v_k)\text{,}\) up to the harmless choice of sign in the Hermitian convention. The skew-symmetric real matrix \(A\) has eigenvalue pairs \(\pm i a_j\text{,}\) with \(|a_j|\leq1\text{,}\) and possibly a zero eigenvalue. The square of the complex determinant is
\begin{equation*} |\Omega(v_1,\ldots,v_d)|^2=\det(I-iA)=\prod_j(1-a_j^2)\leq1. \end{equation*}
Equality holds exactly when \(A=0\text{,}\) which is the Lagrangian condition. The real part after multiplication by \(e^{-i\theta}\) is one exactly when the complex number itself is \(e^{i\theta}\text{.}\) This proves the assertion and the calibration bound.
