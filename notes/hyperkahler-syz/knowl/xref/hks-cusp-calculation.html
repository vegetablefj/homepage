Lemma 3.5.

Every period in the upper half-plane component of \(D_P\subseteq D_h\) has a unique representative
\begin{equation} p(\tau)=f+\tau v-\frac a2\tau^2 e,\qquad \Im\tau>0,\tag{3.2} \end{equation}
satisfying \(q(p(\tau),e)=1\text{.}\) It obeys
\begin{equation*} q(p(\tau),p(\tau))=0,\qquad q(p(\tau),\overline{p(\tau)})=2a(\Im\tau)^2. \end{equation*}
For \(u\in\R\text{,}\) the isometry \(U_u\) given by
\begin{equation} U_ue=e,\qquad U_uv=v-au e,\qquad U_uf=f+uv-\frac{au^2}{2}e,\qquad U_u|_{P^\perp}=\operatorname{id}\tag{3.3} \end{equation}
satisfies \(U_up(\tau)=p(\tau+u)\) and \(U_uU_{u'}=U_{u+u'}\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗

Proof.
A positive period plane cannot be perpendicular to the nonzero isotropic vector \(e\text{:}\) its perpendicular in the space of signature \((2,1)\) is negative definite. Thus \(q(p,e)\ne0\text{,}\) and we can normalize it to one. Write \(p=f+\tau v+\beta e\text{.}\) Its square is \(a\tau^2+2\beta\text{,}\) which forces \(\beta=-a\tau^2/2\text{.}\) Pairing with the conjugate gives
\begin{equation*} q(p,\bar p)=a|\tau|^2-\frac a2(\tau^2+\bar\tau^2)=2a(\Im\tau)^2. \end{equation*}
Changing the sign of \(v\) chooses the component with positive imaginary part.
🔗
The images under \(U_u\) have the same pairings as \(e,v,f\text{.}\) For example,
\begin{equation*} q(U_uv,U_uf)=au-au=0,\qquad q(U_uf)=au^2-au^2=0. \end{equation*}
The pairings with \(e\) and the square of \(v\) are unchanged. Substituting these images in \(p(\tau)\) gives \(p(\tau+u)\text{;}\) substituting in the three basis vectors gives the group law.
