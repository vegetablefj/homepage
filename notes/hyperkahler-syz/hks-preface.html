    Preface Preface and conventions

    The hyperkähler SYZ conjecture asks whether a nontrivial nef line bundle of Beauville–Bogomolov square zero defines a Lagrangian fibration. The numerical evidence is immediate: its numerical dimension is half the dimension of the manifold, and its Euler characteristic is positive. Neither calculation produces a section. The recent proofs instead construct a Lagrangian torus on a deformation, identify the cohomology class of the resulting fibration, and return to the original pair.
    These notes explain this argument and the geometry around it. Chapter 1 formulates the semiampleness problem. Chapter 2 proves the rotation and cohomology calculations. Chapter 3 constructs degenerations adapted to a specified isotropic class, and Chapter 4 states the metric results that produce special Lagrangian tori. These results are combined in Chapter 5. The remaining chapters discuss the base, nonvanishing and smoothing methods, and the affine and special Kähler structures on the smooth part of a fibration.
    The September 2026 OpenAI manuscripts [OAI26a] and [OAI26b], Engel–Mauri [EM26], and the recent work cited in Chapter 4 and Chapter 7 are used at the versions specified in the bibliography. Local calculations and the deductions connecting the quoted results are proved here. The analytic existence theorem for special Lagrangian tori, the global deformation theorem for semiampleness, and the deeper assertions about singular bases are stated with their hypotheses and cited. Their complete proofs belong to the indicated sources.
    The companion Notes on hyperkähler geometry supplies the metric, BBF, deformation, Torelli, and cone theorems. We recall their conclusions when applying them. We do not develop a second account of global Torelli, mixed Hodge theory, or moduli spaces of sheaves in the middle of the SYZ proof. The additional elementary geometry needed for that proof is developed in the present note.
    An IHS manifold is a compact simply connected Kähler manifold \(X\) with \(H^0(X,\Omega_X^2)=\C\sigma\text{,}\) where \(\sigma\) is everywhere nondegenerate. For compact manifolds, hyperkähler means IHS throughout this note. A hyperkähler metric on a local model is understood in the differential-geometric sense. We write \(\dim X=2n\text{;}\) dimension means complex dimension unless real dimension is specified. Unlabelled cohomology groups have rational coefficients. The same letter denotes a closed form and its de Rham class when an integral or a pairing only depends on the class.
    The BBF bilinear form is \(q_X(-,-)\text{,}\) with \(q_X(\alpha)=q_X(\alpha,\alpha)\text{,}\) normalized to be primitive integral and positive on Kähler classes. The Fujiki constant \(c_X>0\) is defined by \(\int_X\alpha^{2n}=c_Xq_X(\alpha)^n\text{.}\) A line bundle specified in the semiampleness problem is denoted by \(L\text{,}\) its first Chern class by \(\lambda\text{,}\) and the positive primitive integral generator of its ray by \(e\text{,}\) so that \(\lambda=m e\) for an integer \(m>0\text{.}\)
    For a hyperkähler metric \(g\) and a quaternionic triple \(I,J,K\text{,}\) we use \(IJ=K\) and \(\omega_A(u,v)=g(Au,v)\text{.}\) Thus \(\sigma_I=\omega_J+i\omega_K\) and \(\sigma_J=\omega_K+i\omega_I\text{.}\) The normalized holomorphic volume form for \(J\) is \(\Omega_J=\sigma_J^n/n!\text{.}\) Special Lagrangian submanifolds carry the orientation determined by their positive calibration. For unipotent monodromy \(T\text{,}\) we set \(N=\log T\text{.}\)
