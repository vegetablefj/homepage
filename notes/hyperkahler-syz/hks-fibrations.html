    Chapter 1 Isotropic line bundles and Lagrangian fibrations

    Let \(X\) be an IHS manifold of dimension \(2n\text{.}\) A fibration gives a nef isotropic class by pulling back an ample class from its base. The converse requires a power of the line bundle to have enough global sections. We first distinguish these numerical and linear conditions, then state the semiampleness theorem whose proof occupies Chapter 7–Chapter 10.

    Section 1.1 Nefness, sections, and numerical dimension

    Definition 1.1.

    A holomorphic line bundle \(L\) on a compact Kähler manifold is nef if \(c_1(L)\) belongs to the closure of the Kähler cone. On an IHS manifold it is isotropic if \(q_X(c_1(L))=0\text{.}\) Unless stated otherwise, a nef isotropic line bundle in these notes has \(c_1(L)\ne0\text{.}\)
    On a projective manifold the definition of nefness agrees with nonnegative degree on every curve. For a general compact Kähler manifold we use the Kähler-cone definition; testing only curves does not give that definition.

    Definition 1.2.

    A line bundle \(L\) is semiample if \(L^{\otimes r}\) is generated by its global sections for some integer \(r>0\text{.}\) Its Iitaka dimension \(\kappa(L)\) is the largest dimension of the images of the rational maps defined by nonzero linear systems \(|L^{\otimes r}|\text{;}\) it is \(-\infty\) if all these systems are empty.
    For nef \(L\) on a compact Kähler \(d\)-fold, its numerical dimension is
    \begin{equation*} \nu(L)=\max\left\{k\in\{0,\ldots,d\}:\int_Xc_1(L)^k\omega^{d-k}>0\right\}, \end{equation*}
    where \(\omega\) is any Kähler class.
    The integer \(\nu(L)\) is independent of \(\omega\text{.}\) Indeed, any two Kähler forms on a compact manifold bound positive multiples of one another. For nef classes, approximation by Kähler classes allows these bounds to be wedged with their powers and integrated. Thus positivity for one Kähler form is equivalent to positivity for the other. The general inequality \(\kappa(L)\leq\nu(L)\) for a nef line bundle is recalled in General HK, Numerical dimension.

    Proposition 1.3.

    Let \(L\) be a nonzero nef isotropic line bundle on \(X\text{,}\) and put \(\lambda=c_1(L)\text{.}\) Then \(\nu(L)=n\text{.}\) More precisely, for every Kähler class \(\omega\text{,}\)
    \begin{equation*} \int_X\lambda^n\omega^n= \frac{2^nc_X}{\binom{2n}{n}}q_X(\lambda,\omega)^n>0, \end{equation*}
    and \(\int_X\lambda^k\omega^{2n-k}=0\) for \(k>n\text{.}\) Moreover, \(\chi(X,L^{\otimes r})=n+1\) for every integer \(r\text{.}\)

    Proof.
    The BBF form has signature \((1,b_2(X)-3)\) on \(H^{1,1}(X,\R)\text{.}\) A nonzero vector on the boundary of the component of the positive cone containing \(\omega\) has positive pairing with \(\omega\text{.}\) This applies to the nef isotropic vector \(\lambda\text{:}\) in Lorentz coordinates with \(\omega\) on the positive time axis, the nonzero boundary vectors in its closure have strictly positive time coordinate.
    Expand the Fujiki relation:
    \begin{equation*} \int_X(\omega+t\lambda)^{2n} =c_X\bigl(q_X(\omega)+2tq_X(\lambda,\omega)\bigr)^n. \end{equation*}
    Its coefficient of \(t^n\) gives the displayed formula. The coefficients of \(t^k\) for \(k>n\) vanish. The largest positive mixed intersection is therefore the one with \(k=n\text{.}\)
    The hyperkähler Riemann–Roch formula expresses \(\chi(X,A)\) as a polynomial in \(q_X(c_1(A))\text{,}\) with constant term \(\chi(X,\cO_X)=n+1\text{.}\) Since \(q_X(r\lambda)=0\text{,}\) this gives the Euler characteristic. We use the BBF and Riemann–Roch theorems in General HK, The BBF form and intersection numbers.
    The equality \(\chi(X,L^{\otimes r})=n+1\) includes all higher cohomology groups. For nef isotropic bundles, the usual vanishing theorem for nef and big bundles does not apply, because \(\nu(L)=n<2n\text{.}\) In particular, this equality does not establish nonvanishing of \(H^0(X,L^{\otimes r})\text{.}\)

    Example 1.4.

    Let \(f:S\to\PP^1\) be an elliptic K3 surface with a section, and let \(L=f^*\cO_{\PP^1}(1)\text{.}\) A fiber has square zero and intersects the section once, so \(c_1(L)\) is primitive. Since \(f_*\cO_S=\cO_{\PP^1}\text{,}\)
    \begin{equation*} h^0(S,L^{\otimes r})=r+1\qquad(r\geq1). \end{equation*}
    Serre duality gives \(h^2(S,L^{\otimes r})=h^0(S,L^{\otimes(-r)})=0\text{:}\) the latter class has negative intersection with a Kähler class and cannot be effective. Riemann–Roch then gives \(h^1(S,L^{\otimes r})=r-1\text{.}\) The positive Euler characteristic is constant even though both \(h^0\) and \(h^1\) grow.
    There is no additional flat ambiguity in specifying a line bundle on an IHS manifold. The exponential sequence and \(H^1(X,\cO_X)=0\) identify \(\Pic(X)\) with the integral \((1,1)\) classes in \(H^2(X,\Z)\text{.}\) The latter group is torsion free, since \(X\) is simply connected. If \(\lambda=m e\) with \(e\) primitive and \(m>0\text{,}\) then \(e\) is also an integral \((1,1)\) class and determines a line bundle \(E\text{;}\) we have \(L\cong E^{\otimes m}\text{.}\) Nefness and isotropy pass from \(\lambda\) to \(e\text{.}\)

    Section 1.2 From semiampleness to a fibration

    Definition 1.5.

    A fibration from \(X\) is a proper surjective holomorphic map \(f:X\to B\) to a normal complex space, with \(f_*\cO_X=\cO_B\) and \(0<\dim B<2n\text{.}\) It is Lagrangian if every irreducible component of every fiber, with its reduced structure, has dimension \(n\) and the restriction of \(\sigma\) to its smooth locus is zero.

    Theorem 1.6 (Matsushita).

    Every fibration from an IHS manifold is Lagrangian and equidimensional. Its base is projective and has dimension \(n\text{.}\)
    We quote the compact Kähler version from [GL14, Proposition 2.2]. The original projective results are [Mat99, Theorem 2] and [Mat01, Theorem 1]. Projectivity of the base is included; projectivity of \(X\) is not required in this version.

    Proposition 1.7.

    Let \(L\) be a nonzero nef isotropic line bundle on \(X\text{.}\) If \(L\) is semiample, then some power has the form \(L^{\otimes r}\cong f^*A\text{,}\) where \(f:X\to B\) is a Lagrangian fibration and \(A\) is ample on \(B\text{.}\) Conversely, the pullback of an ample line bundle under a fibration from \(X\) is nonzero, nef, isotropic, and semiample.

    Proof.
    Choose \(r>0\) such that \(L^{\otimes r}\) is generated by global sections. Its morphism to projective space has Stein factorization \(X\xrightarrow{f}B\to\PP^N\text{.}\) The second map is finite onto the image. Thus \(B\) is normal projective, \(f_*\cO_X=\cO_B\text{,}\) and \(L^{\otimes r}=f^*A\) for an ample line bundle \(A\) on \(B\text{.}\)
    Let \(d=\dim B\text{.}\) A positive multiple of \(c_1(f^*A)\) is represented by the pullback of a Fubini–Study form after an embedding of \(B\text{.}\) It is semipositive, has rank \(d\) at general points, and its power \(d+1\) vanishes. Consequently its numerical dimension is \(d\text{.}\) By Proposition 1.3, we obtain \(d=n\text{.}\) In particular \(f\) is a fibration, and Theorem 1.6 applies.
    Conversely, let \(f:X\to B\) be a fibration and \(A\) ample. Pulling back a sufficiently high very ample power shows semiampleness and nefness of \(f^*A\text{.}\) Its first Chern class has positive intersection with \(\omega^{2n-1}\text{,}\) hence is nonzero. Since \(\dim B<2n\text{,}\) its top power vanishes; the Fujiki relation gives \(q_X(c_1(f^*A))=0\text{.}\)
    The fibers are abelian varieties after a choice of origin. For a smooth fiber \(F\text{,}\) the normal bundle \(N_{F/X}\cong T_bB\otimes\cO_F\) is trivial. The symplectic form identifies it with \(\Omega_F^1\text{.}\) A frame of holomorphic one-forms on the compact Kähler manifold \(F\) consists of closed forms; the dual vector fields commute and integrate to a transitive \(\C^n\)-action. It follows that \(F\cong\C^n/\Gamma\) for a lattice \(\Gamma\text{.}\) Projectivity follows from projectivity of compact Lagrangian submanifolds of an IHS manifold. Without a section of \(f\text{,}\) there is no preferred origin in the fibers.
    On the open subset where \(f\) is smooth, local functions on \(B\) have commuting Hamiltonian vector fields along the fibers. This gives the holomorphic integrable system studied in Chapter 3. The construction of this smooth system and the extension of \(f\) over singular fibers are distinct parts of the geometry.

    Section 1.3 The semiampleness theorem and examples

    Theorem 1.8.

    Every nonzero nef isotropic line bundle on an IHS manifold is semiample.
    This is the strong hyperkähler SYZ statement proved in [OAI26a, Theorem 1.1] and [EM26, Theorem 1.1]. The latter theorem is stated for all nef line bundles. The proof for the isotropic case is organized in Chapter 10; it uses the metric existence theorems stated in Chapter 9 and the deformation theorem stated in Theorem 10.5.

    Corollary 1.9.

    Every nef line bundle on an IHS manifold is semiample.

    Proof.
    Let \(\lambda=c_1(L)\text{.}\) Nefness implies \(q_X(\lambda)\geq0\text{.}\) If \(\lambda=0\text{,}\) the exponential-sequence argument above gives \(L\cong\cO_X\text{.}\) If \(\lambda\ne0\) and \(q_X(\lambda)=0\text{,}\) apply Theorem 1.8.
    If \(q_X(\lambda)>0\text{,}\) the projectivity criterion for IHS manifolds makes \(X\) projective, and the Fujiki relation gives \(\int_X\lambda^{2n}>0\text{.}\) A nef line bundle with positive top self-intersection on a smooth projective variety is big. Since \(K_X\cong\cO_X\text{,}\) the base point free theorem then makes \(L\) semiample. We use the projectivity criterion in General HK and the base point free theorem in [KM98, Theorem 3.3].

    Example 1.10.

    Let \(S\to\PP^1\) be an elliptic K3 surface. Composing the Hilbert–Chow morphism with the map on symmetric powers gives
    \begin{equation*} S^{[n]}\longrightarrow S^{(n)}\longrightarrow(\PP^1)^{(n)}\cong\PP^n. \end{equation*}
    Over a divisor supported at \(n\) distinct smooth values, the fiber is the product of the corresponding elliptic curves. The holomorphic symplectic form on the Hilbert scheme restricts there to the sum of the forms from the surface factors, each of which vanishes on an elliptic curve. Thus these smooth fibers are Lagrangian. The map has connected fibers, and its pullback of \(\cO_{\PP^n}(1)\) is a nef isotropic bundle.
    Another source is a moduli space of sheaves on a projective K3 surface. For a primitive Mukai vector \(v=(0,H,s)\text{,}\) with \(H\) ample and base point free and \(v^2=H^2=2n-2\text{,}\) \(n\geq2\text{,}\) choose a polarization generic for \(v\) and assume the stable moduli space is nonempty. The smooth projective IHS space \(M(v)\) has a support map to \(|H|\cong\PP^n\text{;}\) over a smooth curve the fiber is a torsor under its Jacobian. This is the Beauville–Mukai construction. The conditions for the moduli space and support morphism are recalled in General HK, Moduli spaces of sheaves. Questions about birational changes of this construction belong to that companion account and the moduli-space references, rather than to the metric proof below.
    The name hyperkähler SYZ is used for several related assertions. In this note, Theorem 1.8 concerns a specified nef line bundle and a holomorphic fibration. The metric SYZ statement concerns special Lagrangian tori in degenerating Ricci-flat manifolds, usually on an open subset of measure tending to one. A prediction about the metric limit or an affine structure on that limit is a further assertion. The relation between these statements is developed in Part II.
