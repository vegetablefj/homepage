    Chapter 4 The elliptic coefficient spaces

    The coefficient spaces and theta bases in this chapter are those of [OAI-N, Section 3]. All section spaces on the cubics may be taken to be holomorphic; the algebraic sections obtained by normal specialization belong to them.

    Section 4.1 Nine prescribed zeros

    Keep \(r\geq10\) nonsquare and suppose that \(\mathsf U_r(d,m)\) holds with \(d/m\leq\sqrt r\text{.}\) By Lemma 3.4, \(d>3m\text{.}\) Let \(B\) be a smooth plane cubic, \(L=\cO_B(1)\text{,}\) \(M=3L\text{,}\) and \(P=a_1+\cdots+a_9\) a divisor of distinct points. Let \(\sigma_P\) be its canonical section. Define \(A=M-P\) and \(T=(d-3m)L\text{,}\) and write \(h=\deg T=3(d-3m)>0\) and \(u_0=\lfloor d/3\rfloor\text{.}\) Multiplication by \(\sigma_P\) sends \(A\) to \(M\text{.}\) Write \(w=\sigma_Pv\) for this change of fiber coordinate.
    Define spaces of coefficients by
    \begin{equation*} V_j=\begin{cases} H^0(B,T+(m-j)A), & 0\leq j\leq m,\\ \sigma_P^{j-m}H^0(B,(d-3j)L), & m<j\leq u_0. \end{cases} \end{equation*}
    The second line is an image inside \(H^0(B,T+(m-j)A)\text{,}\) not necessarily that whole space. The polynomial sections
    \begin{equation*} \W=\left\{\widetilde F(v)=\sum_{j=0}^{u_0}g_jv^j:g_j\in V_j\right\} \end{equation*}
    take values in the pullback of \(T+mA=dL-mP\) to \(\Tot(A)\text{.}\)
    1

    Proposition 4.1.

    Fix a coordinate disk in \(B\setminus P\) and a frame of \(A\) there. If \(\mathsf U_r(d,m)\) holds, then for every choice of \(q=r-9\) distinct points \(b_1,\ldots,b_q\) in that disk, a nonzero section of \(\W\) has multiplicity at least \(m\) at all the points \((b_\alpha,1)\text{.}\)

    Proof.
    Apply Proposition 3.2 with zero normal displacements at \(a_1,\ldots,a_9\) and displacements \(\sigma_P(b_\alpha)\cdot1\) at the other points. For the resulting polynomial \(F(w)=\sum f_jw^j\text{,}\) the nine zero displacements give \(f_j\in H^0(B,(d-3j)L-(m-j)P)\) for \(j<m\text{.}\) Therefore
    \begin{equation*} \widetilde F(v)=\sigma_P^{-m}F(\sigma_Pv)=\sum_j f_j\sigma_P^{j-m}v^j \end{equation*}
    extends across \(P\) and lies in \(\W\text{.}\) It is nonzero because the substitution is invertible off \(P\text{.}\) Near each \(b_\alpha\text{,}\) the substitution and multiplication by \(\sigma_P^{-m}\) are invertible, so they preserve multiplicity.

    Section 4.2 Sections on a multiplicative elliptic curve

    For \(0<\tau<1\text{,}\) let \(X_\tau=\C^*/\tau^{\Z}\text{.}\) For \(n\in\Z\) and \(\gamma\in\C^*\text{,}\) denote by \(D(n,\gamma)\) the line bundle whose sections in the covering-space frame are holomorphic functions satisfying
    \begin{equation*} f(\tau z)=\gamma z^{-n}f(z). \end{equation*}
    Tensor products add \(n\) and multiply \(\gamma\text{.}\) The theta product
    \begin{equation*} \Theta_\tau(z)=\prod_{p=0}^{\infty}(1-\tau^pz)\prod_{p=1}^{\infty}(1-\tau^p/z) \end{equation*}
    converges locally uniformly on \(\C^*\) and satisfies \(\Theta_\tau(\tau z)=-z^{-1}\Theta_\tau(z)\text{.}\) Hence \(\Theta_\tau(z/a)\) is a section of \(D(1,-a)\) with a single simple zero at the point represented by \(a\in\C^*\text{.}\) The convention and quasiperiodicity formula are [Ros20, arXiv v3, Section 1.2 and (1.5)].

    Lemma 4.2.

    For \(n>0\text{,}\) the space \(H^0(X_\tau,D(n,\gamma))\) has dimension \(n\text{.}\) If \(f(z)=\sum_{K\in\Z}c_Kz^K\) is a section, then
    \begin{equation*} \begin{aligned}c_{K+n}&=\gamma^{-1}\tau^Kc_K,\\c_{K+np}&=c_K\gamma^{-p}\tau^{pK+np(p-1)/2}.\end{aligned} \end{equation*}
    One freely chosen coefficient in each residue class modulo \(n\) gives a basis.

    Proof.
    Compare Laurent coefficients in the functional equation. Conversely, the displayed recurrence extends each chosen coefficient in both directions. Because \(0<\tau<1\) and \(n>0\text{,}\) the exponents in the resulting series have a positive quadratic term in \(p\text{.}\) The series converges normally on compact annuli and satisfies the functional equation. Distinct residue classes give independent sections.
    The bundle \(D(n,\gamma)\) has degree \(n\text{;}\) this follows from the zeros of theta products. In particular, \(D(3,\gamma)\) has three sections. The same dimension calculation after imposing one or two prescribed zeros shows that its complete linear system has no base points and separates points and tangent directions. It embeds \(X_\tau\) as a smooth plane cubic with \(\cO_B(1)=D(3,\gamma)\text{.}\) The Laurent-series description is also given in [Ros20, arXiv v3, Exercise 1.6.5].

    Section 4.3 A family of marked cubics

    Let \(q=r-9\text{,}\) \(\rho=3(d/m-3)\text{,}\) and \(a=3(\sqrt r-3)\text{.}\) Then \(0<\rho<a\text{,}\) \(h=m\rho\text{,}\) and
    \begin{equation*} q=2a+\frac{a^2}{9}. \end{equation*}
    Choose a rational number \(\Delta\) with \(0<\Delta<1/2\) and \(\rho+\Delta\rho/9<a\text{.}\) Let \(x_0=1/2\text{,}\) and choose distinct \(x_i\in(0,x_0)\text{,}\) \(1\leq i\leq9\text{,}\) such that \(\sum_i(x_0-x_i)=\Delta\text{.}\) Define \(\eta=(\sum_i x_i+a)/3\text{,}\) \(z_i=\tau^{x_i}\text{,}\) and \(z_0=\tau^{x_0}\text{.}\) For each \(\tau\text{,}\) embed \(X_\tau\) as a plane cubic using \(L=D(3,\tau^\eta)\text{,}\) and let \(P\) be the divisor of the nine points represented by \(z_i\text{.}\)
    The product \(\Pi(z)=\prod_{i=1}^9\Theta_\tau(z/z_i)\) represents \(\sigma_P\text{.}\) Its multiplier gives \(\cO_B(P)=D(9,-\tau^{\sum_i x_i})\text{,}\) and hence
    \begin{equation*} A=3L-P=D(0,-\tau^a). \end{equation*}
    This multiplier determines the exponent ranges in the theta bases. Denote by \(\W_\tau\) the resulting space \(\W\text{.}\)
    Near the point represented by \(z_0\text{,}\) use local coordinates \(z=z_0e^x\) and \(v=e^y\text{.}\) Since every \(x_i<x_0\text{,}\) the same sufficiently small \(x\)-disk avoids \(P\) for all small \(\tau\text{.}\) In the covering-space frame, a section has scalar expression
    \begin{equation*} H_F(x,y)=\sum_{j=0}^{u_0}g_j(z_0e^x)e^{jy}. \end{equation*}
    For every fixed \(\tau\) and every \(q\) distinct positions \(\xi_\alpha\) sufficiently near zero, Proposition 4.1 gives a nonzero \(F\in\W_\tau\) of multiplicity at least \(m\) at \((x,y)=(\xi_\alpha,0)\text{.}\)
