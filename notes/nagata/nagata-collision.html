    Chapter 5 Collision and the limiting matrix

    The two limiting arguments follow [OAI-N, Section 4]. The points are collided on each fixed cubic before its theta bases are compared as \(\tau\to0\text{.}\)

    Section 5.1 The collision on a fixed cubic

    For \(q=r-9\text{,}\) define the set of jet indices
    \begin{equation*} I=\{(\ell,b):0\leq b<m,\ 0\leq\ell<q(m-b)\}. \end{equation*}
    For a fixed \(\tau\text{,}\) let \(J_\tau:\W_\tau\to\C^I\) take \(F\) to the derivatives \((\partial_x^\ell\partial_y^b H_F)(0,0)\) indexed by \(I\text{.}\)

    Lemma 5.1.

    Under the hypothetical universal property, \(\ker J_\tau\ne0\) for every fixed \(0<\tau<1\text{.}\)

    Proof.
    For each positive integer \(\nu\text{,}\) choose distinct numbers \(\xi_{1,\nu},\ldots,\xi_{q,\nu}\) tending to zero. The last paragraph of Chapter 4 gives a nonzero \(F_\nu\in\W_\tau\) with multiplicity at least \(m\) at \((\xi_{\alpha,\nu},0)\text{.}\) Fix a norm on the finite-dimensional space \(\W_\tau\text{,}\) normalize \(F_\nu\text{,}\) and take a convergent subsequence. Its limit \(F_0\) has norm one.
    For each \(b<m\text{,}\) the holomorphic function \(h_{b,\nu}(x)=\partial_y^bH_{F_\nu}(x,0)\) has a zero of order at least \(m-b\) at every \(\xi_{\alpha,\nu}\text{.}\) The functions converge locally uniformly, with their derivatives, to \(h_{b,0}\text{.}\) If this limit is nonzero, the argument principle on a small circle around zero gives \(\ord_0h_{b,0}\geq q(m-b)\text{;}\) if it vanishes identically, the same derivative conditions hold. Hence all the derivatives indexed by \(I\) vanish on \(F_0\text{,}\) so \(J_\tau(F_0)=0\text{.}\)1
    The order of the limits matters. The sections in this proof are chosen after \(\tau\) is fixed. The subsequent limit \(\tau\to0\) compares matrices, not a family of selected kernel vectors.

    Section 5.2 Normalized Laurent bases

    Consider \(D(n,\gamma)\) with \(n>0\) and \(\gamma=\epsilon\tau^\sigma\text{,}\) where \(\epsilon\in\{1,-1\}\text{.}\) Set \(U=\sigma-x_0n\) and suppose \(U\notin\Z\text{.}\) Each integer \(K\) in the open interval \((U,U+n)\) represents one residue class modulo \(n\text{.}\) In the Laurent recurrence of Lemma 4.2, choose its starting coefficient to be \(\tau^{-x_0K}\text{.}\) At \(z=z_0e^x\text{,}\) the corresponding section is
    \begin{equation*} \sum_{p\in\Z}\epsilon^{-p}\tau^{p(K-U)+np(p-1)/2}e^{(K+np)x}. \end{equation*}
    The exponent of \(\tau\) is positive for \(p\ne0\text{,}\) since \(0<K-U<n\text{;}\) it grows quadratically with \(|p|\text{.}\) Thus this expression and each fixed derivative in \(x\) tend locally uniformly to \(e^{Kx}\) as \(\tau\to0\text{.}\)
    For the coefficient blocks \(V_j\text{,}\) put
    \begin{equation*} \begin{aligned} U_j&=\frac{h(a-\Delta)}9+(m-j)a,&&0\leq j\leq m,\\ n_j&=3(d-3j)=h-9(j-m),&&m<j\leq u_0,\\ U_j&=\frac{n_j(a-\Delta)}9,&&m<j\leq u_0,\ n_j>0. \end{aligned} \end{equation*}
    For \(j\leq m\text{,}\) the bundle \(T+(m-j)A\) has degree \(h\) and its normalized basis is indexed by the integers \(U_j<K<U_j+h\text{.}\) For \(j>m\) and \(n_j>0\text{,}\) normalize a basis of \(H^0(B,(d-3j)L)\) indexed by \(U_j<K<U_j+n_j\text{,}\) then multiply by \(\Pi^{j-m}\text{.}\) In either formula, the coefficient of \(\sqrt r\) in \(U_j\) is \(d-3j>0\text{,}\) while the other terms are rational. Thus none of these endpoints is integral.
    If \(3\) divides \(d\text{,}\) there is one remaining block at \(j=d/3\text{:}\) it is the image of \(H^0(B,\cO_B)=\C\) under multiplication by \(\Pi^{j-m}\text{.}\) We label this basis element by \((j,K)=(d/3,0)\text{.}\) Let \(S\) be the union of all the pairs \((j,K)\) just described. It indexes a basis \(E_{j,K,\tau}\) of \(\W_\tau\text{,}\) and
    \begin{equation*} |S|=\dim\W_\tau=\binom{d+2}{2}-9\binom{m+1}{2}. \end{equation*}
    Because \(0<x_0-x_i<1\text{,}\) the theta products satisfy \(\Pi(z_0e^x)\to1\) locally uniformly with all fixed derivatives. The normalized basis therefore has the common limit
    \begin{equation*} H_{E_{j,K,\tau}}(x,y)\longrightarrow e^{Kx+jy}. \end{equation*}
    This also holds for the degree-zero block with \(K=0\text{.}\)

    Section 5.3 The matrix

    In the basis indexed by \(S\text{,}\) the matrix of \(J_\tau\) has rows indexed by \(I\text{.}\) The preceding convergence gives its entrywise limit
    \begin{equation*} B_0=\bigl(K^\ell j^b\bigr)_{(\ell,b)\in I,\ (j,K)\in S}. \end{equation*}
    If \(B_0\) has full column rank, one of its maximal minors is nonzero. The corresponding minor of the matrix of \(J_\tau\) remains nonzero for sufficiently small positive \(\tau\text{.}\) Then \(J_\tau\) is injective, contrary to Lemma 5.1. It remains to prove the rank assertion. The passage from one-point derivative matrices to polynomial evaluation on exponent diagrams appears in [Dum07, proof of Proposition 13]. The allowed degrees here are specified by the collision index set \(I\text{.}\)
