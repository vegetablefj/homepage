    Chapter 2 Finite jets and least exponents

    Let \(k\) be an algebraically closed field. We use additive notation \(NA=A^{\otimes N}\) for powers of line bundles. For a smooth \(n\)-dimensional variety \(Y\text{,}\) a line bundle \(A\text{,}\) and \(p\in Y(k)\text{,}\) define
    \begin{equation*} J_p^\ell(A)=A_p/\mathfrak m_p^{\ell+1}A_p\qquad(\ell\geq0). \end{equation*}
    Here \(A_p\) is the stalk of the sheaf of sections. A space of sections separates jets through degree \(\ell\) at a tuple if its evaluation into the direct sum of these spaces is surjective. A section vanishes to order at least \(m\geq1\) at the tuple exactly when it lies in the kernel of evaluation into \(\bigoplus_iJ_{p_i}^{m-1}(A)\text{.}\) Local frames and changes of coordinates preserve these statements.
    1
    Write \(\mathbb T_k^n=(\mathbb G_{m,k})^n\text{.}\) For a finite set \(S\subset\Z^n\text{,}\) put
    \begin{equation*} \mathcal M(S)=\operatorname{span}_k\{z^\alpha:\alpha\in S\}. \end{equation*}
    These are spaces of Laurent polynomials on the algebraic torus. A family \((S_N)_{N\geq1}\) is graded when \(S_N+S_M\subseteq S_{N+M}\) for all positive \(N,M\text{.}\)

    Section 2.1 Rank conditions

    Lemma 2.1.

    For a fixed finite-dimensional space of sections of \(A\text{,}\) a fixed number of points, and a fixed jet degree, injectivity and surjectivity of the jet evaluation are Zariski-open conditions on the space of ordered distinct tuples. The evaluation and its rank conditions commute with field extension. Over \(\C\text{,}\) countably many such nonempty open loci in the configuration space of a smooth integral variety have nonempty intersection.

    Proof.
    The jet spaces are fibers of the sheaf of principal parts. The infinitesimal neighborhood of the diagonal gives this sheaf a filtration whose quotients are \(A\otimes\operatorname{Sym}^j\Omega_Y^1\text{,}\) for \(0\leq j\leq\ell\text{.}\) It is therefore locally free of rank \(\binom{n+\ell}{n}\text{.}\) Evaluation on a tuple is an algebraic map of vector bundles, and maximal minors give the asserted open conditions. The diagonal construction and the evaluation commute with field extension. The complex points of the configuration space form a nonempty locally compact Hausdorff space. Every proper algebraic closed subset has empty interior there, so Baire’s theorem proves the last assertion.
    These rank conditions are used in [OAI-S, Lemma 5.1] and [OAI-P, Section 2]. For the diagonal construction and its compatibility with base change, see [Sta] (Tag 0H90, Tag 0H8Z).

    Lemma 2.2.

    Fix distinct points in a complex manifold and a nonnegative jet degree \(\ell\text{.}\) Suppose a finite collection of holomorphic functions depending on \(s>0\) converges, together with its derivatives through degree \(\ell\text{,}\) near these points. If the limiting jet matrix is injective or surjective, the corresponding matrices have the same property for sufficiently small \(s\text{.}\)

    Proof.
    A maximal minor that is nonzero in the limit remains nonzero for sufficiently small \(s\text{.}\) Locally uniform convergence on slightly larger coordinate neighborhoods implies the derivative hypothesis by Cauchy’s formula. Pullbacks under local coordinate changes and multiplication of columns by nonzero scalars preserve rank.

    Section 2.2 Least exponents

    A monomial order is a total well-order on \(\Z_{\geq0}^h\) preserved by addition. For a nonzero formal series \(f\in k[[z_1,\ldots,z_h]]\text{,}\) let \(\nu(f)\) be its least exponent for such an order. The least term of a product is the product of the least terms, so \(\nu(fg)=\nu(f)+\nu(g)\text{.}\)

    Lemma 2.3.

    If \(W\subset k[[z_1,\ldots,z_h]]\) is finite-dimensional, its possible least exponents number \(\dim_kW\text{.}\) It has a basis with these distinct least exponents and least coefficients equal to one. Suppose, more generally, that \(1\leq h\leq n\) and that finite-dimensional subspaces \(W_{N,\gamma}\subset k[[z_1,\ldots,z_h]]\text{,}\) with \(\gamma\in\Z^{n-h}\text{,}\) have only finitely many nonzero terms in each degree and satisfy
    \begin{equation*} W_{N,\gamma}W_{M,\eta}\subseteq W_{N+M,\gamma+\eta}. \end{equation*}
    Then the pairs \((\nu(f),\gamma)\) form a graded family with cardinality \(\sum_\gamma\dim W_{N,\gamma}\) in degree \(N\text{.}\)

    Proof.
    Choose a series with the smallest possible least exponent and normalize its coefficient there to one. Continue in the kernel of that coefficient functional. Each step decreases the dimension by one. In any nonzero linear combination, the basis element with the smallest least exponent among those with nonzero coefficients determines the least term. This proves the cardinality assertion. For labelled spaces, apply this argument to each label and use the product formula for least exponents.

    Lemma 2.4.

    Let \(f_1,\ldots,f_q\) be nonzero formal series and let \(e_j=\nu(f_j)\text{.}\) One vector \(\lambda\in\Z_{>0}^h\) makes all \(e_j\) the unique least-weight terms in either of these orders: comparison by the first differing coordinate, or, when \(h=2\text{,}\) comparison first by \(\alpha+t\beta\) and then by \(\beta\text{,}\) for a fixed \(t>0\text{.}\)

    Proof.
    For the first order, set \(\lambda_h=1\) and recursively choose
    \begin{equation*} \lambda_i>\max_j\sum_{a>i}\lambda_a(e_j)_a. \end{equation*}
    If an exponent first differs from \(e_j\) at coordinate \(i\text{,}\) its increase there outweighs every possible decrease in later coordinates, because all exponents are nonnegative.
    For the second order, choose a weight bound larger than all selected least weights. Only finitely many nonnegative integer pairs lie below it. A sufficiently close rational \(t'>t\) preserves their strict comparisons and resolves tied first weights according to \(\beta\text{.}\) Terms above the bound have their weights increased and cannot become least. Clearing denominators of \((1,t')\) gives positive integral weights.
    The labelled statement and the integral weights are proved in [OAI-P, Lemmas 2.2 and 2.3]. The exact order is fixed across degrees, although the weights chosen for a finite-degree comparison may depend on that degree. For valuations defined by a complete flag, the cardinality statement is [LM09, Lemma 1.3].

    Section 2.3 Transfer from monomials

    Lemma 2.5.

    Fix integers \(1\leq h\leq n\text{.}\) Let \(Y\) be a smooth integral \(h\)-dimensional \(k\)-variety, and choose formal coordinates \(z\) at \(y\in Y(k)\text{.}\) Let \(f_1,\ldots,f_q\) be regular on a common affine neighborhood \(U\) of \(y\text{,}\) and let \(\gamma_j\in\Z^{n-h}\text{.}\) Suppose \(c_jz^{e_j}\text{,}\) with \(c_j\ne0\text{,}\) is the unique least term of \(f_j\) for one common positive integral weight vector \(\lambda\text{.}\) If the span of \(Z^{e_j}u^{\gamma_j}\) separates jets through degree \(\ell\geq0\) at some distinct torus tuple, then the span of \(f_ju^{\gamma_j}\) does so at a distinct tuple in \(U\times\mathbb T_k^{n-h}\text{.}\) This tuple may be chosen in any prescribed dense open subset of that product. The assertion applies to sections in a local frame.

    Proof.
    Let \((Q_i,U_i)\) be a separating tuple for the monomials. Put \(R=k[[s]]\text{,}\) \(F=k((s))\text{,}\) and
    \begin{equation*} B=R[\delta_1,\ldots,\delta_n]/(\delta_1,\ldots,\delta_n)^{\ell+1}. \end{equation*}
    For each point substitute
    \begin{equation*} z_a=s^{\lambda_a}(Q_{i,a}+\delta'_a),\qquad u_b=U_{i,b}+\delta''_b. \end{equation*}
    These substitutions are defined on the completed local ring: every \(\lambda_a\) is positive and \(B\) is \(s\)-adically complete. The Laurent substitutions are defined because each \(U_{i,b}\) is nonzero.
    2
    Use the classes of the monomials in \(\delta\) of total degree at most \(\ell\) as an \(R\)-basis. The images of the functions give a matrix over \(R\text{.}\) Divide its \(j\)th column by \(c_js^{\lambda\cdot e_j}\text{.}\) All normalized entries still lie in \(R\text{,}\) and their reductions modulo \(s\) are the monomial jet matrix. Its surjectivity gives a maximal minor nonzero modulo \(s\text{.}\) The original matrix is therefore surjective over \(F\text{.}\)
    Setting \(\delta=0\) gives actual \(F\)-points of the affine neighborhood. The substitutions factor through their jet algebras after inverting \(s\text{.}\) These algebras and \(B[1/s]\) each have dimension \(\binom{n+\ell}{n}\text{;}\) the surjectivity just proved forces the induced maps to be isomorphisms. Repeated centers would make two target blocks factor through one jet algebra and contradict surjectivity. Thus the functions separate jets at distinct \(F\)-points.
    The open locus of separating tuples is consequently nonempty. In the integral configuration space it meets the tuples in any prescribed dense open subset. Such an intersection has a \(k\)-point because \(k\) is algebraically closed. A local frame gives the line-bundle assertion. This proves the formal transfer in [OAI-P, Lemma 2.4].

    Lemma 2.6.

    Let \(W\subset\C\{z_1,\ldots,z_h\}\) be finite-dimensional, and use either order of Lemma 2.4. If the space of its least monomials has injective or surjective jets at some distinct torus tuple, then \(W\) has the same jet property at distinct points arbitrarily close to the origin.

    Proof.
    Choose a basis with distinct least terms, normalized to coefficient one, and weights \(\lambda\) exposing these terms. If \(e_j\) is the exponent of the \(j\)th basis element, then
    \begin{equation*} s^{-\lambda\cdot e_j}f_j(s^{\lambda_1}Z_1,\ldots,s^{\lambda_h}Z_h)\longrightarrow Z^{e_j}. \end{equation*}
    On any fixed bounded polydisc, absolute convergence at a small positive \(s_0\) gives a summable majorant for the rescaled series. Every term other than the selected one has positive exponent of \(s\text{,}\) so the convergence is locally uniform. Cauchy’s formula gives convergence of fixed finite derivatives. Apply Lemma 2.2 and send the test points back under the invertible dilation. Their images are distinct and tend to the origin. This is the transfer used in [OAI-S, Proposition 2.2] and [OAI-H, Lemmas 2.4 and 2.5]. For the comparison of surjective jets over \(\C\text{,}\) see [Ito13, Proposition 5.7]. Its consequence for graded linear series and Seshadri constants is [Ito13, Theorem 5.8].

    Section 2.4 Integral changes and compression

    Lemma 2.7.

    For \(B\in\operatorname{GL}_n(\Z)\text{,}\) replacing \(S_N\) by \(BS_N\) preserves cardinality and the graded inclusion. It preserves the existence of injective or surjective jets at distinct torus tuples.

    Proof.
    The lattice map is bijective and additive. The torus map \(\phi_B(z)_j=\prod_i z_i^{B_{ij}}\) is an automorphism whose pullback sends \(z^\alpha\) to \(z^{B\alpha}\text{.}\) Its inverse corresponds to the integral matrix \(B^{-1}\text{,}\) so the jet evaluations differ by coordinate changes.

    Lemma 2.8.

    Select one coordinate of a graded family and write its exponents as \((a,\gamma)\text{.}\) In each degree and label, form the row space \(W_{N,\gamma}=\operatorname{span}_k\{z^a:(a,\gamma)\in S_N\}\text{.}\) Replace the exponents of this row by \(\{\ord_{z=1}f:0\ne f\in W_{N,\gamma}\}\text{,}\) retaining the label \(\gamma\text{.}\) The resulting support sets preserve cardinality and gradedness. Surjective jets for the new monomial space imply surjective jets for the original space. If a row lies in a real interval of length \(b\text{,}\) all its new exponents lie in \([0,b]\text{.}\)

    Proof.
    Expand each row in \(z-1\text{.}\) The cardinality and product statements follow from Lemma 2.3. The jet implication follows from Lemma 2.5 with weight one. For a nonempty row, multiplication by the unit \(z^{-a_-}\text{,}\) where \(a_-\) is its smallest exponent, turns every row element into a polynomial of degree at most \(a_+-a_-\leq b\text{.}\) Its order at one is at most this degree.
    Compression of a bounding polygon replaces each interval slice by an interval of the same length beginning at zero. This gives a bound on the new exponents and commutes with real dilation. It does not assert that the compressed discrete exponents fill the interval. In characteristic \(p\text{,}\) for example, the space \(\operatorname{span}_k\{1,z^p\}\) has only the orders \(0\) and \(p\) at one. These operations are [OAI-H, Lemmas 2.6 and 2.7] and [OAI-P, Lemmas 2.5 and 2.6].
