Proposition 8.2.

Let \(V>0\text{,}\) let \(r\) be a positive integer, and let \(P\subset\mathbb R^2\) be a triangle of area \(V/2\text{.}\) Suppose that a primitive \(u\in\Z^2\) satisfies
\begin{equation*} \max_{\xi\in P}\det(u,\xi)-\min_{\xi\in P}\det(u,\xi)=\sqrt{rV}. \end{equation*}
There is a subgroup \(G\subset\mathbb T\) with exactly \(r\) points such that, for all positive integers \(N,m\) with \(m>N\sqrt{V/r}\text{,}\) the \(J^{m-1}\) evaluation on \(\mathcal M(NP)\) at \(G\) is injective.
🔗
🔗

Proof.
Complete \(u\) to an integer basis \((u,v_0)\) with \(\det(u,v_0)=1\text{.}\) Let \(v=rv_0\) and \(M=\Z u+\Z v\text{,}\) a sublattice of index \(r\text{.}\) For \(\eta=(\eta_1,\eta_2)\text{,}\) write \(\chi_\eta(x,z)=x^{\eta_1}z^{\eta_2}\text{.}\) Define
\begin{equation*} G=\{p\in\mathbb T:\chi_\eta(p)=1\text{ for every }\eta\in M\}. \end{equation*}
The characters \(X=\chi_u\) and \(Y=\chi_{v_0}\) are torus coordinates because their exponent matrix is unimodular. In these coordinates, \(G=\{X=1,\ Y^r=1\}\text{,}\) which has exactly \(r\) distinct complex points.
🔗
Suppose that \(0\ne f\in\mathcal M(NP)\) vanishes to order at least \(m\) at all points of \(G\text{.}\) For \(g\in G\text{,}\) let \((\tau_gf)(p)=f(gp)\text{.}\) Translation permutes \(G\) and preserves local orders. For a coset \(\eta+M\text{,}\) the sum
\begin{equation*} f_\eta=\frac1r\sum_{g\in G}\chi_\eta(g)^{-1}\tau_gf \end{equation*}
retains precisely the monomials in that coset. Indeed, write \(\xi-\eta=nu+\ell v_0\text{.}\) Summation over the \(r\)th roots of unity gives
\begin{equation*} \frac1r\sum_{g\in G}\chi_{\xi-\eta}(g)=\begin{cases}1,&\xi-\eta\in M,\\0,&\xi-\eta\notin M.\end{cases} \end{equation*}
Every \(f_\eta\) has the prescribed orders and remains supported in \(NP\text{.}\) At least one component is nonzero.
🔗

Aside

This proof uses the \(r\) distinct roots of unity and the scalar \(1/r\text{.}\) If the characteristic divides \(r\text{,}\) these steps fail and \(Y\mapsto Y^r\) is not étale. The later proof instead uses polynomial interpolation at arbitrary distinct points.
🔗
Multiply a nonzero component by the unit \(\chi_{-\eta}\text{.}\) Its support is in \(M\text{.}\) In coordinates \(U=\chi_u\) and \(Z=\chi_v=Y^r\text{,}\) there is a nonzero Laurent polynomial \(F\) such that
\begin{equation*} \chi_{-\eta}f_\eta=F(U,Z). \end{equation*}
The map is \((X,Y)\mapsto(X,Y^r)\text{,}\) with derivative \(\operatorname{diag}(1,r)\) at the identity. It is locally biholomorphic there, and hence \(\ord_{(1,1)}F\geq m\text{.}\)
🔗
Let \(A\) be the real matrix with columns \(u,v\text{.}\) The support of \(F\) is contained in \(\Delta=A^{-1}(NP-\eta)\text{.}\) Since \(\det A=r\text{,}\) its area is \(N^2V/(2r)\text{.}\) The second coordinate of \(A^{-1}\xi\) is \(\det(u,\xi)/r\text{,}\) so its vertical range is \(N\sqrt{V/r}\text{.}\) Translation by \(\eta\) does not change either value. Apply Lemma 8.1 with \(\rho=N\sqrt{V/r}\text{.}\) It gives \(\ord_{(1,1)}F\leq N\sqrt{V/r}<m\text{,}\) a contradiction.
