Lemma 5.1.

Under the hypothetical universal property, \(\ker J_\tau\ne0\) for every fixed \(0<\tau<1\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗

Proof.
For each positive integer \(\nu\text{,}\) choose distinct numbers \(\xi_{1,\nu},\ldots,\xi_{q,\nu}\) tending to zero. The last paragraph of Chapter 4 gives a nonzero \(F_\nu\in\W_\tau\) with multiplicity at least \(m\) at \((\xi_{\alpha,\nu},0)\text{.}\) Fix a norm on the finite-dimensional space \(\W_\tau\text{,}\) normalize \(F_\nu\text{,}\) and take a convergent subsequence. Its limit \(F_0\) has norm one.
🔗
For each \(b<m\text{,}\) the holomorphic function \(h_{b,\nu}(x)=\partial_y^bH_{F_\nu}(x,0)\) has a zero of order at least \(m-b\) at every \(\xi_{\alpha,\nu}\text{.}\) The functions converge locally uniformly, with their derivatives, to \(h_{b,0}\text{.}\) If this limit is nonzero, the argument principle on a small circle around zero gives \(\ord_0h_{b,0}\geq q(m-b)\text{;}\) if it vanishes identically, the same derivative conditions hold. Hence all the derivatives indexed by \(I\) vanish on \(F_0\text{,}\) so \(J_\tau(F_0)=0\text{.}\)
🔗

Aside

After \(b\) fiber derivatives, each of the \(q\) separate points contributes a zero of order \(m-b\) in one variable. The resulting conditions are those of the ideal \((x^q,y)^m\text{.}\) This is a statement about specified mixed coefficients, not the addition of ordinary surface multiplicities.
