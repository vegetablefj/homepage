Proposition 7.4.

Let \(N,m,r\) be positive integers. If the \(J^{m-1}\) evaluation on \(\mathcal M(NP_t)\) is injective at some \(r\) distinct points of \(\mathbb T\text{,}\) then the corresponding evaluation on \(\mathcal M(NQ_t)\) is injective at some \(r\) distinct points of \(\mathbb T\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗

Proof.
Use torus coordinates \((x,z)=(u,v/u)\text{,}\) so that \(x^\alpha z^\beta=u^{\alpha-\beta}v^\beta\text{.}\) For each nonempty integer slice \(q\) of \(NQ_t\text{,}\) let \(\beta_q^-\) and \(\beta_q^+\) be its least and greatest integer heights. Convexity implies that every integer between them occurs. The slice has a basis
\begin{equation*} u^qv^{\beta_q^-}(v-1)^h,\qquad 0\leq h\leq\beta_q^+-\beta_q^-. \end{equation*}
Expansion in powers of \(v\) gives a triangular change of basis with diagonal entries one. By Lemma 7.3,
\begin{equation*} h\leq\beta_q^+-\beta_q^-\leq N\ell_t(q/N), \end{equation*}
so each pair \((q,h)\) lies in \(NP_t\cap\Z^2\text{.}\) These pairs are distinct.
🔗
Let \((u_i,y_i)\) be an injective test tuple for \(\mathcal M(NP_t)\) in coordinates \((u,y)\text{.}\) Substitute \(v=1+sy\) into the displayed basis and divide the member indexed by \(h\) by \(s^h\text{.}\) For positive \(s\) tending to zero, the functions become
\begin{equation*} u^q(1+sy)^{\beta_q^-}y^h\longrightarrow u^qy^h. \end{equation*}
Choose bounded neighborhoods of the finitely many points on which \(u\) stays away from zero. For small \(s\text{,}\) one has \(|sy|<1/2\) there. The displayed convergence is locally uniform, including for negative Laurent powers. The Cauchy formula gives convergence of all derivatives of order at most \(m-1\text{.}\)
🔗
The limiting monomials span a subspace of \(\mathcal M(NP_t)\text{,}\) so their jet matrix has full column rank. By Lemma 2.2, the rescaled functions also have full column rank for sufficiently small \(s>0\text{.}\) They form a rescaled pullback of a basis of \(\mathcal M(NQ_t)\text{.}\)
🔗
The corresponding points in the original torus are
\begin{equation*} \left(u_i,\frac{1+sy_i}{u_i}\right),\qquad1\leq i\leq r. \end{equation*}
They are nonzero and distinct. The coordinate map has Jacobian determinant \(s/u\ne0\text{,}\) so it preserves orders of vanishing. The full column rank is exactly the claimed injectivity.
