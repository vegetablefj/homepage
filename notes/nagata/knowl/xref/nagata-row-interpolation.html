Lemma 6.1.

Let \(S\subset\C^2\) be finite, with coordinates \((j,K)\text{.}\) Suppose every represented row has \(N_K\leq m\) and, for each integer \(1\leq t\leq m\text{,}\)
\begin{equation*} \#\{K:N_K\geq t\}\leq q(m-t+1). \end{equation*}
Then the restrictions of \(\mathcal P_{q,m}\) give every function \(S\to\C\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗

Proof.
Order the represented heights as \(K_1,\ldots,K_s\) so that their row sizes \(N_i\) are nonincreasing. The first \(i\) rows have size at least \(N_i\text{,}\) whence \(i\leq q(m-N_i+1)\text{.}\) Suppose values on the first \(i-1\) rows have been matched. Put \(Q_i(K)=\prod_{a<i}(K-K_a)\text{.}\) Since \(Q_i(K_i)\ne0\) and the \(j\)-coordinates in row \(i\) are distinct, a polynomial \(p_i(J)\) of degree at most \(N_i-1\) can supply the required correction on this row. The polynomial \(Q_i(K)p_i(J)\) vanishes on preceding rows.
🔗
Every monomial in this correction has \(b\leq N_i-1<m\) and \(\ell\leq i-1<q(m-N_i+1)\leq q(m-b)\text{.}\) Therefore the correction lies in \(\mathcal P_{q,m}\text{.}\) Repeating this step interpolates the prescribed function.
