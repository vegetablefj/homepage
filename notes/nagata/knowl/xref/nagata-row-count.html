Lemma 6.2.

If \(q(1-\lambda)m\geq1\text{,}\) then the exponent set \(S\) satisfies the hypotheses of Lemma 6.1.
🔗
🔗

Proof.
Each horizontal slice of \(mD_\lambda+(0,U_0)\) has length at most \(m\lambda<m\text{.}\) The integer \(j\)-positions in a row therefore number at most \(m\text{.}\) A row containing at least \(t\) positions requires a nonempty slice of length at least \(t-1\text{.}\) If \(t-1>m\lambda\text{,}\) there are no such rows. Otherwise their integer heights lie in an interval of length \(q(m\lambda-t+1)\text{.}\) An interval of length \(L\) contains at most \(L+1\) integers. Thus
\begin{equation*} \#\{K:N_K\geq t\}\leq q(m\lambda-t+1)+1\leq q(m-t+1), \end{equation*}
where the last inequality is precisely \(q(1-\lambda)m\geq1\text{.}\)
🔗

Aside

Passing from an interval length to its number of integer heights adds at most one. The strict inequality \(\lambda<1\) leaves a gap of size \(q(1-\lambda)m\text{.}\) Taking a common power increases this gap without changing \(r\) or \(d/m\text{.}\)
