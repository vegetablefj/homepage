Proposition 3.3.

If \(r=e^2\) with \(e\geq4\text{,}\) then \(\mathsf U_r(d,m)\) is impossible whenever \(d\leq em\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗

Proof.
Take a smooth plane curve \(B\) of degree \(e\text{.}\) Its genus is positive, and \(\deg M=e^2=r\text{.}\) Choose distinct points \(a_i\) such that, for \(P=\sum_i a_i\text{,}\) the degree-zero bundle \(A=M-P\) is nontorsion. Fixing \(r-1\) points and varying the last gives uncountably many distinct bundles, whereas the torsion subgroup of \(\Pic^0(B)\) is countable. Distinctness follows because a linear equivalence between two different single points would give a degree-one map to \(\Pp^1\text{,}\) contradicting positive genus.
🔗
The restriction sequence from the plane gives \(h^0(B,M)=\binom{e+2}{2}-1=(e^2+3e)/2<e^2\text{.}\) Choose normal vectors \(c_i\) outside the image of the evaluation map \(H^0(B,M)\to\bigoplus_iM_{a_i}\text{.}\) Let \(F=\sum f_jw^j\) be given by Proposition 3.2, and let \(j\) be its largest nonzero exponent. Since \(d\leq em\text{,}\) one has \(j\leq m\text{.}\) The required vanishing of \(H^1(\Pp^2,\cO)\) is included in [Sta] (Tag 01XT).
🔗

Aside

If the highest nonzero fiber degree were below \(m\text{,}\) the equality case would give a section of a nontrivial degree-zero bundle. The nontorsion choice forces the highest degree to be \(m\text{.}\) The coefficient \(f_{m-1}\) is then compared with the chosen normal vectors.
🔗
If \(j<m\text{,}\) differentiating \(j\) times in the fiber shows that \(f_j\) vanishes to order at least \(m-j\) at each \(a_i\text{.}\) The degree satisfies
\begin{equation*} \deg((d-ej)L)=e(d-ej)\leq e^2(m-j). \end{equation*}
Strict inequality is impossible by the zero count. Equality forces \(d=em\) and a nonzero section of \((m-j)A\text{,}\) which is impossible because \(A\) is nontorsion. Thus \(j=m\) and \(d=em\text{.}\)
🔗
Now \(f_m\) is a nonzero constant. On the fiber at \(a_i\text{,}\) multiplicity \(m\) at \(c_i\) forces \(F(w)=f_m(w-c_i)^m\text{.}\) Comparison of the coefficients of \(w^{m-1}\) gives \(f_{m-1}(a_i)=-mf_mc_i\text{.}\) But \(f_{m-1}\in H^0(B,M)\text{,}\) contradicting the choice of the vector \((c_i)\text{.}\)
