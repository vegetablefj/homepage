Lemma 12.3.

Let \(Y\) be smooth, integral and projective of dimension \(n\geq2\text{,}\) let \(A\) be ample, and let \(q_1,\ldots,q_r\) be distinct points. Then
\begin{equation*} \varepsilon(Y,A;q_1,\ldots,q_r)\leq(A^n/r)^{1/n}. \end{equation*}
🔗
🔗

Proof.
Write \(\varepsilon=\varepsilon(Y,A;q_1,\ldots,q_r)\text{.}\) Blow up the marked points, with \(\pi:\widetilde Y\to Y\) and exceptional divisors \(E_1,\ldots,E_r\text{.}\) For every rational \(0\leq s<\varepsilon\text{,}\) the class
\begin{equation*} D_s=\pi^*A-s\sum_iE_i \end{equation*}
is nef. On an exceptional divisor its restriction is \(s\cO_{\Pp^{n-1}}(1)\text{.}\) Every other integral curve is the strict transform \(\widetilde C\) of an integral curve \(C\subset Y\text{,}\) and
\begin{equation*} D_s\cdot\widetilde C=A\cdot C-s\sum_i\mult_{q_i}C\geq0. \end{equation*}
🔗
The exceptional intersection formula used here includes singular curves. The ambient Rees algebra surjects onto the Rees algebra of the restricted point ideal on \(C\text{.}\) Its Proj is integral and agrees with \(C\) away from the marked points, so its closed image is the strict transform. At a marked point with local ring \((R,\mathfrak n)\text{,}\) its exceptional fiber is
\begin{equation*} \operatorname{Proj}(\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak n}R). \end{equation*}
This is the effective Cartier divisor cut out by \(E_i\) on \(\widetilde C\text{.}\) Its length is \(e(R)\text{,}\) because the eventual value of \(\dim_k\mathfrak n^t/\mathfrak n^{t+1}\) is both its length and the Hilbert--Samuel multiplicity. Therefore \(E_i\cdot\widetilde C=\mult_{q_i}C\text{.}\) If \(q_i\notin C\text{,}\) both sides are zero.
🔗
A nef real divisor on a projective variety is a limit of ample classes and has nonnegative top self-intersection. The exceptional divisors are disjoint, \(\pi^*A\) restricts trivially to each, and \(E_i^n=(-1)^{n-1}\text{.}\) Hence
\begin{equation*} 0\leq D_s^n=A^n-rs^n. \end{equation*}
If \(\varepsilon>(A^n/r)^{1/n}\text{,}\) a rational \(s\) strictly between these numbers contradicts this inequality. If \(\varepsilon=0\text{,}\) the assertion holds because \(A^n>0\text{.}\)
