Proposition 3.2.

If \(\mathsf U_r(d,m)\) holds, then for any distinct \(a_1,\ldots,a_r\in B\) and arbitrary normal vectors \(c_i\in M_{a_i}\text{,}\) there is a nonzero polynomial section \(F\) of the displayed form with multiplicity at least \(m\) at each \((a_i,c_i)\in\Tot(M)\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗

Proof.
Choose an equation \(G_B\) of \(B\) and move each \(a_i\) along an affine line \(p_i(s)\) whose first normal derivative is \(c_i\text{.}\) For \(s\ne0\text{,}\) the universal property gives a degree-\(d\) form vanishing to order \(m\) at every \(p_i(s)\text{.}\) The equations for its coefficients are linear over \(\C(s)\text{.}\) Their rank is deficient for every general complex \(s\text{,}\) hence also over \(\C(s)\text{.}\) After clearing denominators and dividing out a common power of \(s\text{,}\) obtain a polynomial family \(S(s)=\sum_{\alpha\geq0}s^\alpha S_\alpha\) with \(S_0\ne0\text{.}\)
🔗
For each nonzero coefficient write \(S_\alpha=G_B^{j_\alpha}R_\alpha\text{,}\) where \(R_\alpha|_B\ne0\text{.}\) Let \(u\) be the smallest value of \(\alpha+j_\alpha\text{.}\) Rescale a local equation \(t\) of \(B\) by \(t=sw\) and divide the family by \(s^u\text{.}\) Its limit is
\begin{equation*} F(w)=\sum_{\alpha+j_\alpha=u}(R_\alpha|_B)w^{j_\alpha}. \end{equation*}
The terms have distinct exponents, so \(F\ne0\text{.}\) Since \(R_\alpha\) has degree \(d-ej_\alpha\text{,}\) the coefficients lie in the asserted spaces. The change of frame for \(t\) is the transition for the normal bundle, so these local expressions glue.
🔗

Aside

A term \(s^\alpha G_B^jR\) acquires a factor \(s^{\alpha+j}\) after \(t=sw\text{.}\) Choosing the least value of \(\alpha+j\) retains the first nonzero normal limit. For other degenerations and fat-point limits, see also [CM98, Section 2] and [Eva07, Sections 2 and 6].
🔗
In coordinates centered at the moving points, the rescaled family is holomorphic through \(s=0\text{.}\) Its derivatives of total order below \(m\) vanish for general \(s\ne0\) and hence at \(s=0\text{.}\) The normal coordinates of the moving points tend to \(c_i\text{.}\) Therefore \(F\) has the required multiplicities.
