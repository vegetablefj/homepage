Proposition 4.1.

Fix a coordinate disk in \(B\setminus P\) and a frame of \(A\) there. If \(\mathsf U_r(d,m)\) holds, then for every choice of \(q=r-9\) distinct points \(b_1,\ldots,b_q\) in that disk, a nonzero section of \(\W\) has multiplicity at least \(m\) at all the points \((b_\alpha,1)\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗

Proof.
Apply Proposition 3.2 with zero normal displacements at \(a_1,\ldots,a_9\) and displacements \(\sigma_P(b_\alpha)\cdot1\) at the other points. For the resulting polynomial \(F(w)=\sum f_jw^j\text{,}\) the nine zero displacements give \(f_j\in H^0(B,(d-3j)L-(m-j)P)\) for \(j<m\text{.}\) Therefore
\begin{equation*} \widetilde F(v)=\sigma_P^{-m}F(\sigma_Pv)=\sum_j f_j\sigma_P^{j-m}v^j \end{equation*}
extends across \(P\) and lies in \(\W\text{.}\) It is nonzero because the substitution is invertible off \(P\text{.}\) Near each \(b_\alpha\text{,}\) the substitution and multiplication by \(\sigma_P^{-m}\) are invertible, so they preserve multiplicity.
