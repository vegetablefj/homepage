Lemma 11.1.

Let \(P\subset\mathbb R^n\text{,}\) with \(n\geq1\text{,}\) be compact and convex with nonempty interior. Suppose that finite sets \(F_N\subset NP\cap\mathbb Z^n\text{,}\) indexed by all integers \(N\geq1\text{,}\) satisfy
\begin{equation*} F_N+F_{N'}\subset F_{N+N'}\qquad(N,N'\geq1) \end{equation*}
and
\begin{equation*} \#F_N=\operatorname{vol}(P)N^n+o(N^n). \end{equation*}
For every compact set \(K\subset\operatorname{int}P\text{,}\) there is an integer \(N_K\) such that
\begin{equation*} NK\cap\mathbb Z^n\subset F_N\qquad(N\geq N_K). \end{equation*}
🔗
🔗

Proof.
The boundary of a full-dimensional compact convex set has measure zero. Lattice-point counting by Riemann sums gives
\begin{equation*} \#(NP\cap\mathbb Z^n)=\operatorname{vol}(P)N^n+o(N^n). \end{equation*}
Thus the sets of missing lattice points
\begin{equation*} H_N=(NP\cap\mathbb Z^n)\setminus F_N \end{equation*}
satisfy \(\#H_N=o(N^n)\text{.}\)
🔗
Assume \(K\) is nonempty. Choose \(\rho>0\) so that the closed ball of radius \(\rho\) about each point of \(K\) lies in \(P\text{.}\) For a large integer \(N\text{,}\) let \(N_1=\lfloor N/2\rfloor\) and \(N_2=N-N_1\text{.}\) Fix \(y\in NK\cap\mathbb Z^n\) and write \(z=y/N\text{.}\) Every lattice vector \(y_1\) satisfying
\begin{equation*} \|y_1-N_1z\|\leq\rho\min(N_1,N_2) \end{equation*}
defines a decomposition \(y=y_1+y_2\) with \(y_i\in N_iP\cap\mathbb Z^n\text{.}\) Indeed, \(y_1/N_1\) and \(y_2/N_2\) both lie within distance \(\rho\) of \(z\text{.}\)
🔗
The ball from which \(y_1\) is chosen contains at least \(cN^n\) lattice points for some \(c>0\) and all sufficiently large \(N\text{,}\) uniformly in its center. An inscribed cube gives this estimate. At most \(\#H_{N_1}\) choices have \(y_1\notin F_{N_1}\text{.}\) At most \(\#H_{N_2}\) choices have \(y_2\notin F_{N_2}\text{,}\) since \(y_1\mapsto y-y_1\) is injective. Their sum is \(o(N^n)\text{,}\) so one decomposition has \(y_i\in F_{N_i}\text{.}\) Addition across degrees gives \(y\in F_N\text{.}\) The bounds are uniform over \(y\in NK\cap\mathbb Z^n\text{,}\) which proves the assertion.
🔗

Aside

A leading lattice-point count alone permits isolated holes in every degree. An interior point here has at least \(cN^n\) possible decompositions, whereas only \(o(N^n)\) use a missing summand. The addition property then forces the point itself into \(F_N\text{.}\)
