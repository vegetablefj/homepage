    Chapter 10 Changing two simplex intercepts

    Let \(k\) be algebraically closed. The construction in this chapter replaces two intercepts \(A,B\) by \(w,AB/w\text{,}\) for every sufficiently small prescribed \(w>0\text{.}\) It preserves the number of exponents in each degree and addition between degrees. Separation of jets by the resulting monomials implies separation by the original monomials. We use [OAI-P, Section 4], whose formal argument also proves the corresponding step of [OAI-H, Proposition 4.1].
    Fix \(A,B>0\text{,}\) and let \(H=AB\) and \(\delta=\max\{3/A,2/B\}\text{.}\) Define \(W=\delta H=\max\{2A,3B\}\) and \(c=1/\delta\text{.}\) Then \(cW=H\text{,}\) and
    \begin{equation*} T=\delta^2H=\max\{9B/A,4A/B\}\geq6. \end{equation*}

    Section 10.1 The two branches of a node

    For \(t>0\text{,}\) compare pairs \((\alpha,\beta)\in\Z_{\geq0}^2\) by the lexicographic order of \((\alpha+t\beta,\beta)\text{.}\) This is a monomial well-order because only finitely many nonnegative integral pairs have bounded first weight. Denote the least exponent in this order by \(\nu_t\text{.}\)

    Lemma 10.1.

    There are formal coordinates \(\xi,\eta\) at the origin of \(\mathbb A_k^2\) such that
    \begin{equation*} y^2+xy-x^3=\xi\eta. \end{equation*}
    For \(t>0\text{,}\) let \(a=Wt/(1+t)\) and \(b=W/(1+t)\text{,}\) and let
    \begin{equation*} Q=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(a,0),(c,c),(0,b)\}. \end{equation*}
    If \(\kappa\geq0\) and a nonzero polynomial \(f\in k[x,y]\) has all its exponents in \(\kappa\Delta(A,B)\text{,}\) then its expansion in these coordinates satisfies \(\nu_t(f)\in\kappa Q\text{.}\)

    Proof.
    Let \(g=y^2+xy-x^3\text{.}\) The parametrization
    \begin{equation*} (x,y)=\bigl(v(v+1),v^2(v+1)\bigr) \end{equation*}
    identifies the part of \(g=0\) where \(x\ne0\) with \(\operatorname{Spec}k[v,1/(v(v+1))]\text{.}\) Its inverse is \(v=y/x\text{.}\) A polynomial vanishes under this parametrization precisely when it is divisible by \(g\text{:}\) after inverting \(x\text{,}\) its class is zero in the displayed coordinate ring, and \(g\) is relatively prime to \(x\) in \(k[x,y]\text{.}\)
    The polynomial \(Z^2+Z-x\) has distinct simple roots \(0,-1\) modulo \(x\text{.}\) Hensel lifting gives roots \(r_1,r_2\in k[[x]]\) with these constant terms. Define \(\eta=y-xr_1(x)\) and \(\xi=y-xr_2(x)\text{.}\) Their linear terms are \(y\) and \(y+x\text{,}\) so \(\xi,\eta\) are formal coordinates and \(g=\xi\eta\text{.}\) At \(v=0\text{,}\) uniqueness of the lifted root gives \(r_1(x(v))=v\text{.}\) Thus this branch is \(\eta=0\text{,}\) and \(\xi\) has order one in \(v\text{.}\) At \(v=-1\text{,}\) the branch is \(\xi=0\text{,}\) and \(\eta\) has order one in \(v+1\text{.}\) The roots and the linear terms remain distinct in characteristic two.1
    Factor \(f=g^ef_0\text{,}\) where \(e\geq0\) and \(g\nmid f_0\text{.}\) Give \(x,y\) the weights \(1/A,1/B\text{,}\) and let \(\deg_{A,B}\) be the maximum weight of a polynomial’s monomials. The highest weight forms of two nonzero polynomials have nonzero product, so this degree is additive on products. Since \(1/A+1/B\leq\delta\) and the monomials \(x^3,y^2\) occur in \(g\text{,}\) its degree is \(\delta\text{.}\) Therefore
    \begin{equation*} \deg_{A,B}(f_0)=\deg_{A,B}(f)-e\deg_{A,B}(g)\leq\kappa-e\delta. \end{equation*}
    In particular, \(e\delta\leq\kappa\text{.}\) The polynomial
    \begin{equation*} F_0(v)=f_0\bigl(v(v+1),v^2(v+1)\bigr) \end{equation*}
    is nonzero, and
    \begin{equation*} \deg F_0\leq W(\kappa-e\delta). \end{equation*}
    The last inequality follows from \(2i+3j\leq W(i/A+j/B)\) for each monomial \(x^iy^j\) in \(f_0\text{.}\)
    Let \(\nu_t(f_0)=(\alpha,\beta)\) and \(\mu=\alpha+t\beta\text{.}\) Every term in the expansion has weight at least \(\mu\text{.}\) Restriction to \(\eta=0\) has order at least \(\mu\text{,}\) and restriction to \(\xi=0\) has order at least \(\mu/t\text{.}\) These restrictions are nonzero because they are the expansions of the nonzero polynomial \(F_0\) at two distinct points. Therefore
    \begin{equation*} (1+1/t)\mu\leq\operatorname{ord}_{v=0}F_0+\operatorname{ord}_{v=-1}F_0\leq\deg F_0\leq W(\kappa-e\delta). \end{equation*}
    Equivalently, \((\alpha,\beta)\in(\kappa-e\delta)\Delta(a,b)\text{.}\) Multiplicativity of least exponents gives
    \begin{equation*} \nu_t(f)=(e,e)+\nu_t(f_0)=e\delta(c,c)+\nu_t(f_0)\in\kappa Q. \end{equation*}
    If \(\kappa=0\text{,}\) the polynomial is constant and its least exponent is zero.
    Choose \(t>T\text{.}\) Then \(a+b=W\) and
    \begin{equation*} \frac ca+\frac cb=\frac{(1+t)^2}{Tt}>1. \end{equation*}
    Thus \(Q\) is a quadrilateral with the vertices in the order of Lemma 10.1. Its area is \(c(a+b)/2=H/2\text{.}\) By Lemma 7.3, the shear \((\alpha,\beta)\mapsto(\alpha-\beta,\beta)\) followed by compression in the second coordinate gives the triangular bound
    \begin{equation*} P=\operatorname{conv}\{(-b,0),(a,0),(0,c)\}. \end{equation*}
    Its area is also \(H/2\text{.}\)

    Section 10.2 A primitive lattice direction

    Lemma 10.2.

    If \(w>0\) and \(\delta w<1/16\text{,}\) there are \(t>T\) and a primitive vector \(u=(i,j)\in\Z^2\) such that the values of \(z\mapsto\det(u,z)\) at the three vertices of \(P\) are distinct and their range has length \(H/w\text{.}\)

    Proof.
    Let \(R=H/w\) and \(a_0=WT/(1+T)\text{.}\) As \(t\) varies over \((T,\infty)\text{,}\) the value \(a=Wt/(1+t)\) varies over \((a_0,W)\text{.}\) For \(u=(i,j)\text{,}\) the three heights are \(jb,-ja,i/\delta\text{.}\) We choose \(j>0\) with \(jW\leq R/2\text{,}\) and then choose \(i\) coprime to \(j\) and \(a\in(a_0,W)\) so that
    \begin{equation*} ja+i/\delta=R. \end{equation*}
    This equality implies \(i/\delta-jb=R-jW>0\text{.}\)
    Let \(j\) be the largest power of two at most \(R/(2W)=1/(2\delta w)\text{.}\) Then
    \begin{equation*} j>\frac1{4\delta w}>4. \end{equation*}
    The open interval
    \begin{equation*} \bigl(\delta(R-jW),\,\delta(R-ja_0)\bigr) \end{equation*}
    has length \(\delta j(W-a_0)=jT/(1+T)>2\text{.}\) Choose an odd integer \(i\) in this interval. Since \(j\) is a power of two, \((i,j)\) is primitive. The number \(a=(R-i/\delta)/j\) lies in \((a_0,W)\) and determines a unique \(t>T\text{.}\) The heights satisfy
    \begin{equation*} -ja<jb<i/\delta, \end{equation*}
    because \(i/\delta-jb=R-jW\geq R/2\text{.}\) Their range is \(i/\delta+ja=R\text{.}\)

    Section 10.3 The resulting simplex

    Proposition 10.3.

    Let \(n\geq2\text{,}\) let \(A,B,a_3,\ldots,a_n>0\text{,}\) and let \((S_N)_{N\geq1}\) be a graded family of finite subsets of \(\Z^n\) with
    \begin{equation*} S_N\subset N\Delta(A,B,a_3,\ldots,a_n). \end{equation*}
    Let \(\delta=\max\{3/A,2/B\}\text{.}\) For every \(w>0\) satisfying \(\delta w<1/16\text{,}\) there is a graded family \((S'_N)_{N\geq1}\) such that
    \begin{equation*} S'_N\subset N\Delta(w,AB/w,a_3,\ldots,a_n). \end{equation*}
    Moreover, \(\#S'_N=\#S_N\) in every degree. For every \(N,r\geq1\) and \(\ell\geq0\text{,}\) separation of jets through degree \(\ell\) by \(\mathcal M(S'_N)\) at some \(r\) distinct points of \(\mathbb T_k^n\) implies separation of the same jets by \(\mathcal M(S_N)\) at some \(r\) distinct torus points. For \(n=2\text{,}\) the lists of further intercepts are empty.

    Proof.
    Use the constants \(H,W,c,T\) defined at the beginning of the chapter, and choose \(t,u\) by Lemma 10.2. These choices and the formal coordinates of Lemma 10.1 are fixed in every degree. For a degree \(N\) and a label \(\gamma=(\gamma_3,\ldots,\gamma_n)\) occurring in \(S_N\text{,}\) let
    \begin{equation*} \kappa=N-\sum_{h=3}^n\gamma_h/a_h\geq0. \end{equation*}
    The pair exponents with label \(\gamma\) lie in \(\kappa\Delta(A,B)\text{.}\) Take their polynomial span in \(k[x,y]\text{,}\) expand in \(\xi,\eta\text{,}\) and replace the row by its possible least exponents for \(\nu_t\text{,}\) retaining \(\gamma\text{.}\)
    The labelled-space assertion of Lemma 2.3 preserves the cardinality in each degree and the graded property: products of row spaces belong to the summed degree and label, and least exponents add. For a fixed degree, choose bases with distinct least exponents in all the finitely many rows. By Lemma 2.4, one positive integral weight vector exposes all their least terms. Then Lemma 2.5 transfers separation from the least monomials to the polynomial row spaces simultaneously, including all label variables. The polynomial functions can be evaluated at points in the dense open torus of \(\mathbb A_k^2\times\mathbb T_k^{n-2}\text{,}\) so this is the required implication for the original monomial space. The exposing weights can depend on \(N\text{;}\) the formal coordinates and the order defining the graded family do not.2
    By Lemma 10.1, the pair exponents of each new row lie in \(\kappa Q\text{.}\) Apply the shear described above, followed by compression in the second coordinate. The slice-length formulas give the bound \(\kappa P\text{.}\) By Lemma 2.7 and Lemma 2.8, both operations preserve cardinality, gradedness, and the implication for jet separation.
    Complete the primitive vector \(u\) to a basis \((u,v)\) of \(\Z^2\) with \(\det(u,v)=1\text{,}\) and use the coordinates in this basis. Its second coordinate is \(z\mapsto\det(u,z)\text{.}\) Write \(q_0<q_1<q_2\) for the three vertex heights of the transformed triangle. Its horizontal slice length is linear from zero at \(q_0\) to a maximum at \(q_1\text{,}\) and linear back to zero at \(q_2\text{.}\) Since its area is \(H/2\) and \(q_2-q_0=R=H/w\text{,}\) the maximum slice length is \(w\text{.}\)
    Compression in the first coordinate gives the triangular bound
    \begin{equation*} \operatorname{conv}\{(0,q_0),(w,q_1),(0,q_2)\}. \end{equation*}
    At first coordinate \(h\in[0,w]\text{,}\) its vertical slice has length \(R(1-h/w)\text{.}\) Compression in the second coordinate therefore gives \(\Delta(w,R)\text{.}\) The lattice changes and all three compressions commute with multiplication of the bounding polygons by \(\kappa\text{,}\) so each label has the bound \(\kappa\Delta(w,R)\text{.}\) If \(\kappa=0\text{,}\) the only possible pair exponent is zero, and each operation sends it to zero.
    Restoring the labels gives
    \begin{equation*} S'_N\subset N\Delta(w,R,a_3,\ldots,a_n). \end{equation*}
    Every operation preserves cardinality and addition between degrees and transfers separation to the preceding space, which proves the assertion.
