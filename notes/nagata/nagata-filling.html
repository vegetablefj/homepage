    Chapter 11 Interior lattice points and interpolation

    Section 11.1 Interior lattice points

    The counting argument in [OAI-H, Lemma 5.1] and [OAI-P, Lemma 5.1] uses addition across degrees to determine which lattice points occur in the interior of a bounding convex set. For a more general approximation theorem for additive semigroups, see also [KK12, Theorem 1.6].

    Lemma 11.1.

    Let \(P\subset\mathbb R^n\text{,}\) with \(n\geq1\text{,}\) be compact and convex with nonempty interior. Suppose that finite sets \(F_N\subset NP\cap\mathbb Z^n\text{,}\) indexed by all integers \(N\geq1\text{,}\) satisfy
    \begin{equation*} F_N+F_{N'}\subset F_{N+N'}\qquad(N,N'\geq1) \end{equation*}
    and
    \begin{equation*} \#F_N=\operatorname{vol}(P)N^n+o(N^n). \end{equation*}
    For every compact set \(K\subset\operatorname{int}P\text{,}\) there is an integer \(N_K\) such that
    \begin{equation*} NK\cap\mathbb Z^n\subset F_N\qquad(N\geq N_K). \end{equation*}

    Proof.
    The boundary of a full-dimensional compact convex set has measure zero. Lattice-point counting by Riemann sums gives
    \begin{equation*} \#(NP\cap\mathbb Z^n)=\operatorname{vol}(P)N^n+o(N^n). \end{equation*}
    Thus the sets of missing lattice points
    \begin{equation*} H_N=(NP\cap\mathbb Z^n)\setminus F_N \end{equation*}
    satisfy \(\#H_N=o(N^n)\text{.}\)
    Assume \(K\) is nonempty. Choose \(\rho>0\) so that the closed ball of radius \(\rho\) about each point of \(K\) lies in \(P\text{.}\) For a large integer \(N\text{,}\) let \(N_1=\lfloor N/2\rfloor\) and \(N_2=N-N_1\text{.}\) Fix \(y\in NK\cap\mathbb Z^n\) and write \(z=y/N\text{.}\) Every lattice vector \(y_1\) satisfying
    \begin{equation*} \|y_1-N_1z\|\leq\rho\min(N_1,N_2) \end{equation*}
    defines a decomposition \(y=y_1+y_2\) with \(y_i\in N_iP\cap\mathbb Z^n\text{.}\) Indeed, \(y_1/N_1\) and \(y_2/N_2\) both lie within distance \(\rho\) of \(z\text{.}\)
    The ball from which \(y_1\) is chosen contains at least \(cN^n\) lattice points for some \(c>0\) and all sufficiently large \(N\text{,}\) uniformly in its center. An inscribed cube gives this estimate. At most \(\#H_{N_1}\) choices have \(y_1\notin F_{N_1}\text{.}\) At most \(\#H_{N_2}\) choices have \(y_2\notin F_{N_2}\text{,}\) since \(y_1\mapsto y-y_1\) is injective. Their sum is \(o(N^n)\text{,}\) so one decomposition has \(y_i\in F_{N_i}\text{.}\) Addition across degrees gives \(y\in F_N\text{.}\) The bounds are uniform over \(y\in NK\cap\mathbb Z^n\text{,}\) which proves the assertion.1

    Section 11.2 Interpolation in an elongated simplex

    The interpolation argument in [OAI-H, Lemma 5.2] and [OAI-P, Lemma 5.2] uses the Chinese remainder theorem in the last coordinate.

    Lemma 11.2.

    Let \(n,r\geq1\) and \(\ell\geq0\) be integers. Let \(c_1,\ldots,c_r\in k^\times\) be distinct, and define
    \begin{equation*} E_{r,\ell}=\left\{(\beta,b)\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}^{n-1}\times\mathbb Z_{\geq0}:|\beta|+\frac br<\ell+1\right\},\qquad |\beta|=\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\beta_j. \end{equation*}
    Then \(\mathcal M(E_{r,\ell})\) separates jets through degree \(\ell\) at
    \begin{equation*} q_i=(1,\ldots,1,c_i)\in\mathbb T_k^n,\qquad 1\leq i\leq r. \end{equation*}
    For \(n=1\text{,}\) the tuple \(\beta\) is empty and \(|\beta|=0\text{.}\)

    Proof.
    Write the coordinates as \((x',Y)\text{,}\) with \(x'=(x_1,\ldots,x_{n-1})\text{.}\) A prescribed jet through degree \(\ell\) at \(q_i\) has a unique expression
    \begin{equation*} \sum_{|\alpha|\leq\ell}(x'-1)^\alpha h_{i,\alpha}(Y-c_i),\qquad\deg h_{i,\alpha}\leq\ell-|\alpha|. \end{equation*}
    For each \(\alpha\text{,}\) the ideals \((Y-c_i)^{\ell+1-|\alpha|}\) are pairwise comaximal. Their product is generated by a monic polynomial of degree \(r(\ell+1-|\alpha|)\text{.}\) The Chinese remainder theorem therefore gives a polynomial \(f_\alpha(Y)\) such that
    \begin{equation*} f_\alpha(Y)\equiv h_{i,\alpha}(Y-c_i)\pmod{(Y-c_i)^{\ell+1-|\alpha|}}\qquad(1\leq i\leq r) \end{equation*}
    and \(\deg f_\alpha<r(\ell+1-|\alpha|)\text{.}\)
    The polynomial
    \begin{equation*} f(x',Y)=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq\ell}(x'-1)^\alpha f_\alpha(Y) \end{equation*}
    has all the prescribed jets. Every monomial \((x')^\beta Y^b\) in its \(\alpha\)-summand satisfies \(\beta_j\leq\alpha_j\) for every \(j\) and \(b<r(\ell+1-|\alpha|)\text{.}\) Hence
    \begin{equation*} |\beta|+\frac br\leq|\alpha|+\frac br<\ell+1. \end{equation*}
    Thus \(f\in\mathcal M(E_{r,\ell})\text{.}\) The proof uses coefficients of powers of local parameters, so it applies in every characteristic.

    Section 11.3 Jets from additive exponent sets

    A smaller simplex translated into the interior allows Lemma 11.1 to be applied to the exponents in Lemma 11.2. This is [OAI-H, Proposition 5.3] and the interpolation part of [OAI-P, Proposition 5.3].

    Proposition 11.3.

    Let \(n\geq2\) and \(r\geq1\) be integers, let \(w>0\text{,}\) and let
    \begin{equation*} P=\Delta(w,\ldots,w,rw). \end{equation*}
    Suppose that finite sets \(F_N\subset NP\cap\mathbb Z^n\text{,}\) for every integer \(N\geq1\text{,}\) satisfy
    \begin{equation*} F_N+F_{N'}\subset F_{N+N'}\qquad(N,N'\geq1) \end{equation*}
    and
    \begin{equation*} \#F_N=\operatorname{vol}(P)N^n+o(N^n). \end{equation*}
    For every real \(0<\theta<w\) and every choice of distinct \(c_1,\ldots,c_r\in k^\times\text{,}\) the space \(\mathcal M(F_N)\) separates jets through degree \(\lfloor N\theta\rfloor\) at the points \((1,\ldots,1,c_i)\) for all sufficiently large \(N\text{.}\)

    Proof.
    Choose \(\lambda\) with \(\theta/w<\lambda<1\text{.}\) Choose a vector \(a\in\mathbb R_{>0}^n\) satisfying
    \begin{equation*} \frac{a_1+\cdots+a_{n-1}}w+\frac{a_n}{rw}<1-\lambda. \end{equation*}
    Then \(a+\lambda P\subset\operatorname{int}P\text{.}\) Choose \(a_N\in N^{-1}\mathbb Z^n\) with \(a_N\to a\text{.}\) For large \(N\text{,}\) all translated simplices \(a_N+\lambda P\) lie in a single compact subset \(K\) of \(\operatorname{int}P\text{.}\) By Lemma 11.1,
    \begin{equation*} Na_N+(N\lambda P\cap\mathbb Z^n)\subset F_N \end{equation*}
    for all sufficiently large \(N\text{.}\)
    2
    Let \(\ell=\lfloor N\theta\rfloor\text{.}\) Since \(\theta<\lambda w\text{,}\) we have \(\ell+1\leq N\lambda w\) for large \(N\text{.}\) For every \((\beta,b)\in E_{r,\ell}\text{,}\)
    \begin{equation*} \frac{|\beta|}{\lambda w}+\frac{b}{r\lambda w}<\frac{\ell+1}{\lambda w}\leq N. \end{equation*}
    Thus \(E_{r,\ell}\subset N\lambda P\cap\mathbb Z^n\text{,}\) and Lemma 11.2 proves jet separation by the monomials with these exponents. Multiplication by the Laurent monomial of exponent \(Na_N\in\mathbb Z^n\) induces an isomorphism of each jet algebra at a torus point, because this monomial is a unit there. The translated monomials belong to \(\mathcal M(F_N)\text{,}\) which proves the required surjectivity.

    1

    A leading lattice-point count alone permits isolated holes in every degree. An interior point here has at least \(cN^n\) possible decompositions, whereas only \(o(N^n)\) use a missing summand. The addition property then forces the point itself into \(F_N\text{.}\)

    2

    Contraction and positive translation place the interpolation simplex in a compact subset of the interior. The interior-filling lemma applies there. The translation becomes a Laurent monomial factor. This factor is a unit at every torus point, so it gives an automorphism of each jet algebra.
