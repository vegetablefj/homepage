    Chapter 8 Finite subgroups and maximal constants on surfaces

    Section 8.1 Multiplicity in a triangle

    For a bounded real set, its vertical range is the length of its projection to the second coordinate axis. The following estimate is [OAI-S], Lemma 4.1.

    Lemma 8.1.

    Let \(\rho>0\text{,}\) and let \(\Delta\subset\mathbb R^2\) be a triangle of area \(\rho^2/2\) and vertical range \(\rho\text{.}\) Every nonzero \(F\in\C[U^{\pm1},Z^{\pm1}]\) supported in \(\Delta\) satisfies \(\ord_{(1,1)}F\leq\rho\text{.}\)

    Proof.
    Let \(\ell(y)\) be the horizontal slice length at height \(y\text{,}\) and let \(y_0\leq y_1\leq y_2\) be the vertex heights. If the heights are distinct, \(\ell\) increases linearly from zero at \(y_0\) to its maximum at \(y_1\text{,}\) then decreases linearly to zero at \(y_2\text{.}\) If two heights coincide, there is one linear segment. In both cases the area is one half the vertical range times the greatest slice length. Thus this greatest length is \(\rho\text{.}\) For \(0\leq e\leq\rho\text{,}\) the heights with \(\ell(y)\geq e\) form an interval of length \(\rho-e\text{.}\) When the heights are distinct, its endpoints are
    \begin{equation*} y_0+\frac e\rho(y_1-y_0),\qquad y_2-\frac e\rho(y_2-y_1). \end{equation*}
    The same formula applies when adjacent heights coincide.
    Write \(F=\sum_qf_q(U)Z^q\) with each displayed row nonzero, and let \(e\) be the least order of these rows at \(U=1\text{.}\) Factor each row as \(f_q=(U-1)^eg_q\) and let \(G=\sum_qg_q(U)Z^q\text{.}\) Then \(F=(U-1)^eG\) and \(G(1,Z)\ne0\text{.}\) The factors \(g_q\) remain Laurent polynomials. The horizontal exponent span of \(f_q\) is exactly \(e\) plus that of \(g_q\text{,}\) because the two extreme coefficients in the product are nonzero. Therefore \(\ell(q)\geq e\) for every row, and the heights in \(G\) span an interval of length at most \(\rho-e\text{.}\)
    Specialization to \(U=1\) can remove rows but cannot add a height. A power of \(Z\text{,}\) which is a unit at \(Z=1\text{,}\) turns \(G(1,Z)\) into a polynomial of degree at most \(\rho-e\text{.}\) Hence
    \begin{equation*} \ord_{(1,1)}G\leq\ord_1G(1,Z)\leq\rho-e. \end{equation*}
    The first inequality follows because restriction cannot produce a term of smaller total degree in the Taylor series. Orders add under multiplication of nonzero germs, giving \(\ord_{(1,1)}F\leq\rho\text{.}\) The orders and exponent spans are integers; the vertices and \(\rho\) need not be integral.

    Section 8.2 A subgroup of prescribed size

    Continue to write \(\mathbb T=(\C^\times)^2\) and \(\mathcal M(\Omega)=\mathcal M(\Omega\cap\Z^2)\) for a bounded real diagram. For \(u,\xi\in\mathbb R^2\text{,}\) write \(\det(u,\xi)\) for their oriented determinant. An integer vector is primitive if its coordinates are coprime.

    Proposition 8.2.

    Let \(V>0\text{,}\) let \(r\) be a positive integer, and let \(P\subset\mathbb R^2\) be a triangle of area \(V/2\text{.}\) Suppose that a primitive \(u\in\Z^2\) satisfies
    \begin{equation*} \max_{\xi\in P}\det(u,\xi)-\min_{\xi\in P}\det(u,\xi)=\sqrt{rV}. \end{equation*}
    There is a subgroup \(G\subset\mathbb T\) with exactly \(r\) points such that, for all positive integers \(N,m\) with \(m>N\sqrt{V/r}\text{,}\) the \(J^{m-1}\) evaluation on \(\mathcal M(NP)\) at \(G\) is injective.

    Proof.
    Complete \(u\) to an integer basis \((u,v_0)\) with \(\det(u,v_0)=1\text{.}\) Let \(v=rv_0\) and \(M=\Z u+\Z v\text{,}\) a sublattice of index \(r\text{.}\) For \(\eta=(\eta_1,\eta_2)\text{,}\) write \(\chi_\eta(x,z)=x^{\eta_1}z^{\eta_2}\text{.}\) Define
    \begin{equation*} G=\{p\in\mathbb T:\chi_\eta(p)=1\text{ for every }\eta\in M\}. \end{equation*}
    The characters \(X=\chi_u\) and \(Y=\chi_{v_0}\) are torus coordinates because their exponent matrix is unimodular. In these coordinates, \(G=\{X=1,\ Y^r=1\}\text{,}\) which has exactly \(r\) distinct complex points.
    Suppose that \(0\ne f\in\mathcal M(NP)\) vanishes to order at least \(m\) at all points of \(G\text{.}\) For \(g\in G\text{,}\) let \((\tau_gf)(p)=f(gp)\text{.}\) Translation permutes \(G\) and preserves local orders. For a coset \(\eta+M\text{,}\) the sum
    \begin{equation*} f_\eta=\frac1r\sum_{g\in G}\chi_\eta(g)^{-1}\tau_gf \end{equation*}
    retains precisely the monomials in that coset. Indeed, write \(\xi-\eta=nu+\ell v_0\text{.}\) Summation over the \(r\)th roots of unity gives
    \begin{equation*} \frac1r\sum_{g\in G}\chi_{\xi-\eta}(g)=\begin{cases}1,&\xi-\eta\in M,\\0,&\xi-\eta\notin M.\end{cases} \end{equation*}
    Every \(f_\eta\) has the prescribed orders and remains supported in \(NP\text{.}\) At least one component is nonzero.
    1
    Multiply a nonzero component by the unit \(\chi_{-\eta}\text{.}\) Its support is in \(M\text{.}\) In coordinates \(U=\chi_u\) and \(Z=\chi_v=Y^r\text{,}\) there is a nonzero Laurent polynomial \(F\) such that
    \begin{equation*} \chi_{-\eta}f_\eta=F(U,Z). \end{equation*}
    The map is \((X,Y)\mapsto(X,Y^r)\text{,}\) with derivative \(\operatorname{diag}(1,r)\) at the identity. It is locally biholomorphic there, and hence \(\ord_{(1,1)}F\geq m\text{.}\)
    Let \(A\) be the real matrix with columns \(u,v\text{.}\) The support of \(F\) is contained in \(\Delta=A^{-1}(NP-\eta)\text{.}\) Since \(\det A=r\text{,}\) its area is \(N^2V/(2r)\text{.}\) The second coordinate of \(A^{-1}\xi\) is \(\det(u,\xi)/r\text{,}\) so its vertical range is \(N\sqrt{V/r}\text{.}\) Translation by \(\eta\) does not change either value. Apply Lemma 8.1 with \(\rho=N\sqrt{V/r}\text{.}\) It gives \(\ord_{(1,1)}F\leq N\sqrt{V/r}<m\text{,}\) a contradiction.
    This is [OAI-S], Proposition 4.2.

    Section 8.3 A direction for every large point count

    Return to the fixed surface data \(D,V,T\) and the triangles \(P_t\) of Section 7.3. The freedom in \(t\) gives the determinant range required in Proposition 8.2.

    Lemma 8.3.

    For every sufficiently large integer \(r\text{,}\) there are \(t>T\) and a primitive \(u\in\Z^2\) such that the determinant range of \(P_t\) is \(\sqrt{rV}\text{.}\) The threshold depends only on \(D,V,T\text{.}\)

    Proof.
    Let \(W=DV\text{,}\) \(a_0=WT/(1+T)\text{,}\) and \(R=\sqrt{rV}\text{.}\) As \(t\) varies in \((T,\infty)\text{,}\) \(a=Wt/(1+t)\) varies continuously and bijectively in \((a_0,W)\text{.}\) For sufficiently large \(R\text{,}\) choose \(j\) to be the largest power of two at most \(R/(2W)\text{.}\) Then
    \begin{equation*} \frac{R}{4W}<j\leq\frac{R}{2W},\qquad jW<R. \end{equation*}
    The open interval
    \begin{equation*} I_r=\bigl(D(R-jW),\ D(R-ja_0)\bigr) \end{equation*}
    has length \(Dj(W-a_0)>DR/[4(1+T)]\text{,}\) which exceeds two for all sufficiently large \(r\text{.}\) It contains an odd integer \(i>0\text{.}\) Since \(j\) is a power of two, \(u=(i,j)\) is primitive.
    The strict inequalities defining \(I_r\) give
    \begin{equation*} a_*=\frac{R-i/D}{j}\in(a_0,W). \end{equation*}
    Choose the unique \(t>T\) for which \(a=a_*\text{.}\) With \(b=W-a\text{,}\) one has \(i/D+ja=R\) and \(i/D-jb=R-jW>0\text{.}\) The determinant values at the vertices \((-b,0)\text{,}\) \((a,0)\text{,}\) and \((0,1/D)\) are \(jb\text{,}\) \(-ja\text{,}\) and \(i/D\text{.}\) Their minimum is \(-ja\) and maximum is \(i/D\text{,}\) giving range \(R\text{.}\) All restrictions on \(r\) depend only on the fixed data.

    Corollary 8.4.

    There is an integer \(r_0\) such that, for each \(r\geq r_0\text{,}\) one can choose one \(t>T\) and one subgroup \(G\subset\mathbb T\) with \(r\) points for which the \(J^{m-1}\) evaluation on \(\mathcal M(NP_t)\) is injective for every pair of positive integers \(N,m\) satisfying \(m>N\sqrt{V/r}\text{.}\)

    Proof.
    For fixed \(r\geq r_0\text{,}\) choose \(t,u\) by Lemma 8.3 and apply Proposition 8.2. The choices of \(t,G\) precede the choices of \(N,m\text{.}\)
    The direction and the corollary are [OAI-S], Lemma 4.3 and Corollary 4.4.

    Section 8.4 Homogeneous vanishing and nefness

    The surface argument converts the injective tests with equal vanishing orders into an inequality for every curve. It uses positive-square divisors, which need not be nef.

    Lemma 8.5.

    Let \(Y\) be a smooth integral complex projective surface, let \(A\) be a nef real divisor class, and let \(F\) be a rational Cartier divisor. If \(F^2>0\) and \(A\cdot F>0\text{,}\) then \(H^0(Y,\cO_Y(qF))\ne0\) for every sufficiently large positive integer \(q\) divisible by the denominators of \(F\text{.}\)

    Proof.
    For such integers, Serre duality gives
    \begin{equation*} h^2(Y,\cO_Y(qF))=h^0(Y,\cO_Y(K_Y-qF)). \end{equation*}
    For large \(q\text{,}\) \(A\cdot(K_Y-qF)<0\text{.}\) An effective divisor has nonnegative intersection with \(A\text{,}\) so this \(h^2\) is zero. Surface Riemann–Roch gives
    \begin{equation*} h^0(Y,\cO_Y(qF))\geq\chi(Y,\cO_Y(qF))=\frac{q^2F^2-qF\cdot K_Y}{2}+\chi(Y,\cO_Y)>0 \end{equation*}
    for sufficiently large \(q\text{,}\) because \(F^2>0\text{.}\) Serre duality is [Sta] (Tag 0FVV); the surface Riemann–Roch formula is [Har77, Theorem V.1.6].
    2

    Proposition 8.6.

    Let \(p_1,\ldots,p_r\) be distinct points of \(S\text{,}\) set \(w=\sqrt{V/r}\text{,}\) and suppose that, for all positive integers \(N,m\) with \(m>Nw\text{,}\) no nonzero section of \(NL\) vanishes to order at least \(m\) at all these points. On their blow-up \(\pi:Y\to S\text{,}\) with exceptional curves \(E_i\text{,}\) the class
    \begin{equation*} D_0=\pi^*L-w\sum_{i=1}^rE_i \end{equation*}
    is nef.

    Proof.
    Choose a Cartier divisor representing \(L\) and write \(A=\pi^*L\) and \(E=\sum_iE_i\text{.}\) The class \(A\) is nef. The blowup intersection identities \(A^2=V\text{,}\) \(A\cdot E_i=0\text{,}\) and \(E_i\cdot E_j=-\delta_{ij}\) give \(D_0^2=0\text{.}\) If \(D_0\) is not nef, there is an integral curve \(C\) with \(D_0\cdot C<0\text{.}\) Since \(D_0\cdot E_i=w>0\text{,}\) \(C\) is not exceptional, and \(A\cdot C>0\text{.}\)
    For a sufficiently small positive rational \(\delta\text{,}\)
    \begin{equation*} (D_0-\delta C)^2=-2\delta D_0\cdot C+\delta^2C^2>0 \end{equation*}
    and \(A\cdot(D_0-\delta C)=V-\delta A\cdot C>0\text{.}\) Fix such \(\delta\text{.}\) Continuity gives a rational number \(s>w\text{,}\) close enough to \(w\text{,}\) such that
    \begin{equation*} F=A-sE-\delta C \end{equation*}
    satisfies \(F^2>0\) and \(A\cdot F>0\text{.}\)
    Choose a sufficiently large positive integer \(N\) divisible by the denominators of \(s\) and \(\delta\text{.}\) By Lemma 8.5, there is an effective divisor \(R\sim NF\text{.}\) Since \(N\delta\) and \(m=Ns\) are positive integers,
    \begin{equation*} R+N\delta C\sim NA-mE \end{equation*}
    is effective. For the blow-up of the distinct smooth points,
    \begin{equation*} \pi_*\cO_Y(-mE)=\bigcap_{i=1}^r\mathcal I_{p_i}^{\,m}. \end{equation*}
    The projection formula gives a nonzero section of \(NL\) vanishing to order at least \(m\) at every \(p_i\text{.}\) This contradicts \(m=Ns>Nw\text{.}\) The local ideal identity for this surface blowup is [Sta] (Tag 0AGS), part (4).
    The displayed blow-up identity expresses the local multiplicity condition: a section of a pullback line bundle descends across the missing points because \(S\) is normal, and the exceptional order of a local equation is its ordinary multiplicity. Thus its order along each \(E_i\) is precisely membership in \(\mathcal I_{p_i}^{\,m}\text{.}\)
    The positive-square argument and the nefness criterion are [OAI-S], Lemma 5.2 and Proposition 5.3.

    Section 8.5 Maximality at very general tuples

    Fix the nodal data from Lemma 7.1 and let \(r_0\) be the threshold in Corollary 8.4. For every \(r\geq r_0\text{,}\) that corollary gives injective torus jets for all positive integers \(N,m\) with \(m>N\sqrt{V/r}\text{.}\) By Corollary 7.5, the evaluation on \(H^0(S,NL)\) is injective at some tuple of \(r\) distinct surface points for each such pair.
    Let \(U_r(S)\) be the configuration space of distinct ordered tuples. Each injectivity condition defines a nonempty Zariski-open subset by Lemma 2.1. There are countably many pairs \(N,m\text{,}\) so the conditions hold together outside a countable union \(Z_r\) of proper closed subsets of \(U_r(S)\text{,}\) with nonempty complement. At every tuple in this complement, Proposition 8.6 gives nefness of
    \begin{equation*} \pi^*L-\sqrt{\frac Vr}\sum_iE_i. \end{equation*}
    The volume bound Lemma 12.3 gives the reverse inequality for the Seshadri constant. Therefore
    \begin{equation*} \varepsilon(S,L;p_1,\ldots,p_r)=\sqrt{\frac Vr}. \end{equation*}
    This proves the surface case of Theorem 1.2, namely [OAI-S], Theorem 1.1. The threshold depends only on \(S,L\text{;}\) it was fixed before the section degree and vanishing order.
