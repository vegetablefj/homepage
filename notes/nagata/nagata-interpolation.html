    Chapter 6 Interpolation and completion

    The row interpolation and polygon estimates are those of [OAI-N, Section 5]. Their degree bounds depend on the distribution of row sizes, rather than only on the total number of exponents.

    Section 6.1 Interpolation by rows

    For the exponent set \(S\subset\Z^2\text{,}\) let \(N_K=\#\{j:(j,K)\in S\}\text{.}\) The rows of \(B_0\) are evaluations on \(S\) of the monomials spanning
    \begin{equation*} \mathcal P_{q,m}=\operatorname{span}_{\C}\{K^\ell J^b:0\leq b<m,\ 0\leq\ell<q(m-b)\}. \end{equation*}
    Thus \(B_0\) has full column rank exactly when these polynomials interpolate arbitrary values on \(S\text{.}\)

    Lemma 6.1.

    Let \(S\subset\C^2\) be finite, with coordinates \((j,K)\text{.}\) Suppose every represented row has \(N_K\leq m\) and, for each integer \(1\leq t\leq m\text{,}\)
    \begin{equation*} \#\{K:N_K\geq t\}\leq q(m-t+1). \end{equation*}
    Then the restrictions of \(\mathcal P_{q,m}\) give every function \(S\to\C\text{.}\)

    Proof.
    Order the represented heights as \(K_1,\ldots,K_s\) so that their row sizes \(N_i\) are nonincreasing. The first \(i\) rows have size at least \(N_i\text{,}\) whence \(i\leq q(m-N_i+1)\text{.}\) Suppose values on the first \(i-1\) rows have been matched. Put \(Q_i(K)=\prod_{a<i}(K-K_a)\text{.}\) Since \(Q_i(K_i)\ne0\) and the \(j\)-coordinates in row \(i\) are distinct, a polynomial \(p_i(J)\) of degree at most \(N_i-1\) can supply the required correction on this row. The polynomial \(Q_i(K)p_i(J)\) vanishes on preceding rows.
    Every monomial in this correction has \(b\leq N_i-1<m\) and \(\ell\leq i-1<q(m-N_i+1)\leq q(m-b)\text{.}\) Therefore the correction lies in \(\mathcal P_{q,m}\text{.}\) Repeating this step interpolates the prescribed function.

    Section 6.2 The polygon containing the exponents

    Recall \(a=3(\sqrt r-3)\text{,}\) \(h=m\rho\text{,}\) and \(q=2a+a^2/9\text{.}\) Let \(U_0=h(a-\Delta)/9+ma\) and use normalized coordinates \(X=j/m\) and \(Y=(K-U_0)/m\text{.}\) The endpoint formulas for \(S\) in Chapter 5 imply
    \begin{equation*} \begin{aligned} 0&\leq X\leq1+\rho/9,\\ -aX+\Delta(X-1)_+&\leq Y\leq-aX+\rho+(\Delta-9)(X-1)_+, \end{aligned} \end{equation*}
    where \(t_+=\max\{t,0\}\text{.}\) For \(j\leq m\text{,}\) these inequalities are the interval \(U_0-aj<K<U_0-aj+h\text{.}\) For \(j>m\text{,}\) they follow by substituting \(n_j=h-9(j-m)\) in the lower endpoint \(n_j(a-\Delta)/9\) and its upper endpoint. The possible degree-zero block lies at the common endpoint of the two bounds.
    Choose \(\lambda\in(0,1)\) sufficiently close to one that
    \begin{equation*} \rho+\frac{\Delta\rho}{9}<\lambda a-9(1-\lambda). \end{equation*}
    This is possible because \(\rho+\Delta\rho/9<a\text{.}\) Define
    \begin{equation*} D_\lambda=\{(X,Y):X\geq0,\ -aX\leq Y\leq-aX+\lambda a-9(X-\lambda)_+\}. \end{equation*}
    The preceding bounds give
    \begin{equation*} S\subseteq mD_\lambda+(0,U_0). \end{equation*}
    Indeed, their lower bound is at least \(-aX\text{.}\) If \(X\leq1\text{,}\) the displayed condition on \(\lambda\) makes the upper boundary of \(D_\lambda\) exceed \(-aX+\rho\text{.}\) If \(1\leq X\leq1+\rho/9\text{,}\) subtracting \(-aX-9(X-1)\) from both upper bounds reduces the comparison to \(\rho+\Delta(X-1)\leq\rho+\Delta\rho/9<\lambda a-9(1-\lambda)\text{.}\)
    The polygon satisfies \(D_\lambda=\lambda D_1\text{.}\) Its unscaled form has vertices \((0,0)\text{,}\) \((0,a)\text{,}\) \((1,0)\text{,}\) and \((1+a/9,-a(1+a/9))\text{.}\) Directly solving its two boundary equations shows that the length of its horizontal slice at height \(Y\) is
    \begin{equation*} w(Y)=\begin{cases} 1-Y/a,&0\leq Y\leq a,\\ 1+Y/[a(1+a/9)],&-a(1+a/9)\leq Y\leq0. \end{cases} \end{equation*}
    The maximum slice length is one. After scaling by \(m\lambda\) and translating by \(U_0\text{,}\) the heights of nonempty slices of length at least \(s\text{,}\) for \(0\leq s\leq m\lambda\text{,}\) form an interval of length
    \begin{equation*} q(m\lambda-s). \end{equation*}
    The identity \(q=2a+a^2/9\) is exactly the sum of the two slopes in this length calculation.

    Section 6.3 The integer row bounds

    Lemma 6.2.

    If \(q(1-\lambda)m\geq1\text{,}\) then the exponent set \(S\) satisfies the hypotheses of Lemma 6.1.

    Proof.
    Each horizontal slice of \(mD_\lambda+(0,U_0)\) has length at most \(m\lambda<m\text{.}\) The integer \(j\)-positions in a row therefore number at most \(m\text{.}\) A row containing at least \(t\) positions requires a nonempty slice of length at least \(t-1\text{.}\) If \(t-1>m\lambda\text{,}\) there are no such rows. Otherwise their integer heights lie in an interval of length \(q(m\lambda-t+1)\text{.}\) An interval of length \(L\) contains at most \(L+1\) integers. Thus
    \begin{equation*} \#\{K:N_K\geq t\}\leq q(m\lambda-t+1)+1\leq q(m-t+1), \end{equation*}
    where the last inequality is precisely \(q(1-\lambda)m\geq1\text{.}\)
    1
    Under this size condition, Lemma 6.2 and Lemma 6.1 show that \(B_0\) has full column rank. By Chapter 5, \(J_\tau\) is then injective for all sufficiently small \(\tau>0\text{.}\)

    Section 6.4 Completion

    Proposition 6.3.

    For every \(r\geq10\text{,}\) no universal system \(\mathsf U_r(d,m)\) exists with positive integers \(d,m\) satisfying \(d/m\leq\sqrt r\text{.}\)

    Proof.
    If \(r\) is a square, Proposition 3.3 gives the result. Otherwise, \(d/m<\sqrt r\) because \(d/m\) is rational. By Lemma 3.4, also \(d/m>3\text{.}\) Choose \(\Delta\) and \(\lambda\) as above. The implication \(\mathsf U_r(d,m)\Rightarrow\mathsf U_r(Nd,Nm)\) in Lemma 3.1 permits a common multiple large enough that \(q(1-\lambda)Nm\geq1\text{.}\) Taking this multiple does not change the ratio or the choices of \(\rho\text{,}\) \(\Delta\text{,}\) and \(\lambda\text{.}\) Apply the construction to this pair.
    For every fixed \(\tau\text{,}\) Lemma 5.1 gives \(\ker J_\tau\ne0\text{.}\) The row count and interpolation give full column rank of \(B_0\text{,}\) so \(J_\tau\) is injective for all sufficiently small positive \(\tau\text{.}\) This contradiction excludes the universal system.
    By Lemma 3.1, Proposition 6.3 proves Theorem 1.1 simultaneously for all degrees and all vectors of nonnegative multiplicities. The argument concerns every nonzero form, including those defining reducible or nonreduced curves.

    Section 6.5 The plane Seshadri constant

    Let \(X\) be the blowup at a tuple covered by Theorem 1.1, with \(H\) the pullback of a line and \(E_i\) the exceptional curves. The class \(N_r=\sqrt r H-\sum_iE_i\) has square zero. It intersects each exceptional curve in one and the strict transform of any plane curve \(C\) in \(\sqrt r\deg C-\sum_i\mult_{p_i}C>0\text{.}\) Hence it is strictly nef. Dividing by \(\sqrt r\) gives the lower bound \(1/\sqrt r\) for the Seshadri constant, and Lemma 12.3 gives the matching upper bound. Thus
    \begin{equation*} \varepsilon(\Pp^2,\cO_{\Pp^2}(1);p_1,\ldots,p_r)=\frac1{\sqrt r}. \end{equation*}
    This is [OAI-N, Corollary 6.2].

    Section 6.6 The plane inequality over other fields

    Corollary 6.4.

    Let \(k\) be an uncountable algebraically closed field of characteristic zero. For every \(r\geq10\text{,}\) the strict inequality of Theorem 1.1 holds outside a countable union of proper closed subsets of \(U_r(\Pp_k^2)\text{,}\) with nonempty complement.

    Proof.
    The incidence loci in Lemma 3.1 are defined over \(\mathbb Q\) by minors of the matrices of Taylor coefficients. Whenever \(d\sqrt r\leq\sum_i m_i\text{,}\) the corresponding locus is proper over \(\C\) by Theorem 1.1. A nonzero rational defining minor remains nonzero under field extension, so the locus is proper over \(k\text{.}\) Exclude these loci for the countably many violating pairs \((d,\mathbf m)\text{.}\) Because \(k\) is uncountable, it contains \(2r\) elements algebraically independent over \(\mathbb Q\text{:}\) at each successive choice the algebraic closure of the field generated by the preceding elements is countable. Using them as affine coordinates gives a tuple avoiding every excluded locus and every diagonal. This proves [OAI-N, Corollary 6.1].
