    Chapter 7 Nodal divisors on polarized surfaces

    Section 7.1 A nodal divisor

    Let \(S\) be a smooth integral complex projective surface and let \(L\) be ample. Write \(V=L^2\text{.}\) The argument in [OAI-S, Section 3] uses an integral member of an ample linear system with two transverse branches at a point.

    Lemma 7.1.

    There are a positive integer \(D\text{,}\) an integral divisor \(B\in|DL|\text{,}\) and a point \(p\in B\) such that \(B\) is smooth away from \(p\) and has two smooth transverse analytic branches at \(p\text{.}\)

    Proof.
    Choose \(e>0\) such that \(eL\) is very ample, embed \(S\) in \(\Pp^q\) by this bundle, and choose \(p\in S\text{.}\) In homogeneous coordinates with \(p=[1:0:\cdots:0]\text{,}\) the restrictions of \(X_1,\ldots,X_q\) generate \(\mathcal I_p\otimes\cO_S(eL)\text{.}\) Away from \(p\) one of these restrictions is nonzero. Near \(p\text{,}\) their ratios with \(X_0\) generate the maximal ideal because the embedding is a closed immersion.
    Let \(\rho:X=\operatorname{Bl}_pS\to S\) be the blow-up and let \(F\) be its exceptional curve. These generators give a surjection
    \begin{equation*} \operatorname{Sym}^{\bullet}(\cO_S^{\oplus q})\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{j\geq0}\mathcal I_p^j\otimes\cO_S(jeL). \end{equation*}
    Relative \(\operatorname{Proj}\) gives a closed immersion \(X\hookrightarrow S\times\Pp^{q-1}\text{,}\) the closure of the graph of projection from \(p\text{.}\) The second factor pulls \(\cO(1)\) back to \(\cO_X(e\rho^*L-F)\text{.}\) The restriction of \(\cO(1,1)\) from \(\Pp^q\times\Pp^{q-1}\) is therefore the very ample bundle
    \begin{equation*} \mathcal A=\cO_X(2e\rho^*L-F). \end{equation*}
    The Rees-algebra description is [Sta] (Tag 01OF).
    A general member \(\widetilde B\in|2\mathcal A|\) is smooth and integral by Bertini. We can require that it meet \(F\) transversely at two distinct points. Indeed, \(\mathcal A|_F\simeq\cO_{\Pp^1}(1)\text{.}\) Its global sections generate this bundle and hence span its two-dimensional space of sections. Products of these sections show that
    \begin{equation*} H^0(X,2\mathcal A)\longrightarrow H^0(F,\cO_{\Pp^1}(2)) \end{equation*}
    is surjective. A section with two distinct zeros on \(F\) is consequently a nonempty open condition, which can be imposed with the Bertini conditions. Bertini for the very ample embedding is [Sta] (Tag 0G4G).
    Set \(D=4e\) and \(B=\rho(\widetilde B)\text{.}\) The divisor class gives \(B\in|DL|\text{,}\) and \(B\) is integral because \(\widetilde B\) is integral. The blow-down is an isomorphism away from \(F\text{,}\) so \(B\) is smooth away from \(p\text{.}\) In a local chart the blow-down is \((a,u)\mapsto(a,au)\text{.}\) A curve transverse to \(F=\{a=0\}\) has \(a\) as a local parameter and maps to a smooth branch. The two distinct intersection points with \(F\) give different tangent directions. Their images are the required transverse branches.
    This is [OAI-S], Lemma 3.1. Fix \(D,B,p\) as in Lemma 7.1. Choose holomorphic coordinates \((x,z)\) in which the two branches are the coordinate axes, and choose a nonvanishing local frame of \(L\text{.}\) A defining section \(\tau\in H^0(S,DL)\) then has local expression
    \begin{equation*} \tau=u(x,z)xz,\qquad u(0,0)\ne0. \end{equation*}
    Sections of tensor powers of \(L\) are represented in the corresponding powers of this frame.

    Section 7.2 Weighted initial exponents

    Fix \(T>1\) such that \((1+t)^2>D^2Vt\) for every \(t>T\text{.}\) Let \(c=1/D\) and \(W=DV\text{.}\) For \(t>T\text{,}\) define \(a=Wt/(1+t)\) and \(b=W/(1+t)\text{,}\) and let
    \begin{equation*} \begin{aligned}\Delta_t&=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(a,0),(0,b)\},\\Q_t&=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(a,0),(c,c),(0,b)\}.\end{aligned} \end{equation*}
    The inequality for \(t\) is equivalent to \(c/a+c/b>1\text{.}\) Thus \(Q_t\) is a quadrilateral with the displayed vertices in cyclic order. Its area is \(c(a+b)/2=V/2\text{.}\)
    For a nonzero holomorphic germ, let \(\nu_t\) be its least Taylor exponent when \((\alpha,\beta)\) are ordered by increasing \(\alpha+t\beta\) and, for equal weight, by increasing \(\beta\text{.}\) This order is compatible with addition. Initial exponents therefore add under multiplication, and a unit has initial exponent \((0,0)\text{.}\)

    Proposition 7.2.

    For every positive integer \(N\text{,}\) every \(t>T\text{,}\) and every nonzero \(s\in H^0(S,NL)\text{,}\) one has \(\nu_t(s)\in NQ_t\text{.}\) The section space has a basis with distinct initial exponents in \(NQ_t\text{.}\)

    Proof.
    Suppose that the divisor \(C\) of \(s\) does not contain \(B\text{,}\) and write \(f(x,z)\) for its local function. Neither \(f(x,0)\) nor \(f(0,z)\) vanishes identically. The intersection \(C\cap B\) is a finite closed subset of the integral algebraic curve \(B\text{,}\) whereas an identically zero restriction would place an entire analytic branch in this subset.1
    Let \(\ell_x=\ord_0f(x,0)\text{,}\) \(\ell_z=\ord_0f(0,z)\text{,}\) and let \(v\) be the weight of \(\nu_t(f)\text{.}\) The pure terms on the two branches give \(\ell_x\geq v\) and \(\ell_z\geq v/t\text{.}\) Local intersection multiplicity is additive over the two smooth branches, so
    \begin{equation*} v\left(1+\frac1t\right)\leq\ell_x+\ell_z=I_p(C,B)\leq C\cdot B=NDV. \end{equation*}
    The local analytic and algebraic intersection numbers agree because both are the colength of \((f,xz)\) in \(\C[[x,z]]\text{.}\) It follows that \(v\leq Na\text{.}\) Since \(a=tb\text{,}\) this says \(\nu_t(s)\in N\Delta_t\text{.}\)
    For general \(s\text{,}\) let \(j\) be the coefficient of \(B\) in its divisor and let \(N'=N-jD\text{.}\) Division by its defining section gives \(s=\tau^j\sigma\) with \(\sigma\in H^0(S,N'L)\text{,}\) whose effective divisor \(R\) does not contain \(B\text{.}\) Ampleness gives \(N'V=L\cdot R\geq0\text{,}\) hence \(N'\geq0\text{.}\) If \(N'=0\text{,}\) ampleness forces \(R=0\text{,}\) and \(\sigma\) is a nonzero constant. If \(N'>0\text{,}\) the preceding bound gives \(\nu_t(\sigma)\in N'\Delta_t\text{.}\)
    The local expression for \(\tau\) yields \(\nu_t(s)=(j,j)+\nu_t(\sigma)\text{.}\) When \(N'>0\text{,}\)
    \begin{equation*} \frac{\nu_t(s)}N=\frac{N'}N\frac{\nu_t(\sigma)}{N'}+\frac{jD}N(c,c)\in Q_t, \end{equation*}
    because the two coefficients are nonnegative and sum to one. When \(N'=0\text{,}\) the normalized exponent is \((c,c)\text{.}\)
    Restriction of global sections to this local chart is injective by the identity theorem. Finite coefficient elimination, as in Lemma 2.6, gives a basis with distinct initial exponents. Each exponent satisfies the bound just proved.
    The bound is [OAI-S], Proposition 3.2.

    Section 7.3 Diagonal slices

    Write \(\mathbb T=(\C^\times)^2\text{.}\) For a bounded real set \(\Omega\subset\mathbb R^2\text{,}\) we use the local convention \(\mathcal M(\Omega)=\mathcal M(\Omega\cap\Z^2)\) for its Laurent monomial space. Define
    \begin{equation*} P_t=\operatorname{conv}\{(-b,0),(a,0),(0,c)\}. \end{equation*}
    Its area is \(V/2\text{.}\)

    Lemma 7.3.

    Let \(a,b,c>0\) satisfy \(c/a+c/b>1\text{,}\) and let \(Q=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(a,0),(c,c),(0,b)\}\text{.}\) Its slice on \(\alpha-\beta=q\) has length in the \(\beta\) coordinate
    \begin{equation*} \ell(q)=\begin{cases}c(1+q/b),&-b\leq q\leq0,\\c(1-q/a),&0\leq q\leq a.\end{cases} \end{equation*}
    There are no slices outside \([-b,a]\text{.}\) Consequently, the shear followed by vertical compression gives the triangle
    \begin{equation*} \operatorname{conv}\{(-b,0),(a,0),(0,c)\}=\{(q,h):-b\leq q\leq a,\ 0\leq h\leq\ell(q)\}. \end{equation*}

    Proof.
    In coordinates \((q,\beta)=(\alpha-\beta,\beta)\text{,}\) the vertices are \((0,0)\text{,}\) \((a,0)\text{,}\) \((0,c)\text{,}\) and \((-b,b)\text{.}\) For \(0\leq q\leq a\text{,}\) the slice runs from zero to \(c(1-q/a)\text{.}\) For \(-b\leq q\leq0\text{,}\) it runs from \(-q\) to \(c+(c-b)q/b\text{.}\) Subtracting the endpoints gives the asserted lengths. The region between zero and these lengths is the stated triangle.
    For the surface parameters, denote this slice length by \(\ell_t(q)\text{.}\) The same real slice calculation is used for the auxiliary node in Chapter 10.

    Proposition 7.4.

    Let \(N,m,r\) be positive integers. If the \(J^{m-1}\) evaluation on \(\mathcal M(NP_t)\) is injective at some \(r\) distinct points of \(\mathbb T\text{,}\) then the corresponding evaluation on \(\mathcal M(NQ_t)\) is injective at some \(r\) distinct points of \(\mathbb T\text{.}\)

    Proof.
    Use torus coordinates \((x,z)=(u,v/u)\text{,}\) so that \(x^\alpha z^\beta=u^{\alpha-\beta}v^\beta\text{.}\) For each nonempty integer slice \(q\) of \(NQ_t\text{,}\) let \(\beta_q^-\) and \(\beta_q^+\) be its least and greatest integer heights. Convexity implies that every integer between them occurs. The slice has a basis
    \begin{equation*} u^qv^{\beta_q^-}(v-1)^h,\qquad 0\leq h\leq\beta_q^+-\beta_q^-. \end{equation*}
    Expansion in powers of \(v\) gives a triangular change of basis with diagonal entries one. By Lemma 7.3,
    \begin{equation*} h\leq\beta_q^+-\beta_q^-\leq N\ell_t(q/N), \end{equation*}
    so each pair \((q,h)\) lies in \(NP_t\cap\Z^2\text{.}\) These pairs are distinct.
    Let \((u_i,y_i)\) be an injective test tuple for \(\mathcal M(NP_t)\) in coordinates \((u,y)\text{.}\) Substitute \(v=1+sy\) into the displayed basis and divide the member indexed by \(h\) by \(s^h\text{.}\) For positive \(s\) tending to zero, the functions become
    \begin{equation*} u^q(1+sy)^{\beta_q^-}y^h\longrightarrow u^qy^h. \end{equation*}
    Choose bounded neighborhoods of the finitely many points on which \(u\) stays away from zero. For small \(s\text{,}\) one has \(|sy|<1/2\) there. The displayed convergence is locally uniform, including for negative Laurent powers. The Cauchy formula gives convergence of all derivatives of order at most \(m-1\text{.}\)
    The limiting monomials span a subspace of \(\mathcal M(NP_t)\text{,}\) so their jet matrix has full column rank. By Lemma 2.2, the rescaled functions also have full column rank for sufficiently small \(s>0\text{.}\) They form a rescaled pullback of a basis of \(\mathcal M(NQ_t)\text{.}\)
    The corresponding points in the original torus are
    \begin{equation*} \left(u_i,\frac{1+sy_i}{u_i}\right),\qquad1\leq i\leq r. \end{equation*}
    They are nonzero and distinct. The coordinate map has Jacobian determinant \(s/u\ne0\text{,}\) so it preserves orders of vanishing. The full column rank is exactly the claimed injectivity.
    The slice formula and compression are [OAI-S], Lemma 3.3 and Proposition 3.4.

    Section 7.4 Jets of global sections

    Corollary 7.5.

    Let \(N,m,r\) be positive integers. If the \(J^{m-1}\) evaluation on \(\mathcal M(NP_t)\) is injective at some \(r\) distinct torus points, then the \(J^{m-1}\) evaluation on \(H^0(S,NL)\) is injective at some \(r\) distinct points of \(S\text{.}\)

    Proof.
    If \(H^0(S,NL)=0\text{,}\) every tuple works. Otherwise, Proposition 7.4 gives an injective test on \(\mathcal M(NQ_t)\text{.}\) Restrict sections to germs in the fixed chart at \(p\text{.}\) This restriction is injective, and Proposition 7.2 gives distinct initial monomials in \(NQ_t\text{.}\) Their span has injective jets at the torus tuple. The weighted initial-monomial transfer Lemma 2.6 gives injective jets for the section germs at distinct points of the chart, hence for global sections at those points.
    This is [OAI-S], Corollary 3.5. The tuple supplied by this corollary may depend on \(N\) and \(m\text{.}\)
